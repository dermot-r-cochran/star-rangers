Agnete Solvang is a Section Lead of the [Survey Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-survey-corps/), Planetology Branch, and for three years to the early spring of 2826 UCSD she was the Section Lead of the Corps' platform at the far staging in [Deadwater's](/star-rangers/lore/universes/deadwater/) one staged system, the posting the Corps rotates its crews home from early. She did not leave it early. The platform files one return a year to the Survey Archive on [Threshold Station](/star-rangers/lore/threshold-station/), and she filed three of them. The record meets her on the third, the platform's twenty-first, which came in with the relief hull's date on a sheet that had not changed in twenty years and now had: a line called *Zone* under the line called *Membrane*, with the Archive Branch's circular clipped behind it. She wrote *Deadwater* where it had always gone, read the circular standing, and left the new line empty with the reason on the back. The record meets her once more, a season later and a membrane away, reading the receipt. It renders her as *she*.

## Known History

- Service before the platform at two home-side staging sites, from which she filed the same annual return with its membrane line blank, as every home return is. Her certification, in which she learned the difference between a [membrane](/star-rangers/lore/concordant-membranes/) and a [Concordant Zone](/star-rangers/glossary/concordant/) and never afterward had occasion to write it on a form. Where she was born, where she trained and where she was certified, the record does not say.
- Section Lead of the Deadwater platform from about 2823 UCSD, three years and three returns. Her first morning there she read down the chart's route column, star after star, *none* against each in the same hand, and read it most mornings after. In her last year the system boat went out to the fourth planet's moons and came back after nine weeks with rock, ice and a thin atmosphere on the largest, nothing in any of it more or less promising than any moon's, and the launch from the boat's own small lattice at the far end of the run fell the way the platform's fell.
- Early spring 2826 UCSD: the twenty-first return. The platform's state; the dose log; item fourteen, the hourly launch, summarised the only way a thing that has happened eight thousand seven hundred and sixty times since the last return can be; the catalogue's additions; the relief list; and at the head, the membrane line, written. Under it the new line, not written, and on the back, under *Remarks*: *Zone line not filled. The platform has no name for it and has not met its edge. Left for the Archive.* Her initials and the hull's date. On the relief list, seven on the platform and three going home: herself and the Senior on the instrument side at term, and a Deputy of her own branch at seven months of it, with the Medical Corps' initials against his name and nothing else; against her own name the column was blank. She did not look at the chart on her last morning. The bag went home by the route at the sixth hour. See [Left Blank (S01E00C12)](/star-rangers/seasons/s01/e00/s01e00c12/).
- Early summer 2826 UCSD: posted to a staging site on the home side, which had a chart on its table too. The Archive's receipt came, a copy of the head of the sheet with the Archive's marks on it, and for the first time she read one past the first line. *Membrane: Deadwater.* Her hand. *Zone: Deadwater's.* Not her hand, and under it two sets of initials she did not know. The relief list came back with it, stamped; against the Deputy's name the initials, against hers nothing. She put the receipt in the file where receipts went.

## The Work

The return is a year on one sheet, and most of its lines summarise. The one that takes the whole year to write is the shortest: the hourly launch, run under full lattice power and absorbed below the board's threshold within light-minutes, every hour of every day since the platform was crewed, with no second way to summarise it. Above everything, before the station's own name, stands the word the first crews chose for the membrane and nothing more official has displaced, and she wrote it three times. The platform measured for twenty-one years in one system with instruments that came home and passed their audits, and every measurement gave the same answer, and the answer was the one on that line: the medium absorbed, the near stars' light came in with the ordinary constants in it, the rule set held everywhere the platform had been. Nothing the platform had reached had an edge in it.

So she knew what the new line was asking and declined to answer it, for a reason she wrote down. A blank on a home return means the home zone, the one everyone measures in, because the first crews here had needed a word and nobody at home ever had; a blank on this sheet would say the platform's year had been measured where the home stations measured theirs, which was false. A word would say the platform had a name for the zone, and it had none and had not met an edge to name it from. Between a false blank and an unearned word she left the line to the office whose line it was, in the plain terms the Archive prefers. She read the circular standing, because the bag closed at the sixth hour and three years as a Section Lead had taught her that a circular read sitting down took the afternoon.

## Character Notes

She reads a relief list with the second column first. The Medical Corps' column on the Deadwater list carries initials and no word, the word having been dropped some years before she came and nothing written in its place, and she takes the initialled space for what it is, a finding. The blank against her own name is a different blank, and she knows of no sheet in the Corps that tells the two apart, and did not write to ask. The Deputy had done the cleanest work on the moons the platform filed in her time, had asked for nothing, and had twice been found in the platform's night with the chart in front of him, not reading it, saying he was fine. She did not ask him what he was looking at. She had been looking at it for three years and knew.

What she declines is as exact as what she files. She went through the term the posting was supposed to cut short and reached the end of it at the ordinary time with the ordinary word, *term*, and no idea why the column had let her. She does not think it was anything she did, and she did not put it in a column either. She wrote the reason for an empty line and not the reasoning, which the Field Officer who read it saw and thought right, the sheet not being the place. The record has her in one posture more than any other: reading a column for longer than a line needs, the route column her first morning and the receipt her last.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

Her age, her birth year and what she looks like. Where she was born, and her kin. Where she trained and where she was certified; which two home stations she filed from before Deadwater, and which she was posted to after it. The names of the platform, the relief hull and the home staging site, which the record keeps in its formula, *the record does not name which*. The names of the Senior, the Deputy and the Principal whose initials she did not know. Why the Medical Corps' column was blank against her and initialled against the Deputy, which nobody was asked to fill, and whether she has asked since. What the chart's route column meant to her past the reading of it. Whether she will be posted to Deadwater again. Any chapter after the receipt.
