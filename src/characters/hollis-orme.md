Hollis Orme keeps a mooring tower. On [Saltmere](/star-rangers/lore/planets/saltmere/) the towers are not infrastructure in the sense a bridge or a seawall is elsewhere; they are the habitat, running continuously and monitored constantly, and the entire difference between a city segment staying where its residents left it that morning and a segment two kilometres off station by evening. Keeping one tower is his trade, and so, without anyone saying so, is keeping one segment of a city where it is. He is the first person of Saltmere the record names, and the Archive calls him the first of nothing else: a mooring tower is somebody's work, as a parish flock or a settlement's shielding is elsewhere in the record.

He was born on a platform hull of a city-group the record does not name, in about 2782 UCSD, to a family the record does not count back, in a settlement four centuries old whose own account of its founding did not survive. Nobody on Saltmere has ever stood on bedrock, and he has not. The planet's page says that a native asked what *ground* means answers with tension and tether cables rather than dirt, and the record has no reason to think his answer would differ. The founders' colony manifest has never been recovered, and the page says the residents discuss the gap with more shrugging than curiosity, on the reasoning that four hundred years of successfully not drowning has answered the practical half of the question. Whether he shrugs, the record does not say.

The name is given as the record holds it. Saltmere's founders are unknown, and their languages and naming customs are unknown with them; the only name on the world's own page is the world's. What *Orme* owes to a founder's family, to a tower or to a hull, and whether Saltmere keeps a family name at all, the record does not say and has not asked. The record renders him as *he*.

## Known History

- Born on Saltmere, in the Annwyn system, in about 2782 UCSD, on a platform hull of a city-group the record does not name. The year is the record's reading from his working age in 2826 and not a date any register on Saltmere has been asked for.
- Keeper of a mooring tower in the record's present, 2826 UCSD. How long the tower has been his, and from whom he had it, the record does not say.
- Never off Saltmere, and never off the water, in any account the record holds.
- Not a party to the open invitation the Star Rangers' first-contact expedition extended, which stands neither accepted nor declined, and not placed by the record at any meeting where it was weighed.

## The Work

The planet's page gives the design in two sentences: a city segment that rides a storm loose on its cable and re-couples afterward is the design's entire point, and a city segment that does not move is the failure mode the design exists to prevent. A tower is what the segment rides against. Its cables pay out and re-tension rather than snap taut, its couplings let go past a stress threshold rather than tear the tower down with them, and between storms it is re-tensioned continuously against currents that circle the whole planet, because on a world with no land there is nothing for a current to form against and every current is the circumpolar kind. Orme's share of the labour is the tension: what the tower feels, written down, and what is done about it.

The tower feels everything the ocean does. [Tides Without Shores](/star-rangers/lore/tides-without-shores/) says that nobody on Saltmere has ever seen the tide, because a platform rises and falls with it and there is no shore for it to break against, and that the moorings have never once stopped feeling it: the tension logs are the tide gauge, and four centuries of them are the longest-running current-meter record on any human world. The log on his tower is his. It holds what the founders' lost archive could not keep, and the record has no account of his finding that remarkable. The same number tells him the weather. [Weather on Other Worlds](/star-rangers/lore/weather-on-other-worlds/) files Saltmere's storms as weather that never makes landfall and never dies of it, read in mooring loads because the moorings are the only fixed thing the wind can push against, and the reading is made at a tower.

Below him the work is not his. Nobody dives to inspect a cable eight hundred metres down, and nobody has needed to in living memory; the cable runs, the foundations and the couplings belong to the aquabots, which patrol them continuously and surface only to dock, resupply, or escalate a finding no onboard routine can resolve on its own. Every aquabot on Saltmere flags the stress reading it cannot account for rather than quietly recalibrating around it, and will not certify a coupling safe on a confidence level it cannot show its work for; the record's entry on [Kernel-Compliant](/star-rangers/glossary/kernel-compliant/) systems gives exactly that as its example of the status at work, *ten thousand small honesties nobody watches*. On his tower, somebody does. When an aquabot surfaces with a finding it will not certify, it surfaces to him. What it brings up is a reading and the work behind the reading. What it does not bring up is a decision, which is not a thing the design gives a machine eight hundred metres down, and which the machine was built to refuse. The decision is the keeper's, and the one thing on his side of the water he can change is the tower's tension. The rest is the design's, and the design was built to give.

The hull on his cable does not stay still either, and is not meant to. It rides the swell and drifts on its tether through a drift cycle measured in days, re-centring against the tower's own tension rather than fighting the ocean outright, and the schedule everyone on Saltmere older than a season carries in their head is the schedule of that travel: cargo at cycle midpoint, when the platform's motion is slowest; nobody on the gantry at the cycle's outer edge. Newcomers are given the schedule anyway, and told, without much sympathy, that everyone learns the hard way exactly once. The record does not hold his once, and has no reason to think he was spared it.

## Character Notes

The record holds his manner from his trade and from nothing else, since no chapter has met him, and the trade is exact in a way that is easy to mistake for an idle one. A tension is read, not felt: the deck under him moves with the swell, and the number on the tower does not care how the day feels. An aquabot that will not certify a coupling has not failed and has not panicked; it has done the one thing it was built to do, and a keeper who treats the finding as the machine's problem has mistaken whose it is. He does not certify what the aquabot will not, on the aquabot's reading. What he does instead is on his side of the water, and is in the log.

He lives on the one kind of ground his world has. The planet's page says there is no coastline to retreat to and no shore to call solid, and [Coasts and Other Edges](/star-rangers/lore/coasts-and-other-edges/) says that Saltmere is maritime to the bone even so: its entire coast is the engineered perimeter of its own hulls, a shoreline measured in gantry rail, owned, maintained and load-rated. His tower is a piece of that coast, and it is moored, not fixed. [The Cost of the Crossing](/star-rangers/lore/the-cost-of-the-crossing/) calls Saltmere the completed judgement, a world where every candidate island lost and a civilisation had to bring its own ground. What was brought is cable under tension, and keeping it is a trade.

The Star Rangers' mapping pass expected an empty system and found four centuries of a civilisation riding on cable and platform hull. The invitation it extended stands open, neither accepted nor declined, and Saltmere holds no charter and no membership, which puts it in the same administrative category as the [Kingdom of the Five Islands](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands/) and [Winterbourne](/star-rangers/lore/planets/winterbourne/) for different reasons again. The record does not place him at any meeting where the invitation was weighed, gives him no view of it, and has no account of his meeting a Ranger. Nothing on his page presses it. His tower was moored before the Rangers came, and its aquabots were patrolling it; what a visiting Survey Corps archivist found when the first-contact report was set beside the Archive's holdings, a resemblance to [a programme on Earth](/star-rangers/lore/subsea-cable-drones/) that the Archive files as convergence and not descent, is in the Archive's holdings and in no log of his.

The record gives him no kin by name, no day, and no account of his own in his own words. It places him on the gantry of a mooring tower in open water, in the Annwyn system in 2826 UCSD, at the dock where the aquabots surface, with a city segment riding on its cable beyond the causeway, and nowhere else.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

His kin. The name of his tower, his hull and his city-group, since the planet's page names none; what *Orme* owes to a founder, a tower or a hull, and whether Saltmere keeps family names at all. How a tower comes to a keeper and how long one is kept; whether a tower has one keeper or a watch of several, and whether he keeps it alone. What a keeper can do about a coupling he cannot reach beyond the tower's tension, and what the city asks of a segment's people when a finding is escalated. What regime certified the aquabots he works beside, since the planet's page gives their standard in the Concord's word and the colony had no contact with the Concord before the Rangers came; the Archive files the resemblance as convergence and has not been asked for more. Whether he was alive when the Rangers' mapping pass came, since the record does not date it; whether he has met a Ranger; what he thinks of the open invitation. His once, the hard lesson the planet's page says everyone learns exactly once. What the segment on his cable specialises in, housing, a market concourse or a reclamation plant, and where its people go at cycle midpoint. What lives in the water under him, since the record names no native organism on Saltmere. What he wears, since the planet's page describes no dress. Any chapter. The body, beyond what a working life on open water gives anyone: the record states nothing of it, and the portrait, when it is made, will be the picture's reading and not the page's.
