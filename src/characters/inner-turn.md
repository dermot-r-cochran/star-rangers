Inner Turn is a [Spiralite](/star-rangers/lore/planets/corryn/) of Corryn Prime and the author of one document in the record, [Beside the Ringing](/star-rangers/codex/beside-the-ringing/): the history of the Spiralites as the author tells it, in pulse, beside the Carillon's ringing, in the way the account says a telling is said so that whoever keeps the count will find it. The Archive did not ask for it and could not have: nobody with standing has visited the Corryn system, and the record has not established how anyone meets a people who speak in a medium the colony there is drowning out. The sequence was recorded by listeners in the system among their recordings of the Carillon, carried in the system's own literature with its bearing and its bands marked, and read at Threshold Station by the Survey Archive's translation desk as a working rendering. Inner Turn is the first Spiralite the record names, and the record names nobody else of the people.

The name is the desk's rendering. The author gives one pulse for the author's own self, and the telling gives the same pulse for the innermost turn of a habitat, so the desk renders the one by the other and asserts nothing about how the Spiralites name. The record declares no pronoun for the Spiralites and this page uses the name throughout, as it does for the Meridian people and the Cerebraun.

## Known History

- Keeps the inner turn of a spiral habitat tuned to the Carillon, by the account's own word: the innermost winding, where the account says the ringing is cleanest and what is not the ringing is least. A turn goes out of tune and is tuned back over returns of the ringing, three at the least. How long Inner Turn has kept it, and from whom the keeping came, the account does not say ([Beside the Ringing](/star-rangers/codex/beside-the-ringing/)).
- Was given the old tellings, by the account's own word, and gives them on as given: that the ringing spoke first; that some of the people once said things beside the unkept, to see. Was present for none of the old tellings and says so.
- Kept a turn through the widening of the unkept, which the account places within the keeping of turns kept now and in no older telling. The record holds the widening as the mining and agricultural expansion of the Corryn Prime colony, about 2796 UCSD; the account identifies it with nothing, and the identification is the record's.
- Told the history of the Spiralites in pulse beside the ringing, at the return, addressed to nobody outside the bands it was said in. When, in any reckoning, the account does not say and the record does not hold.

## Character Notes

The account shows a keeper of one thing who explains the thing exactly and nothing beyond it. Inner Turn gives the people's method before the people's history, because the method is what the history is kept by: a telling is said beside the ringing and found there at the next return, and nothing is put anywhere. The account gives each old telling as a telling, with *I was not there* where it is true, and gives the author's own knowledge separately and plainly: the ringing keeps the count, and the author has never needed it to do anything else. It gives the people's rule for who is somebody, that a thing which keeps a count is somebody, and then applies the rule to the unkept thing in the bands and says in the same breath that the rule may be wrong and that the author is not the one to know. The account's one quiet joke is there, and it costs nobody: the unkept said the same things at every return, and none of them was beside the ringing.

What the account withholds it withholds with a reason given: how a new one is begun, because that is said in the quiet or not at all; how many returns anything took, because the old tellings do not count that way; how many no longer keep a count, because *we do not count that*. The author declines, in words, to speak for the people: a telling is one voice, and anyone who takes it for all of them has mistaken a telling for a count.

The account closes on the reason for its own form. The author has said the history beside the ringing, in the old way, for whoever keeps the count that the author does not know about, if there is anyone, since anything that keeps the count must listen to the ringing. The desk's note records what found it. The author does not know, and the account does not say.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

Inner Turn's age, in any reckoning; the record holds no Spiralite lifespan and no conversion between a return of the Carillon's ringing and any year. Where the habitat stands, how many turns it has, or what a turn is built of. Whether keeping a turn is an office, a craft or something the record has no word for, and how a keeper comes to it. Whether Inner Turn has kin, or what kin is among the Spiralites; the record holds that their reproduction depends on the medium they speak in and nothing of how, and the account says it is said in the quiet or not at all. Whether Inner Turn has ever registered the colony as anything other than a thing in the bands, has ever seen a human or a human building, or knows that the planet has anyone on it who is not Spiralite; the account identifies the unkept with nothing. The author's own view of the Carillon beyond the count it keeps. The two shapes in the sequence the desk could not place. Whether anyone on Corryn Prime has read the sequence, or answered it. Who speaks for the Spiralites, which the author says is nobody and which the record has not established. The body, beyond what the people's page gives: semi-translucent, with bioluminescent neural structure visible beneath the skin, which the account says is not the speech. The portrait on file is an artist's impression, a reconstruction plate painted at the Archive and not a photograph, since nobody with standing has visited the Corryn system. It shows Inner Turn as one of the walking kind, one of the three kin lineages the people are made of.
