Kesta Ul is a [Serephine Dunekin](/star-rangers/lore/serephine-dunekin/) of Kharis Belt IV, formerly of the [Star Rangers](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/), and the author of one document in the record, [Read at the Next Margin](/star-rangers/codex/read-at-the-next-margin/): the history of her people, written at home in the Archive's working language at the Survey Archive's asking through the Corps' rolls of former officers, in parts, each in one of the two hours a day in which her people are open, and sent as written. She did not come to the desk. Each part is headed dawn or dusk as she headed it, ends where its margin closed, and was not revised from a later one; the receiving desk joined none of them and set no count beside them. She gives the history as her people keep one, by what was done at the edges of days and found at the next margin, and gives no year in any reckoning, and says why.

She is Serephine Dunekin and **not humanoid**, as her species' page says of all of them: a closed form, sealed and mineral-hard, for most of the day and night, and an open form at the two margins. Nothing on this page or in her account describes her body beyond the people's. *Kesta Ul* is as the Corps' rolls carry it, in the record's translation register, as [Sethka Ru](/star-rangers/characters/sethka-ru/)'s is; the record holds no Serephine name in any other form. She is *she* in the same register, as Sethka Ru is *he*, and the record asserts nothing by either.

## Known History

- Born on Kharis Belt IV, inside, as she puts it, a delay: a shelter of mass heavy enough to be slow. The account gives no place, kin or year ([Read at the Next Margin](/star-rangers/codex/read-at-the-next-margin/)).
- Went into the Star Rangers, by her own account, because the Rangers are *the people who do things in the middle of the day*, and she had heard it said and thought it a figure of speech. A scout, by her own word; the record names the people as scouts and no Serephine's corps. Her corps, rank, postings and years of service are on the Corps' rolls and not in the account.
- Served on at least one station, which had no sky, and opened anyway; carried its air all day and wore the filter for the comfort of it. On one posting, in a day she gives no place, a thing needed doing in the middle while she was closed; it was done well by others, and she read it at her next margin.
- Served out her time and came home. Sets the mirrors of a furnace at dawn and reads the melt at dusk, the work she describes the account from inside.
- Answered the Survey Archive's asking from Kharis Belt IV, in parts, one to each margin, and sent them back by the route the asking had taken.

## Character Notes

The account shows a person who gives a history by the shape of a day and refuses it a length: everything her people did was done at the edges of days, and everything that happened in the middles happened to nobody and was read afterwards, *like a melt*. She writes only in the open and says so, lets a part end where the margin closed, and will not carry a thought across the shut hours into the next one. She is exact about the record's own sentences, quotes several of them back as right, and adds from inside what each leaves out: the lag the people live in, what *recovery* is when the human word under it is *rest*, what it is to read a day finished among people who were there.

Her humour is dry and costs nobody: a closed body is closed as a door is, *and you may talk to the door*; the colleague who asked whether it frightened her to live on the thing that killed her, and thought she had changed the subject. She declines in words, not by silence, and lists what she has not said so the reader does not look for it: how her people speak among themselves, whose reading opens a window, how they met the Corps, what was in the melt, and the thing a Serephine does at dusk that no other people in the Corps can do, which she will not name because the reader *would not be there for it*. The one cost she names of her service is not the closing, which she has done every day of her life, but the reading.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

Her age, in any reckoning, and the length of her service. Her corps, rank and postings, which the rolls hold and the account does not; that she was a scout is her own word. Where on Kharis Belt IV she lives, whose furnace she sets, and what the melt was. Whether she has kin, or what kin is among her people; the record holds nothing of Serephine kinship, lifespan, polity or Federation standing, and the account settles none of it. How her people speak among themselves, and any word of theirs: the record holds no Serephine language in any medium and no rendering of the ladder from them, and the account, written in the Archive's working language, supplies none. Whose reading of a margin opens a window, which she says she has not said. How the Serephine met the Corps, and when; she was not there. What a Serephine in the open form does at dusk that no other people in the Corps can do; the record holds only that it is done. Whether anything keeps the furnace through the hours she describes beyond the mirrors as they were set. Her body, which no page describes beyond the people's, as the record's one Serephine portrait shows it: a low sealed mineral-hard carapace held clear of the ground on six slender radial limbs, the respiratory vanes furled within it and unfurled at the margins, small eye-spots glazed with light-scattering membranes around the rim. No portrait is on file; the prompt is written and waits on the desktop.
