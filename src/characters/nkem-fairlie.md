Nkem Fairlie paints signs on one residential ring of a member habitat of the [Orbital Habitats Compact](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/). The record holds her through one document: the sheet of terms she keeps posted at her paint store, which a pressure-boundary surveyor attached to an inspection return in 2825 UCSD and the Codex carries as [Two Blocks and Four Strokes](/star-rangers/codex/two-blocks-and-four-strokes/).

## Known History

- Grew up on the ring she paints, by the sheet's own account: she was the fifth of a household, the arrival for whom four strokes come off a door and a block goes on, and was called *the block* on her corridor for some years.
- Holds the Compact's ticket for frame work, and renews the ring's [plainmark](/star-rangers/lore/plainmark/) panels by stencil from the warden's sheet.
- Separately, by brush, paints each household's count on the moving leaf of its pressure door, a custom of that ring which the Compact's schedule does not provide for. She says so first on her sheet, and says what she would do if a count of hers were ever relied on.
- Has trained at least one apprentice, whom the sheet does not name.

## Character Notes

The sheet is a tradeswoman's terms, written to save a conversation she has had many times. Most of it is a list of what she declines: a stroke for someone expected, a stroke left for someone dead, a figure for none, a half, a name, a small bar through one stroke. She gives the reason each time, and it is the plainmark's own: the count says who sleeps behind the door tonight and has no *was* or *will be*. She keeps the standard's figures off the leaf everywhere but the count band, and its stencils away from the leaf altogether.

She is dry and not hard. She repaints for a death early, before the corridor is about, and does not charge. She says she would think less of a household that did not ask her to leave the stroke.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

Which habitat and which ring: the surveyor asked that the ring not be named, and the Archive agreed. Her age, her species, her household and the count on her own door. Who taught her, and whether the custom has had other painters. What the door at the turn of her corridor says now.
