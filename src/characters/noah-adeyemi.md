Noah Adeyemi is the only person on Eden's fold-approach side who has been seen to look at the hatch.

He was grown on [Eden Space Habitat](/star-rangers/lore/eden-space-habitat/), is nineteen or twenty in the spring of 2828 UCSD, and works a shift somewhere on the Compact side of the ring that lets him off at the hour the fold-approach watch changes. The record does not say where the shift is or what it is for. He lives behind the door with the plant outside it — the door on one side of a hatch with a number and nothing else, between it and a door with a name on it — and for the years the plant has stood there it has been watered once a week, on the sixth day, by whoever lives behind that door — his mother, by his own account — and has done well on it.

## The Plant

On the fourth day of [Iklix Varn](/star-rangers/characters/iklix-varn/)'s posting he was crouched at the plant with a watering can when the hatch opened, and looked up, and said *You're the hatch*. The can was full. The plant did not need the whole can. He had looked up the tab before he said anything, so as not to get it wrong, and *three bars* was the nearest true thing to hand when the thing he had come to say did not come. On the fifth day he was there again, and asked what was behind the hatch, and was told *a watch*, and asked what it watched, and was told *it doesn't*, and laughed — not long, nothing in it anyone could have written down — and the Deputy took one step back.

He heard the step. He said *Sorry, Deputy*, and the word had changed shape between the two times he used it — the first time a rank he had looked up, the second a courtesy, the way the word is said on the Compact side of the ring — and he picked up the can, still nearly full, and went in at his own door. See [The Door With the Plant (S09E01C02)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e01/s09e01c02/).

[Zoe Smith](/star-rangers/characters/zoe-smith/) read the whole of it through a hatch left on the latch, and read it correctly: the oldest signal on any ring, sent the ordinary way, by somebody who had never had a reason to learn what a tab meant on that side and had gone and found out. She also read what he had heard in the step, which was not what the step meant. She wrote none of it down.

On the eighth day he came back without the can. That was the whole of what he had come to say, and he said the rest of it in one sentence — *it doesn't need watering, I know that* — and the Deputy read the empty hands before the face, did not step back, did not step forward, and asked his name. He gave it. Nothing in the corridor changed and nobody said *Deputy* the other way. See [Without the Can (S09E01C04)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e01/s09e01c04/).

## The Rest of the Year

The corridor was most of it. From the spring to the late autumn he and the Deputy met there, at the plant, at the hour, and nearly all of what follows is in no log.

In the late spring he took Varn by the Ring-Rail to the near terraces, three stops along from their stretch of the ring. The Deputy would not go in service dress, and a Deputy's kit list carries nothing else, so he brought a shirt from his own household. His mother had cut it for him when he was fifteen, for a reach he did not grow into. Without the tab he calls the Deputy *Iklix*, at Varn's word. At the hatch, at the hour, the name is *Varn* and the step stands ([What People Wear (S09E02C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e02/s09e02c01/), [The Sixth Day (S09E03C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e03/s09e03c01/)).

His mother has lived behind the door with the plant since before he was born, and has seen Deputies come to the hatch and go from it. On a sixth day she told Varn that she knows a Deputy is not left long on a first posting, that her son knows it, and that she asked for nothing ([The Sixth Day (S09E03C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e03/s09e03c01/)). He had looked that up as well. In the summer he found the hatch shut at the hour and a Field Officer he did not know coming out of it, and took it that the Deputy had been moved. He was told that it was one shift, and that there would have been an order ([A Hatch Shut (S09E04C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e04/s09e04c01/)). In the autumn he saw that something had happened on a shift and did not ask what. He was told that nothing was wrong, that it was in a log, and that no more could be said than was in it ([One Interval (S09E06C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e06/s09e06c01/)).

In the late autumn the Deputy knocked at his door for the first time, off the watch and out of uniform. The household gives that door as its address: *Adeyemi*, the door after the hatch, fold-approach side ([The Door After the Hatch (S09E07C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e07/s09e07c01/)). The same evening the two rode the Ring-Rail the light way, the whole way round. He asked for the story the Deputy had been told across a desk and was refused it, as not yet the Deputy's to hand on. He said he could wait. He was promised one thing with a day in it: that when the Corps said where the Deputy went next, he would be told the same day ([The Light Way Round (S09E07C02)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e07/s09e07c02/)).

The Corps said in the early winter, and he was told at the hatch at the hour of the same shift. He asked when the order had come before he asked anything else. His mother had said in the spring that nobody tells the residents when a Deputy goes; this time the door after the hatch was told on the day the order came ([The Same Day (S09E08C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e08/s09e08c01/)).

On the sixth day the Deputy left. Asked what is put on a letter sent to him from off Eden, he gave the household's address, and asked to be told the weather. Offered the archive, where anyone may ask for the account, he pointed out that he had never been given the name of the girl at the well, and asked not to be. He said again that he could wait: *"I didn't say I'd be let. I said I can."* Then he called the Deputy *Iklix*, to the tab, with the step standing. It is the only time on the record that he used that name to the collar, and it was not corrected ([A Different Walk (S09E08C02)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e08/s09e08c02/)).

Some days later there was somebody in the corridor in grey-brown with a kit bag, reading the doors. He knew what three bars were without looking them up, and what was behind the hatch. He said *Evening*, to the tab, and was answered, and went in at his own door ([A Different Walk (S09E08C02)](/star-rangers/seasons/s09/e08/s09e08c02/)).

## What He Declines

He does not pretend not to have looked. Most of the ring walks past the hatch without seeing it, and has for years; he saw it, looked up what it was, and came back with a can rather than pretend otherwise.

He does not push. Two days at the plant, two questions, and when the answer to the second was a step back he took the step as the answer and went in. He did not argue with it and did not ask what it meant. When he came back it was to correct his own side of it, not to press the other.

He does not bring the can twice. Once he knew it had been a lie — a small one, the kind the ring tells every day — he left it inside, and came out with nothing in his hands rather than the wrong thing.

He does not get the rank wrong. He looked it up first, said it right, and then — having been told, as he understood it, that it was a rank after all — said it the other way, which was also right, on his side of the ring.

## Character Notes

The record holds him from two rooms: a corridor, where he is the one person who looked, and a Field Officer's compartment, where he is a signal read correctly by someone who was not the person it was sent to. Neither of the two people in the corridor was wrong about what they saw. Each was wrong about what it meant, and he was the one who paid for the misreading, with a word said the courteous way and a full can carried back through his own door. For four days the Deputy did not know his name; the record did, because Smith knows most of the ring by small things and at a distance, and his was one of them. On the eighth day he gave it himself, too fast, and added that it was on the door, on the inside.

What he does next is not established. Whether a letter comes to the door after the hatch, and whether the waiting comes to anything, the record does not say. The plant is watered on the sixth day either way.
