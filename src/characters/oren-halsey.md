Oren Halsey compares biospheres for [the Institute](/star-rangers/lore/the-institute/), and his best-known paper is about the regions they sit in rather than the life itself. [Selection Without a Selector](/star-rangers/codex/selection-without-a-selector/) asks whether the stability of every Concordant anyone has measured is evidence that the stability was designed, and answers that it is not: a Concordant has to be stable enough to hold its measurers before anyone can measure it.

## Known History

- A fellow of the Institute's biosphere comparison unit in 2827 UCSD, when the paper was circulated open as Institute paper no. 9418.
- The record gives no birthplace, no age and no earlier post, and does not say how long he has held the fellowship.

## Character Notes

The paper is the Institute at its most careful. It states the selection argument at full strength, proposes the measurement that would test it against the design account (the margin of each Concordant's stability above the threshold for sustained chemistry), and then prints, in bold, that the measurement has not been made and that anyone citing the paper as having found a result has misread it.

What marks Halsey out among Institute authors is the step he declines. The Institute's critics say its weakness is treating a successful compression as a completed debunking, and the [Institute page](/star-rangers/lore/the-institute/) says so of its position on dragons. Halsey's paper names that criticism, accepts it as fair, and stops short of the step: showing that one observation does not need a designer is not showing that there was none. *A sample shaped by selection may also have been shaped by a selector.*

The record places him at the Institute in 2827 UCSD and nowhere else.
