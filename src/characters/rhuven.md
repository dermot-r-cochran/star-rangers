Rhuven is an [Ovruhn](/star-rangers/lore/ovruhn/) and a [Star Ranger](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/): a Field Officer of the [Survey Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-survey-corps/)' Meteorology Branch, posted in the record's present, 2826 UCSD, as the acoustic observer of a world with weather worth listening to, which the record does not name. The people's page says that Ovruhn Rangers serve where hearing through things is the job, and names long acoustic watch among the four kinds of work they do; the Survey Corps' page says that the Meteorology Branch's real product is not forecasts but observers, locally posted, locally calibrated, keeping the long records that cannot be imported. Rhuven is where those two sentences meet. The record has named no Ovruhn Ranger before them, and has no reason to call them the first of anything else: the oldest chorus holds the part of the first Ovruhn who went into the quiet, and the Archive's desk did not ask for it ([The Quiet Has No Far Side](/star-rangers/codex/the-quiet-has-no-far-side/)).

The name is given as the record's translation desk renders it, a spelling of a name that arrives already part-translated from a grammar carried as much in the under-song as in the click-speech, as the people's page says every Ovruhn person-form does. It asserts no meaning. Rhuven is *they* in the record's translation register, as every Ovruhn individual is.

## Known History

- Born on Thavren, in the reach of a chorus the record does not name. What chorus kept Rhuven's part before the Corps did, and whether any of it was a keeper's, the record does not hold.
- Entered the Corps by the ordinary road: Novice, Cadet, Deputy, each rank held in turn, none skipped, through the Survey Corps' college at the [Academy](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-academy/). The record does not fix the year, and does not say what the service ages come to for an Ovruhn, those being set per people by the Medical Corps and never by the command ladder.
- Field Officer, Survey Corps, Meteorology Branch, in 2826 UCSD, posted alone as acoustic observer on a world with weather, which the record does not name. How long the posting has run, and where Rhuven served before it, the record does not say. It places them under that sky and nowhere else.

## The Work

A world's weather is read by whoever is standing under it, and the Meteorology Branch's doctrine is that nothing else will do: there is no interstellar weather service and cannot be, so the branch posts observers and keeps their long records. Rhuven is one. What they add to the instruments is the people's far sense. A storm is loud long before it arrives, in the register below hearing where the under-song also lives, and in a world's air that register carries: a front gathering past the horizon, the roll under a squall line, the first thin note of a wind that has not reached the ground yet. Rhuven hears the day's weather coming the way the oldest chorus heard the Federation's descent, by its weight and its shape and the fact that it is not stopping, and the branch's instruments log it hours later and confirm them or do not. The record is careful here, as the people's page is: the sense is a coarse picture at range, presence and motion rather than outline, and how far it reaches in air thinner than Thavren's the record has not established. What Rhuven says they hear is filed as what they hear, and the instruments' record is the branch's record. A reading no colleague can share is not a classification item, and the branch has never been asked to make it one. What it is, is earlier.

The other half of the work is the one no Ovruhn was built for, and the one the posting turns on. An observer's product is a long written record, dated, attributable and legible after the observer is gone. The Ovruhn never built writing, because chorus-keeping solved the problem it solves; a thing worth keeping is retold in living voices and checked against every keeper present, and nothing needs writing down. Rhuven writes it all down. Every watch, what the sky did and when, into the branch's log, in the record's own terms, with the chorus's own habit turned on the page: a reading is entered, and then checked against the instruments as a part is checked against the chorus, and the places where the two disagree are marked and not smoothed. The branch's Archive colleagues, who stamp every measurement with the ruler that took it, are recorded as finding this exact. Rhuven is recorded as finding the log the finest thing about the posting, and the strangest: a part that will not move when they stop singing it.

## What Rhuven Cannot Do

Work in vacuum by choice. The people keep to atmosphere postings where the choice exists, and this posting is one; where the branch's work takes Rhuven into thin air the far sense starves, and the field shell's instruments stand in for it. Read a face, a printed page or a screen at any distance; the small near-sighted eyes do that, slowly and close. Be discreet about a hard day: the under-song is honest by physiology, and it leaks through deck plate and through the ground, felt by anyone with the sense to feel it. Expect anyone else to be discreet either. Stop hearing a colleague's heart. Hear the chorus.

## The Uniform

On duty Rhuven wears the Corps' [modular field shell](/star-rangers/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform/) as the quartermaster record fits it to an Ovruhn body plan: the Survey Corps' deep olive, in fitted sections seated along the top of the back and over the forebody, and nothing heavy over the flanks, because the hide of the flanks is the ear and a shell that covered it would deafen the Ranger it was issued to. The sections carry what the people's page says the shell mostly is for an Ovruhn, a mounting for instruments; on this posting they are the branch's, the recorders and the receivers for the registers below the one Rhuven hears, and they face the wearer and signal nothing to anyone. The rank tab, four bars on the pale grey-green insert of the survey track, is seated at the forward edge of the shell above the tendrils' root, the point on this body that corresponds to a human collar, and is read at a glance by anyone who shares the air. The hood and visor stowed at the shell's edge close over whatever a body plan has in place of a face, as the uniform page says they must; what that comes to for a body with no face to address, the quartermaster record holds and the Archive does not.

The shell's emergency system reads the body it is fitted to, and the uniform page is clear that it reads a body and never a person, and that what it reads is the wearer's own. The Corps has not had to tell Rhuven so. On a posting shared with humans the arrangement is the record's one quiet joke about the Ovruhn in service: the shell keeps a reading of the Ranger's state that only the Ranger may release, on a body that has been announcing its state to everyone in range since before it was issued anything.

## Character Notes

The Ovruhn rendering of the ladder, in [its other tongues](/star-rangers/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues/), gives Field Officer as *one who holds a part alone*, and the record notes without a reading that Rhuven's posting is the rendering taken literally. There is no other Ovruhn in reach. Among the people that is the one condition the chorus has no register for, and so uses the one it keeps for loss, while saying plainly that the ones away are not dead; Rhuven is the first Ranger the record can set beside that passage of the oldest chorus's history, and the record does so and leaves it there. Rhuven does not complain of the posting, which no Ovruhn could do discreetly in any case, and has been heard, through the plating of the station's shelter deck, to be steadier at the end of a long watch than at its start.

With the humans on the posting Rhuven holds the courtesy their people took longest to learn, that what a body says was not addressed to you. A colleague who reports the night calm in a level voice while the heart under it hammers is answered on the words, as an Ilveth declines to answer a blush, and the record has no account of Rhuven offering comfort nobody asked for. What the people's contact workers are trained for, a calm body that acts without warning, has not been needed here, and Rhuven is recorded as saying, when asked what the posting's humans sound like, that they sound like weather: loud, honest, and not addressed to anyone.

One thing from Thavren has come with them and found work. The under-song that steadies a crowded shelter deck as plain acoustics is not a thing Rhuven does; it is a thing Rhuven is, and the branch's doctrine of locally posted observers has put the one Ovruhn on the posting in the one room where the posting's people wait out what the observer heard coming. Nobody has written it into any procedure. The record notes that nobody has needed to.

The record gives Rhuven no age, no line, no chorus of origin by name, no distinction and no history before the Corps beyond Thavren itself. It places them under a low sky on a world it does not name, in uniform, with the storm still past the horizon and the log open, and nowhere else.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

The world, its system, its station and its people: the Meteorology Branch's observers are locally posted, the record has named no observer's station anywhere, and this page names none. Rhuven's age, and what the three service ages come to for an Ovruhn. The year of entry, the postings before this one, and how long this one has run. Rhuven's chorus on Thavren and their part in it. Whether any other Ovruhn is on the roster of any Corps posting, which the record does not say and this page does not settle; the people's page gives Ovruhn Rangers as a plural and names none but this one. Which corps the Ovruhn in Corps service mostly join: Rhuven's is Survey, and the record draws nothing from one case. How far the far sense reaches in a human-breathable air, and whether a station's air starves it, both of which the record has marked not established and this page leaves so. Whether Rhuven has ever served on a Federation disaster crew, which is the compact's work and not the Corps', and is Thavren's as a member world; the two are kept apart on the people's page and here. Any chapter. The body, beyond what the people's page gives every Ovruhn: among the largest bodies in Corps service, a broad, deep-chested quadruped mass standing lower than its bulk suggests, a fan of boneless handling tendrils at the forebody, no face to address, the hide of the flanks the ear, a pair of small mild eyes, a chest of layered resonant chambers; nothing on record tells one Ovruhn from another by sight, and the page does not try. No portrait is on file; the prompt is written and waits on the desktop.
