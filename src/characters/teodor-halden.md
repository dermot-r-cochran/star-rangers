Teodor Halden holds [Field Officer](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/) on the fold-approach watch at the Earth–Moon L4 staging zone, and has held the rank since 2806 UCSD. The watch is one of the permanent monitoring posts the Survey Corps keeps at the inner system's [Tier 1 fold sites](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/): a platform small enough that everyone aboard stands a shift, six instruments reading the approach on six principles, a log, a desk, and the nearest qualified officer to the event if the event comes. Halden has been that officer, on one watch or another, for most of his service. The event has not come in his tenure. He says so to every Deputy on the first day, as a fact about the posting, and has never had reason to say anything else.

He was born on [Mars](/star-rangers/lore/planets/mars/) in 2778 UCSD and entered the Corps at twenty-two, on the ordinary intake, with nothing in his file that a board would later find it had missed. The file is in order from its first page. It carries no citation and no reprimand, no appeal, no gap, and no entry that anyone has had to expand.

## The Work

Halden's postings are of the kind the [decision doctrine](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-decision-and-communication/) was written for and the record visits once: three approach watches in twenty years, the L4 watch the longest of them, since 2808. Authority sits with whoever is closest to the event and holds the relevant rank, and on an approach watch that is the Field Officer, several times a year, for minutes at a time. Then the notice, in the form the watch uses: the time, the duration, the class in the table's words, whether a transit was cleared, with the list's reference beside it. The fifth line is remarks. Halden has never written on it.

The officers who write his assessments say what the doctrine says. He reports what he observed and what it did and did not match. He requests exactly the follow-on action the evidence supports. He does not manufacture confidence and he does not hedge it away, and he has done this on every shift of every rotation for twenty years without the doing of it once becoming interesting enough to cite. On paper his conduct is the conduct of the best officers at the rank, and the paper is the only thing a board has.

## The Deputies

Nine Deputies have served their first posting under him. Four have since made Field Officer. The record names none of the nine, and holds no account of how any of them were routed to him; they arrived in the ordinary way, by the Corps' ordinary list, and left it the same way.

His assessments of them are built from what each Deputy did and what was observed, and predict nothing. They have never been wrong. They have also never been the assessment a board reached for when it wanted to know what a Deputy would be in ten years, because they do not say, and a board that has learned to read one Field Officer's silences has not necessarily learned to read another's. Asked once by a Deputy what the posting was for, he said that the watch stands where it is, which is the posting's first sentence and is true. No Deputy has asked a second time.

## The Line of 2811

In the late autumn of 2811 UCSD a drive-band transient of seven seconds crossed the L4 approach, and the six instruments did not agree about it. Four filed it at seven seconds. Two filed it as three seconds and four, across two of their ninety-second intervals. The agreement log flagged the disagreement, and the watch reported the transient as the instruments had recorded it, with both durations and the flag.

Halden then measured the six instruments' filing intervals against the log and found that they did not begin at the same instant. Each had been synchronised to the log on the day it was fitted, and the six had been fitted over nine years, so their ninety seconds were staggered, and the two instruments that had split the transient were the two whose interval boundary fell at its fourth second. He entered the six offsets in the shift log, to the second, and under them the one sentence the evidence supported: that the boundary of those two intervals had fallen inside the transient. He requested that the offsets be written on the watch's instrument sheet, so that a later disagreement could be read against them. They were, in his hand, and the Starwarden initialled the sheet.

He did not request that the intervals be aligned. Alignment of a certified instrument is the [Safety Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-safety-corps/)' business and not a watch's, and six instruments on one platform do not support a claim about a standard. He referred the offsets nowhere, because nothing in them required escalation; the watch had reported the transient correctly and could now read its own log. The log entry is correct in every clause. The sheet is correct. Both were read by the people whose job it was to read them, and by nobody else.

In 2823 UCSD the Safety Corps revised its certification standard for cross-correlated approach instruments to require that their filing intervals be aligned on fitting, after a disagreement of the same shape at another Tier 1 watch went to a Fold Transit Safety review. The revision's sources name that review. Every approach watch in the inner system re-aligned its instruments at the next recertification, and the L4 watch's were aligned in 2824, which closed, on Halden's watch, a thing nobody had opened. He read the revision the way he reads everything, for what it matched, and it matched, and he initialled receipt. The offsets on the instrument sheet were struck through with one line, so that they can still be read.

The record holds the shift log of 2811, the sheet, and the revision of 2823 with its list of sources. It does not yet hold anyone who has read the three together.

## Character Notes

Promotion boards have noted him eligible for [Senior](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/) twice, in 2816 and 2822. His reply is in the file each time, in the house style: a request to finish the posting he is on. The two replies read, word for word, as the replies of officers who hold the rank on purpose read, and there is nothing on the paper that would let a board tell the difference, which is not a fault in the board. He has never asked what the boards concluded. The posting is the posting, and he is on it.

What the record can say of him is what a record is for. In twenty years nothing he has entered has needed correcting, nothing he has signed has been signed over, and no Deputy has left his watch having been told a thing he could not support. The line of 2811 cost him a finding, and the record says so. It cost him nothing else that the record can see.

He would not dispute that the line of 2811 was his. He has never been asked.
