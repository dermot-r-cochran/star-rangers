Vaurel teaches classifiers. In the Cerebraun Hegemony's survey service the people who file a reading are trained in how to file it, and Vaurel's part of that training is a single question asked of every classification: what has this reading survived?

## Known History

- A [Cerebraun](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun/) of the Hegemony's home cluster. The record does not name the world, and gives no age. Cerebraun lives are long, and the record's only measure of them is that Hegemony officials can hold a post for centuries.
- Teaches in the Hegemony's survey service, which classifies what Hegemony survey passes find. The record holds Vaurel's teaching filings through the standing data-exchange arrangement between the Hegemony and the Survey Corps, in translation.
- Active in 2826 UCSD.

## Character Notes

Ask a Cerebraun a simple question and the answer sometimes arrives in three parts, seconds apart, none contradicting the others. That is the species' ordinary manner and not Vaurel's invention. What is Vaurel's own is how the three parts are used. In Vaurel's answers, and in the filings Vaurel's students are taught to write, the first part is the reading, the second is what the reading has survived, and the third is what would unseat it. A classification that cannot supply the second and third parts is, in Vaurel's teaching, not yet a classification. It is a reading waiting for its grade.

Vaurel grades claims and does not argue for them. A reading may be graded as having survived a great deal and still be wrong, and a reading graded as having survived nothing may still be true. The grade is a statement about the reading's history, not about the world. Vaurel's filings say so at the head of every one.

The Hegemony's own most famous regrading is the [Sentinel](/star-rangers/lore/planets/sentinel/), catalogued as an automated hazard beacon and left there by centuries of classifiers until a Survey Corps officer's request reopened it in 2828 UCSD. The record does not say whether Vaurel taught any of those classifiers, and makes no connection between Vaurel's teaching and the reopening. The resemblance between Vaurel's question and the one the Survey's own linguists are taught, *what would have to be true for this to be wrong, and did you look*, is a resemblance and not a lineage.

The record renders Vaurel as *they*, as it renders every Cerebraun person: the nine centres are one person, and the pronoun counts the person.
