### Receiving desk's note

*The reader who sat [Vela of the Seam](/star-rangers/characters/vela-of-the-seam/)'s [earlier account](/star-rangers/codex/where-it-was-put/) in her section noticed a case by the inner door with a piece of grey metal in it and no label, and asked what it was. She said she would write the answer, since the first asking had been written, and that it would be short, because she would be drying while she wrote it. The desk put the question in writing: what is the piece in the case? Her conditions were the earlier one and one more: that the words she marks stay where they were put, and that the piece is not assayed. The desk has kept both. Her marked words are in italics. The title is the desk's, from the account. The note at the end is ours.*

---

You asked what the *piece* in the *case* is. I have marked *piece*, because it is your word for a part of something, and this was never part of anything. I have marked *case* because I chose it, and I will come to that.

I can only tell you what it is by telling you what it is a small one of.

At one seam on Nerath a thing is kept that is metal and is not made, as you say *made*. I held near that seam when I was learning to hold. I do not know of another seam that keeps one. I have not asked, and nobody from another seam has told me. Write down that much and no more. I will say it again at the end.

How it stands. A seam is where two of the layers lie on one another, and they do not carry the same load. At that seam the lower layer carries more of one metal than the upper. Put a body of that metal across the seam, part of it in each, and the seam does the rest with nothing supplied. Metal comes out of the fuller layer onto the lower face. Metal goes off the upper face into the emptier one. I am told your chemists know this and can do it in a divided jar and have a name for it. The desk may put the name in its note. I would rather not have it in mine.

Three things follow. One of them looks after itself.

The amount looks after itself. What arrives below is what leaves above. The body carries the one across to pay for the other, and it cannot carry more one way than the other. Nobody balances that. I say so because your reader took that to be the skill, and it is the one part that needs none.

The place does not look after itself. A face that is gaining grows downward. A face that is losing draws back, also downward. So the whole shape is going down through the seam, all the time, without falling, and without any of the metal in it moving at all. Left alone it would walk out of the seam into the lower layer and stop. Someone brings it up at the rate it goes down. That someone is holding a depth for it. Holding a depth is the first thing any of us learns to do for ourselves. At that seam it is done for a thing.

The shape does not look after itself either. Metal arriving prefers an edge or a point to a flat. Left alone, the lower face grows a beard, fine and branching, and the upper face goes into pits, and in a short while you have the same amount of metal and no shape. So it is turned, and tilted, and let a little deeper or a little less, by someone reading it across the whole skin. Just under the lower face the layer is carrying less than it was, because metal has just come out of it. Just over the upper face it is carrying more. Where it is thin, turn. Where it is full, lift. That is all there is to perceive, and it is everything.

Your gauges would show it as two lines. Held well, the two lines do not move. I have been told that a visitor would be shown an object and a flat trace and told that this is the best thing at the seam. I understand that would be difficult.

Now what your reader asked twice. Metal put on at the bottom is carried up through the body as the body is brought up. It comes to the upper face and goes. All of it does, in turn. I will not tell you how long one passage takes. It is more than a *season* and I leave it there. The thing at that seam has been held for longer than anyone holding it now has held anything, and they had it from holders who are not there to ask. So it has none of the metal it began with, and has not had for longer than anyone can say. Nobody can find out how long by looking into it. There is nothing old in it to look at.

I am told this is a known difficulty among you, and that there is a boat. Your reader asked whether it is the same one. I said it is doing the same thing. Your reader asked again. I have been here before. At the seam nobody would know what else you wanted to be the same.

What it is for I cannot tell you. The holders take it in turns. To have held it well through a bad day, when weather has reached down and the seam is moving, is known about a person there afterwards. It keeps its shape by not keeping its metal. Nothing at that seam lasts in any other way, so I do not find it strange. I find it well done.

The one in the *case*. When I asked to go into air, the holders at that seam started a second one beside the first, small, a size one body can carry. They held it through one whole passage, until nothing it had started with was left in it. That is when such a thing is *finished*, if that is a word for it: when all of it is what came after. Then they lifted it out of the seam and gave it to me.

They knew what lifting it out does. Out of the seam nothing arrives and nothing leaves. It stopped as it came up. One face was gaining then and one was losing, and they still look it. The gaining end is grey and close, like a thing just laid down. The losing end is brighter, and bitten. In the seam I could have told you which was which with all of me. Here it carries nothing, and I read nothing off it, and I know the ends apart as you do, by looking.

So what is in the *case* is not one of those. It is a reading of one, taken at the seam by people who knew I was going where a thing put down stays down, and who wanted me to have something that would. I think it is the only thing they have made that was meant to stop.

I keep it dry, by the door, because I have nowhere wet that is a seam, and a wet place that is not a seam would only take from it. In your air it is going over slowly at the skin. That is a rate too, a small one, and I have asked that it be let. Please do not find out what metal it is. You would write it down.

One seam. I said I would say it again.

*Dull*, your reader said, and I have marked it. Yes. It is not doing anything. At the seam, the other one is.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. She gives this as the practice of the holders at one seam, says she knows of no other, and says it twice. The Archive files it as that. It is not a practice of the Pelagene Littoral or of Nerath, and the record still holds no Pelagene settlement, polity or social order; "the holders at that seam" is all the account gives, and the desk has not asked who they are to her.*

**The chemistry, plainly.** *The author leaves the name to the desk. A single piece of metal standing in two solutions that hold different amounts of that same metal dissolved will gain metal from the stronger solution and lose it to the weaker, the piece itself carrying the current between its two ends. The arrangement is a concentration cell. It is elementary electrochemistry and works in a divided beaker. Because one current runs through the one piece, what is deposited equals what is dissolved, as she says. That metal deposits faster on edges and points and grows branching when left alone, that a dissolving face pits, and that the liquid is depleted next to a face that is gaining and enriched next to one that is losing, are all ordinary and all as she gives them. A shape that gains below and loses above moves downward while no grain in it moves; raise it at that rate and each grain travels from the lower face to the upper and is gone. The desk has checked the reasoning and not the object. Which metal, and what the two layers are, the account does not say and the record declines to.*

*What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Pelagene Littoral](/star-rangers/lore/pelagene-littoral/) and [Nerath](/star-rangers/lore/planets/nerath/) entries, the account rests on and does not go past: that Nerath's medium is several fluids lying stably on one another, each with its own dissolved load; that the seams between them are the world's principal structures; that the people grow metal from solution, slowly; that galvanic potential and dissolved load are their basic engineering variables; that holding a depth in a shifting column is the skill their culture treats as basic competence; that they sense the dissolved load over the whole body surface; and that they give a thing's history by what it is doing. What the account holds from no source but its author: the kept body itself and that it is held in turns; the second, small one, and that it was grown for her; and that she held near that seam when she was learning. Her page already has her own reason for going into air. This adds what she was given when she went.*

*What a visitor has of it. In the case: a spindle of grey metal about the length of a human forearm, round in section and thickest at the middle, one end matt and fine-grained and the other brighter and finely pitted. There is nothing else to see. At the seam, by her account, an instrument would have two steady traces and an eye would have an object. The work is in the traces not moving, which is perceived over the skin of the one holding it and by no means the Archive has. The desk has not assayed the piece and will not. It notes that the author's request and the record's own decision not to name Nerath's chemistry come to the same thing, and that she gave the better reason.*
