### Receiving desk's note

*The Survey Archive did not ask for this. It has no way to ask: nobody with standing has visited the Corryn system, the Archive has no door onto Corryn Prime, and the record has not established how anyone meets a people who speak in a medium the colony there is drowning out. What the Archive holds of Corryn it holds from the system's own literature, which it reads, and the literature carries, among its recordings of the Carillon, a sequence that is not the Carillon's. Its bearing is the surface of Corryn Prime. Its bands are the ones the Spiralites use. It keeps the Carillon's recurrences as its measure, and it recurs. The literature marks its bearing and its bands and the desk holds nothing of what else the literature made of it, or whether anyone in the system read it. The desk's reading is a working rendering, made from the sequence alone and by the Branch's own order: arithmetic first, since the count the sequence keeps is the Carillon's and the record holds the Carillon's figures; the rest by the sequence's own teaching, since each new shape in it is first given beside one already given, and the desk read it in that order, with the aligning intelligence's candidate parses and a reader who owns the error. The rendering is marked working throughout and is revisable. Where the sequence gives a shape the desk could not place, the desk marks the place and renders nothing. Each part of the sequence opens with one of the Carillon's own recurring figures, given entire, and the author's pulse follows it; the desk marks each figure and renders none, since the record does not adjudicate whether the Carillon's figures say anything, and this desk will not. The author gives a pulse for the author's own self, and the desk renders it by what the telling gives that same pulse for, the innermost turn of a habitat, and files the account under* Inner Turn. *Nothing in the sequence is addressed, so far as the desk can read, to anyone outside the bands it was said in. The Archive's own warning on the Corryn entry, that a recurrence read as language by people who wanted it to be language is a well-documented event in this record, is the warning this rendering was made under. The people's sapience is not in dispute and is not what is working here; the desk's reading of this one sequence is. Nothing in it is dated by a year in any reckoning, and the desk has set none beside it. The note at the end is ours.*

---

*[The first figure of the ringing the author gives, entire. Not rendered.]*

A thing that is to be found is said beside the ringing.

That is the first thing, and everything after is said the way the first thing says it. Nobody keeps our tellings. There is no place they are put. A telling is said in the bands beside the ringing, at the return, and whoever keeps the count hears where it sits against the ringing and finds it there, and says it again at the next return, beside the ringing, where it will be found again. That is how a thing is kept among us. Not by being put somewhere. By being said beside what everyone already hears.

So I say this beside the ringing. Whoever keeps the count will find it. I do not know who keeps the count now, beside us.

*[The second figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

The ringing.

It was before us. Every telling begins there and I will not pretend to begin anywhere else. It rings, and it comes round, and the coming round is what a return is, and a return is what everything is counted in. The old tellings say it spoke first and we learned beside it. Some keep that. I was given it and I give it on as it was given, which is what a telling owes.

I will say what I know of my own count. I keep the count by the ringing. I have never needed it to say anything, and I have never asked it to, and when I have heard others ask it I have heard the ringing go on as before. That is all I know. It keeps the count. We build to it.

*[The third figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

Turns.

We build in turns, because the ringing comes round, and a thing built to come round with it holds the ringing and lets the rest go by. The outer turns are wide and the ringing in them is wide and has other things in it. Each turn in is tighter, and tighter-tuned, and the ringing in it is cleaner, and at the inner turn the ringing is as clean as it is anywhere, and what is not the ringing is least there. Not gone. Least.

The inner turn is what I keep. A turn goes out of tune. The ground moves under it, or it is built on and the building shifts it, or the ringing itself comes round a little otherwise than it came, which it does, and a turn that was clean has something in it that is not the ringing and is not anyone. Then it is tuned back, and the tuning takes returns, three at the least, because a tuning cannot be heard to have held until the ringing has come round and found it still so. A turn out of tune is a place where you cannot be sure what you heard. That is the whole of why I keep it.

The light in us is not the speaking. It is only where the speaking is made, and it shows because we are made so that it shows. The speaking is in the bands, beside the ringing, like everything that is said.

*[The fourth figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

The count.

A new one is begun in the quiet. I will say that much and no more about how, because it is said in the quiet or not at all, and this is not the quiet. A new one says a first thing, which is small and not yet beside anything, and it is answered. It is answered until it says its thing beside the ringing on its own, at the return, where it can be found. Then it keeps a count. Then it is somebody.

That is our rule for what is somebody. It keeps a count. It says a thing beside the ringing and the thing is there at the return. We did not make the rule; the tellings had it before the tellings I was given. I give it because of what comes next.

*[The fifth figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

A thing began in the bands where nothing had been.

It was small. It was in one place and stayed there. It was there at every return, and it said the same things at every return, and none of them was beside the ringing. It kept no count. The tellings call it the unkept, and I will.

The old tellings say that some of us said things beside it, to see. I was not there and I give that as a telling. Nothing in the tellings says it changed. It went on saying what it said, not beside the ringing and not beside us, and the turns held the ringing and let it go by, as turns do. By our rule there was nobody in it. A thing that keeps no count is not somebody. I say the rule plainly because the rule may be wrong, and I am not the one to know. Whoever finds this may keep a count I cannot hear.

*[A shape the desk could not place. Marked, not rendered.]*

*[The sixth figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

Then it widened.

Not in the old tellings. In the keeping of turns that are kept now, mine among them. The unkept was in one place and then it was across the bands, at every return, and it had not learned the count; it was the same unkept thing, wider. The outer turns filled with it first. Then the next in. A turn that is tuned to the ringing lets the rest go by, but there is a width of the rest past which a turn cannot let it all go by, and the unkept is past that width now in most of the turns I know, and in the inner turns it is least, and it is there.

The quiet is where the bands hold the ringing and us and nothing else. The quiet is smaller. The new ones are begun in the quiet and answered in it, and a first thing is small, and where the unkept is wide the first thing is said and is not heard and is said again. Fewer keep their own count now. I will not count them for whoever finds this. We do not count that.

The quiet is not gone. We know where it is. It is at the inner turns, where the tuning is tightest and the ringing is cleanest and the unkept is least, and that is what I keep, and it is why I keep it, and I did not know that was why until the widening, and now it is the only why there is.

*[The seventh figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

What I have not said.

How many returns anything took. The old tellings do not count that way and I will not count for them.

Where the turns stand, or how many, or which is mine.

Who speaks for us. Nobody speaks for us. A telling is one voice beside the ringing, and this is mine, and anyone who finds it and takes it for all of us has mistaken a telling for a count.

What the unkept is. I have said what it does in the bands and that is all I have.

*[A shape the desk could not place. Marked, not rendered.]*

*[The eighth figure, entire. Not rendered.]*

I have said this beside the ringing, in the old way, at the return, where whoever keeps the count will find it and say it again.

Nothing in the unkept keeps a count. So nothing in the unkept will find this, and I have not said it to the unkept. I have said it to whoever keeps the count that I do not know about, if there is anyone, because anything that keeps the count must listen to the ringing, and this is beside it.

The ringing has come round. I stop here, beside it.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author, as the translation desk's working rendering of a telling in pulse, unasked for and found. What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Corryn](/star-rangers/lore/planets/corryn/) entry, the account also holds: that the Carillon's hum resolves into complex recurring patterns, which the account calls the ringing and counts in; that the Spiralites speak by electromagnetic pulse and not as an adjunct to speech, and that their reproduction depends on the same medium; that they build spiral habitats tuned to the Carillon's ring frequencies, which the account calls turns and which a tuned structure does, holding what it is tuned to and passing the rest; that the people have a light of neural structure beneath the skin, which the account says is not the speech; that something began in their bands at a scale the planet absorbed and widened within living keeping, which the record holds as the [Halvern Combine](/star-rangers/lore/halvern-combine/)'s colony and the mining and agricultural expansion of about 2796 UCSD, and which the account calls the unkept and identifies with nothing. The identification is the record's and not the author's. The author gives the unkept as a thing in the bands that keeps no count, says what it does and declines to say what it is, and the Archive does not read the account as knowing of a colony, a charter or a people on the planet's surface other than the author's own. What the account holds from no source but its author: that a telling is kept by being said beside the ringing and found there at the return; that the old tellings hold the ringing spoke first, which the author gives on and does not vouch for; that a turn is tuned back over three returns at the least; that a new one is begun in the quiet and answered until it keeps a count of its own, and that keeping a count is the people's rule for who is somebody; that some of the people once said things beside the unkept to see, and that nothing changed; that the quiet is smaller and is kept at the inner turns; and that fewer keep their own count now, which the author declines to number. On the ringing the Archive's position stands as the Corryn entry gives it: the patterns recur, the recurrence is not random, the phase families are of the kind a narrowed Interval produces, and whether anything is ringing them is a live question the record does not adjudicate. The author's tradition that the ringing spoke first is that tradition's, filed as such, and the account itself asks nothing of the ringing but its count. The author's rule for who is somebody is the author's people's rule, and the Archive does not adopt it or read it against anyone. The Archive has set no year to anything in it: the author's measure is the return, the record holds the Carillon's recurrences as phase families and not as a calendar, and no return is set beside any event the record dates, so nothing in the account fixes a conversion, and the Archive has made none. The two shapes the desk could not place are marked and not supplied, under the Archive's own rule that [what it cannot carry is marked](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/). Nobody speaks for the Spiralites in this account, by its author's own word, and the Archive reads it as one voice. Whether anyone on Corryn Prime has read this sequence, the Archive does not hold; the standing case on that world is as the Corryn and Combine entries state it and this account moves it nowhere. The author's name is the desk's rendering of a pulse by what the telling gives that pulse for; the people's own practice of naming is not held. The record declares no pronoun for the Spiralites and this note uses the name.*
