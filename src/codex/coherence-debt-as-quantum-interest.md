## 1. Scope

[FTL Mechanics](/star-rangers/lore/ftl-mechanics/) defines fold transit as two spacetime regions temporarily brought into fold adjacency, a vessel translated while the adjacency holds, and the fold collapsed afterwards. Fold geometry is a property of the [Common Manifold](/star-rangers/lore/post-eleven-dimensional-manifold/), defined at the scope of the local formcluster. This paper does not model that geometry. It takes the adjacency as given.

What it models is the **aperture**: the place where the fold meets the ordinary four dimensions a vessel actually flies through. The aperture sits in our spacetime and answers to our quantum kernel, which is identical throughout the local archecluster to the physics recorded more than six centuries ago on Earth. So the oldest literature applies to it directly, and that literature turns out to say a great deal.

## 2. Proposition 1: an aperture must defocus

Light passing through ordinary matter and energy converges. The Raychaudhuri equation, published by Amal Kumar Raychaudhuri in 1955 CE (2155 UCSD), makes this exact: where the energy density along every light ray is non-negative, a bundle of nearby rays can only converge.

An aperture a vessel can pass through must do the opposite. Rays entering on one side converge towards the aperture and must spread apart again on the other. Michael Morris and Kip Thorne showed in 1988 CE (2188 UCSD) that this flaring-out requires the energy density to be negative, as some observer measures it, at the aperture itself. **No aperture can be held open by ordinary matter.** Whatever holds one open is, at that place and for that time, negative energy.

## 3. Proposition 2: the energy is lent, and called in with interest

Negative energy is not forbidden by the kernel, but it is rationed. Lawrence Ford and Thomas Roman derived the *quantum inequalities* between 1995 and 1999 CE (2195–2199 UCSD): the more negative the energy density, the shorter the time it may last. In 1999 CE they went further. A pulse of negative energy must be followed, within a time set by its own size, by a compensating positive pulse that is *larger* than it. They called the excess *quantum interest*.

This paper's identification is the one in its title. **The coherence debt a scaffold accumulates while it holds an aperture open is the quantum interest owed on the negative energy the aperture borrows.** It must be repaid. It grows with the aperture's size and with how long it is held. A scaffold that cannot compensate for it as fast as it accrues is, in the Lagrange page's words, [piling up coherence debt faster than ship systems can compensate](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/).

## 4. Proposition 3: why the gradient decides

On this reading the scaffold's budget is spent against the local geometry. In a steep gravitational gradient the aperture is loaded unevenly, the borrowing is asymmetric across it, and the inequalities close faster on the loaded side. The model therefore predicts what the Corps' rules already record: aperture shear, shorter safe transit windows, and larger post-transit re-sync debt near a mass. It also predicts the converse the Lagrange page reports at L4 and L5: wider aperture limits, longer windows and lower re-sync debt where the geometry is flat. On this model re-sync debt is the part of the repayment that falls due at the exit.

## 5. Proposition 4: why a fold is slow

If the debt is interest, the repayment time scales with the loan. The model predicts that staging, scaffold generation and certification will outlast the crossing they make possible, and that the ratio grows with the aperture. That is the pace *FTL Mechanics* records: slow in the way an aeroplane journey is slow, with the drift the least of it.

## 6. Proposition 5: why the fold must close

A shortcut through spacetime whose two ends persist, and move relative to one another, becomes a path into its own past. Morris, Thorne and Ulvi Yurtsever showed this in 1988 CE, and Stephen Hawking argued in 1992 CE (2192 UCSD) that physics acts to prevent it, which he called *chronology protection*. The Corps' rule that a fold is collapsed after transfer is, on this reading, chronology protection done by procedure. This paper reads *persistence drift* as the first measurable approach to that condition. The rule prevents it whether or not the reading is right, and the record's refusal of every retrocausal influence is kept either way.

## 7. Proposition 6: opening and closing are changes of topology

A fold that opens and then closes changes the topology of spacetime twice. Robert Geroch proved in 1967 CE (2167 UCSD) that such a change, in a classical spacetime, requires either a path into the past or a moment at which the geometry is not smooth. Since the first is refused, the model requires the second. It places the non-smooth moment in scaffold formation, which is where the Corps spends most of a fold's time. The model does not say what happens at that moment. It says only that something not smooth must.

## 8. For later authors

**Derived** from the literature, and safe to cite as such: Propositions 1, 5 and 6, and the quantum inequalities and quantum interest in Proposition 2.

**This paper's own identifications**, to be cited as a model and not as a finding: that coherence debt *is* quantum interest (Proposition 2), the account of the gradient (Proposition 3), the pace (Proposition 4), and the readings of persistence drift and of scaffold formation (Propositions 5 and 6).

The thesis in one line, for anyone who needs to quote it: **every fold is held open on borrowed energy, and every rule the Corps keeps about folds is a schedule of repayment.**

## 9. What this model does not do

**It does not explain the fold.** Adjacency is a fact of the Common Manifold, defined a scope wider than any aperture. This paper starts where the fold meets our four dimensions.

**It has no constants.** The model relates coherence debt to quantum interest in form. It has not been calibrated in units, and the bench measurement that would calibrate it, the debt accrued by a scaffold of known aperture and dwell, has not been made in comparable terms across sites.

**It does not account for quiet-zone tunnels.** A [natural conduit](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/) in a quiet zone holds open with no scaffold anyone has found. On this model something must pay its debt, and the paper does not know what.

**It does not reach beyond the local archecluster.** The Common Manifold page says a neighbouring archecluster folds by the same rules, and nothing here disputes that. Whether the debt's arithmetic is the same where the kernel composes differently is a question for a bench that has been there.

**It does not model failure.** The Institute's account of Dock Seven, an aperture held open for four months on overused and miscalibrated equipment, is what this model would call a debt left unpaid. The model adds nothing to that account, and says nothing about what the witnesses saw.

**It excludes every Etheric term.** If the extension of the kernel contributes to an aperture, this paper has not counted it.
