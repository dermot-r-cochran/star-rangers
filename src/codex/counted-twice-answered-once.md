### Receiving desk's note

*The Survey Archive asked, through the Federation record, for the history of the Verdani by one of them. On Verdance an asking from outside is a matter for the Fourfold Accord, the standing council of one delegate from each of the four countries' governments, convened whenever an outside power requires a single Verdani position; the asking was treated as one, and the Accord sat on it. What came back was one position and one delegate to give it: the Aurelin Herdlands' seat, Ruvel Swardkin, who gave it aloud to a Survey Corps reader at one of Verdance's orbital trade platforms, the reader having been asked up and not down. The reader took it in the working rendering. The Verdani original is not on file, and the Archive holds no description of the people's channel. The author gave every interval in Verdance's own years and in the Accord's count of them, declined to give the Accord's present year, and declined, on the Stonewake seat's asking, to set any year of the Accord's beside a year of the record's. The desk has set none beside them either.*

---

You asked Verdance for its history, and Verdance has one way of answering an asking from outside, which is this. Four seats sit. Each says what the answer must have in it and what it must not. What is left when all four have finished is the position, and one of us gives it. It is the Herdlands' turn, so the voice is mine and the position is not. Hear every sentence that follows as four people's, except where I say it is mine, and I will say.

The reader asked me to begin where our history begins. The four of us spent most of a day on where that is, and the position begins where the four of us could agree that it begins, which is the day your Federation's survey came down. Not because nothing happened before. Because before it no two of our countries had needed the same answer to anything, and a count begins where counting becomes necessary. Each country has its own count and its own beginning, and I was asked to give you none of them, and I give you none. The Accord counts from the coming. Everything I give you in years is in our years, counted that way.

### Four lights

We were four countries before we were anything to anyone else, and we were one people before we were four countries, and we have never been confused about the order.

The position puts the reason the way the Concord's seat asked it to be put, from the ground. A child is bonded, bed to bed, in its first season, and the partner that goes into it is bred to one light. The canopy's partner is bred to light that has come through a roof. The fens' is bred to light that has come through mist. The terraces' is bred to light that has come through nothing and must be partly turned away. Mine is bred to a sky that is hot at noon and cold before dawn, and it is red for that reason and for no other. A partner bred to one light does less well in another, for life. So a Verdani stays where its partner eats, and a people who cannot move is four countries, and that is the whole of our politics and the whole of our geography, and the four of us agreed that it would not take a second paragraph.

What each country made of staying is that country's. The Republic's seat would not have the position speak of the Guild's works without saying that they are a duty and not a trade; it says that, and no more, because the rest is the Republic's. The Dominion's seat would not have the position speak of the terraces at all except to say that a right there is held and not owned; it says that, and no more. The Concord's seat asked that the groves be left out, and they are left out, and I have now said that they were left out, which the Concord's seat accepted as the price of leaving them out. The Herdlands' seat is mine, and what I would not leave out is this: the routes are older than the Councils that follow them, the Councils are older than the count, and the count is the only thing in this position with a number on it, by the Councils' consent and not by their preference.

### Before anyone asked

Before the coming, the Accord sat for one kind of thing only: a dispute between two of the four that neither could settle alone and neither could compel. How long before, the position does not say, because the four seats say four things, and a position with four numbers in it is four positions. The position says: before. I will tell you, as mine, that the Herdlands' count has the Accord older than the Republic and the Republic's count has it the other way, and that neither of us has been asked to prove it by anyone who could use the answer.

What the Accord was not, before the coming, was a voice. Nobody outside Verdance had asked Verdance anything.

### The first year and the second

Your Federation's survey came down in the year we count as the first, and it counted us twice.

I am the kind it counted twice. It found the canopy's people, green, under the roof, and it found the steppe's people, red, in the open, and it wrote down two peoples, related, and went on to the fens and the terraces and did not find a third or a fourth, which I have always thought the strangest part of it, since the fens and the terraces are as unlike the canopy as the steppe is; but it had seen two colours by then and had two names, and a surveyor with two names will fit a third thing under one of them. That is not a complaint. The survey was careful about what it could see, and what it could see was the partner, and the partner is what we file ourselves by too. If you file by the partner and do not yet know what a partner is, two peoples is the honest reading.

We told them in the first year. The position records that we told them. Four countries which had never before had one thing to say to anyone found that they did: that a Farrowkin of the canopy and a Swardkin of the steppe are kin by the same act, bed to bed, in the first season, and that the act is the same act and the partner a different partner, and that this is what a people is here. The question closed in the second year of the count, when the survey's own instruments agreed with us about the children. Your record says confirmed interfertility closed it. It did, for you. It had not been open for us. The position asks that both be kept and that neither be written over the other, and that was the Dominion's seat's asking, and I think it is the best thing in this position.

### The ninth year

In the ninth year of the count the Accord gave the Federation the second answer it had ever given anyone, which was yes.

I will give the reason the way it was argued, since it was argued from specifics, in a sitting the Herdlands' count has at a season and a half and the Concord's at a season, the difference being what each counts as sitting. The Federation had asked a world to join a thing built so that worlds which could not share a government need not. It had asked through the Accord because the Accord was the door it had been shown, and it had assumed, correctly, that the Accord could answer. What it had not understood, and what the four of us spent the season on, was that it had built for worlds the thing we had built for countries, and that the seat it was offering was a seat for a world, and we were not one in the sense it meant. The Republic's seat asked whether a Federation that left a member its own law would leave a member's four countries theirs. It would; it did not understand the question, but it would. The Concord's seat asked what the seat cost in light, and was told nothing, and that was wrong: a delegate's vanes are folded in a room, a room's light is worth nothing to a kinbed, and the Concord's seat has paid that in every sitting since, and so has every seat here. The Dominion's seat asked what came back, and was told, and said that it was less than what went up and more than nothing, and that was enough. The Herdlands asked whether a thing joined could be left, and were told yes, and the Councils, whose consent is given a season at a time, consented on that word and on no other.

Your record says our delegates call the fit close to seamless. We do. We say it, and we say why: a confederation that leaves each member its own law had arrived, for worlds, where we had arrived for countries, and neither of us needed the other explained.

### One seat

The seat is one seat. It is held for Verdance by a delegate of the Accord, in a turn between other worlds' turns, and when the turn is Verdance's one of us folds in a room for it. The formula by which the assembly weighs a seat counts a world as one, and the position says what the Concord's seat has been saying in that room for eleven of our years, by a narrowing margin each time: that a world with four governments on it is being counted as a convenience and not as the thing that makes it worth having at the table. The Accord has not instructed its delegate to stop. The position records that the Accord has not instructed its delegate to stop, and the Herdlands' seat asked that it be recorded as a decision and not an oversight, and it is.

I will add, as mine, that eleven of our years of losing by less each time is not what you would call it. On the steppe a line that is walked again each season, a little further each time, is a route being made, and nobody who makes one expects to arrive on it.

### The ring

There is a human ring above us. It has been above us since before some of the seats at this sitting were seated, and it is the one thing in this position that the Accord did not sign.

The position gives the ring the way the four of us could agree to give it. The Federation hosts it; Verdance is a member of the Federation; the Federation's law is that a member's internal governance is its own, and the ring is not internal, it is eight thousand of your kilometres up, so it was the Federation's to host, and it hosted it, and the Accord was told. The Accord has never objected. The Accord has never been asked. Both are true, and the position puts them side by side and asks that nobody read the first as the second.

Two hundred and forty of your people live in it and do not come down. Your record says the bar is protocol, and that they are equipped to land and not permitted to. The position says this: that nobody on Verdance has been asked whether they may, that if anyone asks the answer will be four answers, and that the ring's people, who have had by your count twenty years to ask, seem to know it.

What the ring sees, the Accord has not asked either. The Republic's seat asked that the position say that the Guild knows what can be seen from up there, and that if the Republic ever asks the ring for anything it will be the water, since a flood cycle is best seen from above and the Guild has never seen one from above. The Concord's seat asked that the position say nothing about the roof from above, and it says nothing. The Dominion's seat said that the terraces have nothing to hide and nothing to show, and the position does not say that either, since it was said in the sitting and not asked for. The Herdlands' asking is mine, and it is that the position say what a watch is. The Councils keep one on the routes, and what is seen on a route is kept by whoever saw it until someone with standing on that route asks. That is a watch. Your record says the ring keeps one, and has argued with itself for twenty of your years whether that is restraint. The Herdlands' seat asked that the position tell the ring, if it ever reads this, that the Councils have had the same argument about the routes, and have not settled it either.

### The light, read from outside

The Concord's seat asked that the position include a thing your Survey Corps already knows, and say it as ours. What your Corps learns of a lane with our sky at the end of it is the light of our own sky read from outside, at a resolution none of our four countries' instruments can reach, and a people whose children are bonded to one light have a direct stake in knowing exactly which light a given patch of ground receives. That is what the Concord's seat has said in the assembly, and what it asked the position to say, and the other three let it stand because none of us could say it was not so. The Dominion's seat added, for the sitting and not the position, that the terraces could do with knowing what is under the ground as well as over it, and was told that your Corps does not read that from a lane, and said that it had asked. I include that as mine, because it is the kind of thing that is true about the terraces and about asking.

What the assembly decided when the Concord's seat put it to them, the position does not say, since the Accord was not in that room. One of its delegates was, and a delegate's account of a session is the delegate's, and goes to the Accord in the Accord's own way, which is not this one.

### One of ours

One of ours is in your Survey Corps. The position says that, and says which corps, since you already know it, and says which lineage, since a lineage is not a country; and it does not say which of the four of us your Corps was asked in by, because that is that country's business and the position has no standing in it. I add nothing of mine to this item. That was a Councils' decision too.

### Four gaps

Every seat left something out, and the position names, at each place where it was, that something was left out, which was the Herdlands' asking and the hardest one to get. The Concord's groves, and what the Assembly did about bonding after a case in another part of the Concord. The Republic's drained land, and who wants it. The Dominion's revenue, and who counts it. The Herdlands' waystations, and what they have bought. Each of those is a country's, and the Accord has no authority over internal law in any of the four and does not try to acquire any, and a history that put them in would be the Accord acquiring it by the back way. So they are out. But a history with its gaps unmarked is a history pretending to be whole, and we are a people who were counted as two by someone who did not know what he could not see, and the position marks each gap where it is and does not fill it. You have a rule of your own for that, I am told. It was the one thing of yours the four of us read in the sitting and did not argue about.

### The year we are in

The reader asked me, at the end, what year it is in the Accord's count, so that your record could set its own year beside it. I was asked by the Dominion's seat not to answer, and I have told the reader why, and I will tell you. Two numbers set side by side are divided by the next person who sees them, and what comes out is a conversion, and a conversion is a thing the four of us have never made between our own four counts, and we are not going to make one with yours first. Your record holds no conversion of our years. Good. Keep it that way until the four of us have made one between ourselves, and if that is a long time, it is a long time, and your record will have been exact about us for the whole of it.

That is the history of the Verdani as the Fourfold Accord gives it: four countries from four lights; an Accord that sat before anyone asked it anything; a survey that counted us twice, and a second year in which it stopped; a yes in the ninth year; one seat, and eleven years of losing it by less; a ring we did not sign and have not asked; the light read from outside; one of ours in your Corps; four gaps, each marked where it is; and no year. You asked for one answer. There were four, and you could not have used them, so the four of us sat until there was one, and the one is mine to give and not mine, and I have given it.

The light up here is worth nothing to a kinbed. I am going down.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author and, by the author's account, for the four seats that made it; the Archive has not asked the other three seats and holds the position as given. The Archive has set no year of its own beside anything in it. Every interval the author gives is in Verdance's own years in the Accord's count from the day the Federation's survey came down; the Accord's present year was declined, the record holds no conversion of Verdance's years, and the desk has derived none from the account. Where the record dates an event the author names, the record's date stands on its own pages and is not set beside the author's: the [reciprocal instrument](/star-rangers/timeline/2806-greenward-guest-ring-instrument/) about 2806 UCSD, the Canopy Concord's senior delegate seated in 2819 UCSD and [first holding Verdance's assembly seat](/star-rangers/timeline/2822-isren-farrowkin-federation-seat/) in 2822 UCSD, and [the compact revisited](/star-rangers/timeline/2826-material-support-compact-revisited/) in the early summer of 2826 UCSD; the record dates neither Verdance's contact nor its membership. Six things in the account the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Verdani](/star-rangers/lore/verdani/) and [Verdance](/star-rangers/lore/planets/verdance/) entries and at [Greenward](/star-rangers/lore/greenward-habitat/): that early Federation contact teams catalogued canopy-belt and steppe Verdani as related but separate species before confirmed interfertility closed the question; that a strain is adapted to one biome's light and migration is a metabolic cost, which is why Verdance has four governments; that the Accord has no authority over internal law and does not attempt to acquire any; that Verdance holds full membership through the Accord on the rotating assembly, and its delegates call the fit close to seamless; that the confederation signed nothing at Greenward and has never asked; and that the Concord's senior delegate has argued the assembly's weighted-vote formula for eleven local years. The Accord's count from the coming, that the four countries told the survey in its first year, the second year's closing, the ninth year's yes, that the Accord sat before contact, and what the survey made of the fens and the terraces are the author's and the Accord's, and the record holds none of them. Which delegate held the Concord's seat at the sitting the author does not say, and the desk has not asked whether it was [Isren Farrowkin](/star-rangers/characters/isren-farrowkin/), whose page places her at the assembly in 2826 UCSD. How the Accord chooses which seat gives a position is the author's *turn*, and is not established. The one Verdani in the Survey Corps the record names is [Elsha Rootkin](/star-rangers/characters/elsha-rootkin/), of the Rootkin; which country the Corps was asked in by, the record does not hold either. The four matters left out are marked where the author marked them, under the Archive's own rule that [what it cannot carry is marked and not supplied](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/). What the ring's people would answer, and what the assembly decided on the Concord's proposal, the account does not say and the Archive does not hold.*
