*A note on what this document is. It is an offering to subscribers, written by the Combine about itself to raise capital for the operation that ended at Dock Seven. It is filed here because it is the only account the Combine gave of itself, and because a copy was found in the reconciliation of its licence. The Archive has not established who received it or who subscribed. The subscriber list was not part of the offering and is not held. Read it as the Combine wished to be read. The Archive's own account is on the [Hyperfold Yield Combine](/star-rangers/lore/hyperfold-yield-combine/) page.*

---

### 1. The Issuer

The Hyperfold Yield Combine ("Hyperfold", "the Issuer") was registered in 2822 UCSD under the inner-system commercial fold framework. It has traded without interruption since then, in transit services and in the supply and operation of proprietary aperture equipment. It has never been the subject of a finding, an order or a claim.

The Issuer's trade name was adopted at registration on the advice of its naming counsel. It is used across all of the Issuer's equipment, filings and hulls.

### 2. What we are for

Every hull that folds pays for the aperture it opens. It pays in power, in wear and in the margins its operators will not fly, and the hulls that pay most are the ones that can least afford it: frontier tenders, courier lines, the small operators on the long circuits. A fold that costs less is a fold that more people can make, and a margin that becomes survivable is one that nobody has to refuse.

**Hyperfold exists to make folding cheaper and safer for everyone who folds.** The Threshold Programme is how.

### 3. The product

Fold-transit stabiliser is a consumable that reduces the coherence debt an aperture builds up while it is held. Every operator who folds wants it, and the supply has never met the demand. The first output from the Threshold site was assayed independently and certified at a grade above any stabiliser now on the market (Annex A). All of it has been sold.

### 4. The site

The Issuer's survey team found the Threshold site by reading the public record. Threshold Station's instrument drift reports, filed since 2815 UCSD and held open in the Survey Archive, record a discrepancy between two chronometers in Dock Seven that the station carried for years as a hardware fault. The Issuer read it as a boundary. Its licensed survey (survey exemption, Annex B) confirmed the reading: the boundary there is narrow, standing and stable, and it carries a feedstock density without precedent in the inner system.

The site cannot be moved and does not need to be. It is a property of the place.

### 5. The method

The Issuer opened its aperture at the site at the start of the summer, under its transit rights, and has maintained it since under its rendering permit. Feedstock presents itself at the aperture. No capture operation is required, and no capture equipment is carried. Rendering takes place aboard the Issuer's platform, on station.

Feedstock is boundary-native and replenishes itself. It is unowned, and it is unprotected under every framework that applies to it (Annex D).

### 6. Why it gets cheaper

Stabiliser lowers the cost of holding an aperture. The Issuer's own apertures use the Issuer's own product. Each season of operation therefore lowers the cost of the next season and widens the reach of the site.

| Year (UCSD) | Cost of holding an aperture (index) | Reachable feedstock (index) |
|---|---|---|
| 2831 | 100 | 100 |
| 2836 | 59 | 169 |
| 2841 | 35 | 287 |
| 2851 | 12 | 823 |
| 2871 | 1.5 | 6,765 |

*Basis: a constant reduction of ten per cent a year in the cost of holding an aperture, with reachable feedstock taken to vary inversely with that cost. Illustrative only.*

### 7. Legal position

The Issuer holds transit rights, a survey exemption and a rendering permit. Its licence descends by an unbroken chain of assignment from fold-aperture patents first registered in the Imperium period. The chain is set out in Annex B, and every assignment in it is in order.

Counsel's opinion, appended to the licence in full (Annex C), confirms that no term of the licence requires an aperture to close once it is opened.

### 8. Risk factors

Subscribers should consider the following.

- **Duty cycle.** The programme runs the Issuer's aperture equipment above its rated duty cycle. The Issuer's maintenance schedule allows for this.
- **Aperture duration.** The operating model keeps apertures open beyond the customary length of a transit. See section 7 and Annex C.
- **Voluntary standards.** The Star Rangers' Safety Corps publishes standards for fold-adjacent operations. They bind only an operator who adopts them. The Issuer has not adopted them and is under no obligation to do so.
- **Representations by third parties.** Officers of the Star Rangers may make observations about the programme. The Star Rangers hold no jurisdiction over a licensed commercial operation in open water.
- **Concentration.** The programme depends on a single site.

The Issuer is not aware of any other material risk.

### 9. Holders

The Issuer's shares are held through registered holding instruments, as recorded under the inner-system commercial framework. The framework does not require disclosure of interests below the registered tier, and the Issuer makes none.
