### Receiving desk's note

*The Xenomusicology branch of the [Music Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-music-corps/) keeps its officers' listening files, and copied this one to the Archive on request. [Ilse Varga](/star-rangers/characters/ilse-varga/) kept it in 2822 UCSD aboard a Corps carrier in Freyari, the inhabited air of [Cirrane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/cirrane/), while the carrier stood off a shoal of the [Ollune](/star-rangers/lore/ollune/) and waited for marks to be composed. The file does not name the carrier or the shoal, and the copy gives her branch and not her rank at the time. It is short, as her files are. The days are the carrier's. The title is the desk's, from the one thing in the file that an Ollune said. The note at the end is ours.*

---

**Before the days**

I was aboard for the carriage and not for this. The shoal was composing marks for the carrier to take, which is slow work and theirs, and I was told to expect twelve days of standing off. A carrier in the band listens the whole time, because weather there is called before it is seen. I asked for a line off the pressure instruments and was given one.

What the line is. Four pressure heads on the hull. They give me the swell of the air against the hull, and from the difference between the heads the bearing a call came along. They do not give how far. Nothing in this file is a distance.

What I did to it. The far word sits under my hearing. The figure below runs at six to nine swells a second, and a human ear begins at about twenty. I raised it five octaves by playing it thirty-two times too fast, which is the plain way and the one I can account for. That makes it thirty-two times shorter as well. What the band spent forty seconds on, I heard in a little over one. For timing I went back to the trace.

So I have not heard any of this. I have heard a thing made from it, at a pitch I chose, at a speed nobody there kept, through a hull. I do not know what an Ollune hears the far word with, or what it is to hear it, and I did not ask.

The reader. Each watch has a crew member who reads the far word for the log. Where I say what the words round the figure were, that is the reader's rendering and I mark it R.

**Day 1.** A figure, from the bearing of the shoal we stand off. Five swells. The fourth is held about twice as long as the others. The fifth falls. No words with it. Noted because an hour later it came again from the same bearing, the same.

**Day 2.** The figure from up the band, on a bearing where the carrier knows of no shoal, which tells me about the carrier. The fourth swell is held less. A sixth is added, short, rising. R: the call it comes in is a boast. A hold took two more sleepers through a night than it was made up for, and nobody went under. The figure comes last, where a name would go. The far word has no names. I withdraw the comparison.

**Day 3.** From our shoal's bearing, so it was heard here and taken up. R: an insult. The reader would not give it word for word and said that was the tables and not delicacy. In substance: whoever put a sixth on it has the judgement of a falling object. Then the figure, five swells, the fourth held longer than on Day 1. Emphasis? I cannot show it.

**Day 4.** Up the band. Six swells. No words. Twice.

**Day 5.** Three bearings in one watch: ours, up the band, and one down the band I had not had before. All different. The one from down the band drops the fifth swell and keeps the sixth, which neither of the others would call a correction. No words with any of them, all watch. R: nothing to render. The reader logs these as *figure* and has for years, on other stand-offs in other bands, and had no reason to think one day's was the next day's. Nor had I, until I had five days written down.

I have put *tune?* in the margin. The question mark stays.

**Day 6.** I asked. The reader put the question out through the hull for me, in the far word, which a carrier may do, since asking there costs nobody anything: whose is the figure?

An answer came inside the minute, from our shoal's bearing. R: *it weighs nothing.*

I asked who had answered. The reader looked at me for a while. Nobody can say who answered. The far word does not carry it, and my four heads give me a bearing with a shoal on it. I asked whether it was an answer to my question or a way of declining it. The reader said it might be either, or a joke, or untrue, that nothing said in the far word is held to, and that for an answer anyone would stand behind I should have to ask in the near word. That means holding station beside one person who consents to it, for however long a mark takes, about a noise. The reader did not recommend it. I did not ask.

**Days 7 and 8.** Weather. Calls from every bearing, both days. R: shear, position, shear. No figure. Nobody had dropped it. There was something else to say.

**Day 9.** Down the band only. Four swells now, and the held one is the third. If I had not had the days between I would not know it for the figure of Day 1. Side by side on the trace the two share a held swell with a fall after it, and nothing else. Every step between them I can show. I cannot show the whole, because there is no first form to measure the last one against. There is only the first I happened to hear, which was not the first.

**Day 10.** Once, down the band, fainter. I cannot say farther. A figure called quietly and a figure called from a long way off look the same on four heads.

**Days 11 and 12.** None. The marks were ready in the middle of the last watch. The carrier took them aboard and went up out of the band.

**After**

What I can show. A figure of about five swells was called in one band, over ten days, from three bearings. It changed at each taking. No call of it carried who made it, since nothing in the far word does. It came with a boast, with an insult and alone, and it was put down for weather without anyone remarking on that.

What I cannot show. That it is music: I have *tune?* and I am leaving it. That it was one figure to them from day to day, as it is to me on a trace. What any of them thinks of it. I have one answer, from nobody, to a question put by a hull, in a channel that binds no one. It is not the Ollune view of anything. I record it because it was said.

Who began it. Here the difficulty is not my instruments. Nobody in that band can show it either, and nobody there seemed to want to. I am told the shoals on those three bearings ride one band at one speed, and will never be nearer to one another than they were that week.

I wrote aboard that I lost it on Day 10. That is wrong and I have struck it. I left. It was last on my heads going down the band and getting fainter, and it may be going still. I would know it for perhaps two more takings. After that it would be somebody's Day 1.

I have been asked since whether it was beautiful. Five octaves up and thirty-two times too fast, it is a short phrase a person could whistle, and I have caught myself at it. That is a fact about the raising. At its own pitch it would have gone through me as it went through the hull, six to nine times a second for most of a minute, and I would have stood there and heard nothing.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. It is one listener's account of ten days of calls in one band, from one stand-off, by instrument. The author says it is not the Ollune view of anything, and the Archive files it as no more than she says: not a practice of the Ollune, of Freyari, or of the shoal she stood off.*

*What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Ollune](/star-rangers/lore/ollune/) and [Cirrane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/cirrane/) entries, the file rests on and does not go past: that the far word is low-frequency pressure, heard by everyone in the band, gone as it is uttered, and carries no speaker's identity; that it binds nothing, and that argument, news, poetry, insult, boast and alarm all live in it; that a falsehood there is unremarkable; that the near word is slow to compose and needs a consenting body holding station; that two shoals in one band drift at one speed and never meet; and that off-world hulls carry marks between shoals. That a human ear does not hear a tone at six to nine cycles a second, and that playing a recording thirty-two times too fast raises it five octaves and shortens it by the same factor, is ordinary acoustics.*

*The same entry says that anything an Ollune keeps must be lifted, hourly, by the body that keeps it. The answer of Day 6 sits easily beside that sentence. The desk draws nothing from it. It was a far word, its speaker cannot be known, and the reader who rendered it said it might have been a joke.*

*What the file holds from no source but its author: the figure and its changes; the carrier's four pressure heads and its far-word reader, who is on the Corps' rolls by office and is not named; the boast and the insult, in the reader's rendering; and the question and its answer. The Archive holds no recording. The branch holds the traces and the raised copies, and the author's first page says what a raised copy is. What an Ollune hears the far word with, the record does not say, and she did not ask.*

*The author corrects one word of her own and the desk has left the correction in. She notes that she has caught herself whistling the figure as she raised it. The desk notes only that this is one more taking, on a bearing of its own, and that nobody on Cirrane will hear it.*
