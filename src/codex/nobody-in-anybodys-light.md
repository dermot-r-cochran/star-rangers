### Receiving desk's note

*The Archive did not ask for this. The Tidewrights' Guild of the [Fenmarch Republic](/star-rangers/lore/planets/verdance/#four-countries) keeps its keepers' season notes open, and the Federation record carries them among Verdance's public papers in a working rendering. Most of a keeper's note is water levels. This one, from [Meril Duskkin](/star-rangers/characters/meril-duskkin/), has a second part that is not, and the desk has taken that part whole and left the levels where they are. The Verdani original is not on file, and the Archive still holds no description of the people's channel. "Hour" and "week" are the rendering's words for the Guild's divisions of a day and a season; the record holds no conversion of them. The title is the desk's, from the note's own phrase. The note at the end is ours.*

---

**Second part. Not levels.**

The Guild lets a keeper put down anything the gate was used for. The gate was used for this, so I put it down. I also put it down because last year's note says only *figure set, clear day, held*, and that is not true. It held for all of the day but an hour.

What it is. On this channel, on the first clear day after the flood goes down, the households keep places on the bank together from first light to last. That is all of it. Other channels do something else, or nothing. The channel above ours holds that we do it to be looked at. They are welcome to come and look. It takes all day and nothing happens.

Why it needs working out. Under mist nobody can be in anybody's light. Mist-light comes off the whole sky, and one of us opened beside another takes nothing from them that either would notice. Most days in the fens are like that, and it is why fen people keep close and think nothing of it. A clear day is different. The light has a direction. Open your vanes then, and there is a strip of ground behind you having a worse day because of you. It moves as the sun moves, and at first light and last light it is very long. On wide dry ground none of this would matter, and everybody would keep apart. The bank after flood is not wide. It is as wide as the flood left it, and all of us are on it.

So it is a problem, and it has an answer, and the answer has to be found beforehand. Nobody can try a place on the day and leave it if it is wrong, because to shift is to put your shade somewhere nobody planned for. You keep the place the figure gives you, and you turn as you would turn anyway, and if the figure was right, then from first light to last nobody was in anybody's light.

How it is worked. It comes to the gate because the gate is the one straight line on the channel, and I know which way it runs. The gate's shade on the water tells me where the sun is, better than anything else I have. Each household gives me its reach, opened. The households measure themselves; a keeper who offered to do it for them would not be asked back. I peg the bank out small, on the flat by the gate, one peg to a household, and work each peg's shade for each hour of the day I expect, and move pegs until no shade touches a peg. Then the households come and look, and move them back.

That takes most of the weeks the flood takes, and it should. The bank is never last year's bank. A household is never last year's size. Somebody was at the good end last year and knows it. And every year there is one arrangement that very nearly works, which puts the smallest household at the downstream end in shade for the whole morning, and every year somebody proposes it, and every year it is the smallest household that does. Every year the rest say no. That takes a week by itself.

Last year. The flood went down later than I had worked for. The clear day came later again, and by then the sun was lower than the sun I had pegged, and a lower sun throws a longer shade. I knew that. I had not gone back to the pegs. The morning held and the middle of the day held. In the afternoon, for about an hour, the shade of the household third from the gate lay across the household fourth.

The fourth household kept its place. I want that in the Guild's book. They knew at once. You know shade the way you know a missed meal, because it is one. If they had moved clear, their own shade would have gone onto the two households below them, the bank being what it is. So they stayed where the figure had put them and went short for an hour, every one of them, on the one clear day. The third household did not know. You do not feel the shade you throw. I did not know either. I learned it that evening, from the pegs, working the day back, and I went down the bank and asked. They said the figure had been very nearly right.

What I have changed. I work the figure now for three days and not one: the day I expect, and a day each side of it, as far as the flood has ever run late in my keeping. It costs a wider figure, and the bank will not always give that. Where it will not, I say so before the day, and to the household it falls on, and they decide.

I used to say that the figure is the fine thing: a day on which nobody cost anybody anything. I still say it. But I have had a year with the pegs, and the hour that failed is the only hour of that day I can tell you anything about. When the figure holds there is nothing to tell. People are on a bank, turning, as anyone turns. We work for weeks to make a day with nothing in it, and last year it had one hour in it, and in that hour one household paid for the rest of us and did not say so. I cannot tell the Guild which of those two is the custom. I think the channel would say both, and then argue about the pegs.

This year's figure is pegged. It is wider. If the day comes and it holds, next year's note will say *held*, and now the Guild knows what that word leaves out.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. The author gives this as one channel's custom and says other channels do otherwise. The Archive holds it as that: it is not the Fenmarch Republic's, not the Guild's, and not the Verdani's, and nothing in it is a law.*

*What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Verdani](/star-rangers/lore/verdani/) and [Verdance](/star-rangers/lore/planets/verdance/) entries, the note rests on and does not go past: that the kinbeds are carried on broad vanes, folded when the light is poor and opened when there is sky worth taking, and that a vane opening is a meal; that photosynthesis pays by area, so that shade is food not had; that a Verdani orients continuously toward the light without deciding to, which is all the note means by turning; that a fenland strain is adapted to reduced light beneath marsh mist; that the fenlands are fed by seasonal flood cycles; and that a flood-gate runs straight across a fen channel. That light under mist has no direction and clear light has one is ordinary optics. What a clear day is worth to a fenland strain, set against a day of mist, the people's page does not say and the note does not either; the author speaks only of shade, which he has stood in.*

*What the note holds from no source but its author: the custom itself, the bank, the pegs and the three days; that a household gives its own reach; the third and fourth households, whom he does not name; and the late flood and the lower sun of the year before. The record names no fenland place and the note names none. The record holds no picture of the figure. A still would show people on a bank beside a channel, which is what a visitor would see. The figure is in the turning, which takes the whole day, and in nobody going short, which no instrument the Archive holds can show and nobody outside the people can share. The one hour the author can describe is the hour it failed, and the desk notes that he noticed this before the desk did.*
