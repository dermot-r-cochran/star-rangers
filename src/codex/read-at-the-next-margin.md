### Receiving desk's note

*The Survey Archive asked, through the Corps' rolls of former officers, for a history of the Serephine Dunekin by one of them. The author answered from Kharis Belt IV, where she has lived since her service, and did not come to the desk: she wrote the account there, in the Archive's working language, and sent it back by the route the asking had taken, in parts. Each part was written in one of the two hours a day in which her people are open, and is headed dawn or dusk as she headed it. The parts came as they were written, one at a time, and the desk has joined none of them: a part ends where its margin closed and the next begins where the next margin opened, and nothing was carried from one into another by any hand but hers, who says she carried nothing. The desk has set no count beside them. What her people speak among themselves the Archive does not hold and the account does not supply; it is written in our language and in no other, and that is all the desk can say of how it was written. The note at the end is ours.*

---

### Dawn

The desk has asked for the history of my people, and I am writing it in the open, and I will say what that means before anything else, since it decides the shape of what you are holding.

My body is closed for most of the day and most of the night. Your record has this right and says it well, and I will not improve on it: we are a closed form and an open form, and the open form has two hours, the one after the cold and the one after the heat, and the rest of the time the sky would kill us, so we are shut. Nothing in this account was written closed. A closed body does not write; it waits. So every word here was set down in a margin, and when the margin ended the writing ended with it, wherever it was, and I did not go back into that part from the next one. If a part stops where you would not have stopped it, that is where the margin closed.

Your record says our day has a shape rather than a length. It does. You are about to be given the shape, one margin at a time, and I have headed each part by which margin it was, because the two are not the same hour and you will read them differently once you know. At dawn I have come through the cold and am slow for the first of it, and the day's work is in front of me and wants setting. At dusk the heat is behind me and the work is there to be read. This is a dawn. The mirrors want setting. I will come back to you at dusk.

### Dusk

The work is read. What that means comes later; it is the whole of the history, and I do not want it first.

Here is what is first. Our history has no middle.

Every day of it has two lethal hours in the middle of it, one of heat and one of cold, twelve hours apart, and nobody was ever in them. So the things that happened to us happened at the edges of days, and the things that happened in the middles happened to nobody, and were found at the next margin, done or not done, and read. That is how we keep a history and it is the only way we could have kept one. A people whose witnesses are shut for most of every day do not have a history that runs; they have one that is read at intervals, like a melt. Half of what we are was done while none of us was there.

So when your record asks when a thing happened, I can tell you which margin found it. I cannot tell you when it was done. Nothing can. That is not a withholding, and it is not a gap in the record; it is where the record is. You will want a year all the same, since you have years. I have not given one anywhere in this, and I will say why at the end.

The margin is closing. Tomorrow, the first thing we did.

### Dawn

The first thing we did that anyone remembers was find something heavy enough to be slow.

I mean that exactly. A body that must shut for the heat and shut for the cold needs a place to be shut in that the heat and the cold reach late. Rock is slow. Ground is slow. Enough of either between you and the sky, and the worst of the day arrives in your shelter at the hour the day is already turning, and the worst of the night arrives toward morning, and inside it is always the hour before. We built that. Everything we have ever built is slow on purpose. Your record calls it mass used as a battery and says our structures buffer rather than insulate, and both are right, and I will add the thing the words leave out: we live in the lag. Every one of us was born inside a delay, and the delay is the first thing we made, and we have never made anything since that was not a version of it.

I set the mirrors this morning. I am slow, as I said, for the first of a dawn, and the mirrors do not mind. That part comes at dusk, because at dawn it has not happened yet.

### Dusk

The mirrors.

Your record says that thin air burns badly, and it does. We have fire. It is a poor, slow, costly thing that eats a gas none of us can spare, and nobody built anything on it, and nobody mourned that, because the sky was handing us the other thing for nothing. Light. Hard light, through air with nothing in it to soften it, from a sun none of us could stand under. So: a mirror. Then a better mirror. Then more of them, set so that they agree. That is the whole of our industrial history, and I have given it in the order it happened, and I will not give it a length, since it did not have one that anyone counted. It had margins.

Here is what a furnace is, for us. At dawn, in the hour we have, someone sets the mirrors: the angle, the stops, what is to be in the focus when the focus comes. Then the margin closes and that someone goes in. Through the middle of the day the mirrors do what they were set to do, and the focus reaches the heat that does the work, in the one hour of the day when none of us can be beside it, and none of us is. At dusk the someone comes out, and the work is there, and reads it. Done, or not done, or done wrong, and the wrongness is the lesson for tomorrow's setting. Your record says the furnace runs unattended because it has to, and that is the plainest sentence anyone has written about us. It has to. The hours in which it runs are the hours in which we cannot be.

I read the melt this evening. It was done. I will not tell you what it was, since you did not ask for a history of my furnace, and because the point is not the melt. The point is that I set a thing in one margin and read it in the next and was not there between, and that this is not a method I learned. It is the shape of the day, with a mirror in it.

### Dawn

Something short, since I am slow and the setting is slow today too.

The thing that kills us is the thing we run on. You have that sentence from your own record and I expect you read it as a flourish. It is a description. A swing of heat that size, twice a day, without fail, is a gradient, and a gradient is work for anyone who puts a thing across it, and we put everything across it: machines that take the difference between the shut heat of a wall and the open cold of the night and turn it, slowly, into something we can use at the next margin. I have been asked, on a station, by someone who meant well, whether it frightened us to live on the thing that killed us. I said that it was the one thing that had never once failed to come. He thought I had changed the subject.

### Dusk

What we cannot do. I give it at dusk, after the heat, because that is the hour I am most able to say it plainly.

We cannot keep going. A body that opens twice a day has a ceiling on how long it can work hard, and nothing moves the ceiling: not will, not training, not shame. We can work with great care for a long time at a low rate, or hard for a short one, and not both, and when the hard one is spent the closing is not a choice. Your record has written that the recovery is not a matter of fitness, and I want to say what it is instead, since you will read *recovery* with your own word under it, and your word is *rest*. It is not rest. Rest is a thing you can be talked out of. A closed body is not resting. It is closed, as a door is, and you may talk to the door.

And we cannot do the thing that will not wait. You know the sentence; it is in your record and it is about your Corps, and I was in your Corps, and I will give it its own margin.

### Dawn

I was a scout. Your record names us as scouts; it has one of us on file whose method it has read and called a metabolism with a career built on top of it, which is right, and it is his file and I will not add to it. There is nothing in it any of us would not have written. I will give you mine.

I went into the Rangers because you are the people who do things in the middle of the day. I had never seen it. I had heard it said of you, and thought it was a figure of speech. It is not. You go out at noon. You go out in the dead of the night. You go out when the thing that needs doing needs doing, and the hour is nothing to you but a number, and there is no margin anywhere in you, and I wanted to see that, the way you might want to see a sea.

I saw it. Your record says that a people built around acting when conditions permit met, in the Rangers, the one institution built around the opposite premise, and that we manage it, and that the record does not pretend it is free. All three are true. Here is the cost, from the side that pays it, and I give it as one day and no place.

There was a day, on a posting, when a thing needed doing in the middle, and I was closed. It was done. It was done by others, and it was done well, and I read it at my next margin the way I have read everything in my life: finished, by the marks it left. Nobody blamed me. Your Corps had scheduled around me, as your record says a service learns to, and the schedule had held, and the thing was done. And I was the one person in that room whose history of that day had no middle. That is the cost. Not the closing; I have closed every day of my life. The reading. Reading a thing at the next margin is the oldest skill we have, and I had never once minded it until I did it among people who had been there.

The margin is closing. You will want to know whether I minded enough to leave. I will tell you at dusk, which is the margin I tell things in.

### Dusk

No. I served out my time and came home, and the rolls have the rest, and I have left it there.

Instead, what you got wrong about me, since your record has it right in general and I can give it in particular.

You thought I was patient. Patience is a thing you have about something: you want it, and you wait, and the waiting is the patience. I had nothing about it. I was not waiting for the margin any more than you are waiting for the floor. And then, because you had decided I was patient, some of you decided I was slow, and that was the worse one, because it was kind. In my margins I was the fastest thing on that posting, and nobody saw it, because at my margins you were doing other things; and the thing we do at dusk, that your record says no other people in your Corps can do at all, I did, and your record is right not to say what it is, and I will not either. It is not a thing you would be there for.

Your air I will give one line, since your record gives it one. It is heavy. I carried it all day, every day, for the length of my service, and I wore the filter for the comfort of it, as one of us has said on file, and the comfort was real. Your stations have no sky. I opened anyway.

### Dawn

What I have not said, so that you do not go looking for it in the parts.

How we speak among ourselves. Your record has no rendering of your ladder from us, and says it does not know how we speak. It still does not. This is written in your language and that is all it is written in. I have put no word of ours into it. The words are not kept from you; it is that your record has said it could not carry them, and I take it at its word.

Whose reading opens a window. You have a ladder for everything, and you will want to know who says a margin is open. I have not said.

How we met you, and when. I was not there. I have given you no place and no count, and I will give you none; it is a thing found at a margin by people who are not me.

The melt. Which I said I would not tell you, and have not.

### Dusk

The last part, and I have kept the reason for the end, as I said I would.

I have given you no year because we have none, and we have none because a year is a thing that runs, and nothing of ours runs. Your record says the dangerous hours are the productive ones and the safe hours are for living. It is right. But read it again from inside: the hours for living come twice a day and are short, and everything else is either work nobody watches or a shut body in a slow wall, and a life made of that is not a line. It is a count of margins. I have not counted mine for you, and you could not use the number if I had.

Your day has a length. I did not know, until I served among you, that a day could have a length and no shape, and I did not know, and still do not, whether that is a thing to be sorry for. You can be out at noon. You can do the thing that will not wait. You have never once read a day finished that you were not there for. I do not know what that makes of a people. I know what the other thing made of mine.

I have written this in the open, in the hours we have for living, and I have given you a good many of them, and I do not count that against the account. Read it at your next margin. You have none. Read it when you can; that is what you have instead, and it is a great deal, and I have seen it.

The margin is closing. I am closing with it. That is the shape of the day, and now you have had the width of it.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author, as her own account, in the parts it came in. The Archive has set no year to anything in it and has asked for none: the author gives none and says why, the record holds no Serephine reckoning and no conversion from one, and the Corps' first meeting with the Serephine Dunekin is undated and unplaced in the record and stays so. What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Serephine Dunekin entry](/star-rangers/lore/serephine-dunekin/), the account also holds: that Kharis Belt IV's two lethal extremes come twelve hours apart; that the people are a closed form and an open form, open at the two margins; that planning by exposure windows is a metabolism and not a method; that their metallurgy is concentrated solar, mirrors, then better mirrors, then arrays, in a furnace that runs unattended through the hours none of them can stand in; that the daily swing is the gradient their engineering runs on, with buried mass as a battery and structures that buffer rather than insulate; that they cannot sustain effort and cannot meet what will not wait for a window, and that Serephine Rangers manage it at a cost the record does not pretend is free; that the patience is read as temperament and is not, and that they are not slow; that station air is a burden carried all day; and that a service schedules around them. What the account holds from no source but the author: that nothing of hers was written closed; that her people's history has no middle and is read at margins; that the first thing they made was a delay, and that they live in the lag; the day on a posting that she read finished; that the stations have no sky and she opened anyway; and the reason for the absence of a year. Her corps, rank, postings and years of service the rolls hold and this account does not. That she was a scout is her own word; the record names the people as scouts and no Serephine's corps. Whether anything keeps the furnace through the hours she describes, beyond the mirrors as they were set, the author does not say and the Archive does not hold. Whose reading opens a window, the people's own words for anything and how they speak among themselves are not held: the record holds no Serephine language in any medium and [no rendering of the ladder from them](/star-rangers/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues/#where-there-is-no-rendering), and the account, written in the Archive's working language, supplies none, under the Archive's own rule that [what it cannot carry is marked](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/). What a Serephine in the open form does at dusk that no other people in the Corps can do, the author declines to say, and the record holds only that it is done. The author's name is as the Corps' rolls carry it, in the record's translation register, as the record's one other Serephine name is; she is* she *in the same register, and the Archive asserts nothing by it. The parts are filed in the order they came, and the desk has set no count beside them.*
