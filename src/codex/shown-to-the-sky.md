### Receiving desk's note

*The Survey Archive asked, through the Survey Corps' contact with Sardain, for the history of the Ilveth by one of them. The asking was given in click-speech, and was answered, after an interval the desk did not measure, by Idrun Hesk, a sealer of a buried working floor, who said she would give it and named her terms: in warmth, not in a room, at night, on the ice within sight of her province's ground. A Survey Corps reader sat it in the field shell, with an imager that passes the thermal band and decides nothing. The warm-write is on file as the imager recorded it, and the Archive cannot read it: nobody in the Archive reads the register, and the record already holds that an Ilveth's full name is a pattern in it the record cannot carry. What follows is the author's own gloss, given in click-speech after each passage had cooled, and rendered by the reader into the working language. Three passages she wrote in warmth and did not gloss; the desk marks where, and renders nothing. Where the imager showed the vanes running brightest the desk marks that too, since in that language emphasis is expenditure and is part of the text. The telling took most of a Sardain night. The author rested the day after, and said the rest was not to be counted against the telling, since the telling had been paid for already.*

---

You asked for a room. I have given you the sky.

Hear why before anything else, because it is the price of everything after. A thing said in warmth is paid for. Under a roof the price is small; the heat goes into the wall, and the wall gives some of it back. Under the sky the price is the whole of it. The sky takes what it is shown and returns nothing. So I am saying this at full price, and you will know what each thing cost by being here, and by what I leave unsaid. That is how a history is read among us. Not by what it says. By what it cost to say.

*(The vanes ran brightest here of anywhere in the telling.)*

### The first wall

Your record says our cultures agree on very little. That is correctly heard. Here is the one thing: shelter is not about wind. It is about not showing the sky your heat.

Every history we have begins at a wall. Not at a first fire; we have no first fire, and I will come to that. At a wall, between warm ground and the sky, so that what the ground gave was kept. Who built the first one is not kept. What is kept is what it cost not to build it, which was everything. Before the wall, a body in the open at night was a body being spent. We have no word for that time. We have a word for the cold that was in it, and it is our word for the sky.

### Down

Our air will not carry a flame. You have seen it tried; it is a thing we show visitors, a small flame that costs more than it gives, and we watch you watch it and do not answer what your faces do. What the world had instead was old warmth underground, in the provinces where everything living already crowded, and we went down after it. Floors buried. Working heat banked. Metal made slowly and kept close.

Your record says we raise insulation to the level other peoples reserve for art. I will spend one sentence on that, since it is my craft. A floor that shows its heat is a floor saying something it did not mean to say. I keep floors from speaking. That is all a sealer is. There is no art in it. There is a ledger: what comes up out of the ground, what is spent on the work, and what the sky is allowed. The sky is allowed nothing. When I have done my work a province at night is as dark as the ice around it, and you could cross it with your pits open and see nothing, and that is the finest thing I know how to say about a place.

So when you ask what we were doing in all the time before you came, that is the answer. We were going down, and keeping what we found there, and learning to say less with it.

### The small eyes

I will spend a little on the stars, because your record is careful about them and I want to be careful back.

We always saw them. The small eyes see well enough on a clear night, and the stars were never hidden from us. They were beside the point. A sky that holds no warmth holds nothing to want, and ours is the coldest thing there is, and every wall we ever built had taught us not to look at it for long. Astronomy came to us late and through instruments, a discipline of the cold; the instruments were made on buried floors by people who had never once wanted to go where they pointed. We counted the stars, and banked what we learned, and nobody spent on them. Your record says we were reached rather than reaching, and that there is no scale to be behind on. I have read that sentence. I will not spend on whether it is kind.

*(Warm-written and not glossed. The desk marks the place and renders nothing.)*

### Manners

Our vanes leak. You know this; your record has it right. A body that is tired, or ill, or afraid, flickers, and every one of us in sight reads it, and the oldest courtesy we have is that you do not answer it. What a body says was not addressed to you. We had that rule before any wall now standing. It was never about you. It was about us, and about how little is private among a people whose feelings are lit.

I give it a section of its own because it is the thing we had ready, and did not know we had ready, and because without it the next part would have gone another way.

### Three nights

Then there were lanterns on the ice.

I was not on the watch that saw you, and I will not spend on another's telling. I give you what everyone has.

Something came down onto a plain, at horizon distance from a province, and lit. Not a flame; a flame goes out. It did not go out. It burned all night, and it burned through the day, which here is a long dusk and hides nothing, and it burned the next night, and the watch counted what moved in it by its glow, as a watch does, and what moved in it were bodies, and every one of them was a fire that did not go out. Three nights. The watch did not go closer. It did not need to. Nothing warm crosses Sardain unseen, and nothing that warm had ever crossed it.

On the fourth day somebody on your side noticed there were people on the world. We had known there were people in the lantern since the first hour. I say that plainly and not for pride. It is the shape of the whole thing, and you should have it.

*(The vanes ran brightest here of anywhere after the opening.)*

### What we got wrong

We answered your faces.

You know this already; your own teaching has it. A warmed surface, among us, is a statement, and your faces warmed constantly, and we answered them with care and at cost, and you had said nothing. I do not know what you thought we were doing. I know what we thought you were doing: spending without stopping, on everyone, on nothing, a people who warm a sentence for every stranger and every wall and the sky itself. We thought you were the most generous people that had ever lived, and we were ashamed to be so poor beside you; and then we understood that you could not help it, and the shame went the other way for a while; and then we remembered the manners, and stopped answering.

That is the whole of our first error and I have given it at its price. You read us as cold for a long time after. We read you as careless for about as long. Both readings were wrong in the same way. Each of us had priced the other's speech at our own cost of speaking.

### The ones who go into the dark

Some of ours went with you. One of them, among the first, gave you a phrase for yourselves, and you have it on file and quote it as if it were a kindness. It is one. It is also simply true. I will not spend on repeating it; you have paid for it already, and it was well paid.

What they do with you is the thing the dark is for, which is finding the living. Through smoke, which to us is nothing; through blackout, which to us is noon; through fallen structure, where a body going cold is read from the edges in before any instrument of yours has finished warming up. Your record has this right and I add nothing to it but a cost, which your record also has, and which I will spend on anyway, because a cost stated by the one who pays it is a different thing from a cost stated by the one who watches it paid.

In your hulls it is warm. In warm air the pits grey out and the vanes cannot speak, so ours aboard your ships are held to click-speech for the length of a posting, which is to say they are held to the register we keep for the day's work and for nothing that matters. You note that they are quiet, even for us. Hear what that is. It is not reserve. It is a people who can say what matters, and have agreed, for your sake, to go where they cannot. I have not met one who regretted it. I have not met one who did not count it.

*(Warm-written and not glossed. The desk marks the place and renders nothing.)*

### Your ranks

A small thing, and then I close.

You asked us once to render your ladder, and we did, and your record keeps the rendering and says that we alone read command entirely as a cost to the commander, and that our Rangers do not find this remarkable. They do not, and I will tell you why, since your page does not. Among us, what you hold is not a measure of anything. Anyone can bank heat; the ground gives it. What is measured is what you spend on others, and the one who spends most is the one with least left, and we have never thought there was another way to read a person. Your lower ranks are warmed. Your higher ranks warm. That is what a ladder is. We did not make the rendering strange. We made it plain.

### What I have not spent on

How long any of this took. Our measures are watches and nights, and they do not travel, and I will not give you a count to convert.

What we call ourselves in warmth. Not glossed, and not because it is kept from you; there is no click for it. The name you have for us is one we can say in the day-register, and we let you keep it.

Who speaks for Sardain. I do not. Nobody has told me who does, and I have not asked, because asking costs.

Who lit the first fire underground, who built the first wall, who kept the watch that saw you. Not kept, or kept in a province that is not mine, and not mine to spend.

### Go in

You asked for a room, and I said the sky, and you have sat in it all night in your shell with your heat going up out of you like a thing on fire, because that is what you are, and I have not answered one word of what your body has said, and it has said a great deal.

Here is why the sky. You came down onto our ice and burned for three nights without going out, and we did not know, until that, that a people could show the sky its heat all day and all night and live. Everything we are was built on the one fact that you cannot. So when you asked for our history I decided you should have it the way you live: in the open, at full price, with the sky taking its share. Now you have been given a thing the way you give everything. Go in. I will stay out a while. It is dark, and I can see all of it.

*(A last short passage, warm-written after* all of it *and not glossed. The desk marks the place and renders nothing.)*

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author, as her own account in her own gloss. The Archive has set no year to anything in it and has asked for none: the author's measures are watches and nights, the record holds no conversion from them, and the survey's first landing on Sardain and the contact that proceeded from it are undated in the record and stay so. What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Ilveth entry](/star-rangers/lore/ilveth/), the account also holds: that shelter on Sardain is about not showing the sky one's heat; that Ilveth industry went down after old warmth and raised insulation to the level others reserve for art; that astronomy came late and through instruments, and that the Ilveth were reached rather than reaching; that the survey's first landing party was watched from horizon distance for three days before anyone knew the world was inhabited; that it is bad manners among the Ilveth to answer what a body says; that early contact records are full of Ilveth answering human blushes as statements; that shipboard Ilveth are held to click-speech in a warm hull; and the rendering of the ladder on [its own page](/star-rangers/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues/#ilveth-who-spends-heat-on-whom). What the Ilveth thought the landing party was doing, and which way the shame went, the record holds from no Ilveth source but this one and files as hers. The author's trade is her own account of herself; the record holds buried floors and the sealing of heat on Sardain and names no trade. Whether anyone speaks for Sardain the record has not established, and the author's statement that she does not is hers alone. The three passages written in warmth and not glossed are marked and not supplied, under the Archive's own rule that [what it cannot carry is marked](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/); the imager's record of them is on file and unread. The author's full name is a warm-write pattern the record cannot carry, and* Idrun Hesk *is the click-speech handle, as the record's one other Ilveth name is.*
