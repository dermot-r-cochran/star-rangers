### Receiving desk's note

*A recording clerk of the [Federation assembly](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/) deposited this in the Federation record, and the record copied it to the Archive. The clerk had been let in, on [Ulessa](/star-rangers/characters/ulessa/)'s word, to a piece played by [Mnemari](/star-rangers/lore/mnemari/) for Mnemari, in a room lent for an evening while the assembly sat. The next day the clerk asked her why it had seemed half missing. She answered by mouth, in the assembly's working language, and the clerk took it down, as clerks do, and signed by the office. The clerk's account of the evening stands first, and the clerk's questions are left in where the answers need them. The deposit gives no place and no year. The title is the desk's, from the clerk's question. The note at the end is ours.*

---

**The clerk's account**

I was at the back. There were fourteen in the room besides me, all Mnemari, and two more who played. I did not note what they played on. I had been told I was a guest, and I left my notes at the door.

It lasted most of an hour by the session clock. I would put the sound at a tenth of that. It came in short figures, none longer than a breath of mine. Several began as if in the middle of something and stopped as if interrupted. The rests between ran to minutes. Nobody moved in them. Nobody seemed to be waiting, either, and I have recorded enough adjournments to know a room that is waiting. At the end there was no sign I could read. People began to leave.

Before we left the witness asked me what it had been like. I said: short, and like one side of a conversation. I asked her the next day what I had missed. This is what she said, in order.

---

**The witness**

You missed nothing that was played. I want that taken down first. You had all of it.

What was played last night was a reply. The piece it replies to was played on Ilenne, at a gathering I can give you by the chair of that day, and will if you ask. Twelve of the fourteen were at that gathering, and so were both players. I was. I was near the door, and what I have of that piece has the door in it.

*(I asked how long ago. She said I would want a count, and that she does not keep one, but that it was a little before she was first requested to the assembly.)*

Among us a piece is not played again to people who have it. They have it. To play it to them again would be to tell them they might not, and there is no one of mine of whom that is true. So a maker who has something to say about an old piece cannot do what I understand your makers do, which is to play the old tune and then turn it. The old piece is in the room already. Everyone who was there has it, entire. The maker plays only where the new piece departs from the old. Where the new one agrees with the old, the players rest.

*(I said: then the rests are the old piece.)*

The rests are the players resting. I will be careful here, because your people like this part more than it deserves. Nothing was in that room in the rests but the people in it. Nothing passed between us. Each of us has the first piece as she heard it, from where she was, and in a rest each of us had her own called up, as anything I have is called up when something touches it. The wording of a term comes the same way when a chair asks for it. Mine has the door. Another's will have whoever was in front of her. Twelve listeners had twelve first pieces, and the players played the departures from all twelve at once. That is the skill. I do not know how it is done. I am a witness.

*(I asked whether she would hum me a little of the first one, so that I should have something.)*

No. I would be giving you my hearing of it, with the door, in a voice it was not made for, and you would take that for the piece. And I would be playing it again to someone, which is not done.

*(I said I did not have it, so for me it would not be again.)*

That is well argued. No.

I can tell you about it. It is longer than last night's, by your clock, and nearly all of it is sound. That is a description. I am told a description is what your people have of most things after some years, and that you manage very well on it.

*(I have left that in. She said it kindly. I said that our music repeats on purpose: the tune comes back so that we can know it.)*

I have sat through your music. The tune comes back so that the listener may have it. I understand that as a courtesy and I take it as one. By the third return I have been given it three times. I do not say so at the time.

*(I asked about the two.)*

Two in the room were too young to have been at the gathering. They heard what you heard: everything that was played, and nothing under it. Nobody can give them the first piece. I cannot hand anyone a hearing. I can describe, and they have had descriptions, and a description does not rest where the players rest. They will wait for a reply to something they were at. That is ordinary. Everyone begins by standing through replies to things she was not at. Most of us would say it is how one learns to be in a room.

*(I asked why, if it was only ever going to be a tenth for me, she had had me let in.)*

It was not a tenth. I have said you had all of it, and I hold you to the wording, as I would hold a chair.

Everything the maker made, you heard. The rest of what was in that room was made by someone else, before I was first requested here, and each of us carried it in. You carried nothing in. You left your notes at the door; I saw you. So you are, with the two young ones, the only listeners who heard last night's piece and only last night's piece. None of the rest of us can do that. I cannot hear it without the first. I have no door to leave the first one at.

What you did not have is what it was said to. A reply without that is the sound of the work and not the work, and I will not tell you otherwise to be pleasant. You heard someone answer. You did not hear what was asked. That is a great deal not to have.

But I asked you what it was like, before you asked me anything, and you said: short, and like one side of a conversation. That is exact. I have had the first piece since I stood near that door, and I had never heard anyone say what a new part is like by itself. The young ones are not asked. It did not occur to me that it could be asked until I was beside someone who could answer.

That is why I had you let in. I should have said so beforehand.

*(I asked whether anyone would reply to last night's.)*

When someone has something to say about it. Not soon.

*(I said I should not be at that one.)*

No. I shall have "short", though. It is in mine now, where the door is in the other.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. The record held nothing of Mnemari music before this deposit and holds nothing beside it. The author speaks as a listener and says she is not a maker; she says "among us", and the Archive files that as her account of the music she knows, not as a rule of Ilenne. Neither piece is on file. The clerk made no recording and noted no instrument, and the record holds none.*

*What a listener who is not Mnemari has of such a piece, by both accounts: all of the sound and a fraction of the work. The author does not soften this and the desk does not. A recording of the evening would hold what the clerk heard and no more. A recording of the first piece, if one existed, would hold its sound; it would not hold what any of the twelve brought into the room, which by her account was each listener's own hearing and differed with where she had been.*

*What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Mnemari entry](/star-rangers/lore/mnemari/) and [Ulessa's page](/star-rangers/characters/ulessa/), the answer rests on and does not go past: that a Mnemari's recollection of an event centuries old carries the fidelity and sensory weight of one from four days back; that what she holds is available in full whenever something calls it up; that she gives words in the order they were said and then stops; and that a Mnemari gives an occasion by the chair of that day, as [Osune's account](/star-rangers/codex/by-the-chair-of-that-day/) does. The record states no means by which one Mnemari's memory reaches another, and this account states none: the author says in terms that nothing passed between the listeners. How memory is kept in a Mnemari the record does not say, and nothing here says it.*

*The gathering on Ilenne is given no chair in the deposit, because the clerk did not ask; the author said she would give it. Her "a little before I was first requested" the desk leaves as hers. The record's own conversion puts her first request shortly before 2626 UCSD, and sets no year to the gathering. The clerk is on the Federation record by office and the Archive's copy carries no name. What the two young listeners made of the evening nobody asked, which the author noticed before the desk did.*
