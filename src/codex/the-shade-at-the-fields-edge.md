**FROM THE HOUSE'S BOOK AT CILL AOIFE**
**Entered by the one who keeps it, at her asking, from what she told the house. The leaf gives the harvest and not the year.**

---

She asked that this be entered. She does not ask that of much.

Her reason was the women who will mind the well after dark when she is not here to be asked. She said they would see it, or one like it, and that they should have something to go on other than the townland. So it is written for them. I give what the house saw, then what she saw, then what she would not say, which was the greater part.

### The Lower Field

It was the seventh harvest since the water ran warm. The lower field was in barley that year and was cut in the week after the feast of the Cross, and the stooks stood in it.

From the door of the well-house the lower field lies to the west. In the evening the sun goes down behind its far mearing, where the ditch is and the house's ground ends. On the third evening after the cutting one of the women came in from the door and said there was a man standing at the mearing with the sun behind him, and that he had been standing there since she first looked.

We all went to the door. It stood where she said. It was dark all through, the way a doorway is dark when you look into a house from a bright yard, and there was a thin line of light along the edge of it. It had the height of a person. That is all I can say of it from the door, and it is more than some of us would agree on.

It was there the next evening, and the next. The sick who could get up came to the door, and those who could not asked those who could, and by the third evening the townland had it. A man who had cut that field said he would go down to it with the hook. One of the old women said it was a *scáth*, a shade, of the same people as the ones in the hill and the ones that keep to certain trees, and that a bowl should be left at the ditch and nobody should look. A good many said it was one of the dead. Nobody could say what it would do next, and everybody was sure it would do something.

They asked her to send it away. She said she would go and look at it first.

### What She Saw

She went down on the fourth evening by herself, across the stubble. She was gone as long as it takes to say the evening office, and she came back up with the light going. She told it to the house that night, and again to me in the morning so that I could write it, and it was the same both times. I give it as she gave it.

> *"I walked to within six paces of it and stopped. I will tell you what fitted and what did not, and I will not sort them for you.*
>
> *"It had the height of a person and the going of a person. It was dark, and not as a thing standing in shade is dark, for I could find nothing in it: no cloth, no skin, no nearer side or further. There was a brightness along the edge of it where the sun came round. The stooks threw their shadows up the field toward me, each as long as a house. It threw none.*
>
> *"It made no sound. The stubble stood under it unbent. I heard my own feet the whole way down, and the rooks, and from it I heard nothing, not so much as you would hear from a man standing still.*
>
> *"There was no warmth off it and no cold. I held my hand out toward it from where I stood, as you would to a hearth, and my hand found the evening and nothing else.*
>
> *"One thing more, and I do not know what to do with it. Near it the ground leaned. Not to the eye; the field is as level there as it ever was. But my feet took it for a slope, the way they will on the floor of an old house, and I stood crooked for it.*
>
> *"It was at work. If I were let say one thing only I would say that. Something of it went down and came up, went down and came up, and it moved along the mearing a little each time, the way a woman goes along a row. I do not say it had hands. I did not see what it worked at. There is nothing at that ditch to gather.*
>
> *"I stood where it would have seen me, if it sees. It did not turn. I could find no face on it to turn. When the sun was under the hill it was not there, and it had not walked anywhere. It went the way the shadow of a post goes."*

### What She Would Not Say

The house asked her what it was, and she said she did not know. I set down the rest as it was asked and answered. The answers were short, and I am sure of them.

They asked was it Muirenn come again. She said no. She said that what came on the eve had spoken to her, and this had not. That one had been at every distance, and this was at one, six paces off, at the edge of a field. The well ran warm after the eve, and she had stopped at the well on her way up and put her hand in, and it was cold. She said: *They are two things. Do not let the one be said for the other.*

They asked was it one of the dead. She said: *I know no more of the dead than you do. It was like nobody I have buried.*

They asked was it what the old woman called it. She said the old women had a word for it and she had none, and she would not take their word off them. She had seen what she had said and no more.

They asked was it a good thing or a bad thing. She said: *A man standing on the far bank of a river is neither good nor bad to you for standing there. I was not afraid of it. That tells you about me. It tells you nothing about it.*

One of the women who sit with the sick asked what was to be done for it. She said: *Nothing. It is not sick, and it has not asked.* The same answer did for the bowl.

The man with the hook asked should he go down. She said: *You may. There is nothing in that field to cut but what you cut already.* He did not go.

They asked a second time would she send it away. She said: *There is nothing here to send. I do not think it is in our field at all, though I saw it there, and I cannot make that plainer. You cannot put out of the house what never came in.*

### What She Asked

She asked the house to count the evenings and write the count, and not to trust what we would remember. From the first evening to the last was nine. On seven it was seen. On two the west was clouded and there was nothing to see it against, and I cannot say whether it was there.

On every one of the nine she drew the water at the hour it stood, in sight of the lower field, as she would have done had the field been empty. One of the women asked why she did not keep inside. She said: *If it sees, let it see what is here. A woman carrying water.*

She did not go down to it again. She said she had seen what could be seen from six paces, and that to go nearer would be to go looking for something to say.

The last of it she said to me, when I read her back what I had written.

*"Put this down too, and put down that I do not know it. It stood dark to me because the light was behind it. I have wondered since what I stood as. There may be a house somewhere with its sick at the door, and a woman going down a field to see what the dark thing is at the edge of it."*

It has not been seen since the feast of Michael. The lower field is ploughed. The women look west from the door in the evenings still, and so do I.

*Scholarly annotation (M. Ó Fiaich, Union Cultural Archive, Tír na nÓg, 2826 UCSD): I annotated the* Life *in 2819 and passed over this leaf. A cultural scholar reads visions. This is not one, and I had not then gone to the Survey Corps' register to see what else it might be. Read beside [Membrane Shadows: Gravitational Imprints from Adjacent Membranes](/star-rangers/lore/membrane-shadows/), what Aoife reports from six paces is nearly that page's own list: a dense figure lit along its edge, which throws no shadow where everything round it throws one; no sound; no warmth and no cold; stubble unbent beneath it; gone with the light and not by walking. Her leaning ground is the detail no fireside account of a shade troubles to include, and the page has a place for that too. It also says that the old traditions which filed the people of the hill, the tree-people and the dark thing at the field's edge together "had noticed a real kinship centuries before anyone measured an Interval". Her townland's old women filed it exactly so, under* scáth*, the word the Union's own term for the class,* Scáth Seicne*, is still built on. I do not say she met the Good People. Nobody has reported them away from the thin ground of my own world, and the old women's word is fourteen centuries older than our use of it. Three cautions follow, and together they outweigh the fit. A Shadow needs a narrowed Interval, and nobody has measured one at Cill Aoife in her century or in ours; a witness whose every particular agrees is still one witness, and a leaf is not a gravimeter. If it was a Shadow, it says nothing about the eve of 1216. A Shadow is silent and carries no heat, so it could not have spoken to her, stood at every distance or warmed a well, and she said so herself with the cold water still on her hand. Whoever reads this leaf back into the* Life *does what she forbade in the same room. And her not being afraid is, as she told the house, a fact about her. The Survey Corps' page holds that the channel is disposed neither way, and that whether any such figure is shaped to be seen has not been settled. She left both where the page leaves them.*

*Archive note: this leaf is the house's, entered from what Aoife told it, and it is valid for the house. It is not in the* [Life](/star-rangers/codex/life-of-saint-aoife/)*, which Brother Daire set down in 1216 AD. The leaf's own count, the seventh harvest since the water ran warm, places it at 1222 or 1223 AD (1422 or 1423 UCSD), according to whether the house counted the harvest of the vision's own year, and the Archive fixes it no closer. The record keeps it apart from the vision, as she did. What she met on Muirenn's eve spoke, stood at every distance at once and left the well warm for three days. What stood at the mearing was silent, stood at one distance and left the water cold. Nothing in this leaf explains the vision, the warmth or* [the Warm-Edge Correlation](/star-rangers/codex/the-warm-edge-correlation/)*, whose file stays open as its authors left it. Nor is it* [The Apparition at Cill Aoife](/star-rangers/codex/the-apparition-at-cill-aoife/)*: that leaf's apparition came toward her and was refused, this figure approached nobody and she refused nothing, and the record does not say which leaf is the earlier. The annotation's reading is the scholar's, offered as a possibility and filed as one. The record holds no measurement of a narrowed Interval at Cill Aoife in any century, and a description cannot supply one, since only a cross-membrane gravimeter registers a* [Membrane Shadow](/star-rangers/glossary/membrane-shadow/) *directly. So the Archive does not file the lower field as a boundary zone, and does not file the figure as a Shadow. It registers that a careful witness described what she saw and declined to say what it was. If it was a Shadow, the rest of that page applies with the fit: nothing crossed, it could not act on this side, nothing done or left undone at the well had any purchase on it, and whether it was kindly, or was shaped to be seen, is not known. Her answers to the house are filed as what she said, not as a method. The thought she asked the keeper to add, of a house with its sick at the door, is hers; the record holds no account from the far side of any Interval. What Aoife met at the thorn well remains unplaced. What stood at the edge of the lower field is filed beside it, and explains none of it.*
