### Receiving desk's note

*When [Shown to the Sky](/star-rangers/codex/shown-to-the-sky/) was filed, the desk was left with a question. The [Ilveth entry](/star-rangers/lore/ilveth/) says insulation on Sardain was raised to the level other civilisations reserve for art. [Idrun Hesk](/star-rangers/characters/idrun-hesk/), whose craft it is, had said: there is no art in it; there is a ledger. The desk asked the Survey Corps reader to put it to her, if a second sitting were ever given, whether a sealer keeps nothing at all for the work's own sake. A second sitting was given. She answered in click-speech, under a roof, and asked that the imager stay shut, since nothing was to be written in warmth. The rendering is the reader's. Nothing in it is dated. The title is the desk's. The note at the end is ours.*

---

You may have your room this time.

It is a small thing, and it is about the craft, and the craft is the day's work. So I give it in clicks, under a roof, and the wall may have the little it costs. I have priced it before saying it. You will know by the end whether I priced it right.

You ask whether I take back what I said outside: no art in it, a ledger. I do not take it back. I said it under the sky and it was paid for. But a ledger has a keeper, and on my floor the keeper is let to write one line in it that is her own. I will give you that line. It is my floor's. I do not give it as anyone else's, and I will come to why I cannot.

### What cannot be seen

First the trouble.

A finished wall shows nothing. That is what finished means. Cross it at night with your pits open, and there is ice, and then more ice, and under one stretch of it a floor is working, and you would not know which stretch. So the better a sealer is, the less of hers there is to find. A poor wall can be read from a day's travel. A good wall cannot be read at all, and the best wall on Sardain and the next best are the same dark.

The young ask how they will know when they are good. They will know when nobody can tell.

The small eyes are no help in it. By day they will tell you what a wall is made of and that its joints are true, and a wall with true joints can shout all night. What a wall is, is what it does in the dark. A good one does nothing.

### The sealing

A wall is closed a seam at a time, and the closing of the last seam is the sealing.

On my floor the sealer chooses beforehand which seam will be last, and chooses a place in it. At the sealing she closes all of that seam but the place. It is small: smaller than the least thing a vane can say. For one night the floor's warmth comes up through it and shows.

The next day it is closed. It is not opened again, for a visitor, or for the one who made it, or after she is gone. One night.

It goes in the ledger. I told you the sky is allowed nothing. On my floor each wall has one line under the sky's name, and that is the line: one place, one night, so much. It is counted as a loss, like any loss. The floor bears it, because the floor agreed to it before the wall was begun. A sealer who left a second place would be told she had left a leak, and it would be true.

### What it is not

You will want to know what it says. Your people ask that first of anything warm. I know where you learned it. We asked it of your faces.

It is not the warm-write. The warm-write is a body speaking, and a body that speaks has spent on someone. A wall spends on no one. There is only the sky on the far side of it. It is not a name. It is not a mark to find the floor by: it is gone the next night, and everyone it was shown to came up out of that floor to see it. Nobody learns from it a thing they did not have.

It is a shape. The sealer chooses where the warmth will be let through and how it will lie. That is the whole of the choice, and it is the only choice in the craft the ledger does not make. Everywhere else the wall tells me what to do. In that place, for a night, I tell it.

### The old sealer's

I have seen one that was not mine. Once. Nobody sees one twice.

It was the old sealer's who taught me, on the wall I learned on. I was young enough to be told I might come up, and old enough to know what coming up costs: a night on the ice, in the open. Those of the floor who choose it, come. Not many choose it, and nobody is thought less of for staying down.

You have to stand near. The pits make a coarse picture, and a thing that small is nothing from where a careful person would stand. So a few of us stood near, low, showing what little we could not help showing, and looked at the place.

It was a line with one turn in it. That will mean to you about what a line with a turn means, which is little. What I cannot give you well is that it moved. A floor's work rises and falls through a night, and the warmth in the seam rose and fell with it. It came along the line slowly. At the turn it slowed more, and gathered, and then went on. All night it did that. The old sealer had put the turn where the warmth would have to wait. That was what had been chosen. Not the line. The waiting.

Nobody answered it. A wall is not a body, and the courtesy is not owed to a wall. Still, nobody warmed a word that whole night, and the old sealer least of any. We looked. The next day it was closed, and we went down.

I have crossed that ground many nights since, and I cross it as a sealer does, looking for the fault. It is dark. I know to a pace where the place was. I cannot find it. If any part of that wall is quieter than the rest, it is that part: it was closed last, by the one who knew the wall best, on the day she knew most. You came for a judgement of the craft. That is mine, of the wall I learned on. I cannot find it.

### Mine

I have sealed walls since. Each had its place and its night. I will not give you the shapes. They are closed.

I give you the first in part, because it is to my cost. I chose a place and left it, and went up to look, and could not bear it. A leak, in my wall, showing. Everything I had learned, I had learned so as not to do that. I closed it before the night was half gone.

The old sealer said nothing of it, then or after. Among us that is a great deal to say.

The next one I left the whole night.

### Other floors

You will ask whether all of us do this. I do not know. I know my floor.

I have not asked a sealer of another floor, because asking costs, and because of what the thing is. A floor that did it would show it for one night in the life of a wall, to its own, and close it. If every floor on Sardain kept the custom I would have no way to know. If no floor kept it but mine, I would know exactly as much. I find I am content.

Write it as my floor's. If another sealer tells you it is theirs too, write that as theirs.

### What you would have had of it

With your eyes, nothing. With your imager, on that night, standing near: a small mark, warmer than the ice. I do not think you would have had the turn. You would not have had the waiting unless you watched for hours and knew to.

And you would have had to be there, and that is the trouble. I do not say it unkindly. One of you on that ice beside the old sealer's figure is a furnace beside a spark. Every pit present would have had you, and nothing else. It is the one thing we make that we could not show you even if we wished to. We have not been asked. You are the first to ask whether there was anything.

### Closed

That is the line the keeper writes. I said there is no art in it. You may leave that standing beside this. I will not spend on which of them is true.

While I gave you the last part I warmed one place on one vane. It was small. You did not see it; your imager is shut, as I asked, and I asked because nothing was to be written. It is closed now.

You may write that it was there.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. She gives this as her floor's custom and says she does not know whether any other floor keeps it. The Archive holds it as that: it is not the Ilveth's, not Sardain's and not the craft's, and nothing in it is a law. The record still holds no Ilveth polity or office, and names no trade; "sealer" remains her own word for what she does.*

*What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Ilveth entry](/star-rangers/lore/ilveth/), the answer rests on and does not go past: that Ilveth industry is buried, and its insulation raised to the level others reserve for art; that for a people who see heat a warm building at night is a building shouting, and a leak in a wall is an indiscretion and not merely a waste; that heat-thrift carries the weight other cultures give honesty; that the mirror-pits read presence, temperature and flow, and make a coarse picture; that fine work belongs to the small photic eyes, which do not see warmth; that the warm-write is a body's and is paid for; and that it is bad manners to answer what a body says. That a figure made of warmth moving and waiting is a thing the pits could read follows from those. That anyone made one, the record holds from her alone.*

*What the answer holds from no source but its author: the sealing and the one place left; the line under the sky's name in a floor's ledger; that those of a floor may come up for the night; the old sealer, whom she does not name and the record cannot, and who is *she* only in the rendering's register, as the author is on her own page, the record asserting nothing by it; the line with one turn; and her own first wall. She says she was taught. How a sealer comes to the work the record does not otherwise hold.*

*The Archive holds no image of any such figure, and by the account none could now be made of the ones she describes: each showed for one night and is closed. What an imager would have passed she has said herself, and the desk has nothing to add to it but agreement: a long wavelength makes a coarse picture in an instrument as in a pit, and a warm-bodied observer in a field shell is, in that band, much the brightest thing present. Of the last thing in the answer the reader confirms only that the imager was shut by her terms, and that the reader saw nothing. The desk has written that it was there, as she said it might, and files it as stated and not observed.*
