### Receiving note

*A Federation mathematician who studied under [Pekkat](/star-rangers/characters/pekkat/) deposited this on coming home, and asked that the deposit carry no student's name, since nothing in it is the student's. It has three parts. The first and third are Pekkat's remarks to the students, struck as speech and rendered by the translating set the teaching floor keeps for visitors. The second is the demonstration, which is not speech. The set declined it, and what the Archive holds in its place is the trace of a contact pickup the student had fixed to the floor. The settlement is not named in the deposit and the record does not name it. Nothing in the deposit is dated. The note at the end is ours, and so is the title, taken from the remarks.*

---

Stand where you like, so long as you are standing on it. I am going to strike you something old. The set will give you my remarks and will not give you the thing, and it is right not to.

There is a room on this floor that was cut square. I did not cut it, and it was not cut for this. Two members were left in it when the cutting stopped. One runs along a side. One runs across, corner to corner. They were left from the same rock, so a strike travels both at one speed and comes back along each in the time its length gives it. That is all the instrument there is: a side, and the line across.

Now the question, which is older than this floor. Is there a length, however small, that goes into both a whole number of times? A measure they share?

If there is, then somewhere down the count the two come back together: so many returns of the side, so many of the line across, landing on one instant. So I could strike both and let you wait for it. But waiting proves nothing. However long you stood here and did not feel them land together, you could say the instant was still to come. Nobody is convinced by a thing that has only not happened yet.

So we do not wait. We take away.

Take the side from the line across. It goes once, and something is left, less than the side. The room gives you that for nothing: it is the gap between the two returns. Keep it. Now take what was left from the side. It goes twice, and something is left again, less again.

Here is the turn, and I give it in remarks because the set will not give you the rock. Cut a square whose side is the first remainder. Its line across is the first remainder and the second together, just as this room's line across is its side and the first remainder together. So we are where we began, in a smaller room, taking a side from a line across. It will go twice, and leave a remainder. And that remainder is the side of a smaller room again.

Twice, and a remainder. Twice, and a remainder. Each round arrives in the figure of the last one, and sooner.

It does not come out. That is the theorem. If the side and the line across shared a measure, every remainder would be a whole count of it, because each is only what is left when whole counts are taken from whole counts. And each remainder is smaller than the one before. A whole count cannot go on getting smaller for ever. It reaches one, and has nowhere to go. These go on. So there was never a count, in this room or in any room cut square.

A student told me once, through the set, that where they come from being convinced is a quiet thing that happens to a person sitting down. Here it comes up through the limbs, and you have to be standing on something. I will strike it now. Do not count. You will lose the count early, and that is not the part you are here for.

*[Not rendered: the demonstration. The set renders struck speech, and its tables hold nothing for this. It offers* see trace*, and marks that the trace is a record of the floor moving and not of what was struck on it.]*

You have a trace, and it stops at a round. I will not strike you which round I stopped at. You would keep the number, and it is the rock's number and not the theorem's. On this floor we stop where the rock stops carrying it. That is a custom, and a different rock would give a different custom.

That is also how you tell what was struck. Retune this room and the returns come at other times. Cut it larger and they come slower. The figure comes back all the same: twice, and a remainder. If a retuning could change it, it would have been a harmony, and I would have told you so and we would have enjoyed it. This one is not the room's.

One thing more, because students ask how long it would take to finish. Not long. Every remainder there will ever be, struck one after another, is over by the time a strike from the corner would have reached the middle of the room. All of them. It is short. It only has no last strike in it.

You stopped following it early. I stopped a little after you. The rock stopped after me. It was over before any of us had moved, and none of the three of us finished it. Now you may sit down.

### Receiving note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. What the Archive holds is a rendering of the remarks and a picture of a floor moving. It is not Pekkat's proof, which the Archive cannot read, and nothing here translates it: [Pekkat's page](/star-rangers/characters/pekkat/) says the record does not turn a proof carried in resonance into written mathematics, and this note does not. What the desk can do is set beside the remarks the written proof of the same theorem, which the Solar System has held since antiquity. The two prove one thing and are two proofs.*

**The written counterpart, plainly.** Call the square's side s and its diagonal d. Then d = s + r1, where r1 is less than s; and s = 2 × r1 + r2, where r2 is less than r1. A square of side r1 has diagonal r1 + r2, which is the first line over again at a smaller size, so the same step repeats without end: r1 = 2 × r2 + r3, and so on. If s and d were both whole multiples of some one length, every remainder would be a whole multiple of it too, and a run of whole numbers cannot decrease for ever. So no such length exists. The side and the diagonal of a square are incommensurable. The oldest statement of the test in the Solar System's literature is Euclid's, in the second proposition of the tenth book of the *Elements*: two lengths are incommensurable if this taking-away never comes out even.

*Pekkat's last remark is arithmetic and the desk has checked it. Each remainder is the one before multiplied by the same figure, a little over two fifths (the diagonal's ratio to the side, less one). Remainders shrinking at that rate, added together without end, come to exactly half the diagonal: the distance from a corner of the room to its middle.*

*What a listener in the air hears, by the student's own page: taps on plating. Two, well apart; then a run of them closing up, as a dropped ball's bounces close up; then a short buzz; then nothing, with Pekkat still on the rock. The student counted four rounds by ear and would not swear to a fifth. The pickup trace shows seven rounds as separate arrivals, and after the seventh a single thickening of the line that the pickup could not resolve. Neither is where the demonstration ended. Pekkat declined to say where that was, and the desk has not derived it from the trace, which measures the pickup.*

*Three things in the remarks the Archive already holds in its own voice: that a Chthonari settlement is tuned as it is cut; that the Undersong does not translate, and a student takes home a description; and that Pekkat keeps a line between a theorem and a harmony, the test being whether the result survives a retuning. That within a regime [mathematics is discovered and its notations are made](/star-rangers/lore/mathematics-and-its-notations/) is the Archive's position, and this is one more hand for a theorem found many times. What the remarks hold from no source but their author: the square room and its two members, which the record places in no named settlement; that the teaching floor's custom is to stop where the rock stops carrying it, which is that floor's custom and is not given as the Belt's; and how old the demonstration is among the Chthonari, of which Pekkat says only that it is older than the floor. How a student who cannot feel the rock receives what Pekkat teaches, the record still does not describe. This student's page says only that the pickup shows the outside of it.*
