### Receiving note

*The Survey Archive asked, through the Survey Corps' fold-route confirmation work with the Prismeri astrogation guilds under the standing compact, for a history of the Prismeri by one of them. A guild answered that a history is not a thing a guild confirms, and sent a reader anyway. The reader came to the confirmation table, which on Prismere is held where there is a beam, and gave the account hanging, the Corps' reader standing below, and is recorded as saying first that this meant nothing, and the desk records that the Corps' reader had been told so beforehand. The account was given in the working rendering the guild's confirmations already come to the table in. The Prismeri original is not on file and the Archive does not hold the people's language. Asked which guild, the desk did not press, and the account does not say. The note at the end is ours.*

---

You have asked a guild for a history, and a guild has sent you a reader, which is what a guild has. Hear first what a reader does, since it is how I will give you this, and you may hold the whole of what follows as a reading and not as a record. We are read ourselves, by others, from the ground, and were read wrong twice by people who meant well, and I will come to that.

A reader reads the sky. Your instruments reconstruct what is over Prismere, and I look at it, and when the two agree the Corps writes down that they agree, and when they do not the Corps writes down that too, in its own hand, and what it does then is the Corps' business and is written where the Corps writes. That is the whole of what passes across this table and it is good work. I have told your Corps so before and the Corps wrote that down as well.

A reader also reads a life. Not as your medics do, by an age. A guild takes a reader at the Emergence and never before it, so every reader in every guild has been two things, and the guild knows each of us twice: what we were under the canopy and what we are in the haze. A life is read across both forms, with the shell between, and the guild reads a people the same way. So that is the history I will give you: our First Wings, our shell, our Full Wings. You will want to know when each thing was. I give you nothing by a year. I give you each thing by the form of the one who saw it, which is how we give anything, and if that is not a date at your table it is still an order and a distance, and a reader lives on those.

You call us Prismeri. At this table we answer to it.

### First Wings

Everything we are was made under the canopy, and most of it still lives there.

We hang. You have been told this, and told that a hanging Prismeri is a workshop, the feet taking the weight and both hands free, and that is true and it is also the first thing. A people who work with the eyes beside the hands have never had to put a thing down in order to think about it. Our settlements are in the spires because a spire is a thing to hang from, and the spires were there before we were, lit from below by a root we did not plant, and we shaped the openings and the ledges and nothing else, and your surveyors walk through one and do not see that anything was made until they look at where the weight goes. That is the oldest praise we have had from you and you did not know you were giving it.

The forest glows and we do not. Hear that plainly. We see by the forest, and the forest is brightest low, and so our eyes are low on the head and look forward and down, and above them is the band, which sees nothing and reads one thing, which is a change in the light overhead. Every one of us has had the shadow on the band once. I will say of the ones who had it twice only what the settlements say, which is nothing. The curfew says it for them. After dark nobody leaves the stand without one of us, and that is the oldest thing a guild does and it is older than the guilds.

Nothing here burns. Your record says we came to high-energy work late and by another road, and that is your record being kind. We came to it by the road there was. The leaves of the forest are lenses; a leaf lit from below throws a point of light on whatever hangs over it, and the first of us to hold a leaf still and move a thing into the point learned what the point does to glass. That was a First Wings in a settlement, since it is First Wings work and always was, and the trade is still done from the hang by First Wings with a lens in one hand and the work in the other, and the guilds have no part in it and want none.

And we kept who was whose. A people whose body changes in the middle of a life, and not every life, and on no day anyone can name, keeps a record of persons that the body cannot contradict. It is the oldest written thing we have. It was not written for you. It is the thing that corrected you.

That is our First Wings, and I want you to hear what I have not said. I have not said it was a beginning. A First Wings life is a life, entire; the people's page in your record says so now, and said it late. A good part of us live the whole of a life in that form and are not waiting for anything and are not less. I was taught the forest by a scout who never left it, and nothing I read in the haze is read better than she read the groves.

### The shell

Between the two forms there is a shell, and between the two halves of our history there is one, and I will treat them alike: I will tell you it is there and what came out of it, and not what happens inside.

Nobody chooses it. Nobody predicts it. Nobody earns it and nobody is refused it by anyone with the standing to refuse. Our historians say that is why the guilds never became what a body of the able becomes elsewhere, and I am a reader and not a historian and will not argue with them, except to say it more shortly: a guild is the able, and the able are not a rank, and the plate I hang in is a stage of a life and not a seniority, and a Ranger who reads it as one has made the mistake your own survey made and corrected.

You will ask what brings it on. The Federation's medical missions have asked for longer than I have been in plate, and the guilds have answered as the guilds answer, and your record has a word for how we answer, and the word is fair. I am a reader and not a guild, so I will say one thing more than the guild says, which is why. A thing that nobody earns must stay a thing that nobody can earn. If the shell could be brought on, there would be a way to it, and a way is a door, and a door has somebody at it. We have nobody at that door. We would like to go on having nobody there. Write that down as the reader's reason and not the guild's; the guild has not given one and will not.

Now the people's shell. Before anyone I was taught by was taught, some of us came out heavy, as all of us who come out do, and found the forest too close, as all of us find it, and went up because up was where the body now went. The sky over Prismere had been doing what it does since before there was anyone under it: the haze draws the lines, and the lines move, and anything with eyes can see them. What came out of the shell could read them. Not see. Read. The lines over the world were a thing that had been shown to everyone for as long as there had been anyone, and it took a body too heavy for home to go up and find out what it was that was being shown. That is the whole of our Emergence as a people, and it is the same as the Emergence of a person: a sense was added, and a forest was taken, and neither would trade.

The first readers were not a guild. A guild came later, by the form of the ones who tell it, when there were enough readers for the settlements to ask the same question of several and want the answers to agree. We have been making the answers agree since. That is what a guild is for and it is all a guild is for.

### Full Wings

Then you came, and you counted us twice.

I was taught this as a thing that happened to us and I give it to you as one, and not as a grievance, since it was not. Your survey came down, and looked, and filed a people, and stayed a local year, and looked again, and filed another. Two reports. I have read the second; the guild holds a copy of the sheets that went round with it, and I have hung under the third of them for longer than the naturalist would think it deserved. The mistake was a reader's and not a liar's. A reader reads from where they hang, and your survey hung nowhere and read from the ground, and from the ground a First Wings and a Full Wings are two things. They are two things. They are not two peoples. One of yours was willing to be told, and went to the thing we had kept, which was who was whose, and read in it the same person at two points in one life, and was, your record says, frustrated, which I take to mean that the finding cost something to the one who made it. It did. It cost the first report. I would like it recorded that we do not think less of the first report for being wrong. It was a careful account of what could be seen from the ground, and it is still that.

We were in the Federation from the start of it, your record says, and the guild says of that what it says of everything before living memory: it was before anyone I was taught by. No year. Hear why there is none and not only that there is none. What the guild keeps of that time is confirmations, and a confirmation is a thing that was so on a day, and the day is kept by the sky, and the sky does not count. I could give you the standing of the corridors over Prismere when the first of the Federation's sat under a beam and asked us to join, as it has come down to me, and it would be as exact as anything your record holds, and it would mean nothing at your table, and you would write beside it that the Archive holds no conversion, and you would be right. So I give it by form: the ones who said yes were in plate, since it was a guild that was asked, and the ones who had to live with the answer were mostly not.

What we give the compact is what I give this table. Your Corps folds along corridors it has mapped, and the maps are good, and over our world and ours only the corridors can be seen as well as measured, so the Corps asks a guild whether the sky agrees with the instrument before it goes, and a guild says. That goes one way, toward the Corps, as the compact's words go, and the guild has never asked for the other direction and does not want it. I will give you the reason, since it is a reader's and not a secret: a confirmation is worth something only while the one who gives it wants nothing back for it. The day a reading can be traded for a reading, it is a price and not a reading, and the Corps would be right to stop asking. We know what a thing that nobody earns is worth. We would like our confirmations to stay one.

Some of us go to your postings. I have not. The guild says of those who have that they come back soon and by their own wish, and the ones I have hung beside afterwards say the same thing in the same order: the light is wrong and the contract says so and the Corps mends it; the sky is wrong for the tendrils and the contract says so and the Corps mends that; and there is nothing to hang from, and no contract says so, because nothing can be done about it, and so a reader spends the posting on four knuckles in corridors with a ceiling a hand's breadth over the head and nothing in it, and comes home, and hangs, and does not talk about it much. The Corps is not unkind. A ceiling is not a thing the Corps can give. What a reader is on the Corps' rolls while they are there, the guild has not told me and I have not asked, and the desk may mark that as it likes.

### One life

The desk asked for a history, and a history that has nobody in it is a chart and not a history, so I will give you one life, since it is the one I have read closest and since it is the form.

I was a scout. I will say I was good in the close spaces, since nobody in the guild will say it for me and the settlement that could has not been asked. I knew the groves by their stall, which is how a First Wings knows a grove: where the air is thick enough to hang on and where it is not. I had the shadow on the band once, as everyone has. I was under the canopy, where they do not hunt, and it was not a ray. I have never found out what it was. I did not go back to look. That is the whole of the story, and I tell it because it is the one that teaches a scout what the band is for, and because the desk tells me your record has a rule about a thing seen once through a gap, and I think the rule is right.

Then the shell. Some weeks. I will not describe the inside of it, since it is not history, and since nothing said about it from outside is true and nothing said from inside has been said.

I came out heavy. The groves I knew by their stall I could not fly; the close spaces had closed. Your record says the Emergence takes the forest, in one line, and the line is right. I went up, because up was where the body went, and the sky was doing what it had always done, and for the first time it was not lines. The guild took me because I could read it. Nobody asked me what I had been. They knew. A guild knows each of its readers twice.

What the forest lost when I came out of the shell, the settlement knows and I do not ask. What I lost, one of yours asked me once, kindly, at this table, and I gave the answer the body gives, which is that a Full Wings does not hang from a First Wings ledge. The ledge is still there. I can see it from the haze on a clear night, by the forest's light, which is how I was taught to see anything.

### The reading

That is the history, read across the forms: a forest, a shell, a sky. Now the correction, since a reading is nothing until something has been set against it, and I have one to set.

Your first report said two peoples. Your record keeps it as a mistake, and I have called it one, and so it is. Read it again from the hang. A First Wings life is a life. A Full Wings life is another. The report that said two was wrong about the people and right about the lives, and the report that said one was right about the people and said nothing about the lives, and the naturalist who kept the first two sheets as drawn and redrew only the third is the one of you who filed what we are, on one paper: two points, one life, and the same hand. We have no quarrel with that sheet. We hang under it.

And then this, which I had not read until the desk asked and I had to read something. Your record met us at two points in one life and called us two, and then one of you was willing to be told, and the record was corrected, and the correction was irreversible, and it was not universal, since there are survey teams who still describe us from the ground, and have not been told, and mean no harm. Your record's acquaintance with us has a First Wings and a Full Wings and a shell between them, and the shell was the kinship finding, and you are in plate now and cannot go back to the forest where we were two. I do not know what that took from you. You have not said. Nobody at this table has asked you, and I am asking, and the desk may write that the reader asked and was not answered, since that is how we file a thing of that kind.

Set the Archive's date beside none of this. Write beside it what the naturalist wrote beside her third sheet, which is the only date we have ever been given that meant the thing it stood for: *redrawn after the kinship finding.*

### Receiving note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author. The Archive has set no year to anything in it and has asked for none: the Federation's contact survey of Prismere and Prismere's charter membership are undated in the record, and the author gives every interval by the form of the one who saw it, which is not a count the Archive converts; the author's offer of the standing of the corridors over Prismere at the Federation's asking was declined by the desk, for the reason the author gave in advance. Seven things in the account the Archive already holds in its own voice, on the [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/) and [Prismere](/star-rangers/lore/planets/prismere/) entries and in [Plates of the Prismeri](/star-rangers/codex/plates-of-the-prismeri/): that two preliminary species reports were filed a local year apart and reconciled by a cross-reference of the people's own kinship records; that a Prismeri rests by hanging and does fine work from the hang; that the glass is fused by focused sun and not by flame; that the Second Emergence is irreversible, not universal, and ranks nothing; that Full Wings crew the guilds and First Wings cannot, as a fact of biology and not a verdict; that the guilds have been consistently and politely unhelpful to research on the trigger; and that the compact's expertise runs toward the Corps. The author's reason for the guilds' reticence is the author's own, given as such, and the record's own account of the guilds' institutional interest stands beside it unaltered. Where the author says the first report was right about the lives, the desk notes that the record's correction stands and the author does not dispute it. The shadow under the canopy is kept as the author gave it, unidentified, under the Archive's own rule that [what it cannot carry is marked and not supplied](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/). Nothing of the inside of the shell is on file, at the author's choice. What a Prismeri on a Ranger liaison posting is on the Corps' rolls, the account does not say and the Archive has not established. The reader's question at the close was not answered at the table, and the desk files that it was asked.*
