### Receiving desk's note

*The Survey Archive asked, under the standing data-exchange arrangement between the Cerebraun Hegemony and the Survey Corps, for a history of the Cerebraun by one of them. The answer did not come from a person. It came from the Prime Ganglion's office, under the office's seal, as a filing, which is how the Hegemony answers a party it does not command. The desk asked for a name to file it under, since the Archive files a document under the hand that made it, and the office gave the name of the officer who drafted it and noted that it had done so. The filing is set out in items. Each item is answered in three parts, as a Cerebraun answers, and the desk keeps the parts numbered and in the order they came. Several items carry a further line set apart beneath the third part; the desk keeps those as they came and reads them as what they say. The exchange carries the first channel and no other. Where the filing marks a passage as also given in display, the desk has written so in brackets, and has written that it is not carried; the Archive holds no reading of any display, and could not make one from what it was sent. Nothing in the filing is dated by a year, and the desk has set none beside it. The note at the end is ours.*

---

### Acknowledgement

**First.** The Survey Archive's request for a history of the Cerebraun, by one of them, is formally noted.

**Second.** The office answers it. The office is one of them, and it is the one of them that answers outside parties. A history given by a single Cerebraun to a party the Hegemony does not command would be that Cerebraun's, and the Archive would file it as one voice among the Hegemony's limbs. The office does not decline the Archive that reading. It supplies the other.

**Third.** Each item below is answered in three parts. The parts do not contradict one another and are not revisions of one another. A reader who takes the third part for the true one, because it comes last, has read the filing as a singly-brained party reads, and the office notes that the error is common and is not the reader's fault.

### Item one: what a history is

**First.** A history is the record of a nervous system settling. The limbs act first, because limbs do. The voice arrives afterwards and negotiates with what the limbs already were. The office files the history of the Cerebraun in that order and in no other.

**Second.** The office has no other order to give. Every Cerebraun knows this one from the inside. A limb that has moved before the voice has decided is ordinary, and Cerebraun physicians file it as ordinary.

**Third.** The Survey's own histories begin, the office understands, with a first act, and go forward from it. This one begins with the voice, and files what came before the voice as the limbs'. Nothing in the order is reserved.

> *Beneath.* The office advises that a history filed in this order is not to be read as a ranking of its parts.

### Item two: the limbs

**First.** Before the voice, the Cerebraun were limbs. The office files that period in one sentence, this one, because limbs do not file.

**Second.** What the limbs did is known by what the voice found when it arrived. It found work already done, places already held, and ways already kept, none of them decided by anyone who could have said so. The voice did not make these. It found them and negotiated with them, and the negotiation is the Hegemony.

**Third.** The office does not number the limbs, name them, or say where they were. That is reserved.

[*Second channel: marked as also given in display. Not carried.*]

### Item three: the voice

**First.** The voice arrived. The office files its arrival as the beginning of the Hegemony and of this history, and gives it no date, since the office's dating is not supplied to the exchange.

**Second.** The voice is the Prime Ganglion. It is a seat and not a person. It is held in rotation, by whichever Cerebraun shows the clearest capacity to hold the whole polity's competing signals in coherent balance. How that capacity is shown is reserved.

**Third.** The limbs are not the voice's equals. They are not silent either. Doctrine holds that a subordinate part executes the voice's decision and does not bargain over it as an equal, and the office files the doctrine as stated. It files beside it, as it would file beside any doctrine, that a body is still a body, and that what the limbs already were is what the voice holds in balance.

### Item four: the worlds

**First.** Worlds came under the voice. Each is governed by an Administrator-Voice, a Cerebraun official who answers to the Prime Ganglion and holds final authority over that world's governance, on a world the Cerebraun settled or on any other.

**Second.** Where a world's people are not Cerebraun, they keep the day-to-day keeping of their own affairs, under their own customs. Trade, extraction and defence posture rest with the voice. The office files this as the body's order turned outward: the limbs keep what limbs keep, and the voice holds what a voice holds.

**Third.** The worlds are counted for the Prime Ganglion's use. The count is not supplied, and neither is the number of persons who live under the voice. Both are reserved.

> *Beneath.* The office advises that objections lodged through an Administrator-Voice are recorded in full.

### Item five: the Halyx file

**First.** In the Halyx System two survey passes filed the reef-cities of Meridian as formations of the ground, and a later one did not. The office files the two passes as the limbs' and the correction as the voice's. The surveys acted before anyone could say what they were looking at, as limbs do. The correction came after, as a voice does. The office faults neither.

**Second.** In the same system, survey passes filed the Sentinel's pulse as an automated hazard beacon, low priority. The filing was signed and the case was closed. It was reopened under the same signature when a Survey Corps officer's request came through the exchange, and the reclassification survived the Survey's own review. The office formally noted the reopening.

**Third.** Whether the Sentinel and Meridian have one author is reserved to the Prime Ganglion's office. The reason the question has not been put to the reef-cities is on the Sentinel file. It is not supplied.

> *Beneath.* The office advises that a filing closed is a filing, and is not to be read as a finding.

[*Second channel: marked as also given in display. Not carried.*]

### Item six: parties the Hegemony does not command

**First.** The Hegemony deals correctly with the parties it does not command. The Star Rangers' neutrality is honoured because it is enforced the same way against every party. An invitation into Hegemony space that has been withdrawn is withdrawn, and the office files no reason with a guest.

**Second.** The Federation of Sentient Beings keeps no voice, on principle. The office has said what it holds of the principle, on the diplomatic record, and does not repeat it in a history.

**Third.** Particular worlds of the Hegemony hold arrangements with particular worlds of the Celtic Union of Planets, made world by world through the Administrator-Voice on the spot and within the Prime Ganglion's standing policy. Which worlds, and what each arrangement covers, are not published. The office's reading of humans is already on the Survey's file, attributed to the Hegemony, and is not repeated here.

### Item seven: what is reserved

**First.** Reserved: the office's dating of any item in this filing; the count of the Hegemony's worlds and of the persons living under the voice; the worlds of the home cluster, by name; how the Prime Ganglion's capacity is shown; the limbs, by number, name or place; the Sentinel's authorship; the arrangements with worlds of the Celtic Union; defence posture.

**Second.** A matter reserved is not refused. It is held by the office, for the office's direction, and it is supplied when the office directs and not before. A reader who files a reservation as a refusal has filed the office's *not yet* as its *no*.

**Third.** The office does not say when any reservation will be lifted. That, too, is reserved.

### Filed

**First.** Filed for the Prime Ganglion's office.

**Second.** Drafted by Essaran. The name is given at the Survey Archive's request, so that the Archive may file the document under a hand, and the giving is formally noted.

**Third.** The office's filings go out under its seal and under nobody's name. This one goes out under both, once.

> *Beneath.* The drafting officer advises that this filing has been rendered on the first channel only, and that its evenness is not to be taken for the whole of it.

[*Second channel: marked as also given in display. Not carried.*]

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author, as the Prime Ganglion's office's filing drafted by Essaran, under the office's seal and the name the office gave. What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Cerebraun](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun/) and [Cerebraun Hegemony](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun-hegemony/) entries, [Meridian](/star-rangers/lore/planets/meridian/)'s and [the Sentinel](/star-rangers/lore/planets/sentinel/)'s, the filing also holds: that a Cerebraun's answer sometimes comes in three parts, none contradicting the others; that the central brain negotiates with the limb-ganglia and does not fully override them, and that a limb acting against the central brain's stated intention is treated by Cerebraun physicians as ordinary; that the Hegemony reads a history as a nervous system settling, the limbs first and the voice after, which the Archive's entry holds of the Hegemony's account of humans; that the Prime Ganglion is a rotating seat, held by whichever Cerebraun shows the clearest capacity to hold the polity's competing signals in balance; that each world under the Hegemony is governed by an Administrator-Voice answering to the Prime Ganglion, and that subject peoples keep day-to-day self-management with no veto over trade, extraction or defence; that the Hegemony counts its worlds for the Prime Ganglion's use and has given outsiders no figure for its people; that two Hegemony surveys filed Meridian's reef-cities as geological formations; that the Sentinel's pulse was filed as an automated hazard beacon, low priority, in about 2528 UCSD by the record's conversion from the Administrator-Voice's span of service, and the filing reopened under the same signature in the late winter of 2828 UCSD; that the Prime Ganglion's office formally noted the reopening; that in the late spring of 2828 UCSD the office recorded the reason the authorship question had not been put to Meridian and reserved the question to itself; that the Hegemony's dealings with the Star Rangers are correct rather than warm, and that it has once withdrawn an invitation without explanation; and that its arrangements with the Celtic Union are made world by world and are not published. What the filing holds from no source but its author: that the office answers as one of the Cerebraun because it is the one that answers outside parties; that the period before the voice is filed in one sentence because limbs do not file; that the voice found its work already done and its ways already kept; that the two surveys of Meridian are filed as the limbs' and the correction as the voice's, and that a later pass made the correction; that the limbs, though not the voice's equals, are not silent; and the lines set beneath three of the items and beneath the close. The Archive holds no Hegemony dating of anything and no conversion between it and UCSD, and has set no year inside the filing; the two years above are the Archive's own. The Hegemony's population is not established. The reason the office recorded against the Sentinel file is on the Archive's record from its own sources, and the filing does not supply it. The question of the Sentinel's authorship is [reserved to the Prime Ganglion's office](/star-rangers/seasons/s03/e01/s03e01c04/) and stays open; the filing settles nothing of it, and the Archive does not read its reservation as either answer. Which Hegemony worlds hold arrangements with which Union worlds, and what each covers, the Hegemony has not published and the Archive does not hold. The Hegemony's account of humans is filed on the [Cerebraun entry](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun/#what-the-cerebraun-make-of-humans), attributed and not adopted, and the filing does not repeat it. The lines beneath the items are read as what they say. Whether any of them was written over a dissent among the drafting officer's centres the first channel does not show, and the Archive holds no reading of it. The display the filing marks in three places was not sent, and the Archive has none. The drafting officer is* they *in the record's translation register, as every Cerebraun person is: the nine centres are one person, and the pronoun counts the person.*
