### Receiving desk's note

*The Survey Archive asked, through the Corps' rolls of serving officers, for a history of the Pelagene Littoral by one of them. The author answered the asking from Threshold Station, where she is posted, and asked that the desk come to her, since the desk is dry and her section is not; a reader went down to it, a few tenths above standard and near saturation, and sat the asking there. She asked that the asking be put in writing before she began, and the desk did so, and the reason is in the account. She wrote it herself, in the Archive's working language, and passed it to the desk with one condition, which the desk has kept: that every word in it which the record's first filers set beside her world is left standing where they put it, and marked. Those words are set in italics below, as she marked them. The desk has replaced none of them, and has supplied no original beside any, since the Archive holds none. The note at the end is ours.*

---

The desk asked for the history of my people, and asked in writing, because I had asked that it should. A thing written stays where it is put, and I wanted to be able to read the asking again when I had drifted from it, which I have, several times, in the writing. That is the first thing to know about us, and I give it first because everything after leans on it. Nothing where I come from stays where it is put. We hold a depth in it, which is not the same thing, and the difference is most of what I have to tell you.

I am going to give the history by your words. Your record says now that the words your first filers set beside my world were conversions, made early, for your convenience, and that the courtesy was mistaken for the fact for most of a *century*. I have read the sentence. It is a good sentence and it is late, and I will not write it again. What I will do instead is take each of the words your filers set beside us, and leave it standing where they put it, and mark it, and under it say what it was set beside and which way the thing has gone since. That is how we give a history. Not where a thing was, which nobody from Nerath could tell you, but which way it was moving, and how fast, and whether it still is.

The desk has agreed to leave the words standing. It would have been easy to take them out. Then you would have had a clean account and no history, because the history is the words.

### *Water*

Your filers wrote *water*. I was not there; I have it from your file, which is where you have it too.

What they were writing beside was this. Dense, and cold, and not one thing but several, lying on one another and staying there, each with its own weight and its own load and its own dark, and between each two of them a seam. I use *seam* without a mark, because it is a word from cloth and not from *water*, and it is the one your filers got right without meaning to: a seam is where two things that will not become one are held together, and that is where we live.

I am not going to tell you what the fluids are. Your record has decided not to, and I think it was right to. Name them and you would have a fixed thing, and you would keep it, and it would be wrong by the time it was read, and you would not know, because that is what a kept thing is for.

What *water* did was bring the rest of your world in with it. A shore. A sky you could breathe at the top of it. A bottom you could stand on. A thing you could set alight beside it. None of those came with ours. The word was never wrong about what we are in. It was wrong about everything around it, and you cannot see that from inside the word, and your filers were inside it.

### *Littoral*

Your filers wrote *littoral*, and your record says now that the name is the first thing to correct, and corrects it to a depth band. I will take the depth band and leave the word where it was put.

A *littoral* has a *shore* on one side of it. Ours has none. It has an up and a down, and they are not the same and were never the same: up is light, and weather that reaches down into the band on a bad day and takes what it finds; down is weight, and cold, and a dark that nobody I know has been far into and come back from with anything to say. The band between is where a body can hold. Your record says it is the hardest place on the world and that we are what lives in the hardest place, and that is right. I would add only that we do not think of it as hard. We think of it as the place where you can always tell which way you are going, because above and below are different, and a body that cannot tell which way it is going does not last.

Holding is what growing up is. You are given a depth and you lose it and find it and lose it, and then one day you have not lost it since you last noticed, and that is the day you are let do something else. I do not know what your record would call that day. We do not call it anything. It is not a day. It is a rate that has gone to nothing, and everyone around you can read that it has.

### *Brine*, *salinity*

Your filers wrote *brine* and *salinity*, and the first of them is in a name on your rolls that your record has already marked, so I will not mark it twice.

*Salinity* is the word for what your record now calls the load: what a layer carries dissolved in it, and how that changes from one side of a seam to the other, and how fast. It is not a claim about *salt*. I have never met *salt* and I have read what it is. What the word was set beside is the thing we read with. All of it, all of the time, over the whole of the skin: what the layer is carrying, and whether it is carrying more or less than a moment ago, and from which side the change is coming. You read your instruments. We are read by what we are in. Your record says this is a sensorium no instrument replaces and few peoples intuit, and I will add only that it is not a thing we can stop, which your record also says, and which I can confirm from inside a station that is talking the whole time.

That is what your early filers set the word *brine* beside, and I have left it standing, and under it I have put a skin.

### *Fire*

Your filers wrote that we never had *fire*, and that is correct, and then your record went looking for why and found it. There is no dry place. Not one. Your record has a list of what that closed to us, and the list is right, and it is a list of your words. I am going to say the same thing from the other side without using any of them.

We had weight, and we had the load, and we had time. A thing can be made to come out of what a layer carries and go onto a form, a little at a time, if you hold the form at the right depth in the right layer and keep it there and do not hurry. That is all of it. Your record says our metal is *grown*, and I mark the word but I keep it, because it is close enough that I would not know what to put beside it. It is slow. For a piece the size of a body it is a *season* and more, and it was always a *season* and more, and nothing we ever made was made in a hurry, because there was nothing to make it in a hurry with.

And we had down. If a thing needs to be pressed, you take it down and the world presses it, and you bring it up when it is done, and the only tool is knowing how far and for how long. Your record calls that a machine tool no fire-using people thought to build. We did not think to build it either. It was there. I am told you find it strange that our hardest tool is a direction, and I find it strange that you find it strange, and I think that is a seam and not a disagreement.

### *Fix*

Your word *fix* I mark twice, because it is two words, and we are poor at both.

The first is what you do in an afternoon with heat: take a thing that has gone wrong, make it hot, bend it, join it, go on. We have no afternoon of that kind. Everything we ever made took a *season*, and a people who have never once made a thing quickly do not have the idea of a thing made quickly, and I have served among you long enough to know that this is the hardest thing about us for you to believe. You think it is reluctance. Your record says, kindly, that it is like being asked to write a sentence in a tense one's language does not have. I have read that. It is a good sentence and it is yours. I would say it this way: you have asked us to hold a depth that is not there yet. We will get there. We will get there at the rate we get there, and the thing will hold when we do, and it will not need doing again; and in the meantime you will have done it three times with heat and gone on to something else, and both of us will have been right.

The second *fix* is a thing that stays where it is put. We do not have that one either. Nothing we grew up among was ever where we left it, and so we never learned to say where a thing is, only where it is going. Your boards ask what a system is, and we tell them what it is doing, and they ask again, and we tell them again, and your record says they sometimes have to ask twice. Twice is generous. I have heard the question four times in one sitting and given the same answer four times and watched the board decide I was being difficult. I was answering. It was the only answer there was.

### *Hydration*

Your filers wrote *hydration*, and on your stations the word has a cycle attached to it, and the cycle has a look attached to it, and the look is the one your record has already noticed and called wrong. It is wrong. I will not spend long on it, because your record has it, and because I am drying as I write this and would rather not make the sentence longer than it needs to be.

Air is a fluid. It is thin, and it carries almost nothing, and it is pulling at the skin the whole time, and I can read it as I can read anything, which is to say I can read a corridor's air for which way it is moving and what it is carrying, and I cannot stop. The cycle gives back what the air took. That is all it does. It is no more a frailty than the reader's coming down into my section with the share of your gas in the air lowered, so that nothing in here can catch *fire*, is a frailty. We each go into the other's fluid with a thing that lets us. Yours is a number on a valve. Ours is wet. Nobody looks at the valve.

### *Season*, *century*

These two I have used and marked, and I owe you a line on why. I do not know what they are set beside. Your record uses *season* for how long our metal takes, and *century* for how long your word for our world stood unrevised, and I have let both stand because I have nothing better to put there, and because the length of what they stand beside is a thing I would give you as a rate and you would write as a number, and then it would be where it was put. So they stay yours. I have not given you one of ours in their place, and I am not going to, and that is not a withholding. It is that I would be making a word stand still, and I have not learned how.

*Nerath* I have left unmarked, and I say so, because I do not know whose word it is. I will not mark a thing I cannot read the direction of.

### The word your filers did not write

There is one thing about the words that does not go under any of them.

For most of a *century*, your record says, the courtesy was mistaken for the fact. Your record says it as a thing it got wrong. I want to say what it looked like from the other side, since nobody has asked.

We knew. We were in it, and we could read your file as soon as we could read anything of yours. And we did not say, and I have been asked, since the file changed, why we did not say, and the answer is the thing I came down here to write.

We thought the words would move.

Everything moves. A word set beside a thing is a reading, and a reading is true at the rate and the direction it was taken, and the next reading will be different, and the one after that, and the thing you keep is not the reading but the way the readings are going. That is what a record is, where I come from. So we read your file and saw a first reading, and we waited for the second, and it did not come. And we waited. I will not tell you how long, because you would write it down. And then one day the file changed, and said what we had known, and marked it as a mistake, and we understood for the first time what you had done. You had put the word down, and it had stayed. You can do that. A thing you write stays where it is put. That is the thing about you we were slowest to learn, slower than anything else about you, and it is the reason I asked for the asking in writing, and the reason I have left your words standing where your filers put them. Here, that is where they stay. I wanted that to be true of mine as well.

### Going into air

Some of us go into your Corps. I did. I was asked why, when I asked to, and I gave the answer I give here: because a station is a column that has agreed to stay, and I had never read one, and I wanted to.

What we do there your record has right. We find the thing going wrong in your plumbing before your instruments have decided it is a thing: what is crossing a seam that should not, what is eating a metal, what a cooling line is carrying today that it was not carrying yesterday. We do that because the whole class of what goes wrong in a station is the subject we have studied longest, and because we cannot stop reading. I am not telling you anything your record does not hold. I am telling you that it is not a gift. It is what we are in.

Your record has a page on how the other peoples among you read your ladder, and we are not on it. I have been asked whether that is an absence. It is a page where a thing is not where you would have put it. I leave it as it is.

### Three words, set beside you

The desk asked for a history, and I have given one by your words, which was the only form I had, since you took the readings and we did not. I will close by returning the courtesy, since your record calls it one.

Here are three words for you. They are not ours; I will not give you those, not because they are kept, but because you would put them where I set them down and they would stay. These are readings, in your language, made late, by one of us, for our convenience, and I mark each as revisable, which is more than was done for us.

*Dry.* What you are. Not a lack. A fluid so thin it carries almost nothing, and a skin that keeps the inside in, so that a body can go where the load is nothing and not notice. We noticed.

*Still.* What you can do that we cannot: put a thing down and come back to it. Everything you have built that we could not is this one thing, done many times.

*Put.* The hardest of your words, and the one I have named this for. Where a thing was put is, for you, where it is. I have left your words where your filers put them, and marked them, and they will stay, because this is your record and that is how it keeps. I did not want them taken out. A conversion that has been taken out is a history that did not happen, and these happened. Set these three beside the original, which is you, and revise them when you next read them, which is what we would have done, and did not know you would not.

Nothing where I come from stays where it is put. These will. I have put them here so that they do.

### Receiving desk's note, continued

*Filed Cultural Record, valid for its author, as her own account. The Archive has set no year to anything in it and has asked for none: the record holds no Pelagene reckoning, the author gives none, and she says why; the two measures she marks,* season *and* century*, are the Archive's own words, as she says, and stand beside nothing. What the Archive holds in its own voice, on the [Pelagene Littoral entry](/star-rangers/lore/pelagene-littoral/), the account also holds: that Nerath has no stable landmass and no dry place; that its medium is not water but several fluids lying stably on one another, which the record declines to name; that the people's littoral is a depth band and not a shore; that they sense pressure, flow and the dissolved load over the whole body surface and cannot easily stop; that they never had fire, grow their metals slowly from solution, and use depth itself as a press; that nothing on Nerath stays where it was put and they are superb at rates and poor at fixed states; that they cannot improvise fast; that the hydration cycles are read as frailty and are not; that the record's early words for the medium were conversions made for human convenience and the courtesy was mistaken for the fact for most of a century; and that Pelagene surveyors serve in the Corps and wear the shell. What the account holds from no source but the author: that her people knew the early words were set wrong and let them stand because they expected them to be revised, and what it was they learned when they were not; that a station is a column that has agreed to stay; and the three words at the close, which the Archive files beside its own and does not adopt. No Pelagene word for anything is on file: the author gives none, says why, and the Archive has not asked for one and holds no Pelagene language in any medium. Whether every Pelagene keeps a history as rates and directions, the Archive does not know. The author's name is as the Corps' rolls carry it, in the record's translation register, as the record's one other Pelagene name is. Her corps, rank and section the rolls hold and this account does not. The name* Nerath *the author leaves unmarked, saying she does not know whose word it is; the Archive has not established that either, and marks it beside hers.*
