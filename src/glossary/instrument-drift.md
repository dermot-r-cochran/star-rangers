**Instrument drift** is the phenomenon by which two correctly calibrated instruments produce different readings when placed at different positions within a boundary zone.

This is not a malfunction. The instruments are each accurate for their specific positions. Local physical conditions differ across the boundary, and each instrument is measuring its immediate environment correctly. The environment itself differs.

**What is observed, and what is not established.** Two clocks running at different rates would draw further apart every day, without limit. The Dock Seven pair do not. After a reset they separate, settle near forty seconds and hold there: Rook-7 logged a range of 38 to 42 seconds over forty-seven watches ([S01E01C04](/star-rangers/seasons/s01/e01/s01e01c04/)). What the instruments record is an offset that re-establishes itself, not a difference in rate. The record has not established why the offset settles where it does. Nor has it established how the offset relates to the hand corrections logged at the lighthouse on the same shore, which have grown over the light's record and point to the discrepancy being older than the station's reports ([S01E01C03](/star-rangers/seasons/s01/e01/s01e01c03/)).

**Why the difference is kept by hand.** A digital device that keeps time near a boundary is pulled, to some extent, toward agreement. It re-synchronises against its reference and corrects itself, so it keeps the corrected value and loses the difference, while still reading its own position correctly. The two records that carry the Dock Seven discrepancy beyond the station's reports were both kept by hand: the [Sceir Light's register](/star-rangers/seasons/s01/e01/s01e01c03/), whose keepers read and set a mechanical clock and wrote the time down themselves, and a cadet's [watch log](/star-rangers/seasons/s01/e01/s01e01c04/), which holds both chronometer readings on one line. Neither was more accurate than the station's instruments. Each set two readings down side by side before either was corrected, and a written line is not recalibrated.

**Example:** At Threshold Station, the two wall chronometers in Dock Seven differ by forty seconds: one keeps the station's own time, and the other repeats the reference standard kept at [Lúnasa](/star-rangers/lore/lunasa/) in Earth orbit. This discrepancy has been stable for eleven years and cannot be resolved by recalibration: when recalibrated, the discrepancy returns within forty-eight hours. This indicates a stable oscillating boundary condition rather than simple drift. The two chronometers show the difference only as it stands now: after each recalibration they show none until it returns, so the wall keeps no line of what the difference was.
