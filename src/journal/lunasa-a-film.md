This is a rough cut with no words in it, and it is filed as an impression, not as the record.

Before dawn, from a terrace above a bay, a low arc of separate points of light hangs in the southern sky. Across the water a winged craft rides a sled along a level rail and out along a causeway, lifts off and climbs, and its trail catches the sun while the bay is still dark. Through a round port the horizon sinks and the sky goes black. Then the whole earth is seen from far out, with a straight line of stations across it, and one dark station grows slowly larger.

*Lúnasa. 1 minute 49 seconds, the third rough cut. It was made as a chain of 10-second clips in Grok Imagine, each new shot starting from a still made first on Gemini, and cut together locally. The sound is Grok's own. There is no speech. An impression, not the record.*

## What it was briefed from

It comes from no chapter. It is a remake of [The Long White Trail](/star-rangers/journal/the-long-white-trail/), keeping that film's terrace, trail and ring, and dropping its narrator, its clocks and its dates. The craft is the record's [ascent javelin](/star-rangers/lore/ascent-javelins/).

## What the third version fixed

In the first two versions the view from orbit showed the whole ring as a closed loop round a small earth. That is a view from outside the ring, so the film went beyond the ring and then came back to one of its stations. I caught that by watching. The third version starts the orbit shots from a still of the ring seen exactly edge-on, from its own height.

## Where it departs

- **The ring.** The record holds no ring of habitats round Earth. The ring is in my films only.
- **The length.** The brief asked for two to three and a half minutes. This is under two.
- **The terrace.** The sky darkens where it should pale.
- **The climb.** It is steeper than the gentle climb the brief asked for, and the long rise seen from the terrace is cut to six seconds.
- **The earth from orbit.** The brief wanted the earth large and the ring curving away on both sides into its edge. The film has a small earth and a straight line.
- **The ending.** The brief ends on a drift toward one lit opening. The film stops earlier, with the station still a plain dark shape.

## One question left open

The record's [Lúnasa](/star-rangers/lore/lunasa/) is a Survey Corps station in Earth orbit that keeps a reference time. Whether the station in this film is that Lúnasa, or the film should carry another name, is open. I have not decided it.

## Where it stands

It is fine as a Journal impression. It would need more polish before it could be attached to a chapter or an episode, and it is not linked from the lore page.
