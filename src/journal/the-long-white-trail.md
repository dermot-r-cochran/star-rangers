This is a film about the forty seconds, and nothing in it is true.

That needs saying first. An old woman stands on a terrace above a bay at first light, watching the trail of a spaceplane climb from the end of a rail toward a ring in orbit, and remembers. She was the new deputy on a watch whose two clocks disagreed by forty seconds. She recalibrated them until they agreed to the billionth of a second; they held for two days, and on the third morning the forty seconds were back. She went on to fifty years of other work, Deputy to Chief, and whenever she came back through the station she went to the clocks first, and never once found them agreeing for longer than two days. Now someone young is about to see the forty seconds for the first time.

*The Long White Trail. 4 minutes 32 seconds, made as a trailer in Grok Imagine's Director from this site's address, then remade after I asked it to reread the site. This is the tenth version, which opens with the launch from the bay; it replaced a 2 minute 33 second cut and then the seventh version the same day. The generator wrote the narration and everything in it. An impression, not the record.*

## Why it is not a chapter

The rule that decides this was made before the film existed. On 12 September I ruled that the screen never becomes the record, and the record never adjusts to the screen. The chapters are canon because they are prose, written in viewpoint blocks and checked against everything published before them. A screen telling of the story is, at the very most, *chartered*: approved derivative work that canon binds and that cannot bind canon. A film of mine does not rise above that by being mine.

So nothing the film shows is a fact about the world, however it is narrated.

## Why it is not a Codex entry

The Codex was the closer call. The film is a first-person account by a named woman, in her own voice, which is the Codex's form exactly: a source's account, valid for its author, never binding. But the Codex has a test, and the test is the reader: after reading, is canon less settled than before? Measured against what the record already holds, this film would leave it less settled in five places.

- **The dates.** The forty seconds belong to the Dock Seven chronometers at [Threshold Station](/star-rangers/lore/threshold-station/), first recorded in [Incident Report 001](/star-rangers/codex/incident-report-001/) in 2815 UCSD. At the record's present, 2826 UCSD, the discrepancy is eleven years old. The film dates itself: the narrator joined in the spring of 2841, *ten years after the Last Stand*, and watches from her terrace fifty-three years later, in 2894. The tenth version makes the fifty years a career on *more stations than I can name*, which is kinder to the record than a single fifty-year watch, but the dates stand. No document in the Archive can come from a morning that has not happened.
- **The place.** Her station is called *Lúnasa*, and it has a docking spine in orbit. Threshold is a coastal station whose docks face the thin water off a shore ([Boundary Zones](/star-rangers/lore/boundary-zones/)). A second station with exactly forty seconds would leave a reader unsure whether Threshold's discrepancy is one thing or a kind of thing.
- **The physics, in words.** *Reality is layered like the sea*, the narrator is told, and the station sits *on a seam between two layers*. In this record *layer* is already taken: it names [the five layers of reality](/star-rangers/lore/five-layers/). A boundary zone is written in terms of membranes and the narrowing of the Interval between them.
- **New particulars.** A town called Carrigbawn, a Starwarden called Ciarán Doyle, a spaceplane called *Brightwater* flung from a rail across the bay, a ring of habitats circling the whole world and visible from the ground, and a wristband showing a ground time and a ring time forty seconds apart. Each of these would have to be settled in lore before anyone could cite it.
- **The light.** The two clocks are drawn as columns of atoms glowing violet. The record's own clips keep to one rule for light: nothing glows that would not glow.

## What it gets right

Set the particulars aside, and the film understands the forty seconds better than its own facts do. Even its one borrowed date is right: the [Last Stand at Dock Seven](/star-rangers/seasons/s05/e02/s05e02c03/) is 2831 in the record, so *ten years after* is exactly 2841.

Recalibration fails, and fails on a schedule: the clocks agree, hold, and drift home. That is the record's own finding. Threshold's discrepancy was *carried for most of that period as a calibration fault rather than a boundary reading*, and the lore says flatly that *recalibration alone cannot remove Type II drift*. The [night-watch addendum for Day 52](/star-rangers/codex/dock-seven-night-watch-addendum-day-52/) records the chronometers diverging by forty seconds again after a clean reset at the start of a shift.

Then the doctrine. *Where the layers meet, honest instruments disagree, and they disagree by rules we can learn.* Change *layers* to *membranes* and that is the record's own position: the anomalies at a boundary are *lawful expressions* of where it lies, not faults in anything. *Our work was never to force them into one answer. Our work was to measure the drift, to write both times into the Survey Archive.* That is the whole method of the record: two readings kept, neither silenced, the disagreement itself filed as the finding.

## What it was asked for

It was made as a trailer, and a trailer is a promise: this is what you will find if you come in. The generator had only the site's public pages to go on. Its first cut got the ranks wrong; I asked it to reread the site, and the second has them right: a Deputy rising to Chief, a Starwarden commanding a station, exactly as the rank ladder reads. It kept the doctrine too, because the doctrine is stated on almost every page. What a reread could not supply, it invented: the deputy herself, the station, a vessel, fifty years. The register is right and the facts are invented, which is how a fluent summary of a record usually goes wrong. A reader who came looking for Maeve would find nobody, so it cannot stand on the front page as a promise.

## What an impression is for

In [Where the Record Lives](/star-rangers/journal/where-the-record-lives/) I wrote that an illustration cannot be testimony, whoever makes it: it is an impression of a record that lives elsewhere. A film is the same, with more time to convince. What this one can do that the record cannot is stand at a point the record has not reached, and ask what it would be like to have kept the forty seconds for a lifetime and stopped wanting the clocks to agree. The record's present is 2826, and it does not know whether anyone will keep that watch for fifty years. A feeling for a future the Archive may not describe is a proper job for an impression, provided it is labelled as one.

So it is labelled. The film stays here, in the Journal, where the reader is someone interested in how the work is made. It is not linked from Threshold's page or from the chapter it echoes, and it carries no claim into either. If the story ever reaches the 2840s, the chapters will decide who kept that watch, and this film will not get a vote.

*But the Archive will hold this morning*, the narrator says near the end, *and the drift will be written down, and both clocks will still be right.* The second half of that is true in this record now, and the first half belongs to a morning the record has not reached.
