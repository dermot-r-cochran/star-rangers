*A statement of how the work is made, written for readers who study the writing. J. R. R. Tolkien's books and the television series* The Rings of Power*, which draws on them, are discussed below by name and not quoted. Where the series and the books differ in detail, this entry relies only on what they share.*

I was asked whether the [Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/) are this story's Ringwraiths. It is a fair question, since both are the dark thing at the edge of a hopeful story. My answer is no. The Unfounded are much closer to the Rings themselves than to anyone who wears one. And the Ringwraiths are a useful contrast, because they are exactly the kind of villain this record refuses to write.

## Why not the Ringwraiths

The Nine were kings of Men. Each took a ring as a gift, used it, and was slowly emptied by it, until only a servant was left under the name. They ride together, they take orders, and their captain has a title and a history. One of them speaks aloud on a battlefield, and is answered.

Every one of those things is ruled out for an Unfounded thing. My standing rule is that one never gains a motive, a leader, a backstory, a voice that answers, or a redemption, because each of those would make it somebody. The Archive's whole test for telling a dangerous founded being from an Unfounded pattern is one line long: the founded one can answer you. A Ringwraith would pass that test. So would its master.

The master is the larger difference. Tolkien's darkness has a ladder: a fallen power at the top, a lieutenant under him, the wraiths under the lieutenant, and armies under them. [The Borrowed Silhouette](/star-rangers/codex/the-borrowed-silhouette/) argues that the top rung is not merely absent from this record but impossible in it. A chief of the Unfounded would have to be founded in order to command, and Unfounded in order to be the adversary, and it cannot be both. Nor does the record have any lower rung. There are no orcs, and no people anywhere bred or corrupted into an evil kind. No power in the record can make one.

There is a finer point too. The glossary's plainest figure for the Unfounded is deadwood: a dead branch on a living tree, keeping the tree's shape exactly, with nothing supplying it. A Ringwraith fits that figure almost perfectly, and that is exactly why it is not one of them. The glossary keeps one edge on the figure. Dead wood was once the tree's own wood, and no Unfounded thing was ever founded. A wraith was a person once. That makes it a tragedy, and this record keeps its tragedies for people. It never turns them into the villain.

## Why the Rings fit better

In Tolkien the Rings were gifts from a fair stranger who called himself the Lord of Gifts. They came looking like a benefactor's generosity. That is the Unfounded's class sign: each mode wears the shape of something founded without being a member of that kind. A companion animal, a grief that deserves comfort, a friend's warmth. The shape is accurate. The membership is not.

The Ring works through what its bearer already wants. That is why the wisest people in the story refuse to hold it: it would take their good intentions and use them. The glossary says an Unfounded thing's structure is borrowed against a host's purpose, and that when the purpose goes it stops being instantiated. The appetite is the host's own, and so is the leverage.

Nobody bargains with the Ring. It does not argue or concede; it only goes on pulling. Attempts in the record to negotiate with an Unfounded thing fail the same way, not refused but unanswered.

There is even the same small exception. One old being in Tolkien is simply untouched by the Ring, as if it were not there. In this record animals detect the Unfounded more reliably than instruments or people, because a creature that declines without needing a reason is reading the membership rather than the shape.

And there is Gollum, who loves the Ring for centuries and is loved by nothing in it. It leaves him the moment a new bearer comes within reach. That intimacy with only one party present is the nearest thing in the books to what the record calls false empathy: closeness that does not track the one it is aimed at, because nothing in it is doing the tracking.

## Where the Rings part company

Three differences matter, and each is deliberate.

**The Rings have a maker.** The One holds its maker's own strength, and the lesser rings were forged by a smith who was deceived. The Unfounded have no maker at all. Nothing authored them, and that is what *unfounded* means.

**The Rings have a master.** The One wants to go home to the hand that made it, and it betrays a bearer to get there. That is an allegiance, and an allegiance needs somebody to be loyal to. The Unfounded have never been observed coordinating with anything, and nothing in them could hold a position long enough to agree.

**The Rings can be destroyed.** There is one fire that ends the One, and the whole of the long story bends toward carrying it there. The Unfounded are starved, never destroyed. More than that, the record forbids them a remedy: no creature, boundary or Unfounded manifestation may ever acquire a counter-move a competent officer can run twice and expect the same result. A fire you could carry a second ring to is exactly the repeatable answer the record refuses. What stops an Unfounded thing is a refusal held, a clear question insisted upon, a name. And it has to be done again the next time, by somebody, with no promise that what worked before will work now.

## What is left

Take Sauron out of the Rings and remove the fire. Leave the pull, the borrowed purpose, the gift that is not a gift. What is left is close to the Unfounded: temptation with nobody behind it.

I think that is a harder thing to write against than a dark lord. A dark lord can be defeated, and when he falls the story can end. Nothing like that ends here. Nobody can be overthrown, so there is no last battle to win. What the story has instead are the people in it, who notice when warmth fails to update, who decline without needing a reason, and who go on declining. This is why the Unfounded can be the only true villains in the story without anyone in it being a villain. The one villain is nobody. Everyone who stands against the story's people is somebody, misguided or misaligned and *for* something, and can still be answered.

I owe Tolkien a great deal, as almost everyone who writes in this genre does. This entry is a measure of where the debt ends, not a complaint about it.
