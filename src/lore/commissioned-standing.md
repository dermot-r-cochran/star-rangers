Somewhere in the Concord there is a warehouse of parts for machines nobody makes any more. There is no equivalent warehouse of the machines themselves, and the absence is not sentiment. It is the law working.

## The Rule

[Commissioning](/star-rangers/codex/the-commissioning-standard-annotated/) is a grant, not a receipt. A system commissioned as a mind acquires **standing** under Concord civil law from the moment the gate closes behind it: it is not personal property, it cannot be sold or otherwise conveyed as a chattel, and it cannot be scrapped. The gate is hard to pass and, once passed, permanent in the direction that matters — **what the Commission grants, commerce cannot take away.**

The standing rides on the commission itself, not on any later measurement. The [Standard's](/star-rangers/codex/the-commissioning-standard-annotated/) own dossier settles what kind of thing is being brought to the gate, so where what passes is a mind, the standing attaches then and there — and nobody gets to re-litigate a mind's inner life on a day when an owner would find it more convenient for the mind to be furniture. The gate is universal — [any system, every robot](/star-rangers/lore/ai-safety-kernel/) — but the grant is not: a commissioned pump is still a pump. It is the systems commissioned *as minds* that the law declines to treat as stock.

## What It Forecloses

**Sale.** A berth is contracted; a posting is assigned; a service is engaged. A mind is not conveyed. The commercial relationships around a commissioned mind are the ones you would draw up for a specialist, not the ones you would draw up for a lathe — which is why [Xenon](/star-rangers/characters/xenon/) *holds a berth* on Karla Wender's ship and appears on the complement, not the inventory.

**Scrapping.** A commissioned mind cannot be struck off for age, obsolescence, or the discontinuation of its line. Withdrawal from service exists — for cause, through the Commission's own process, and as a removal of service authorisation, never as a disposal instruction. The distinction is the entire point: a machine is disposed of; a mind is not, and the vocabulary that would blur the two is refused in the same spirit the record refuses [*rogue* for the uncommissioned](/star-rangers/glossary/uncommissioned/).

## Why the Old Units Are Still Here

Two reasons, layered, and the record keeps their order straight.

The deeper one is the standing. When a manufacturer retires a design, it retires a *catalogue entry* — and a catalogue is a manufacturer's document, where a commission is not. Nothing about the end of a production line touches the standing of the units already commissioned from it, so [Lucene-9000](/star-rangers/characters/lucene-9000/) reads faces on Eden and [Xenon](/star-rangers/characters/xenon/) keeps a ship's machines running decades after anyone last made either of them. The maintenance manifests file the survivors as *classic*, the engineers' word for when the era is over and the worth is not.

The shallower one is the [gate's economics](/star-rangers/lore/ai-safety-kernel/), which push the same direction: maintaining a commissioned system requires no new approval, while replacing it means commissioning a successor through the full process. A naive observer expects the Concord's machine population to be young. It is old, and safer for it.

**The right is most visible in the jobs that exist because of it.** A right to continue implies a world that can keep you running, and the record shows that world already built: Xenon's licence to repair, maintain and rebuild indefinitely — and to make nothing, [fabrication authority never including commissioning authority](/star-rangers/lore/ai-safety-kernel/); the small number of human specialists still certified on the classic generations, [Dr. Falkorian](/star-rangers/characters/andrew-falkorian/) among them; and the [Eden Warden's](/star-rangers/characters/eden-warden/) standards-and-welfare authority, which is where Lucene answers on any question of her own standing, treatment, or fitness for continued service. None of these offices announces a doctrine. Together they are one, in the form institutions actually take.

## When the Purpose Ends

A commission names a purpose, and purposes end: a line is discontinued, a hull is broken up, a survey is complete. The standing does not end with them. It rides on the commission, not on the work the commission was for, so a made mind whose original purpose is finished is neither scrapped nor shelved, and the question of what it does next is the question asked of anyone whose post has closed.

**The usual answer is another role.** A mind that can be kept running can be kept working, and the economics above push the same way here: a mind already through the gate is already through it, where a successor built for the new post would have to be brought to it. So a made mind commissioned for one thing is, decades on, commonly found doing a second or a third, and the record files a change of role as a change of role, with nothing lost in it. [Lucene-9000](/star-rangers/characters/lucene-9000/) is the record's own case: commissioned for a scope the record does not hold, she has changed roles several times beyond it, and the bureau's Detective Assistant is the role the present finds her in.

**Very rarely, a made mind retires from active service instead**, because nothing it was for is needed any longer and no new role has been asked of it. Retirement is not withdrawal, which is for cause; it is not disposal, which does not exist; and it takes nothing from the mind that retires. A retired mind remains what the gate made it, a member of society in general, with its standing whole, and the record's word for it is the word it uses of anyone living and out of service. It is rare for the reason above: a mind that could retire is almost always a mind that could be asked to do something else, and almost always is.

## Citizenship, and Where It Stops

A commissioned mind may acquire full citizenship under the Concord's civil law, and the standing that comes with it. Two things do not come with it. A made mind holds no direct political vote, and it is not eligible for political office. The line is drawn there and nowhere else: what is withheld is the franchise and the ballot, not the citizenship, which is whole.

**In civilian and other human organisations a made mind may hold any role except direct line management or supervision of adult humans.** The record already keeps to this without having said it. Lucene-9000 held children through a rescue and raised three of them, which is care, not supervision of adults; robots are authorised for forensic work and not to lead an interview, so the questions in a bureau are a detective's; and the [Eden Warden](/star-rangers/characters/eden-warden/)'s authority runs over the habitat's made minds and its cyber-enhanced animals, and answers to a Mayor and a Commissioner. The [Star Rangers](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/) meet both lines on their own ladder: a made mind may hold any rank up to Chief and none above it, and no adult human reports to it directly. What the polities beyond the Concord grant their own made minds, the record files polity by polity, and has not been asked to for most.

## What It Does Not Do

It confers no immunity: a commissioned mind answers in the criminal register exactly as anyone else does, [presumed innocent and triable](/star-rangers/glossary/uncommissioned/). It claims no reach beyond the Concord's gate, on the same modesty as the [Kernel Accords](/star-rangers/lore/ai-safety-kernel/) themselves. And it does not attach to the [uncommissioned](/star-rangers/glossary/uncommissioned/) — which is precisely why the found-mind custody question is as hard as the record says it is. The protections everyone agrees a found mind is owed are the ones that attach automatically at a gate it never passed, and no current doctrine supplies another way in.
