A Line Captain may walk onto a survey deck where a Section Lead outranks no one and is the only Fellow of the Survey Corps in the system. Both facts are true at once, both are visible on the two uniforms, and neither is allowed to look like the other. This page is about the second kind of fact.

The rank ladder says what an officer may command. Her guild says what her discipline has found in her work, and [distinctions are not rank](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/): none requires a rank, none confers one, and none moves with a promotion. Every specialist corps is governed as a guild, and its guild leaders hold its branches (see [The Star Rangers Safety Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-safety-corps/) for the worked example). So recognition comes from the discipline side, in four kinds, and the record keeps them apart for the same reason the uniform keeps rank and certification apart: they fail independently.

## The Four Kinds

**1. Qualification.** A certification is a licence to do a kind of work. It is checked against a standard, it is passed or failed, and some kinds lapse when the thing they certify drifts. A [Navigation](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-navigation-corps/) certificate expires where a hull's does not. A certification says that an officer may be trusted with the work. It says nothing about how well she does it. Most Rangers hold several. An officer who has rotated widely may hold many, each at the entry standard and none beyond it, and [Tissadelle Shepherd](/star-rangers/characters/tissadelle-shepherd/) is the record's example.

**2. Distinction.** A distinction is a guild's judgement of a body of work, made by that guild's peers. Each corps awards three grades in each of its branches:

- **Licentiate**: a body of work that shows competence across the branch's range, done consistently and done well. It is the commonest distinction, and most career specialists hold one in their own branch by mid-career.
- **Associate**: a body of work that is distinctive as well as competent, so that the panel can tell whose work it is without being told.
- **Fellow**: a body of work that has changed how the branch does something, with a written account by the applicant of what was changed and why. Fellows are few in every guild, and they sit on the panels that award all three grades.

A distinction is not examined. The applicant submits what she has already done, which may be logs, findings, inspections, instruments built, a teaching record at an [Academy](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-academy/) college or charts that held. A panel of the branch's Fellows reads it. The panel does not ask whether she could do the work, because the certification has already answered that. It asks what the work shows. A distinction requires the branch's certification. Beyond that, each guild sets its own rules: most take the grades in order, and the record holds no rule for all of them. A distinction does not lapse. A guild can withdraw one for misconduct in the work it recognised, and it never does so for anything else.

**3. Guild award.** Each guild also awards a small number of named medals, at most a handful a year, for a single contribution to its discipline: a standard rewritten, a failure explained, a method every later officer uses. An award marks one act, and a distinction marks a body of work. The guild keeps the names and the criteria. The Archive lists neither, because they change at each guild's own pace.

**4. Academy grades and prizes.** The Academy's colleges grade their candidates and give prizes in each cohort. These go on the record and are not worn. A grade describes a candidate, and once an officer has done the work the record has better evidence of her than how she sat an examination. No Ranger is asked her Academy grade after her first posting, and asking for it is a small discourtesy.

**Decorations** are a fifth thing and belong to none of the guilds. Valour citations and service ribbons are the Corps' own, and they record what a Ranger did (see [Rank, Insignia, and the Ranger Uniform](/star-rangers/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform/)).

**No equivalence runs between guilds.** A Fellow of the Safety Corps in Boundary Safety and a Licentiate of the Survey Corps in xenolinguistics are not compared, and no table in the record ranks one against the other. That is the point of the system: each discipline is the only judge of its own work.

## How They Are Worn

The uniform already has three marks, and each has its own vocabulary:

- **Rank** is counted. It uses solid bars, rings and clusters on a plain-finished tab, coloured by service track.
- **Corps and branch** use a single open outline with no ground behind it, struck in a matte finish.
- **Qualification** is a plate, worn beside the rank tab.

The guild's recognition goes on that plate and nowhere else. That keeps the count of marks at three, and it keeps a distinction tied to the work it recognises.

**A plate is a figure on a ground.** It is a small rectangle carrying the branch's own figure from the [badge set](/star-rangers/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform/), worked into the plate's surface. A badge is a figure with nothing behind it, and a plate is a figure set into something, so the two cannot be confused even when they carry the same shape. A plate carries one figure only. A second qualification is a second plate, never a second figure on the first, so a reader who counts plates is counting disciplines, and every plate in the row looks different.

**The grade is the depth of the figure.** It is never a count and never a colour:

- **Certified**: the figure is flush with the plate, worked into the surface with no depth.
- **Licentiate**: the figure is cut into the plate.
- **Associate**: the figure is cut in and filled with a contrasting finish.
- **Fellow**: the figure stands up from the plate in relief.

Nothing is added as the grade rises. The same figure on the same plate is worked deeper, so the system has nothing to count and nothing that could be read as rank. Every plate is struck in one finish, because colour already belongs to the rank tab's track insert.

**A distinction is read close.** A rank tab has to be read by a stranger across a dock in poor light, in seconds. A plate does not, and was designed not to be. The person who needs to know whether an officer is a Fellow in Fold Transit Safety is deciding whose judgement to trust on a particular question, and is standing close enough to read the plate or to touch it. The depths can be read by touch, as the Medical Corps' curved badge can, and that serves body plans and conditions that sight does not. An analytical chassis bolts its plates to its hull like any other mark.

**Plates are worn for the posting and held on the record.** An officer wears the plates for the work she may be asked to do where she is posted, and holds the rest on her record, where anyone entitled to them can read them. An officer who has rotated widely may wear a single plate at her desk and hold a record of plates from almost every corps. A Fellow posted where her branch's work is not done wears nothing of it, and remains a Fellow.

**Guild awards hang with the decorations**, below the rank tab, on a ribbon carrying the awarding corps' figure in outline. A Corps decoration's ribbon carries no figure. A reader who wants to know whether a medal came from a discipline or from the Corps looks for the outline on the ribbon.

## Why It Was Built This Way

The [Military Space Command](/star-rangers/lore/military-space-command/)'s full-dress order told a reader exactly who outranked whom and almost nothing about who was right. The charter's framers had watched what that cost, and they kept the two questions in separate places on the shell. The guilds added a third question: who among the certified is best at the work. They answered it in a vocabulary the first two did not use.

So a Section Lead with a raised figure on her plate can stand beside a Line Captain with every plate flush, and both of them know exactly what each mark means. The Line Captain gives the order, and on the question the plate names, she will usually ask the Section Lead first.
