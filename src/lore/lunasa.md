The best-known forty seconds in the record are a comparison, and Lúnasa is the far end of it.

## What It Is

**Lúnasa** is a [Survey Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-survey-corps/) station in Earth orbit. Among its duties, it keeps the reference time standard against which [Threshold Station](/star-rangers/lore/threshold-station/) checks its own. It is one of the many well-kept standards whose readings the Concord reconciles into [Universal Concord Standard Date](/star-rangers/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate/). It is not a master clock: the record keeps none. It is simply the standard Threshold is set against.

Its time comes down to the shore by link, and Dock Seven shows it. Of the two chronometers on the dock wall, the one above the pressure seal keeps Threshold's own time, and the station's watch log is kept by it. The one above the inner hatch is a repeater of Lúnasa's standard: *the station's and the other one*, in the words of the [first-night log](/star-rangers/seasons/s01/e01/s01e01c04/).

## The Forty Seconds

A clock in orbit and a clock on the ground do not run alike, and the record expects them not to. Each clock's proper time is [reduced to a stated convention](/star-rangers/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate/) before any two are compared. Between Earth orbit and Earth's surface, the predicted correction comes to microseconds a day, and the light-time of the link to a fraction of a second. Both are applied, routinely, before Threshold's time and Lúnasa's are set side by side.

What remains after that is forty seconds. It does not grow and does not shrink, and it returns within forty-eight hours of every recalibration. The record files it the way its timekeeping files any residual that survives every predicted correction: not as bookkeeping slack, but as a reading. It is the best-documented Type II boundary reading on the books ([Boundary Zones](/star-rangers/lore/boundary-zones/)).

## Which End Disagrees

The forty seconds belong to the shore, not to the station in orbit. Lúnasa's standard reconciles with the rest of the ensemble as any good standard does. Threshold's own clock, checked on its own, is accurate too. It is only the comparison between this shore and the standard that comes out forty seconds apart.

The disagreement is also older than either station. The lighthouse keeper's clock records from the same coast, read long after the fact, show the same offset against the standard time of their day, carried then by broadcast signal. The offset has always been between this shore and whatever kept the standard. Lúnasa is only the present holder of the far end.

What produces the forty seconds is not established. The comparison is.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

Lúnasa's shape, size, orbit and crew, the year it entered service, and why it bears the Irish name of the old harvest month are not on file.

## Provenance

The station's name first appeared in the film *[The Long White Trail](/star-rangers/journal/the-long-white-trail/)*, and it entered the record from there on 13 November 2026. The film's other particulars did not come with it. The film itself remains an impression, filed in the Journal.
