The record holds two names for this people. One it coined itself, and the other it has never been told.

**Pandoids** are the sapient people of the deep cavern networks of [Fliade](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/). The name is the survey's and stands in every filing. Their own name for themselves, in the record's translation, is [the Told](/star-rangers/glossary/the-told/). They are large, upright and thickly furred. They do not travel in space, and they keep their record by speaking it. Most of what the Archive holds about them was learned by watching from the upper cavern levels. The rest reaches it by way of the *Below the Roof* thread, which is told from inside the deep. This page keeps the two apart and says which is which.

## Two Names

*Pandoid* is a survey coinage, built the way survey coinages are built: quickly, from a resemblance, by people with nothing better to hand. The first survey to see one of the people reached for the nearest familiar shape, and the shape was a bear. The record reads the name the way it reads [every such name](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/), as a fact about the observers. The people descend from a bear-like Fliadan lineage, and *bear-like* reports the comparison and nothing more. No bear is anywhere behind them. Fliade's biosphere began on Fliade, and nothing on it is related to anything on Earth.

*The Told* is what the people say, as far as the record holds it. It is not a name they gave the survey, and the survey has not been told it. So *Pandoid* remains the name in every filing until somebody is in a position to be told otherwise. The two names belong to two sides of one encounter, and the record keeps both, marked.

## The Body

What follows is what the survey has filed, and it is observational.

Pandoids are large, powerfully built and upright, with thick fur that reads as grey against the stone. A human in a cold suit is almost the size of an adult. The body is heavy and pear-shaped, widest at the hips, with narrow sloping shoulders, long arms and short thick legs. They stand, sit, squat and lie down.

The head is small for the body and blunt. It is carried low, with no neck, and has no external ears. The eyes are very small and dark, and nearly hidden in the coat. A wide fan of long, stiff, pale whiskers stands round the blunt muzzle and over the brow.

The coat is thick, coarse and uneven. It is long and shaggy over the chest, shoulders and back, shorter on the forearms and shins, and damp and matted with silt at the feet. Individuals differ in shade. The hands and feet are broad, with thick blunt digits and bare, pale, leathery pads on the palms and soles.

They move through broken cavern ground quietly. An adult's presence is often announced by the shift of a boulder and not by any sound the adult makes. Strength, gait, mass and face are all their own. They hold the deep networks by advantages the deep networks reward: strength, and an intimacy with vertical broken ground that no visiting instrument has matched. By every account the survey has filed, they are calm and reclusive.

The bodies filed are not all alike, in size or in coat. The one individual the survey learned to tell from the others is logged as Individual Four and called [Teddy](/star-rangers/characters/teddy/) by the surface party. That one is smaller and rounder than the adults, with a denser coat. The survey has no method for ageing a Pandoid, so its log says *smaller* and does not say *young*.

The thread adds two things that watching could not supply. The people hear the ground, so that a stone moved at the edge of the deep is heard by the whole community below. They also read warmth, which tells them who is where. At the line where the survey's tracks stop, that sense runs out and only the eye is left. Sight is the lesser sense. The whiskers belong to the same account: this is a body that feels its way in the dark and hears the ground.

## On the Frames

On the [cross-biosphere frames](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/) the Pandoids fall in Endoskelia, the frame of the internal skeleton. That places them beside humans, the [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) and the [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/) in a table that asks one question about skeletons and answers nothing else. A frame is a description and claims no ancestry.

The placement does not make them humanoid in the sense the record refuses. The Krenyi remain the only other people built on the human frame. An observer who mistook a Pandoid for a person at any distance would be reporting on the light and not on the Pandoid.

A frame also says nothing about what a body can live in. The record keeps that as a separate fact, the [envelope](/star-rangers/lore/habitable-for-whom/). The Pandoids' home is a geothermal cavern network under a surface that would kill an unprotected human by cold.

## The Deep Networks

Fliade's life is underground, and the deep networks belong to the Pandoids. Geothermal warmth holds the caverns at temperatures the surface never approaches. Bioluminescent fungi light them in amber and blue-green, steadily, with no dawn and no dusk. The thread gives the consequence from inside: the people keep no day. They measure the time between events in tellings, by how long a known story takes to tell.

The survey sees adults at the margin, where the upper levels shelve down into the deep, and never above it. Its sightings are not of one community. The deep networks are wider than anything the survey has mapped, and Pandoids have been filed in more than one cavern system that no mapped passage joins. How many such communities there are, whether they know of one another, and what any of them calls itself, the record has not established.

## A Record Kept by Speaking

**They do not travel in space, and they keep their record by speaking it.** Those are two facts about what the Pandoids do. The record states them as facts and not as a level, a stage or a distance behind anyone, because [there is no such scale to be behind on](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/). A people whose world is a warm interior beneath a lethal surface was never presented with the sky as a problem to be solved.

A record kept in speech and in the memory of a community has different properties from a written one. It cannot be seized. It cannot be edited by a later hand. It does not survive the loss of the people who carry it. That is a different shape of record, and it is not a smaller one.

From inside, the record is made of tellings. A telling goes from someone who was there to someone who was not, and it becomes a person's own when someone else has heard it. A small one is told the first warm seep, the long dark, the coming of the up-people, and every telling before those, back to where the tellings run out. When they can tell them all without a mistake, they are one of the Told. There is no other way in. Among the Told an argument is settled by who was there, and an argument nobody was there for stays unsettled.

The Archive holds one history of the people in the people's own voice. It is [*Where the Tellings Run Out*](/star-rangers/codex/where-the-tellings-run-out/), told by [Went-Round](/star-rangers/characters/went-round/), and it is filed in the Codex as that author's account.

## Names That Are Tellings

Among the Told a person is called by the first true thing said of them that others went on repeating. [Stone-First](/star-rangers/characters/stone-first/) moves a stone before entering a place, every time. [Carried-It-Sleeping](/star-rangers/characters/teddy/), the survey's Individual Four, carried the survey's relay through the squeeze and it woke, and they held it, and it slept. Went-Round goes round a thing and not through it. [Keeps-A-Hand-On-It](/star-rangers/characters/keeps-a-hand-on-it/) puts a hand to a stone and does not move it until everyone is waiting. The people's name for the people is made the same way: the first true thing said of all of them is that they were told.

A name grows. When a truer thing is said of a person later, it is added, and the name gets longer. Stone-First became *Stone-First-Who-Waited*, and is still Stone-First on most days. The record renders every such name in translation, with hyphens. [The Told](/star-rangers/glossary/the-told/) has the whole of it.

## Two Manners

The thread gives the people two manners. The first is to move a stone before entering a place, so that the ground announces you before you are seen. The second is to leave a place to a stranger until they ask.

The survey logged the first of these from its first month without reading it. The shift of a boulder that announces an adult is this custom, heard from the other side. On the second, the people have withdrawn from the upper levels since the survey came, and left the place to the strangers until they should ask.

The people held the survey party, whom they call the up-people, to be *people who have not been told*. They hold that to be a different thing from not being people.

## Shadows

Pandoids are wary of shadows, and most wary of a [Membrane Shadow](/star-rangers/lore/membrane-shadows/). The record holds this from both sides of the line.

The survey's side came first. In 2832 UCSD the party's imager showed adults at the margin drawing back from the moving shadow the party's lamp threw across the ground, before they drew back from the light itself ([*Nothing Happened*, S11E02C02](/star-rangers/seasons/s11/e02/s11e02c02/)). The survey lead's reading, filed then, is that nothing below the roof had ever moved a shadow. The fungus-light has no cycle and no source that moves, so a shadow there had always been a thing that stays where it is.

The thread gives the people's side. They have a word for what crossed the floor ahead of that light: *a shadow*, a thing that moves and moves no stone. The old ones went back from it, and the small ones learned the word that season. A shadow is not announced by the ground, carries no warmth and does not ask. So the people's two manners have no place for it, since it is neither a stranger nor a person.

A Membrane Shadow is the hardest case of the word. In 2833 UCSD the thread tells of a shape at a seep in the deep and then of one standing on the line beside [Stone-First](/star-rangers/characters/stone-first/) ([*No Warmth in It*, S12E01C01](/star-rangers/seasons/s12/e01/s12e01c01/); [*The Light Went Round It*, S12E01C06](/star-rangers/seasons/s12/e01/s12e01c06/)). The survey party saw the second and filed it as consistent with a Membrane Shadow: an optical return, no thermal return, and no shadow of its own ([*Two on the Line*, S12E02C03](/star-rangers/seasons/s12/e02/s12e02c03/)). To the people it was a shadow that stood still, which they had not known a shadow could do. It moved no stone coming or going, left no mark in the silt and answered nothing. The thread says the community was a little afraid of it, with the small fear of a story that has no end on it. Stone-First made no rule for it from one telling.

The record's word is *wary*, and it means no more than that. It is not panic and not hostility. A wary Pandoid goes back from a shadow, or keeps still and keeps watching, and Stone-First stood on the line beside one until it was gone. Nothing the people do toward a shadow acts on it, and the record holds no way of turning one aside. A Membrane Shadow cannot act on this side of the boundary at all.

## The Survey and the Line

Fliade is a first-contact world. The [Frontier Transformation Protocols'](/star-rangers/lore/frontier-transformation-protocols/) sapience clause applies to it on the plain reading, and applied before any proposal for the planet was on any table. The Star Rangers' charter forbids them to transform, certify, or lend survey data to the transformation of any world bearing existing life of any kind, so their own position needed no assessment. There is no settlement authorisation and none has been sought.

The Survey Corps' surface party began work in the upper levels in 2831 UCSD, under [Survey Lead Hesper Nakagawa](/star-rangers/characters/nakagawa/). Its doctrine is to slow everything down: the longest survey windows, the lightest presence, and nothing on the surface that the planet would keep if the station were abandoned. It has not gone down into the deep networks.

In 2832 UCSD one of the party's relay units was carried from the junction where the party had left it and stood upright at the line where the party's own tracks stopped. The survey filed two readings of the act and kept both. After it, the survey lead ruled that the party announces itself. Before every shift one of the party sets a stone of the upper levels on the line and steps back, and an adult standing on the line answers by moving a rock. Neither side has crossed the line.

In 2834 UCSD, the survey's fourth year on the ground, something was said at the line by the one who answers to the one who knocked. The party heard it and did not record it, and the survey holds no word of it. The people's side is held by way of the thread: one of the up-people was told a telling, and the Told no longer hold the up-people to be people who have not been told. Being told one thing does not make a person one of the Told.

The charter has a harder question here than the Protocols do. The Rangers enter a world by invitation and leave when it is withdrawn, and there is no body on Fliade that can issue an invitation in any form the charter recognises. The Pandoids did not come to anyone. They were arrived at. [Fliade](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/) carries that question, and the record does not resolve it.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

**The people's own words.** No phonetic form is held for the people's name or for any person's. The thread is a telling rendered, not a transcription. Whether the people's word for themselves is one word or a phrase, the record has not been told.

**A pronoun system.** The record's *they* for an individual Pandoid is its translation default. It is not a claim about a system of their own, which is the kind of fact a people states and an observer cannot.

**Age and sex.** The survey has no method for either.

**The other communities.** How many there are, whether they know of one another, and what any of them calls itself.

**Their air.** What the deep networks contribute to their own air, or draw from it, has not been measured. No survey instrument has been below the margin.

**What the people say of the up-people now.** The record has not been told.
