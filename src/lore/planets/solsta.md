On Solsta the year is not warm and cold so much as lit and unlit, and everything alive there is built for both.

**Solsta** is a rocky planet of about Earth's size and a little under its surface gravity, and the home world of a people whose page the record has not yet written. Its star is yellow-white, a little heavier than the Sun, about 1.2 times its mass and about twice as bright. The planet circles it at about 1.4 times Earth's distance from the Sun, where it receives roughly the sunlight Earth receives, and its year is about a year and a half standard: the square root of 1.4 cubed, divided by 1.2, is about 1.51. A star of that mass lives for about six billion years, long enough for a biosphere to have grown large animals.

## The Tilt

Solsta's axis is tilted at about fifty degrees from upright, where Earth's is tilted at about twenty-three and a half. The polar circles lie at ninety degrees less the tilt, so on Solsta they lie at the fortieth parallels, north and south, and everything poleward of them, more than a third of the surface, has a stretch of each year in which the sun does not set and a stretch in which it does not rise. Just inside the circle each stretch lasts a day; at the poles each lasts half the year, about nine standard months.

A tilt this steep also evens out where the light falls. Over a whole year the poles receive nearly as much sunlight as the equator, but all of it in one half, so the high latitudes swing between a summer that never cools and a winter that never brightens, while the low latitudes have milder seasons and nights all year.

## Air

The air is nitrogen and oxygen at about Earth's surface pressure, with about thirty times as much carbon dioxide as Earth's. The carbon dioxide holds heat through the dark, which is why the high-latitude seas do not freeze to the floor in the long winter, and it is also what keeps a human from living on the air: unassisted, a human aches and thinks poorly within hours. A mask that scrubs carbon dioxide is all the help needed, and it is still help, the line [Habitable, for Whom](/star-rangers/lore/habitable-for-whom/) draws.

## What Kills a Large Animal

Two things, one in each half of the year, and the survey's biologists read the bodies of Solsta's large animals against both.

**Heat, in the summer that does not set.** Where the sun stays up for months, there is no night to shed the day's heat into, and a large body, which makes more heat than a small one and sheds it more slowly, must shed it in daylight. The large animals of the high latitudes carry thin crests and ridges of blood-filled tissue that fill and stand in the heat and give it to the air. The filling runs by itself, as breathing does, and can be taken in hand, as a breath can be held.

**Dark, in the winter that does not rise.** Where the sun stays down for months, an animal that forages by sight must go on seeing in very little light. The large animals of Solsta have a reflecting layer behind the retina that changes over some weeks with the season. In the lit half it is gold, and returns most of the light it is given, so the eye sees sharply; in the unlit half it turns deep blue and scatters what little light there is across more of the retina, so the eye sees more and less sharply. Earth has one known case of the same change, in reindeer, whose eyes are gold in summer and blue in winter, which the survey cites as evidence that the mechanism is ordinary biology and not a reach.

Both answers are to the one tilt.

## What the Answers Became

The people of Solsta descend from its large high-latitude animals and carry both traits, and the survey notes, with the caution a cause deserves, that each has taken on a second use.

The change in the eye cannot be willed. It reports the light its owner has lived in lately, and nothing else. Under the steady lighting of a ship or a station the eye settles, over some weeks, to one colour between the two, so that another of the same people can read, roughly, how long someone has been away from home. A colour that cannot be chosen cannot be used to deceive, and the record knows of no case where it has been.

The crests can be raised on purpose, and are. Raised, they make the body's outline more pronounced for some minutes before it settles: emphasis, the way a voice is raised to be heard across a room, and as easily overdone. Nothing in either change alters the body's shape or lets it pass for anything it is not.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

Solsta's distance from Sol and its place on the fold routes. The length of its day, its moons, and the names of its seas, lands and settlements. Whether *Solsta* is its people's own name for the world, and their name for its star. Their name, their numbers, their body beyond the two traits above, and their language.
