A Solstavi can tell you what they think by raising their fans, and cannot hide from you where they have been, because their eyes have already said.

The **Solstavi** are a sapient people of [Solsta](/star-rangers/lore/planets/solsta/), a world tilted so steeply that more than a third of it has a summer in which the sun does not set and a winter in which it does not rise. *Solstavi* is the record's name for them, taken from their world. Whether it is close to any name they use for themselves, the record does not know.

## The Body

A Solstavi is long, broad and low: a heavy body about three metres long, its back level at about the height of a human's chest, carried on **six thick, short legs** in three pairs, each ending in a broad rounded pad. The summer thaws the high ground of Solsta to a soft, waterlogged surface over frozen subsoil, and six broad pads spread the weight of a heavy body over it. The head is short, broad and blunt, carried low with no neck, and has a small mouth beneath. Two smooth, tapering, muscular limbs grow from either side beneath the head, and curl outward and apart. They are the hands the Solstavi work with. The hide is soft, thick and matte, in warm buff and dun.

Along each flank, from shoulder to hip, there is one great **fan** of thin crest skin, ribbed and scalloped at the edge and translucent at the rim, with fine dark vessels visible through it. In the cold the fans fold flat and pleated along the body. In the heat they fill with blood, flush a deep rose red and stand wide and upward, the two together far wider than the body. On Solsta's summer ground there is no night to shed the day's heat into, and the fans shed it in daylight ([Solsta](/star-rangers/lore/planets/solsta/#what-kills-a-large-animal)).

The eyes are small, dark, deep-set and calm, one on each side of the head and set wide apart. Behind the retina is a reflecting layer that changes over some weeks with the season. It is gold in the lit half of Solsta's year, when it returns most of the light and the eye sees sharply. It is deep blue in the unlit half, when it scatters what little light there is and the eye sees more and less sharply. The colour shows at the pupil as a sheen.

## The Eye That Cannot Be Chosen

The change in the eye cannot be willed. It reports the light its owner has lived in lately and nothing else. Under the steady lighting of a ship or a station it settles, over some weeks, to one colour between gold and blue, and stays there. Another Solstavi can read from it, roughly, how long someone has been away from home.

The record knows of no case in which a Solstavi has disguised the colour, and nothing in the body could. Among the Solstavi the question *how long were you gone?* is therefore seldom asked. A Solstavi coming home is met by people who can already see the answer, and the custom is to say nothing about it: to let the eye have said it, and to go on from there.

## The Fans, Raised

The fans fill by themselves, as breathing does, and can be taken in hand as a breath can be held. A Solstavi raises them on purpose for emphasis, and the outline of the body grows more pronounced for some minutes before it settles. It is the nearest thing the Solstavi have to raising a voice, and as easily overdone. Nothing in it changes the shape of the body or lets it pass for anything it is not.

So a Solstavi has two signals about themself, and they differ in kind. The eye is honest because it cannot be chosen. The fans are chosen, and say what the one raising them wants said. Other Solstavi read the two differently: the eye is believed, and the fans are heard.

Public life in this century suits the second signal well. Its politics is quieter and less reactionary than its past, and public opinion is measured carefully by made minds, not read from the size of a crowd's feeling ([Made Minds](/star-rangers/lore/made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype/)). A Solstavi who raises their fans in a hearing is recorded as feeling strongly, and that feeling is part of the record. It does not count as more weight on the question, and nobody expects it to. A whole hall of Solstavi with their fans up is a hall saying how much the question matters to it. It is not a vote.

## The Winter Cover

The fans that save a Solstavi in summer cost them in winter. They are thin skin with the blood close under it, built to give heat away, and in the winter that does not rise they lose it even folded flat, and can take frost. So for the dark half of the year the Solstavi wear a **winter cover**: a fitted layer of thick felted or woven fibre laid over the folded fans and the back, following the hide and stopping short of the legs, the head and the grasping limbs. It goes on as the dark season comes and comes off with the thaw. In summer nothing is worn over the fans, because a covered fan cannot do its work. Indoors, on a ship or a station at a steady temperature, no cover is needed, and that is how most people off Solsta meet a Solstavi.

Under the cover the fans cannot be raised. So for half the year the chosen signal is put away, and the one thing a Solstavi shows of themself is the eye, turning blue in the dark.

## Smaller Kin

The Solstavi are not the only crested animals on Solsta. Smaller animals of the same biosphere answer the summer heat with crests of their own: a low rounded mound on short legs with a ruff of crests on its crown, and an upright barrel on one broad foot with flushed crests running down it like ribs. Since they are lineages of one biosphere, a distant ancestor shared with the Solstavi is possible, and the blood-filled crest would then be an old inheritance from it. The record holds this as a possibility, not a finding.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

How and when the Solstavi were first met, and their standing with the Federation and the Star Rangers. Any name they have for themselves, their world or its star. Their language, and whether the fans or the eyes play any part in it. How long a Solstavi lives, and anything of sex, kin or how they raise their young. How many Solstavi there are, and whether any live away from Solsta for long. A silence about any of these is not a finding of none.
