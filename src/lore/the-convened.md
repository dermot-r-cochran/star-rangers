Ask who dug a den on Aphel and the answer is true and of no use: someone did, for exactly as long as the digging lasted, and every body that was that someone is asleep inside it.

**The Convened** are a sapient people of [Aphel](/star-rangers/lore/planets/aphel/), and the first people in this record without individual persons. Each body is an animal of its own. A person exists among them only in a **working**: many bodies converging on one task and acting, while it lasts, as one person, who ends when the work does. The record states this as it states every other people's shape, as a fact about them and not a deficiency in them ([The Shape of a Life](/star-rangers/lore/the-shape-of-a-life/#persons-counted-otherwise)).

*The Convened* is the Survey's English name, made on the pattern of [the Told](/star-rangers/glossary/the-told/), with one difference: the Told named themselves, and the Convened have given the record no name for themselves. A people whose only persons are workings may have none, since a name for the people would be nobody's task. The record keeps the Survey's word and marks the absence.

## The Work a Season Holds

Aphel's year is a long dark and a short bright season, and the close of the season comes back to the day. On Aphel a large animal dies, far more often than of anything else, by being caught awake and in the open when it does; so everything alive there stores fast, shelters and goes still ([Aphel](/star-rangers/lore/planets/aphel/#what-kills-a-large-animal)). Each Convened body answers that alone. It is a heavy, low, domed animal about the size of a boulder, built to dig, whose hide opens along its back in the bright months to take the light as warmth and closes over it for the dark. The seasons are written on the body.

What a body cannot do alone is finish the season's work. A den deep enough to hold many bodies through the dark, channels to carry the meltwater away from it, and stores laid in against the waking are more work than any one body can do before the close. So bodies converge on the work. While they work on it they act as one: the den goes into the bank as one plan, nothing is dug twice and nothing is left undone, and when the bank turns out to be rock a few metres in, the plan changes, all at once, as a plan changes when one person changes their mind. That is what the Survey filed as a person.

## The Person in the Thing Made

No body directs a working, and nothing passes between the bodies but the work. Each reads the half-made den or channel, by touch through the claws and by eye, and adds what it calls for; the next reads that and adds again. The coordination is in the thing being made, the principle by which mound-building animals raise structures no single builder could hold in mind, carried on Aphel to the point where the structure holds a person. The person is not in any body, and not in a signal between them. It is in the den and the bodies reading it, for as long as there is den left to dig.

When the season closes the work is done, by necessity, since the close does not wait. The bodies seal themselves into the den they made and go still, and there is no *who* left. A body between workings, in the open months, is an animal of its world, feeding, storing and scraping out the small work one body can do. The record files no person at that scale, and does not claim to know there is nothing there to file.

A den outlasts the dark that it was dug for. At the next opening, bodies wake inside it, or come to it across the thawing ground, and read what the last working left, and go on with it. Whether the working that reopens a den is the one that sealed it, the Convened cannot be asked and the record cannot tell.

## Translating What a Person Is For

Consent, testimony, responsibility and standing are all built on the person, and each has to be translated, case by case, into the Convened's own shape. A working can consent, and only to what its task is. A working witnesses what its work touched, and after the season there is no one to call. For what a working did, no single body is answerable, and the working that did it has ended. The Concord's own answer to plurality, which [binds an undertaking to every member together](/star-rangers/glossary/plural-minds/), does not carry over as it stands, because a working is not a system of persons and has no members who go on; a Convened working is no plural mind, and the record files neither in the other's terms. What the record holds to is the habit under that answer: **translate the obligation, never the people.** It does not make a committee of individuals out of a working's bodies, and it does not make a person out of one body in order to have someone to hold.

## The Invitation

The Star Rangers' charter enters a world only [by invitation](/star-rangers/lore/formation-of-star-rangers/), and leaves when the invitation is withdrawn. On [Fliade](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/) the mechanism had nothing to grip, because nothing there could issue an invitation in any form the charter recognises. Aphel is a second case of the same kind, and in one way a harder one, because on Aphel there is someone, sometimes.

In the dark there is nobody to ask. In the season the only someone is a working, and a working is someone only for a task. For an invitation to be issued at all, issuing it would have to be a working's task; and when the task is done, its person ends. An invitation is an undertaking that lasts, and a charter that must leave when one is withdrawn needs someone who could withdraw it. Who is held to the undertaking next season, when the bodies wake and convene on something else? Who withdraws an invitation whose giver no longer exists?

The record states the case and does not answer it. It is kept open in Ranger doctrine beside Fliade's, as the second of the two cases that show what the invitation clause assumes: that the party on the other side is a someone who persists.

## Standing

**The Convened are met and filed.** The Star Rangers' Survey Corps met them on the ground, in an open season, under the first-contact doctrine that Fliade made standing: the longest survey windows, the lightest presence, and nothing left on the ground that the world would keep. It filed them as a people, not as a clever animal, on what workings do: a working weighs ways of doing its task, gives up a plan the ground will not hold, and leaves its work laid out so that the next season's bodies can read it.

**They are not chartered.** No invitation has been issued in any form the charter can hold, and the record does not know whether one can be. The Survey's presence on Aphel rests on the same reading as on Fliade, that nobody on Aphel can grant or withhold it in a form the charter holds, and the record does not pretend the reading is comfortable on a world where, for a few months a year, an authority of that kind might be there to be asked.

A note on rendering: a working is **they** in the record's translation register, the ordinary conversion, and claims nothing about how the Convened mark a person, if they do. Where this page says *it*, it is speaking of one body, the animal, and says nothing about whether anything is there to be met.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

How a working begins, and what draws the first body to a task. How many bodies a working takes, beyond few for a short channel and many for a deep den. Whether a body is part of one working in a season or of several. Whether a working knows that it will end. Whether anything of a working remains in a body after the season. How the Convened come to be, how long a body lives, and anything of sex or kin; a silence about these is not a finding of none. How many Convened there are. Any name they have for themselves or their world.
