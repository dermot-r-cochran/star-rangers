::: pov donncha-o-laoire

The afternoon was still, which was the first thing he would be asked, and the only thing he would be sure of.

The holding had been his mother's and was his, and the post up the hill was newer than the byre. Donncha Ó Laoire had three goats in the top field, a dog at his heel and a brush standing where he had left it, and at some time after the middle of the afternoon the three goats went to the far wall all together, the way they went for a dog, and the dog was at his heel. Then the straw by the gable got up off the ground. Not much of it. A skirt of it, the height of a man, turning in air that had not moved all day, and it went along the yard wall and over the wall, and he was at the door by then, and there was nothing in the lane. The straw lay down along the wall where it had been carried and stayed lying.

A sí gaoithe, his mother would have said, and gone in to see about the milk.

He went in to see about the milk.

The custom had three parts, and he kept them the way he kept the gate, without asking whether the gate agreed with him. A bowl on the threshold stone before dark. The brush left standing after dark, wherever it stood. And the words at the door, *by your leave*, going out and coming in, said to nobody he could have pointed at. He had never claimed the three did anything. Nobody he knew claimed it; the post up the hill had a form with a line for them and he had answered that line before, with a plain *yes* and no more, and the Ranger had written the *yes* and nothing more. They were manners. You did not go through a door without a word in a house that was not your own, and for the evening after a wind like that it was not entirely settled whose house it was.

He put his hand to the back door, going out with the bowl, and the door went away from him.

Outward. On its hinges, which were on the inside, where his grandfather had hung them. He stood with the bowl and looked at the hinges, and they were where they had been, and the door was open the wrong way on them and holding there in the still air, with no complaint from the wood.

He did not force it back. He did not go through it either. He went out by the front, round the gable, and set the bowl on the stone by the back step from the outside, and said what his mother said, and went back round.

The shears were the other thing. His were ground for the left hand, and he found them on the right-hand peg, in his father's kit, which he kept as it had been kept. He put them back on his own peg. He did not look about for what might have been put in their place, because nothing had, and he knew the kit.

The Ranger came down the lane before the light went, with the case. Na Maoir Réalta, the valley called them, when it did not call them the post. She said the greeting at the gate the way the valley said it, and he gave her the valley's answer, and she had five bars on the tab, which the valley could read now, since the mast went up on the ridge. She asked him four things. The time, as near as he had it. The wind, which there had not been. The animals. What was changed. He told her the goats and the straw and the shears, and then he said, "You'll want the door, a Mhaoir," and took her round by the gable, because he was not going through it and did not intend to say so.

She tried it herself, which the form did not ask of her. It opened out under her hand the way it had opened out under his. She looked at the hinges the way he had looked at them, and wrote.

"Is anything missing," she said.

"No."

He had looked. He had looked twice, and the second time he had not known what he was looking for, and had looked anyway.

She wrote that too. Then she went in by the front, as he had, and when she came out again by the back step she stepped over the bowl on the stone without looking down, the way you step over a thing you know is there, and at the door she said the words. Going out. Quietly, and not to him.

That was not on the form. He had seen the form.

The light went. He left the brush where it stood. He went to bed with the back door open the wrong way on its hinges in the dark, and the bowl out, and the dog across the front step, which was the dog's own custom and had no form at all.

::: pov eilis-ni-mhurchu

The column the post logged on the minute was headed *Type*, and in two years at the post Éilis Ní Mhurchú had watched it say three things in every order three things can be said in.

That was the zone. The register called the whole far side of it [Sí Gaoithe](/star-rangers/lore/universes/si-gaoithe/), which was the valley's old word for a dust-devil taken up by a survey for a universe, so that the word for a thing that lifted straw was now the Corps' word for a membrane, and nobody in the valley had been asked. The valley did not seem to mind. The post was two Rangers, a mast on the ridge and the Union's invitation — and of the three only the invitation could not be replaced from a hull.

She had the clocks, which were the Corps' and disagreed with each other by the usual amount, and the Etheric line, which was hers, since she held the certification and the other Ranger did not, and the strain channel, which was the Corps' and was the one that on most days said least. [Type I](/star-rangers/lore/concordant-membranes/) was the clocks drifting and nothing on the line. Type II was the clocks going periodic and the line showing resonance, the neighbour's templates readable and displacing nothing. Type III was the clocks running apart and the line showing the neighbour's templates competing with ours for the right to be true here, and at every other site on the Corps' books Type III was an event with a name and a file. At this post it was a line in the minute log. The column went I, II, III, II, I on the minute and settled on nothing, and it had done that since before the mast, and the working hypothesis for why was on a page she had read and could not have measured.

A little after three by the post's own clock, and a little less after three by the other one, the column went I, II, III inside four minutes, which it did most weeks. Then the strain channel did a thing it had not done in her two years, which was resolve.

Ordinary shear was diffuse. The strain had somewhere to be, and it went there, and the channel showed it as a figure that rose and fell and belonged to no place in particular. This gathered. It drew in to a range and a bearing, a point, and held there, and the board gave her the duration counted backward from its end: fifty-one seconds, give or take the two the instrument was honest about. She had it after it had closed. The board had it after the ground did, which was the one fact about this site every officer who had ever kept the post had written into the log, and she wrote it into hers. *Point resolution, post-event. No precursor.*

The range and the bearing were the Ó Laoire top field.

She was down the hill inside twenty minutes, under the invitation, which covered the instruments, the ground inside the zone and a form. The goats were grazing. There was straw lying along the yard wall in a line the air had not made, and the hand instrument read the strain channel finding its floor again, nothing more, and the line read resonance, Type II, the post's ordinary weather. The drain column read blank. Blank was a finding — the instrument could read a drain, and she had pointed it at the place, and it had read nothing, as it had read nothing at every site on the register since the register began, and the page she had read did not say whether that meant there was nothing or meant the Corps had not yet found what to point it at.

The form was the Union's, by the agreement, with the Corps' columns on the back. The keeper of the holding gave her the time as near as he had it and the wind, which there had not been, and the animals, and the shears, and the door.

She tried the door herself. It opened outward on hinges hung to open inward, with no mark on the wood and no play in the hinge pins, and she wrote it as it was. *One door. Opens outward; hung inward. No damage. Duration pending.* The shears she wrote as the usual: *one tool, wrong kit, returned by the householder before inspection*, because households put things back and the form had long since stopped asking them not to.

*Missing: none.* He had looked twice. She wrote the *none* and not the twice.

The practices line was on the back, in the Corps' half, under the Union's heading, and she wrote the three of them as she had seen them. A bowl on the stone. A brush standing. Words at the door. The column beside it was the Corps' position and she wrote the position, because it was hers to write and she agreed with it: *cultural practice; no measured correlation with incident rate.* Then the classification box, where the register had only ever let anyone write one word, and she wrote it. *Open.*

The report would go up with the next hull, under the agreement, to a regional office that had filed every one of these the post had ever sent and had never sent a question back. There was nothing in the file to ask.

The bowl was full in the morning, with a skin on it, and the dog had an opinion about the morning, which the custom did not cover, so the dog had the milk.

He swept. He put the brush back where it stood.

He tried the back door at about the hour, and it came in to him on its hinges, in, the way it had come in to his grandfather, and he went through it the way a door is gone through, and did not stop in it.

::: pov eilis-ni-mhurchu

She walked down at the hour the board had given, as near as a door can be timed, and the door opened inward, and the keeper of the holding had swept.

*Duration: one day.* It was what the register said at every site, and she had read it there as a figure somebody had rounded. She wrote it now as a figure she had watched.

The strain channel had given her fifty-one seconds. The door had given her a day.

The instrument had a tolerance. The door did not.
