The question came up with the morning's papers, and Arashobal read it for its length, as she read everything.

It was shorter than the papers around it. *Post: fold approach, outward frontier, Starwarden. Falling open. Lists carry Shepherd, T., Chief, general operations, no department since late spring. A word is asked of the last Chief the officer answered to.* No form under it. A form would have made it an assessment, and an assessment went to a board, and above Chief there was no board for it to go to. A word went to a desk and stopped there. It was not kept. The Corps had settled that long before Arashobal's time, and she held it correct: a command posting was a judgement about judgement, and the one thing that could not be written down without becoming something else was the reason a particular officer had been thought fit to exercise it. The desk that kept the lists asked, and was answered, and the posting list that came out of the asking carried a name and nothing under the name, and nobody who read the list afterwards could tell from it who had said so.

So the paper asked her for the one thing she did not keep.

She looked at that for a moment. The Quiet-Built kept no fact whose use was not yet shown; it was the plainest thing about her people and the one humans found hardest, since humans kept everything and called the keeping memory. A year ago, on an evening in the early autumn, a watch log had come up to this desk with one initial on it where there might have been two, and she had read the absence for its edges and kept it, which the Quiet-Built do not. She had told herself at the time that its use had been shown twice on the same sill. That was true, and it had also been a way of keeping a thing against a use not yet come, which was a human habit, and it had sat in her a year the way a human habit sits in a Krenyi, noticeably.

The use had come. She found that she had known it would, which was the other half of why she had kept it.

She wrote the material part. The section, one summer: the watches set; thirteen years of readings read before a window was stated; the window stated as a band and left standing when the room pushed, a Starwarden presiding. One Principal's cost noticed, and the watch moved, nine days before the Principal reported it. The department she had not seen, and did not write; the desk had the Archive for that, and a Krenyi does not vouch for a floor she did not stand on. The board had examined the summer. The paper on the desk could have read the board's one line for itself and had asked her instead, because the board had examined whether the officer could answer for people, and the question now was whether she could answer for a post, which was a different question, and the difference was the whole of the command ladder.

Then the fifth thing, which the board had not had.

*On the evening of her order to Chief, the officer went down to a dock she no longer answered for, at an hour no roster set, and read the watch log standing, and stood where her initial went, and did not write it. The log came up with the cadet's initial alone. I have kept this a year against its use. Its use is a post.*

She read the four lines back. They were so. A Krenyi did not write what was not, and a Krenyi did not write what was and then decline to say why it mattered when the why was material, and the why was material: a Chief who could stand at the place her hand had gone for a summer and not put it there, because the dock was no longer hers to vouch for, was an officer who knew where a signature stopped. Fold approach was a stretch of boundary and a post the size of a modest ship and a crew a long relay from anyone who could share a judgement with them. What a Starwarden answered for out there was mostly what she did not sign. The desk could make of that what it liked. Arashobal had given it the fact, which was hers to give, and the fact's use, which was why she had been asked, and she stopped.

The paper had a second line, under a rule, which she had read first and answered last, as she answered anything that concerned herself.

*Lists carry Arashobal, Chief, general operations, Threshold, marked by the officer's own hand: not at present. Renewed yearly. The desk asks whether the mark stands.*

It asked every year. It was asking now, in the same paper, because a desk that had been handed a Chief's word for a junior Chief's readiness for the usual road would, being a desk, set the word beside the road's being open to the one who gave it, and would ask, as it did, without asking why. Nobody had ever asked her why. She had been a Chief on the general operations track for longer than the officer had been in the Corps, and the road from a department to a post had been open to her for most of that time, and every year she had put her hand to the same three words, and every year the desk had taken them, and nobody at any desk had asked her for the reason, which was material, and which she would have given in the material form if asked, and declined the rest.

She wrote: *The mark stands.* Then, since the paper had set the two things side by side and a Krenyi does not leave a thing set beside another to be read as agreeing with it: *The officer's readiness and my mark are two facts. Neither is a reason for the other.* That was the declined elaboration, declined in words, so that what followed could not be mistaken for anything.

Nothing followed. She filed the paper where such papers went, which was nowhere; the desk had its word and would not keep it either. The thing she had kept a year had gone out of her with the four lines, and she registered its going the way she registered a reading settle: not the number, whether it stopped. It stopped. What she noticed, and had not expected to, was that it had weighed something while she kept it, and that she had not known the weight until it was off her, which was a thing humans said about keeping and which she had taken for a figure of speech.

The outer docks' log came up at the turn of the station's day, as it did, with a Lead's initial under the cadet's. She read it for its length and sent it down, and the department went on with its work, which had not stopped for any of this.

The posting list came down the line with the morning's others, and Wender read it standing, because a list is a thing one has already been told.

The line was what the rank said it was: a stretch of the outward frontier with its posts strung along it, each one somebody's, each one up to nine hours from her by relay and further by hull, each one a Starwarden's and not hers, which was the whole art of the rank and the part the rank did not teach. A Line Captain who stood over her posts had a line of officers doing what she would have done, slowly. A Line Captain who did not had a line. She had come to it from the top of one thing, a pilot department flown as a department flies, and had found the step up to be a step away, and had not yet decided whether she liked that or had only been told to.

The list had five lines on it. Four were rotations she had been expecting since the summer. The fifth was the fold-approach post at the line's far end, which had been falling open since its Starwarden went up, and under *Starwarden* it said *Shepherd, T., effective on assumption*, and under that it said nothing, which was what a posting list said under a name.

She read the fifth line again, which she did not do.

She knew the name. Officers who read posting lists knew it, by this year; a rank and a half ahead of a cohort was the kind of thing a list showed without anyone having to say it, and the lists had been showing it since a Senior had declined to call a reading significant in a certification board two autumns ago. Wender had been in that board. She had been in a briefing room at Threshold the summer after, at the end of a table no briefing had required her at, and had said one sentence. She had read, by report, of a finding reproduced in a review chamber without its finder, and of a stream listened to on a cold membrane for most of an hour and nothing heard. None of it was on any record as hers. She had seen to that without deciding to, by the simple method of giving no reason on any record for being where she was, three rooms in a year and some since.

Somebody had been asked. That was how a name got onto a list under a post: the desk that kept the lists asked a word of the last Chief the officer had answered to, and the Chief gave it or did not, and the list came out carrying the name and nothing under the name, and a Line Captain reading it nine hours from the post could tell that a word had been given and nothing else about it. Not who. Not what. The list was built not to say. Wender held that correct and had never once wanted it otherwise until this morning, when she found she would have liked to know the lines, and knew in the same moment that wanting to was exactly the standing she did not have.

She had not been asked. It was not an oversight. The last Chief that officer had answered to was Arashobal, across the deck at Threshold, a Krenyi who kept no fact whose use was not yet shown and would have given the desk the material part and stopped, and whose word Wender would have taken over her own on this officer, since Arashobal had commanded her and Wender had only watched. A Chief Pilot did not command a general operations Section Lead and was not asked about one. A Line Captain was not asked about a Chief who had never served on her line. The desk had asked the right desk. That was what the list, saying nothing, said.

What she could do was the thing in front of her, which was acknowledge the posting as a Line Captain does, with an initial that said the line would have its new Starwarden and nothing about whether the line was glad of it. She did that. She did not add a note of her own under the fifth line, though there was room under a line for a Line Captain's note and some used it; a note of hers under that name would have been a reason, on a record, after two years of giving none, and she had not kept the silence that long to spend it on a list nobody would read twice.

And she did not go out to the post.

She considered it, which was as far as it went. A Line Captain went out to a post when the line's business took her there, and there was business at the far end of the line most months, and a hull would carry her in most of a day. She would be met at the lock by a new Starwarden a week into the rank, who would say *Line Captain* and be answered *Starwarden*, and who would read, correctly, that a Line Captain at her lock in her first month with no inspection due had come for something the business did not cover. Two years of giving no reason, undone by a hull. Wender had watched that officer not reach for the easy sentence in a room that wanted it. She could decline a hull.

So the name sat on her line, at the far end of it, nine hours away, and Wender let it sit there and did not touch it, and found that not touching a thing was work, which she had known about corridors and had not known about people.

The other four lines she initialled standing. Then she sat, since the agreeing was done, and read the day's business, and the day's business did not take her to the far end of the line, and she did not make it.
