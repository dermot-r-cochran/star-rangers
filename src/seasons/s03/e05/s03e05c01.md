:::pov signe-tamura

Two thousand two hundred and fourteen.

Tamura kept the number on a slate of her own, beside the terminal, because the queue's form had no line for it. The form had a line for the classifier's note and a line for the officer's confirmation. It had never been asked to hold a count of the times an officer had put a stream through a headset and heard nothing at all.

The rule was hers. Any file whose note read *no protocol match* got one minute as sound before she confirmed it. Not four seconds. The officer before her had needed four seconds, once, for a thing nobody had told her was there, and Tamura did not suppose her own ear was as quick as that when it had no idea what it was waiting for. A minute, by the terminal's clock. Then *confirmed*, and the next file, and a stroke on the slate.

Fourteen months of strokes. She had come out to Halyx in the late spring of the year before, to a relay station at the edge of somebody else's jurisdiction and a queue a good deal shorter than the hand-over file said it had once been. The file had been thin. A reclassification, ratified. A consultation, closed. And a method, four paragraphs, written so that a cadet could follow it, with a note that it stood on the exchange record as a documented technique and as nothing more.

She had followed it. On her third day she had found the old file in the archive, the four hundred and twelfth of somebody else's shift, and routed it to audio, and there it was: a pulse with a pulse under it, and a phrase that came back some seconds later almost the same. It had taken her about four seconds. She had been a little annoyed by that.

So the method worked, in her ears, on the one stream where everybody already knew.

Since then, nothing. Beacons that were beacons. Pump noise, solar hiss, a survey drone's own housekeeping tick filed under a moon. Two thousand two hundred and fourteen streams with no protocol match, and every one of them had sounded like a thing with no protocol because it was not saying anything. The Hegemony's side of the channel had sent her schedules, correctly worded, and never a line longer than it needed to be.

The paper from the library came on the Survey's own channel, on a morning in the station's summer, which was a date and not a season.

*The Survey library holds nine requests for the audio-routing method cited in the Halyx reclassification of 2828. The method stands on the exchange file as correspondence and cannot be issued. To file it as a technique sheet the library requires a statement of use from an officer other than the one who wrote it. The hand-over file shows that it passed to you. Statement requested: the streams, what was found, and anything the user would have a later user know.*

She read it twice, and then looked at the slate.

It was the only statement of use there was. She understood that from the paper's own wording: if there had been another officer anywhere who had used the thing and said so, the library would not have come all the way down a relay to a liaison archive. Nine people wanted the method. What stood between them and it was whatever she wrote this morning. And what she had to write was that she had used it two thousand two hundred and fourteen times and it had found nothing.

There was a shorter way to say that. *Used routinely since the late spring of 2828, as documented. No difficulty in use.* Both sentences were true, and a desk that wanted a statement would have taken them for one.

She did not write them. She had spent fourteen months confirming notes that said less than they knew, and she was not going to add one.

There was a longer way, too, and it had the spring in it. One evening in the spring, a stream off an outer-system pass, nine minutes long: something in the fifth minute that had made her sit up. A figure. Three beats and a held one. She had played the minute again, and it was not there. She had played it four times. She had gone to her bunk and come back and played it a fifth, which she did not count, and it was hiss.

She had wanted it very much. That was the thing she knew about that evening, and it was the only thing.

She wrote the statement in the form she had been taught for anything that would be read by someone who had never met her.

*Used as documented, one minute each, on 2,214 streams in fourteen months: every stream whose classifier note read no protocol match. Nothing heard.*

*One of these, heard once as a figure and not heard on four replays. Entered above as nothing.*

*Control: the Sentinel stream, heard as documented, in the user's first week.*

Then the line for a later user. She took longer over it than over the rest.

*The user cannot say whether there was nothing to hear in the 2,214. She can say only that she heard nothing. A later user should know that the two are different, and that this statement is of the second.*

She read it back. It would very likely sink the sheet. A method with one success and two thousand silences did not read like a method. It read like one good night.

She sent it, and took up the next file, and gave it its minute.

::::: scene 2

:::pov ingrid-halvorsen

Halvorsen did not listen to streams, and the library had written to her about listening to streams, which she thought showed a certain thoroughness in the library.

The Survey had given her the summer to write up the seventh. It was mostly written. The paper came in among the proofs, with a second paper pinned behind it.

*The library is asked to file a method of running a raw stream as audio. One statement of use is in hand, attached. The Archive holds a second use: general operations log, seventh revisit, surveyed membrane, two single-source streams run as audio by the commanding officer at the Survey bench; nothing heard. The bench was yours. The library asks what the use shows.*

She read the attachment first, because it was somebody's work and the other was only a question.

A Principal at Halyx. Two thousand two hundred and fourteen streams and nothing in them; one that had seemed to be something and had been entered as nothing; a control. Then four lines at the end that she read slowly. *She can say only that she heard nothing. The two are different.*

It was exact. Halvorsen had read reports from Chiefs that were less exact. Whoever this was had sat alone at the far end of a relay for fourteen months, listening to a thing that gave her nothing back, and had been asked at the end of it to say what she had, and had said what she had. No more. The line about the spring was not required by anybody. A user less careful would have left it out, and one less honest would have left it in differently.

She thought of a custody log with a stroke through two words, and then set that aside, because it was a different officer and a different winter and she did not file by resemblance.

The question, then. *What the use shows.*

She had no opinion of the method. A single source run as sound would sound like a single source; she had known that on the membrane before the Chief Ranger asked for the stream, and had handed over the stream without a word. She had not said what would be heard. To say it would have been to give a reading, and the Survey did not give readings on that floor.

She stopped on that.

She had known. The other had not been told. Most of an hour on the first source, some minutes on the second, and then the stream out of her ears and three words, flat, not asking to be agreed with. *Nothing is there.* And nothing had been, by the measure of six revisits and of a floor at home that had never left.

Halvorsen had been arranging blind checks on instruments for thirty years. She had not noticed herself arranging one on a crate at the cold end of a shelter. She had only been keeping her mouth shut for a reason of her own.

It did not matter what she had meant by it. The check was the same check.

She wrote the word the desk had asked for.

*On the seventh revisit I knew, before the stream was given, what a single source sounds like on that membrane, which is a single source. I did not say so. The officer listened and reported that nothing was there. She was correct. That is one use in which the result was known beforehand to someone other than the user and was kept from her. I know of no other such use.*

*It shows that the method said nothing, once, where there was nothing. It does not show more.*

Then the scope, since nobody else was placed to write it.

*The method takes one stream at a time. What lies only between streams it does not report, on that membrane or at home. A user who hears nothing has learned nothing about that.*

There was a sentence after it. She could feel where it went. Two thousand two hundred and fourteen streams, heard one at a time, were two thousand two hundred and fourteen single sources, each doing its one thing; and nobody at Halyx or anywhere had been asked what they did together, because there was no bench for that and no rule to braid them against.

It was not a statement of use. It was the thing she had brought home from the membrane six times, in a new place, and it had no desk here either.

She left it off. She initialled the three paragraphs that were hers and sent them back with the Principal's statement pinned behind, in the order they had come.

::::: scene 3

:::pov signe-tamura

The answer took most of a month, and when it came it was a card.

Tamura had expected a paragraph. She had drafted the paragraph for them, in the weeks between, in several versions, none of them kind: *insufficient basis*, *a single instance*, *the library thanks the officer*. She had gone on giving each stream its minute meanwhile. Two thousand three hundred and six.

*Technique sheet filed: audio routing of a raw stream. Four paragraphs, as written. A sheet carries no name.*

*Uses on file.*

*One: finding of structure. Reproduced by independent analysis, under a review chamber.*

*One: nothing heard. The result was known beforehand to a second officer and was not told to the user. Correct.*

*2,214: nothing heard. Unverified. One of these heard once and not again.*

*Scope: one stream at a time. The method reports nothing of what lies only between streams.*

*The library will take the count each quarter, if the user is willing.*

She read it standing, and then she sat down.

They had filed it. Nine people would have their four paragraphs by the next relay, and some of them would put on a headset, and most of them were about to hear a very great deal of nothing. She found that she wished them well.

*Unverified.* She had looked for that line first. It was not *insufficient*, and it was not *confirmed*. It was her fourteen months set down at the width they had, by a desk that had no reason to flatter her and had not troubled to be harsh. She could not have asked for a kinder word and she would not have believed one.

It was the second use she read again.

Somebody had done this before her. Somebody had sat with a stream, somewhere, long enough that it went on a record, and had come out of it with nothing, and had said so. And another officer had been standing by who knew the answer already and had let her find it. The card did not say who either of them was. It did not say where, or what kind of stream, or how long she had listened.

It said *correct*.

Tamura had not known, until she saw the word, how much of fourteen months she had spent supposing that the ear in the hand-over file only ever said yes. It had found a pulse under a pulse in four seconds. She had taken that for the whole of it, and had measured two thousand silences against it and come up short each time.

It had also said no. Once, at least, where somebody could check. That was on the card, one line below the finding and the same size.

She did not know whether her own silences were that kind. The card did not know either, and had said so in one word.

She moved the slate a little nearer the terminal, where a count that was going to be asked for ought to sit. Then she opened the next file. *Unmodulated source, no protocol match, catalogued as debris return, low priority.* She put the headset on and gave it its minute.

Hiss. The grain of a thing doing nothing in particular, honestly, for sixty seconds.

She took the headset off and made the stroke. Two thousand three hundred and seven. Nothing heard, by her; she wrote that much, at the top of the slate, where it would stand over all of them.
