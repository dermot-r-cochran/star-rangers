The middle watch closed at four, by the post's clock, and the close had five lines.

Four of them she could have written asleep. The hour. *Instruments in agreement throughout.* *Nothing inside the approach.* Her initial. She had written them on every night of nine weeks, under a hundred and sixty agreements read one at a time, and the watch had stopped looking at her while she read. It had made up its mind about her somewhere in the fifth week, and had not told her what it had decided.

The fifth line was *remarks*, and on sixty-odd closes in her hand it had been empty. Tonight there was writing on it, and the writing was not hers.

*Second instrument, raw stream, heard as audio, off watch, on three days. A figure of about seventy seconds that returns nearly the same and not the same. It is not in the agreement. I do not know what it is. I.V., Deputy.*

Under a Deputy's line the form kept a space for the officer who supervised her. The space was empty. Senior Ostrow stood at the far end of the desk with his hands behind him, and had the look of a man who had already said what he had to say and would say it again if required.

"I have not initialled it, Starwarden."

"I see that."

"Raw streams are not read on this post. The instruments read their own agreement, and it is the agreement we log. All six have agreed all night. A remark under a clean close tells whoever reads it that the close was not clean." He did not look at the Deputy. "I told her so. She asked to leave it for the officer of the watch."

It was a position, and a sound one, and he had given it in the order she would have given it herself. She turned to the Deputy.

Venn was three bars and a long dark plait and fourteen weeks at the post, which was five weeks more than her Starwarden. She had a headset in her hand. She was not holding it out. She was holding it so that it could be asked for.

"How did you come to route it as sound?"

"There is a technique sheet, Starwarden. In the Survey's library. Four paragraphs." Venn's ears had gone red, and her voice had not changed at all. "It is written so that a cadet could follow it."

It had been written that way on purpose, at a terminal on a relay station two winters ago, for analysts on a world she had never seen. A council had sent word back that the method cost nothing to receive. She had not thought of it as a thing that would go on being received.

The answer was already drafted. She noticed it before she had decided to draft anything: *the remark is not supported by the log, and is struck.* It was true and she could have defended it. Ostrow had given her every word of it. A Starwarden who wrote it would have a clean close and a Senior whose judgement had been upheld, and a Deputy who had learned, in her fourteenth week, what the fifth line was for.

Shepherd knew what the fifth line was for. At Eden, a Deputy herself, she had left one empty that she could have filled, and had been initialled for the emptiness by an officer who never asked what had been left out. But she had left it empty because what she knew, she knew from a room no instrument had been in. Venn had not written an opinion. She had written an instrument, a method, a period, and *I do not know what it is.* Every clause of it could be checked by somebody who had never met her.

The other way of closing it was in the Deputy's hand.

She had an ear. It was in her file, in a review chamber's flat words, and it had not once been wrong about whether a thing was there. It had found a pulse under a pulse in four seconds. It had listened to a cold bench on another membrane for most of an hour and reported, correctly, nothing. A minute with that headset and she would know.

And then it would be hers. If she heard the figure, the remark would be the Starwarden's by the forenoon, and Venn would have been right because her commanding officer said so. If she heard nothing, the remark would be gone, and gone on a thing nobody could ever check, which was one officer not hearing something. The chamber at Halyx had run her waveform through three other people's hands while she sat and did not fill the silence. She had hated the twenty-two minutes. They were the only reason the finding had been hers afterwards.

She did not ask for the headset.

"Has anyone else heard it?"

"No, Starwarden."

"Do not play it to anyone. Not to the Senior, and not to me."

She took up the pen. Under the Deputy's line, in the space that was not the supervisor's, she wrote two lines of her own. *Remark stands as entered. Not struck; not confirmed.* Then the second, under the first:

*Independent verification to be requested of the Survey desk at the line office. The Deputy's remark and the stream as recorded to be preserved unmodified pending review.*

She read it back, which she did with anything she had initialled, and stopped on it.

She had not composed that sentence. She had read it, once, nearly word for word, at an hour very like this one, on a liaison terminal with a queue running behind it. It had come down a channel under a seal she was still learning to read, beneath a first line that declined her, and she had taken it for what a careful office adds.

She put her initial under it. "It will go up with the forenoon summary," she said. "The instruments are on the Chief's sheet. He is owed the hours."

Ostrow said, "Starwarden," which was not agreement and was not meant to be mistaken for it. Venn put the headset down on the desk, squarely, as though it had been borrowed.

The morning watch came up. She gave them the post.

:::pov emeka-lindahl

He read it at a quarter to eight, in the log, where he read everything, and went to the office before his watch and not after it.

Two of the charts on the bulkhead were hers by now. The other four were still the dead Starwarden's. The chair had not been altered. She was at the desk with the forenoon summary open and unsent, and she had plainly not been to bed.

"The second is within date," he said. "The Survey desk audited it in the autumn. I stated it sound on the sheet, and it is sound."

"I have no reason to think otherwise."

"Then here is the line office's side of it, since you ought to have it. A request for verification goes up in clear. Every desk on the relay will log that this post has asked a question about an instrument that agrees. It has not happened in my nine years."

"They will log it," she said. "Yes."

"And here is mine. The watch has made up its mind about you. I don't say which way; you can see which way. A watch that has done that begins to do what it thinks its Starwarden would do. Venn went looking for you in the library and found four paragraphs. Send her remark up the line under your rank and I will have six of them in headsets by the spring."

"Then there will be more remarks. A Senior may send back any that says more than it can show. This one does not."

That was true. He had read the line twice for the word that claimed too much, and it was not there.

There was one thing left, and he gave it to her straight, as the last part of a position. "You could settle it at the desk in a minute. It is your own method, and your ear is in the file. Nothing would go up at all."

"If I hear it, it is mine," she said. "If I do not, it is nobody's, and nobody can audit a Starwarden not hearing something."

He had thought of the first half. He had not thought of the second.

"You have a view of what it is," he said.

"I have not heard it."

He did not put it a second time. She sent the summary while he stood there. That was a courtesy: he saw the hour go on it.

He stood his watch, and in the quiet of it he worked the thing through twice, as he would a bill that looked wrong.

The first time he worked it for himself. It cost him nothing. His sheet said *audits within date*, and they were. If the desk found something amiss in the second, the desk had missed it in the autumn, and that was the desk's.

The second time he worked it for her. If it came back empty, a Starwarden nine weeks in the rank had asked her line to check a sound instrument on a Deputy's hearing, in clear, at every desk. If it came back with something in it, she had been nine weeks at a post with a fault that six instruments' agreement had not shown, and a Deputy had. He could not make it come out any other way. Whatever the desk said, it would say it to her. Ostrow had not signed. Venn was a Deputy, and a Deputy works under another officer's eye and answers for very little. And she had not listened, so she did not even know which of the two she had bought.

He had served under officers who would have put the headset on. He would have put it on himself.

:::pov emeka-lindahl

The answer came on the third day, in the second hour of the forenoon. Fifty hours, of which the relay had taken eighteen. The desk had not hurried and had not sat on it.

He opened it at the watch desk.

*Second instrument, raw stream, as remarked. Periodic structure present; period sixty-eight to seventy-three seconds; recurrence not identical. Origin: the instrument's own cooler, cycling against a heat load that follows the post's internal schedule. Maker's schedule, section nine. Removed by the agreement function, as designed. Audit of the autumn stands. No further action recommended.*

The Line Captain's initial against the date. No query and no note.

He fetched the maker's schedule, which lived in a locker under the second housing and had lived there since before his time. Section nine was a page and a half. He had cited it by number in nine years of audit returns. He read it now, standing, and it said what the desk said it said: that the cooler ran in strokes, a little over a minute apart, and that the strokes lengthened and shortened with the heat it was asked to carry.

*The post's internal schedule.* He looked at that for some time.

A post this size has one source of heat that changes, and it is twenty people. Some of them go to their bunks and some get up. A galley is lit. The compartment over the second housing is the mess. It happens six times in the day, at hours he could have said in his sleep, since he had set them.

The Deputy had been listening to his bill.

He did not find that he minded. He looked up at the hook where it hung, and it seemed to him no better and no worse a bill than it had been in the autumn. Then he put the schedule back in its locker.

So Ostrow had been right. The instrument was sound and the approach was empty, and the close had been clean. And Venn had been right, to the second: about seventy, nearly the same and not the same, not in the agreement. She had not said what it was. If she had written that there was something out in the approach, the desk would have sent back a different paper, and it would have been about her.

He put the finding on the watch desk for the middle watch, face up, where a Deputy would see it before a Senior did. He did not put on a headset. He did not need one to know when the forenoon came on.

:::pov tissadelle

Venn read the finding at midnight, standing, and then looked up for an order.

"Under your remark," Shepherd said. "In your hand. As the desk gives it."

The Deputy wrote it. It took four lines of the log, and she copied the period as a range and the section by its number and did not shorten anything. When she came to the last sentence she stopped, with the pen up.

"All of it, Starwarden?"

"It is the finding."

*No further action recommended.* Venn wrote it, and her initial, and stood back. Ostrow, who had not been asked, took the pen from the desk and put his own initial in the supervisor's space under the four lines. He left the space under the remark as it was. Both of those were correct, and she did not comment on either.

Then the watch was only the watch. A hundred and sixty agreements. She read each one.

She had seen that sentence before as well. On one night at Halyx she had read it some sixty times, at the foot of sixty classifier notes, and at the sixty-first her hand had stopped. For two years she had carried it as the sentence a system writes when nobody has looked. Tonight a Deputy had written it in a fair hand under a thing that had been looked at for fifty hours by people with no reason to be kind, and it was the same four words, and it was true. The note on the Sentinel's file had not been wrong in its form. It had been a finding that nobody had made.

At four she wrote the close, and the morning watch came up, and the post was theirs.

Venn had not gone below. The headset was on the desk where she had set it down three nights before.

"It changes about now, Starwarden," she said. "When the watch turns. If you wanted to."

The remark was entered and answered. Nothing she heard now could be added to it, and nothing could be taken from it. There was no operational reason at all.

She put the headset on.

It was a small sound. A stroke, and a little over a minute, and a stroke. It came back almost the same. Then, some minutes past four, it began to come back sooner, because the people overhead had got out of their bunks and somebody had lit the galley.

*Seventy-one,* Tobble said. *Seventy. It was seventy-three when you had the desk.*

*That was three of us in a cold compartment.*

*It is not a message.*

*No.*

*It is a bill,* he said, with some satisfaction, and went on counting it.

She listened to the post get up. Then she gave the headset back, and thanked the Deputy, who went below at last.

In the office she filed her copy of the request with the finding. Four of the charts were still not hers.

Somebody had written that second line for her once. They had written it over a signal they had no means of hearing, at a desk she had seen a single time, on a file three centuries closed. They could not have known what would come back. She had spent a winter and a spring building that person out of two-line messages, and had thought, all the while, that she was the one in the exchange who was not sure.

It had come back a signal, for her. It had come back a cooler, for Venn. The line was the same line both times.

She did not know what it had cost to write. It was not in her file, and it would not be in Venn's.
