:::pov eden-warden

Custos lit the wrong girder.

It was the third time in the shift, and the Warden does not often light a thing nobody uses. It lights the dock ring for whoever is working it. A crew walking a carrier toward Berth 41 has the deck come up ahead of them, because the Warden knows where a carrier at that angle is going. The Field Observer has had a girder come up a wingbeat ahead of her since the autumn, on the same principle. She sets her wings for a perch some way before she reaches it, and the Warden had learned the set.

She had set her wings for the rib over the tug berths. The light had come up along the cable tray. She had come down on the rib before it, in the dark, and stood there. After a moment Custos had lit that one, and put the other out.

She was on Eden for five days, on a small matter of tug consumables between Eden's dock and another habitat's, announced in the usual way. The task force's letter had gone to the Commissioner. The annex had come to the Warden as a copy, for the file. The file was Custos's to keep, and it kept it.

The entry was the ordinary one, and Custos wrote it in the order Eden's style asks for.

What it knew. Three shifts on station, with a clear day after the second, as the guidance requires. Perched over the tug berths. Left the ring to drink four times in each shift, by the same hatch; in the winter it had been twice. Landed one girder short of her set on seven occasions in three shifts. In the autumn and the winter, never. On the fourth day the ring's sweepers had logged two items under the rib as *debris, organic,* and had sent them where debris goes. They were flight feathers. Custos had them brought back, and logged them again under her service number, because it does not file part of a person as sweepings.

What it inferred. That she was in moult. The Warden runs the habitat's lamps, and the lamps had said summer for seven weeks. It had read what the certification body publishes about ravens, which is not much, and that much was consistent. A moult is not an injury. A bird in moult flies on fewer feathers, and comes down sooner.

What it did not know took longer.

Eden's file held Eden's days. She had been on Eden's deck for twenty-three days since the turn of the year, and on every one of them the Warden could say where she had perched and for how long. Between those days the file said *clear.* It said so because Eden's style, like the other four, enters as clear any day on which she is not on station, and on those days she had not been on station on Eden. She had not been on Eden at all. She had left by the dock, in a tug, and come back by the dock weeks later, and the Warden had lit the ring for her both times and had no line for the interval.

It had never needed one. The file had been in order every quarter since it was opened. It was in order now. The guidance asks for a clear day after every two on station. Eden's file showed that with room to spare.

*Ward,* Custos said, inward, *does the Commissioner's office hear where she goes when she leaves?*

*It hears where the task force is going next, when the Chief Commissioner's office tells it. That is about the task force.* Ward had the Commissioner's morning post open, as it usually did. *You want to know about her.*

*I want to know what I am calling clear.*

*You could ask her. You did before.*

Custos considered it. In the winter it had asked, because a column was empty and only she could fill it. This column was not empty. It was full, and correct, and Custos did not know what it meant. A question put now would be the Warden asking a person to account for the Warden's own word.

So it did what it has done since the winter with every entry it writes on her, which is send it to her: through the task force's office, because that is the lawful door, and copied to the Commissioner. It is the only one of the five offices that does. Nobody told it to begin and nobody has told it to stop.

It wrote the third part plainly. *Not known: where the Field Observer is on the days this file enters as clear. Eden's file is correct for Eden's deck. It is one of five.*

Under the fourth heading, where the Warden's form puts a question, it left the line empty. It had not done that before.

:::pov alala

The entry came up the task force's channel on the second day she was home, and the Commander passed it across to her unopened, as he did with all of them.

She read it on the rail behind his chair. It was Eden's, so it said what it knew before it said anything else, and what it knew was seven short landings and two feathers. She had not known the count. She had known the landings.

The moult had come when the lamps said summer, as it came every year. Two outer primaries were out on each wing and the new ones were a third grown, still in the sheath. A raven flies a moult. She had flown several. It is a matter of setting the wings for a nearer perch and not minding.

She had been minding, a little, since the spring.

*Not known: where the Field Observer is on the days this file enters as clear.*

She read that line several times. Then she asked the subsystem for all five files, for the year so far, side by side.

It could produce any one of the five on request, and had, often. It had never been asked for them together. Nobody had. The five offices do not send each other their files, and the body above them receives five returns in five house styles and has declined to say which is right. She was the only place in the Compact where all five were held at once.

They did not look alike. Halcyon's ran to tables. Antariksha's was in four scripts and had, since the spring, a word at the head of it she had learned to recognise. One of the others gave her mass to the gram at every visit, taken by weight, on a spinning deck. But the clear days were the one thing the five styles agreed on, since they all took the figure from the same standard: a clear day after every two on station.

Every file showed it. Every file was in order.

The subsystem laid the five calendars over each other, and she looked at what was left.

Between them the files entered sixty-two days that year as clear. On twenty-nine of those she had been on station on another habitat's deck, flying two shifts, in another office's file. On nineteen she had been in a tug. In a tug's crew space what weight there is comes and goes with the drive, and a raven does not fly there; she holds a rail and waits. No file had a word for the tug. It lay between the day one habitat stopped counting and the day the next one started.

Fourteen days had been clear. She had spent them here, on this rail.

The subsystem drafted a note before she asked for one. It said that the Field Observer's deployment was within guidance in each of the five files of record, that her moult was seasonal and unremarkable, and that the certification body had reviewed the task force's practice and found it adequate. All of that was true. She had seen that draft before, or its close relation, and knew what it was for.

She wrote it herself, in the Warden's form, since the Warden had sent her the form with a line left empty in it.

*Known: the five files enter sixty-two days this year as clear. On twenty-nine the Field Observer was on another habitat's deck. On nineteen she was in a tug. Fourteen were clear.*

*Inferred: that no file is in error. Each is correct for its own deck.*

*Not known: whether she is landing short because of the moult or because of the year. She has not flown a moult in a year like this one, and has nothing to set it against.*

The fourth line took her longest. The Warden's form ends on a question. She had answered one, in the winter, and been glad to be asked. She found that she did not want to ask this. A question could be answered *no,* correctly, by five offices, each of them looking at its own file.

*The Field Observer asks that crossings be counted, and other decks. This is not put as a question.*

Then she gave it to the Commander to send. It was his office, and they were his letters.

:::pov alala

She had watched a great many people read. It was most of her service. From a rib, from above, a person reading is a head that stops moving, and she had timed more of them than she could have said: ninety seconds for a raven, forty for a sheet with a sentence over it, nine for a table with none.

She had not often timed him. He read with his back to the rail.

He read it once, at the pace he read everything. Then he did not put it down. She counted four minutes on the same leaf, which was longer than any floor had given anything in her tallies, and at the end of it he got up and went to the cabinet where the task force keeps its own copies.

He took out the year's annexes. Each was one leaf: the reference, the habitat, the dates she would fly. He had written every one of them, and sent each to a Commissioner in advance, and each had been filed where it was sent and had meant what it said. He laid them out on the round table in the order of their dates, edge to edge, which was what he did with other people's ledgers. She had seen him do it with berth registries from two habitats and with two hundred and twelve pairs of figures. She had not seen him do it with his own hand.

Laid out, they nearly touched. In some places the last date on one leaf and the first on the next were two days apart, and the two days were a crossing.

"Each of these is in order," he said. He was not speaking to her, particularly.

He stood over the table for some time. She knew how he stood when a ledger had shown him a thing. This was that, and something else as well, which she had seen from above on other decks and had not seen on him. He was reading the ends of the lines.

"They're the Governor's taskings," he said. "They're my dates." He turned round then, and looked at her, and at the wing, which she did not move. "I can't close this one. I'm the party."

That was correct, and she had known it when she wrote the fourth line. The task force takes what falls between five habitats. This had fallen between five habitats, and the task force had been the one doing it.

He sent it to the Governor that afternoon. She watched for whether he would shorten it, and he did not. It went as she had written it, four lines, under a covering sentence of his own that gave his name as the officer who had set the dates. The same four lines went to the five welfare offices, one text to all five. The subsystem had offered to put it into five house styles, and she had declined.

Then he asked her one thing. He asked how long the outer primaries would take.

The subsystem had a range from the certification body's guidance. She had her own moults to go by. She gave him her own figure, which was thirty-six days.

He wrote five letters, to five Commissioners, by hand. She read them as he finished each. They had the reference and the date, as his letters always did. Where the annex would have said when she was flying, each said: *The Field Observer will not be overhead on this habitat or any other for thirty-six days from this date.* He had not rounded it to five weeks. He had not added a week of his own.

"Somebody has to keep the count," he said. "I'd keep it. I don't think it should be me."

She had worked that out as well. Five offices kept five files and each was right. A sixth office would be right about a sixth thing. The only party who was on every deck she was on, and in every tug, was already holding the other five.

She had the subsystem open a file. It was in no house style. It had a column the five did not have, and she headed it *crossings.*

"Then I'll append it," he said. "Each quarter. As it stands."

He went back to his desk. She stayed on the rail, which that day was a clear day, and would be counted as one.

The letter reached the Warden as a copy, and Custos read it twice, because it was the first of its kind.

Every notification on the file said when she would fly. This one said she would not. Commissioner Wayland's office forwarded it without comment, and by the next day it had reached the dock crews, in the way written things reach dock crews. On the first shift after, thirty-one people on the ring looked up at the rib over the tug berths, where they had been told in writing that there was no raven. None of them had looked at it twice while she was there.

Custos did not light the girder. It lights the ring for whoever is working it.

Her four lines had come to Eden with the Commander's letter, and a week later something else came, from her, through the task force's office and copied to the Commissioner. It was the first leaf of a file. Eden had sent her its entries since the winter, and she had sent hers back by the same route.

Custos read it against its own.

Eden's twenty-three days were in it, and agreed. Between them, where Eden's file said *clear,* hers said Halcyon, and Antariksha, and the other two, and under the column the five files did not have it said *tug,* nineteen times. Fourteen days in it were clear. They were not the days Eden had thought.

*The Commissioner will ask whether Eden's file was deficient,* Ward said, inward. Ward was already composing the answer.

*It was complete. It was a fifth.* Custos wrote that into the entry, in those words, under what it knew. Under what it inferred it wrote that the short landings had been two things at once, and that it could not divide them. It left the word *clear* in Eden's file wherever it stood. It was Eden's word, and true of Eden's deck. Beside each one it put the reference of her leaf.

She came back up the ring forty days after the letter, on the task force's business, announced. The heads came up, and the pointing, and then the forgetting.

She set her wings some way out, as she does.

Custos knew two things about where she would come down. It knew the rib over the tug berths, from the autumn and the winter. It knew the one before it, from the summer. It did not know which was hers now, and it had a new file that did not say.

It lit both.

She came down on the far one. Custos put the near one out, and wrote the entry, and sent it to her.
