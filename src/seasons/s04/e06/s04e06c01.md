:::pov dorian-calloway

The money came back for the fourth time in the quarter's tenth week, and Calloway knew it by its size before he read whose it was.

It was not much. At the quarter's rate it was the price of three kilograms and four hundred grams of pressing-grade ceramic feedstock, which is a little less than supper for two on either deck concerned. It had gone from Eden to Halcyon in the spring and been returned. It had gone again and been returned again. The Compact's bonded settlement sits in his office, because cross-habitat logistics do and because no habitat ever wanted it, and a payment that the payee sends back stands there in suspense until somebody claims it. A suspense line goes on the quarter's sheet with everything else. It takes one of the four words.

He had given it *carried* at the spring close and again at the summer's, without thinking, which is how the word is usually given. A carried line is one that somebody will finish next quarter.

This time he read the covering lines. Each crossing had one, because the settlement asks for a reason, and the two parties had been supplying reasons for half a year with great courtesy.

The payer was a fabricator on Eden's Deck 9. *Received nine crates per HC-2290, certified on arrival at Eden's bed. Invoice short of that by 3.4 kg. Balance enclosed. I do not hold goods I have not paid for.*

The payee was a feedstock mill on Halcyon. *Sold nine crates per HC-2290, certified at our hall's door. Invoice is correct to that. Balance returned. We do not take payment for mass we did not send.*

There were four of each. They did not vary much. By the third exchange the fabricator had added that the next order was attached, and the mill that it would ship on the usual day.

So that was where the cradle had got to. Calloway had the reading itself on the sheet in front of him, two lines down, as he had had it at every close since the winter: the one line that was none of the four words, under the Commander's name. He had forwarded it to the Governor three times. He had thought of it as a thing that stayed where it had been put.

The feedstock had been sold by certified mass. The contract did not say whose certificate. No contract on a bonded route did, because two halls had never before differed about a cradle by more than the trade was willing to let go. These two had. The mill believed the figure from the instrument on its own floor, which said it was owed less. The fabricator believed the figure from his, which said he owed more. In the late winter two floors had each been certain that the fault lay at the other end. These two were each certain of their own end, and it was costing both of them.

His deputy, who meant it well, had flagged the line with a suggestion. The office may write off a suspense sum below a stated threshold as *de minimis,* and this sum was below it by a wide margin. One entry. *Closed.*

He looked at that for a while. It was permitted. It was even sensible. But a write-off is the office saying that the sum is too small to be anyone's, and two people had spent half a year and eight covering lines establishing that it was not small to them. He could not see that it was his to tell them otherwise.

He left the flag where it was and the line without a word. Then a copy came across from the task force's office, as copies did, and he learned that both of them had written to Larsen.

:::pov larsen

The two letters arrived in the same week and had plainly not been arranged.

Neither was a referral. A referral comes up from a bureau, in four sentences, with a Commissioner's countersign. These had come from a shop and a mill, by ordinary post, and they were addressed to him by name. The record was open. Anyone could read the page, and anyone who read it could see whose name was at the foot.

The fabricator's was the longer. *You filed two figures for my cradle and put your name under both. I have paid on one and tried four times to pay up to the other and had it sent back. I am told nobody can say which is right. You are the name on the page, so I am asking you. Which do I owe on.*

The mill's was three lines. *Our hall's figure is the figure we sold on. Deck 9 is an honest shop and will not be told. He may believe it from you.*

He read them at the round table, with the raven on the rail behind him. Her primaries were in. Her file for the quarter was on the desk, waiting to be appended.

He had written, in the winter, that the office holding a thing it cannot explain should be the office that can afford to hold it. He had meant the task force and not a habitat's bureau, and he still thought it sound. A bureau carrying an open metrology finding into its next certification cycle is in difficulty. He had not asked himself who else had a ledger with HC-2290 in it. There had been two all along, a two-press shop and a mill, and each had a box for one figure.

He looked for what he could do, and went through it in order.

He could not say which figure. He had had eight days and two metrology offices and had not been able to make the file say. He could not dispose of the sum. There was no offence in it at either end; there was not so much as a disagreement, in the ordinary sense, since each party was trying to act against his own interest and being prevented by the other. The task force takes what a bureau cannot finish. No bureau had started this.

He could go to Deck 9. He turned that over longer than the rest. He had once written to a Commissioner that he would walk her dock ring at a quarter past three, on no open case, and had gone, in uniform, and stood. He looked for the sentence that would take him onto the deck this time and could not find one. Then he had gone to look at a dock. Now he would be going to stand in a man's shop while the man waited to be told something, with nothing to tell him.

So he answered. He wrote one letter and sent it to both, in the same words, and copied it where he copied everything.

*I cannot tell either of you which figure is right. Nobody can. I tried, with both metrology offices, and what I found is on the page you have read. Both instruments are sound. I had each tested against the other habitat's reference mass, before the crossing and after. Each of you is standing behind the instrument on your own floor, and neither of you is wrong to.*

*This is not a matter the task force can dispose of. There is no offence in it. I put my name under both figures so that neither habitat would have to carry them. I did not know that two of you were carrying them already. I am sorry that I did not ask.*

There was a place below that where a recommendation would go. He had left it empty on closes before, when the rest was somebody else's business. He left it empty here. He did not know what they should do.

He sent it. Behind him the raven shifted along the rail.

:::pov dorian-calloway

The instruction reached the settlement nine days before the close, on one leaf, with two signatures.

Calloway had expected, if he had expected anything, that one of them would give way. He read it standing.

*To the bonded settlement. The sum in suspense against HC-2290 is to stay where it is. The mill does not claim it. The shop does not take it back. We have each written both figures in our own books, his above ours in his and ours above his in ours, with the reference of the Commander's page beside them. Hold the sum between us until somebody can say which it belongs to. If nobody ever can, hold it. If the settlement charges for holding, we will each pay half.*

Under the signatures the fabricator had added, in a different pen, that the mill's people had come over on the Thirdday tug to see the presses, and had brought their own lunch.

The settlement does not charge for holding. He checked.

His deputy asked which word it took. She had the write-off still flagged and did not press it.

He had been entering *carried* on that sheet for sixteen quarters. It meant, in practice, that nobody had finished yet. The office put it on lines that were waiting for a signature, a tug or a hearing, and took it off again the next quarter or the one after. He had never before been instructed to carry something, in writing, by the two people it lay between, with no date on it.

He entered the word. It was the first time, in sixteen quarters, that he had put it on a line and it was exactly what was happening.

Two lines down was the other one. Same cradle, same two figures, the Commander's name, no word. He considered it as he did every quarter, and found it as it had been. It said nothing about which hall was right. The shop and the mill had not found that out either. They had only decided who was holding it.

He forwarded the sheet to the Governor, with the line that said *carried* and the line that said nothing. He wrote no covering note. He thought she would see where to look.

:::pov petra-voss

Voss read the task force's closes herself, and at the autumn close she read the year's.

It was a year, near enough, since the first one Larsen had worked entirely in the open: a maintenance drone berthed at two habitats in the same hour, a raven announced in writing, and a last line she had kept. *We should assume it was not the only thing parked there.* She had taken him to mean frauds. She had commissioned the unit for offences organised to cross a line, and had expected, once it was looking, to find the seams between her habitats full of them.

She laid the year out. The drone had been charged, in two halves, on two decks. A loading supervisor had been charged on his own. After that the closes changed. A sheet of true figures under a wrong heading, corrected by the woman who wrote it. Forty kilometres of glass fibre that was forty-four metres shorter lying down, with no office to say which the Compact meant; that paragraph was still on her desk, and she had not yet found whose it was to answer. A raven's year added up across five files, each in order, sent to her unshortened under the name of the officer who had set the dates. And now a supper's worth of money that two honest parties would not take from each other.

Two closes had ended with somebody charged. The rest had ended with nobody to charge and something true.

Three Commissioners had told her, when she set the unit up, that a Governor's task force was an insult to five competent bureaus. She had overruled them and had not regretted it. She did not regret it now. But the bureaus had been competent. What lay between them had mostly not been anyone's doing. Each habitat had done its own part properly, and nobody had been asked about the rest.

Calloway's sheet was under the closes. She found the line he had not pointed her to. *Carried.* She read the instruction attached to it twice, and the addition in the other pen, about the lunch.

Then the line two below.

She had initialled it three times. A Governor initials what she has read. It was not a disposition and she had never thought of it as one. *Unresolved reading, no disposition,* a cradle heavier at one end than the other, eight days of measurement and nothing under it. She had asked Larsen once, in the spring, whether he wanted a word for it. She could have given the sheet a fifth. He had said that he would rather she did not, because a word would be an account of it, and he had none.

She looked at what was on the line. His name, since the winter. Calloway's forwarding, four times now, without a note. Her own initial, three times.

She initialled it a fourth time.

The next referral was already in the tray under the sheet, copied to her as everything of his was. It had come up from Antariksha's bureau, which had not sent one before. She did not open it. It was addressed to him.
