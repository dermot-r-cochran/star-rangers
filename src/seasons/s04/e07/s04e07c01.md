:::pov larsen

The referral from Antariksha had been in the tray since the close, and Larsen opened it on the first morning of the quarter.

It was the first that bureau had ever sent him. He had expected four sentences and a Commissioner's countersign, since that is what a referral is, and they were there, in his language and four of the habitat's. He had not expected the form. A bureau sends up a copy. Antariksha's had sent the thing itself, a transfer form on the paper of the first cylinder's stores, and its fourth sentence said that the stores would be glad to have it back by the end of the month.

Six cases of lamp tube. They had been signed out in the summer and carried by a bulk contractor on a route of four legs, and the form had been stamped at each place it passed. That is what a transfer form is for. Each stamp says *this passed through here*, and the last one says *this stayed*. The form had three. Where the fourth belonged, the paper was clean. Antariksha's stores had six cases out and nothing to say where they had been signed in. The bureau could ask anyone it liked on its own decks and nobody anywhere else, and it had written that it did not know whose the matter was.

He knew the shape. He laid the form on the round table and put it to the Compact's manifest in the form of query that the Compact's own offices use and a habitat's are not taught. It showed him what Antariksha's screens had been told to leave out, which was a fourth routing code with one digit changed. The leg had been reassigned. Freight is sometimes given a different last dock after it has left its first one, and the manifest does not delete the old code. It stops showing it. The habitat that sent the freight goes on seeing the route it signed, less a leg.

So the fourth stamp existed, and it was in somebody's stores. The code did not say whose. The new leg would be on a freight authorisation in the Chief Commissioner's office, with some hundreds of others, and an authorisation names a carrier and a leg and no consignment.

He began the letter he had sent more than sixty times: to the Commissioner, in writing and in advance, with the reference, the dates and the annex. He stopped at the annex. There was nothing in this on any deck for a raven to see. Alala was on the rail behind his chair, and he heard her shift.

Then he stopped at the address, because the letter goes to the Commissioner of the habitat concerned and he did not know which that was. He could find out. He could have the quarter's authorisations up from Calloway's office by the afternoon and read through them, and he would then know whose stores it was before the stores knew that anybody was asking.

He considered that for a moment. Then he addressed the letter to all five.

*A transfer form from Antariksha's stores is attached in copy. Six cases of lamp tube, signed out in the summer, three stamps. It lacks its last stamp. The manifest shows the last leg reassigned after the freight left Antariksha's dock, and Antariksha is not shown where to. If your habitat's stores hold the receipt, the task force asks that they strike the stamp on the form itself, against their own ledger. The original will be sent to whichever stores answer. The task force asserts nothing about the consignment and has opened nothing on it.*

He read it through for anything it asserted and found nothing. Then he read the third sentence again. He had read that sentence before. He had not been the one who wrote it.

The task force keeps its own files in the cabinet behind the door. The oldest open one was in the bottom drawer. It had gone onto the quarter's sheet as *carried* at eight closes, and he had not had it out since the winter it was opened. Eden's ration manifest was inside, on paper, as the bureau's Inspector had handed it to him at a side door in the late autumn of 2826. Eleven crates had been signed out of Eden's stores. There were four stamps, and no fifth. Behind it was a sheet of his own printing, folded once, with one digit of a routing code circled twice in ink.

He had gone to Halcyon with it. The Chief Commissioner had made a call there and had not liked how well it was answered, and Larsen had wanted to see the man's face. He had not written first. He had copied the Governor, so that she would not hear of it from Halcyon's Mayor.

He had sat in Commissioner Duarte's office and asked him about a carrier called Threnody Logistics, and had watched him while he answered. Every answer was true. He had read the carrier's contract through, and it was as quiet as he had been told. He had come home with nothing false in his hands and nothing he could close on, and he had put the file in the drawer.

The form had been in his case for the whole of that visit. He had not put it on anybody's desk. He had been sure that somebody had made a stamp unreadable, and a man does not show the paper to the office he thinks has done it. For the better part of two years he had not thought of it as a form at all. He had thought of it as evidence.

It was a form, and it wanted a stamp. It had spent that time in a drawer on no habitat, where no stores could have struck one.

He added a second paragraph.

*A second form is attached in copy. Eden's stores, eleven crates of ration, signed out in the autumn of 2826, four stamps. It lacks its last, and the last leg was reassigned in the same way. The task force has held the original since the late autumn of that year and has shown it to no habitat's stores. It asks the same of whichever stores hold that receipt.*

He copied it to the Governor and the Chief Commissioner, as he copied everything. There was no annex. He sent it.

:::pov dorian-calloway

The letter reached Calloway as a copy, and he read its first paragraph as he read anything of the task force's that was not his office's to answer.

At the second paragraph he stopped.

He knew the line. It was on his sheet, and it had been on his sheet for longer than any other. He had entered *carried* against it eight times, in the way the word is usually given, which is without thinking. He had learned something about that word at the last close, from a mill and a two-press shop. He had not gone back up the sheet to see what else it was sitting on.

He kept one file for things that had not finished arguing for themselves. He took it out. The note was where he had left it in the late autumn of 2826, in his own hand, and it was accurate. *Threnody Logistics. Quarterly reconciliation a hair outside variance, resolved by the next cycle. Freight authorisation, requested and countersigned by the Commissioner's office, Halcyon. Signed here without a second reading.* Under that was a line about a call: *Answered every question correctly and at once.* The authorisation was clipped behind the note.

He had not sent it to Larsen. He could remember deciding that. One countersignature was not a fact about a Commissioner, and to pass it across would have been to pass across a name. So he had sent three careful sentences in its place, and Larsen had gone to Halcyon with the form, and the authorisation had stayed in this file. He had thought of that as discipline, and he still thought it had been. He saw now, from the letter, that Larsen had been as careful. Each of them had kept back the one paper that would have read as an accusation. The two papers had not been in the same room since.

He laid the authorisation beside the copy of Eden's form and read it for the third time in its life. It gave the carrier a leg to Halcyon's stores in place of one it had held. That was all it did. It named no consignment, because an authorisation does not.

He had told Larsen, across this desk, that whoever had taught a manifest to stop showing a routing code had better access to the Compact's systems than he knew how to bound. The signature at the foot of the authorisation was his own.

It was not a fact yet, and he held himself to that. The paper did not say eleven crates. Only a stores ledger could say what had come in on that leg, and the ledger was Halcyon's. But it was, for the first time since he had clipped it there, a thing somebody could be asked.

Then there was the call. He had placed it in the middle of a morning, unscheduled, as routine, so that the man at the other end would have no warning of it. Duarte had answered everything. Then Duarte had asked a question of his own. *Anything on your end I should know about?* Calloway had listened to that for what it cost the man to ask, and it had cost him nothing visible, and Calloway had carried that out of the call as the detail worth keeping. His own answer had been that there was nothing that needed the Commissioner's office yet.

He asked himself now what the same question would cost a man with nothing on his end. It would cost him nothing.

He took down Threnody's account, which he had once given Larsen in copy, and found the hairline where he had left it. It was the carriage on one leg, billed in the cycle before the authorisation and matched in the cycle after. He had said that it resolved too cleanly to be a mistake. It had not been a mistake. There had never been anything in it but the order in which two papers were filed.

He wrote to everyone on Larsen's circulation, in one text.

*Attached: a freight authorisation of the late autumn of 2826, requested and countersigned by the Commissioner's office, Halcyon, and signed by this office. It reassigns one leg of Threnody Logistics' route to Halcyon's stores. This office has held it in a file beside Eden's referral since that season, and sent it neither to the task force nor to Halcyon. It names no consignment. Whether it is the leg on Eden's form is for Halcyon's stores to say, from their ledger.*

He read it for a conclusion dressed as a fact, which was how he read everything of his own. There was none. There was one sentence that was an admission, and he left it in.

The note he put back in the file, with the date under it and a line saying where the authorisation had gone. He did not strike anything. It had been accurate on the day he wrote it. It had only never been sent to anyone who could answer it.

:::pov larsen

Duarte's office answered in a day. It always had. For two years Larsen had had a word for the way that office answered, and the word was *helpfully*, and he had not meant it kindly.

It was one leaf. *Halcyon's stores ledger holds eleven crates of ration from Eden, received in the autumn of 2826 off Threnody Logistics, on the leg set out in the authorisation that the Chief Commissioner's office has now circulated. The entry has stood since the day. Send the form and the stores will strike it. They would have struck it on any day it was shown.* It was signed by the Commissioner himself.

Larsen read the last sentence twice, looking for the edge on it. He did not find one. It was a statement about a stores counter.

He set the leaf beside the authorisation, which had come across from Calloway the afternoon before with five lines he had not expected and had read standing. Then he set his own sheet beside those, the one with the digit circled twice. There was a code that had been told it no longer applied, and the paper that had told it, and a ledger that had held the crates all along. They had been in three offices, and each office had been careful with its own.

Eden's office answered the day after, about the other form. Eden's stores held six cases of lamp tube from Antariksha. They had been signed in during the summer, on a leg Commissioner Wayland's office had asked for itself. Her note said that the stores had not known anyone was waiting on a stamp. A habitat is shown the freight that arrives, she wrote, and not the form it left on.

So Eden had been owed a stamp since 2826, and since the summer it had owed one, and it had known neither.

He sent both originals out on the Thirdday tugs: Antariksha's to Eden's stores, and Eden's to Halcyon's. With Eden's he put a covering leaf for the Inspector who had handed it to him. He wrote it twice and sent the shorter.

He did not ask for either form to come back through his office. A form goes home from the place that finishes it.

The file went back into the bottom drawer without the form in it. He wrote on its cover where the form had gone, and the date.
