:::pov barsik

The form came back by the front desk.

Barsik saw it from the top of the records cabinet, which is where he sees everything. He knew what it was before it was out of its envelope. The envelope had Halcyon's seal on it, and he has read a good deal of Halcyon's paperwork, upside down.

It had gone out by the side door. That was nearly two years ago, in a Commander's hand, with a thermos. The side door is for things that are leaving. The front desk is for visitors and for the post, and this was post. It was addressed to Eden's stores, care of the bureau, because the bureau had sent the form out and it was coming home the way it had gone.

It went into the post tray on the Senior Sergeant's desk. The trainee was in the post tray. That was her exercise for the week: sit in the tray, and count how much of the post gets read on the day it comes. Nobody looks twice at a rabbit in a tray. The envelope was laid half across her, and she stayed where she was.

The Inspector opened it standing up, the way he does everything. Out came a covering leaf and one sheet. He put the sheet down flat on the desk while he read the leaf, and so Barsik read the sheet.

Upside down, you read slowly.

Eleven crates. He remembered them. Four stamps, each one saying *this passed through here*. He remembered those too. And where the fifth belonged, where there had been nothing to read, there was a stamp.

*Halcyon stores. Received.* The date was in the autumn of 2826 and had been written in by hand, because the stamp had been struck this month and the crates had not come this month. Beside it were three lines in a small upright hand, and under them a name he knew from memos.

*Struck late, at the task force's written asking, against the stores ledger, which has held this entry since the day. A. Duarte, Commissioner, Halcyon.*

So the crates had been in Halcyon's stores the whole time. Somebody there had signed for them and written it in a book, on the day, in the ordinary way. The book was Halcyon's and the form was Eden's, and in nearly two years nobody had put one on top of the other.

Barsik thought about his own note. On the first day he had written that he did not know where the fifth stamp had gone. That is a different note from writing nothing, and it had been a good note. He saw now that it had been a little wrong as well. The stamp had not gone anywhere. A stamp lives in a drawer at the place it belongs to, and waits for the form to come. It was the form that had gone.

The agency does not rub a note out. He put a second under the first. *Known now. The stamp was at Halcyon. The form was not.*

Then he thought about the form, which he had watched for six days when it was new. For six days it had been boring, and nobody read it. Then it had been interesting, and for nearly two years everybody who read it had read something else. Now it was boring again, a form that wanted a stamp, and it had been finished in a week.

The Inspector said a word. He picked up the sheet and the leaf and went out by the front.

When he had gone, the screen on his terminal was still showing four stamps. Nobody had told it anything. Barsik noted that as well.

:::pov albercombe

The covering leaf was in the Commander's own hand, and it was short.

*Inspector. This is Eden's form, finished by Halcyon's stores. You handed it to me in the late autumn of 2826 and it has been in my cabinet since. There is no offence in it and there never was one. Neither stores is short. I took a form for evidence, and did not ask the one counter that could have stamped it. I asked this month. The close will come to your Superintendent in the ordinary way. Larsen.*

Albercombe read it, and then the form, and then the date written into the fifth stamp.

He had handed that form out of the side door so that it would stop being his. He could remember the relief of it. For nearly two years it had not been his, and he had not missed it once. He had told the Commander that day that he would have closed it as spoilage, except that spoilage does not route itself through four transfer stamps and come back missing a fifth. He had been rather pleased with the sentence. He had been right that it was not spoilage. It now appeared that the crates had been on a shelf on Halcyon, signed for, before he had ever read that they were missing.

He looked for his two words. He had a form of words for a thing that began on another habitat, and he had used it for years with a clear conscience: it was *not Eden's*, and it went upstairs. He could not find anywhere to put them. The form was Eden's. The line it would close was in Eden's stores ledger. Nothing in the whole matter had belonged to anyone but Eden and Halcyon, and each of them had had its own half written down correctly from the start.

He said the word he says about once a week.

The envelope was addressed to the stores, care of him, and a thing sent care of a man is his until he hands it over. He took it down himself. He told the Senior Sergeant where he was going, and then he told the lift.

The keeper of Eden's stores found the folio without being given the date. The line was there, in a hand that was not hers. *Eleven crates, ration. Out. Referred.* It had been brought forward at every stocktake since. She copied the stamp's particulars against it and ruled it off.

"Nearly two years I've carried that," she said. "Referred. I never knew who to."

"To me," Albercombe said. "And then to somebody else."

There was a second form on the counter. It was printed in several scripts down one side, and her stamp was out beside it with the pad open.

"That's not ours," he said, before he could stop himself.

"The form isn't. The stamp is." She turned it round for him. "Antariksha's. Six cases of lamp tube. They've been on my shelves since the summer, and I signed them in myself. It came over with a note from the Commissioner's office telling me to strike it." She looked at the clean place on the paper. "Nobody ever said they wanted one."

"They'd not have known who to say it to."

She inked the stamp and struck the form. She wrote the summer's date into it by hand, and initialled the date.

He stood at the counter with one form that had come home and one that was about to leave. He could not have said of either that it was not Eden's.

He wrote the disposition there, on the slate, against the counter. *Closed. No discrepancy. Eleven crates signed in at Halcyon's stores, autumn 2826. Stamp struck late.* It was the third thing in a year that Eden's bureau had closed with nobody to blame. He had resented the first two on principle. He looked for the principle now and found that he had left it upstairs.

He went back up. The side door was shut, as it usually was, and he came in at the front.

:::pov larsen

Antariksha's bureau wrote in the third week, in his language and four of its own, to say that the form was home. Its last sentence asked whether forms of that kind should come up to him in future.

He answered the same day.

*No. I did nothing in this that needed the Governor's authority. I wrote to five Commissioners. Your own Commissioner's office can write to the other four, through the liaison line the Chief Commissioner keeps, and whichever stores hold the receipt can strike the stamp. If an answer ever comes back that is something other than a stamp, send that up.*

Then he wrote the close. It was the shortest he had written, and it did not split. There was no half to send down to either bureau.

*Eden's referral of the late autumn of 2826. Eleven crates of ration, signed out of Eden's stores, were signed in at Halcyon's, on a leg reassigned under an authorisation the Chief Commissioner's office had signed. Neither ledger is short. There is no offence in the matter and nobody is charged. Why the leg was reassigned is on neither paper. The task force has not asked, since nothing in the file requires it.*

*The task force read a missing stamp as somebody's doing and went to Halcyon to find whose. It did not show the form to Halcyon's stores. It held the form for two years, less some weeks. The stamp was struck by Halcyon's stores in the week they were asked.*

The seam went to the Governor, as the seam always did, in one paragraph. *A habitat is not shown a leg reassigned after its own dock, and is not told that another habitat's form is waiting on its stamp. Neither end has an office to ask. On two forms this month the task force did the asking. I do not think the office is this one.*

Under that was the place for a recommendation. He left it empty. What the Compact's screens should show a habitat was the Compact's business.

He read the close once for anything in it that he thought, and found only things he had done and one thing he had not.

He had resigned a commission over cases that died quietly at a wall, where nobody had the standing to ask across it. He had had the standing for four years. This case had not been near a wall. It had been in his own bottom drawer. He had been taught on a fold-side watch that the remarks line is where a report stops being evidence, and he had kept his own clean for a career. In two years he had not written one word about Commissioner Duarte. He had held the opinion all the same, and had put it in a drawer with a form on top of it.

He looked at the quarter's sheet before he put it away. The old line would take a word at the winter close, and for the first time it would not be *carried*. Further down was the other one, a sealed cradle that had arrived heavier than it left, under his name, with no word against it. He left that where it was. He had asked everything there was to ask about that one, of everybody, in the open, and it was still there.

Behind him the raven had had nothing to count all month. By her own file they were clear days.

:::pov barsik

The trainee's log had a line in it that evening which he had not set.

*A form came back today. It had been to Halcyon. It has one more stamp than when it left. I did not know they came back.*

Barsik read it twice.

When the form went out by the side door, he had thought about telling her to watch it. *This is what a report does after it is filed.* He had decided against it, and had put the reason in his own log and not in hers: a thing like that is better found than told. He had meant to find her another one sooner. Eden had sent a great many forms across the floor since, and they had done what forms mostly do, which is nothing that anybody can see.

This was the one. It had taken nearly two years, and she had found it by being sat on by an envelope.

He wrote under her line, because she would read it. *Most do not. This one went to Halcyon twice. The first time, nobody showed it to anyone.*

Then he went back to the cabinet top, where he can see the screen and the door at the same time. There was a queue to read. On Eden there is always something boring in it, and he read it slowly.
