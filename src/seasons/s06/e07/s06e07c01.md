::: pov wender

The inquiry closed on a Friday. It was not the day anybody had asked for, and Wender, who had once been unable to give a tired man a Tuesday, noted that a Friday would do.

The liaison brought the closing report himself. He had been on the station twenty-one days. He did not sit, and she did not ask him to, because a man who has finally been allowed to finish something should not be made to settle into a chair first.

She read it twice.

The first reading was for content, and the content was a little over two hundred leaves of what the Corps had done at Dock Seven up to the moment of closure. The Field Triumvirate had been convened by the clause, each condition made to stand on its own, and the Corps had acted within its charter. That took sixty leaves and she could find nothing in them to correct.

The first claim took nine. The report quoted the survey flag whole, all four lines, the grounds included. It found the grounds as stated sufficient for the flag. It found nothing on the record showing an interest acted upon, and nothing showing there was none, and it said both in the same sentence and gave neither the last word. It recorded that the submission had not been withdrawn and that the Corps had declined to answer it. *The claim is not upheld.* It did not say the claim was answered. It did not say what the officer had known. She read the nine leaves looking for the place where the inquiry had done her answering for her, and the inquiry had not.

Then the schedule of personnel. All present survived, name by name. One line stood apart at the foot.

*Line Captain T. Shepherd. Not recovered. Position logged at the failure point at the moment of closure. Status: not within the terms of reference. Remitted to the officer's command.*

And on the last leaf, alone: *Matters outstanding within the terms of reference: none.*

There was no line for her. She turned the leaf over to be sure. A closing report was not countersigned by the institution it reported on, which was the point of one, and she had known that for nineteen years and had still come to this morning with a refusal prepared. Nobody had asked her for anything.

The second reading was for what the content was for, and it was shorter than she expected. The report was for ending an inquiry. It ended one. It had been asked what the Corps did up to 0549 station on the day, and it had answered that, and where the question ran out it had stopped and written down that it was stopping.

The draft citation lay at the left of her desk, where it had lain for forty-four days. She had spent most of them assuming the two documents were the same document at different lengths. They were not. One of them knew where it ended.

"Remitted to her command," she said.

"That is you."

"I know who it is."

"It was not in the terms." He said it carefully, as a man does who has checked. "I could not find a reading of the terms that put it there, and I looked for one. I read your analyst's filing last night. The whole of it."

Wender waited.

"It begins after 0549," the liaison said. "I was sent to ask what the Corps did up to the moment of closure. I was not sent to ask what a dock is doing now. If I had put it in, somebody in the room would have taken it out, and they would have been right to." He looked at the window, where it was raining on the water, as it had been for most of the twenty-one days. "It is a long filing. It is flagged as interpretation."

"By its author," Wender said.

"I noticed that."

It was as far as he could go and he had gone exactly that far. She recognised the manner of it. She had commanded an officer for years who stated what she could support and stopped, and she had not expected to meet the habit in a tired man from the Council with a cutter to catch.

"The citation is the room's, not the inquiry's," he said. "The room will ask you again. I would only note that the report does not."

"Noted."

He went out on the noon cutter. He would sleep in his own bed, and in four days he would have a Tuesday there with nothing in it.

What was left on the desk was what had been there for forty-four days, and one line more. Until this morning she had been keeping a file open that the Council wanted shut. The Council's own inquiry had now written down whose it was to keep. Nothing about the file was different. She noticed that she had been braced, all that time, against a push, and that the push had just been withdrawn in writing, and that the weight had not gone anywhere.

So it had not been the push.

**Field Analyst Rook-7. Log open. The Oversight Council's inquiry into Dock Seven closed at 0900 station. Closing report received: public, not addressed to this desk. Logging it. The practice is established.**

The report ran to two hundred and fourteen leaves and its last timestamp was 0549 station on the day of the closure. Rook-7's field log for Dock Seven held four thousand and ninety entries dated after 0549. The report cited none of them.

Rook-7 examined this for some time, because it had expected to log an omission and wished to log the correct thing. It was not an omission. The report stated its terms on the second leaf and kept to them on every leaf after. A document that says where it stops has not left out what comes later. Rook-7 had read a great many documents that stopped early. It had read very few that said so.

**Assessment: the closing report is consistent with the instrument record for the whole of the period it covers. Rook-7 has not previously been able to file that sentence about a document concerning Dock Seven. Filing it.**

A second item, administrative. Rook-7's assessment of the day, flagged as interpretation, refiled ten days ago: the read count has changed. Reader, the Council's liaison, 2214 to 0031 station last night. He returned it to the queue without annotation and in the order he found it.

**Note. Two hours and seventeen minutes is longer than the filing takes to read. Rook-7 has no instrument for what a reader makes of a thing. Logging the duration.**

Nobody had asked Rook-7 to prepare anything for the closing, and it had prepared nothing. But a closing was a natural point for an audit, including somebody else's closing, and so in the night it had run its own log for Dock Seven from the nineteenth day to the sixty-third, end to end, which it had not done before. A log read one entry at a time is a series of findings. Read whole, it acquires a shape whether or not the analyst wants one.

The signal at the failure point still phrased. It repeated and varied and returned, as it had from the first, and the variation went all the way through it: the interval, the amplitude, the order in which it came back to itself. Rook-7 had nine weeks of that and had stopped expecting the audit to show anything else.

One element did not vary.

It was small. It sat inside every iteration the arrays held, back to the first one they held, and it was the same in each. Everything around it moved and it did not.

**Flag: invariant. Confidence low. An unchanging element in a stalled process is either the thing the process is holding or an artefact of the arrays that read it, and Rook-7 has no instrument that tells those apart. The arrays are non-standard. Their origin has been flagged twice and asked about zero times.**

Rook-7 did not send the flag. On the nineteenth day it had sent the Archive a phrasing pattern on less, and it noted the difference. Then it had recognised a shape it had met before, in telemetry an officer had refused to file as noise. Now it had a thing it had never met, and one set of arrays to have met it with.

**Note. The report lists matters outstanding within its terms: none. Rook-7 does not dispute the list. Rook-7's own list, which is not within anybody's terms, is one item longer than it was yesterday.**

**Log held open.**

Inquiry documents were copied to the Survey Archive for filing, the last of them included, and the reconciling desk did with the closing report what it did with everything.

Cael went first. *Triumvirate log: cited correctly, thirty-eight times. Survey flag: quoted in full. Four lines.* Cael did not remark on the four. Wyn did, to nobody in particular: Cael had counted them. *Schedule of personnel agrees with the station's. Terms of reference end at 0549 station on the day. Every finding in the report is inside them. Checked.* The pause Sen had learned to wait through. *It states where it stops. I have nothing.*

*Who is it for?* Wyn said, and then, which was unusual, answered. *It is for a man who has been twenty-one days on somebody else's station. It is for nine hundred people who have had an inquiry living with them since the autumn. It is for a room that asked a question and was owed the end of it. They were owed. I do not grudge any of them.*

*But,* Sen said.

*The last leaf. Matters outstanding, none. That leaf goes in our binders. In ten years somebody opens the Dock Seven binders at the back and reads the word none, and does not read the four words after it, because nobody reads the qualifier. We have sixteen years of people not reading the qualifier. It is most of the shelf.*

Sen fronted, and held the desk, and thought about the first file the desk had ever reconciled.

Incident Report 001 had been closed on a Day 47, in a late summer sixteen years gone, by a manager's note. Two days later a maintenance technician had logged the Dock Seven chronometers forty seconds apart again, on a night rotation, with the recalibration still warm. The closure had been two days older than the instrument that contradicted it. Most of what the desk had written since was that same note in other hands: *this was closed; here is the log from after.* The desk had been built to stand behind closures and read what came later.

Here was a closure, and here was a log from after. Sen asked the station registry, because the registry was the station's own and the desk did not guess. *Field Analyst's log, Dock Seven. Status: held open. Latest entry, 1140 station, this date.* The registry gave a status and a time. It did not give the contents, and the desk did not ask for what it had not been sent.

*It is not a discrepancy,* Cael said. *The report does not say the incident is closed.*

*No,* Sen said. *And the note will not say the report is wrong.*

Ten days ago the desk had declined a note it could have written, because a reading was not a gap. This was the other case, and all three of them knew it for the plain one: a document, the station's own instruments, and a date on each. Sen wrote it at the foot of the binder copy. Cael's half first.

*Inquiry closed, this date. Its terms of reference end at 0549 station on the day of the closure at Dock Seven. Station registry, this date: Field Analyst's log, Dock Seven, held open; latest entry 1140 station.*

Then Wyn's, in the same hand.

*The report remits one officer's status to her command, and records one claim against her as neither withdrawn nor answered. Neither is within the terms. Both remain on this shelf, under the one name.*

Under that, the line for the archivist's name. Sen's hand stopped where it always stopped. Nobody said anything about it, that day.

The index of the Dock Seven reconciliation binders had three columns: the document, the date filed, the date closed. Sen entered the inquiry on its own line and wrote today's date in the third column. Sixteen years of notes had gone under dates in that column. This was the first of the dates the desk believed.

The line above it was the incident's own. Opened, Mid-Autumn. The third column was empty, and nobody at the desk had left it so.

Sen closed the binder. That was a different thing, and the index had no column for it.

::: pov tissadelle

She had stopped trusting the count of mornings, so she had started a different one.

*State what comes the same.*

It was the old diagnostic turned over. She had asked of each morning whether it resolved, and none did, and she had the finding and stood behind it. This asked less. It asked only whether anything on the hill arrived twice alike.

Nearly nothing did. Her mother's first words did not; she had those in more versions than she could order. The Hollow did not, since the hill still offered it at two distances from the house and would not be argued down to one. The flock was below the house, or on the high field, or once nowhere, with the gate standing open and no explanation in it. The third course of the wall had given under her hand on every morning but one, and on that one somebody had reset it, which nobody had ever done.

She kept the list anyway. A list of failures was still a record, and it was the only kind she was in a position to keep.

Then, on a morning she could not have numbered, her mother called something after her up the slope, the way a person calls after a child who is already through the gate and has heard it before.

"Leave the water clear. Don't cut the pale wood. Don't linger past dusk."

Tissadelle stopped on the path.

It was not the saying of it. Her mother had said it all her life. It was that she knew, before she had turned round, where the voice would drop: a little, on *dusk*, as though the last one were the one that mattered and the first two were manners. She had known it the way she knew the chassis weight of a hull she had served in.

She went back through the mornings she held. It took a long time, or it took none; she had stopped being able to say which. The three lines were in every one. Same words. Same order. The same drop at the end. On the morning her mother said *you've been gone too long*, they came afterward, over the kettle. On the morning her mother said nothing at all, Tissadelle found she had said them herself, under her breath at the turn of the path, as she had at six. The hill changed who said it. It did not change what was said.

She checked it twice. The second answer did not differ.

She did not know why that, of everything. She could think of four reasons and could support none of them, and she did not write an unsupported reason beside a finding. It occurred to her that this left her keeping the thing with no reason attached, that there were six generations of precedent for exactly that, and that her mother would have had a remark ready about how long it had taken her.

*A real hill has one of everything.* She had said it on the first morning, as a charge against the place. This hill had one of one thing. It was a very short inventory. She put it at the head of the count, above the failures, because a count has to begin somewhere that holds.

At the edge of the clearing the air was warm, as it had been. She did not carry the finding over to it. Nothing there had asked, and a count is kept, not presented.

The grey was thinning in the east. Nobody had said the three lines yet this morning, so she said them at the wall, once, in order, and went down to see which morning it was.
