::: pov wender

She sent the account at 0610 station, in her own hand, and then did the thing the order she had given left her to do, which was the paperwork.

The Council would have the account within the week. It would go with the reconciling desk's note under it, because that was what a copy for the binders carried and because she wanted it there. A desk that had read her account against the station's own instruments had said, by what it found and what it did not, more than a High Captain could say in a page, and the Council knew the desk's hand even if it had never traced the hand to a name.

She knew what the note would look like, because she had read a hundred of them. Two things set side by side, and the cost of the gap between them, in a hand that did not vary, over a line left blank. She had never once minded the blank. A note nobody had signed was a note nobody could be ordered to withdraw, and she had spent four years building a doctrine out of officers who stated their grounds and said nothing further; the blank line was the same discipline in a different corps, and she had not asked for it, any more than she had asked Shepherd about item sixteen.

She would collect the copy at the end of the shift. She did not expect it to have changed.

::::: scene 2

::: pov sen

The account went to the warm edge at 0610 station, in the High Captain's hand and nobody else's, and what came back to the reconciling desk was the copy for the binders, which was the only part of it that had ever been the desk's.

A copy for the binders took a note at the foot. That was the form. The form had a line under the note for the reconciling archivist's name, and the line had been blank on every note the desk had filed since the desk was Sen's, and nobody had ever asked about it. A form could carry an empty line for as long as a desk cared to leave one, provided the line above it was true.

Cael wrote the first half, in the sense that Cael dictated it and Sen's hand kept up.

*The account states what the shelf holds and nothing the shelf does not. Verified, item by item. The signal at Dock Seven carries three lines the Archive does not hold. The absence was checked twice, last night. The second answer did not change. End.*

*That is not the end,* Wyn said.

*It is the end of what the shelf holds.*

*It is the end of what the shelf holds and the start of what the holding cost.* Wyn did not hurry. Wyn never hurried, which was the thing people who had only ever heard Cael took for Cael. *Three lines a family said to its children for six generations and never wrote down. Nobody in this Archive asked for them, nobody at the Union register wanted them, and the one person who could have brought them to this station kept them, as she was raised to, and carried them to Dock Seven, and the station wrote her down as a loss and kept writing it for a season while the lines came back in every iteration. That is what it cost. It is on the file. The inquiry has the holding, the register has the six generations, the arrays have the lines. I am not asking for a word that is not on the shelf. I am asking for the ones that are to be put next to each other.*

Sen wrote them. That was the desk's other half, and it had been Wyn's half for as long as there had been a desk. The note said what the shelf held, and under it, in the same hand, what the holding had cost and who had paid it, and nothing a reader could not turn a page and find.

Then Sen's hand reached the line, and stopped where it had always stopped.

*Sign it,* Wyn said.

Wyn had said it before, in the way a person says a thing they expect to say again.

*The note is true,* Sen said. *It is true unsigned.*

*Everything this desk writes is true unsigned. That is why nobody has ever had to do anything about any of it.* The long sentence came, the one Wyn built when the short ones had done their work. *A note with no name under it gets read or it gets ignored and either way the Council goes home, and a note with a name under it has to be answered — somebody has to write back to this desk and say what they are going to do about three lines a Line Captain carried for them and none of them ever asked her for. I want them to have to write back.*

*You want the desk to make a claim.*

*I want the desk to be answerable for what it found. Those are not the same thing, and you know they are not.*

Sen did know. The desk read. It did not argue. An unsigned note at the foot of a document was a gap set beside a record; a signed one was an archivist standing in the gap, and from then on every note the desk filed would be read for the archivist and not for the gap. That was the reason Sen gave, and it was a true one, and the desk had been built on it. There was another reason under it, the way there was a question under every question Cael asked, and Sen did not say it and was not asked, which was how the desk did most things.

*A name moves no standing,* Cael said, to both of them. *Signed or unsigned, the note holds what it holds. I have no view.*

*You have a view,* Wyn said. *You want the line left because a blank line is tidy.*

*I want the line left because a name is not evidence.*

*Nobody said it was.*

Sen fronted, and held the desk, because somebody closed the binder at the end of the shift and that somebody was Sen, the way the timesheet was Sen's: a job the other two had handed over, not a ruling they had lost. Sen filed the note as it stood. Both halves, no name.

*I will want it tomorrow,* Wyn said.

*I know.*

*And the day after.*

*Yes.*

*And you will say the same thing.*

*Probably,* Sen said.

The High Captain came for the binder copy at the end of the shift. She read the note once for content and once for what it was for, and her eye went to the blank line and stayed there for the length of a breath, and she did not ask. The Council's draft citation sat in the same binders. It had a line at the foot too, and her name was not on it, and Sen did not suppose the two blanks were the same blank, only that she would know what one looked like.

She took the copy and went. The light had gone some time before; it was Deep Winter and the station kept the station's hours. Sen wrote the date in the index. The three of them went home by the one door, in the one scarf, and left the matter where it would be in the morning, which was on the desk. Wyn had said as much.
