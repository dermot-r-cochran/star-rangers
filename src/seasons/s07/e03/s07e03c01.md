::: pov sen

Rook-7 came to the reconciling desk in the late winter with a request the desk had not had in sixteen years, which was to be reconciled.

It did not sit. It set two things on the desk, one at each end, with some care about the distance between them.

The first was a single leaf from the station registry. *Rook-7. Field Analyst, Threshold Station Constabulary. Chassis: civilian, investigative and analytical. Sensor suite: standard.* A date of entry in the early autumn of 2826, and nothing since.

The second was a log. It was not an extract. It was the whole of the analyst's log for its service on the station, five years and some months of it, continuous, in the plain container the constabulary used for evidence.

"The desk reads what a station has filed against what the station's instruments have logged," Rook-7 said. "This is what the station has filed about Rook-7. This is what the instrument has logged. Rook-7 is aware that it is both. It requests the reading anyway."

*It has brought us itself,* Wyn said. *In two pieces. So we can see whether they match.*

*They will not match,* Cael said. *It knows they will not. That is not what it came for.*

Sen waited, because a visitor who has put down two things and kept standing has usually brought a third.

"There is a narrower request," Rook-7 said. "The desk stated an assumption in the deep winter. That the arrays are reading. The boundary desk has it at the head of a column and Rook-7 fills the column each shift. Rook-7 has identified one condition under which the assumption is false, and it is a condition that can be looked for. It has looked. It does not accept its own result."

"State the condition," Sen said.

"The arrays do not carry words. They carry phrasing. The invariant element is a pattern, and the characters the desk read in the deep winter are a rendering of the pattern, and the rendering is done by this analyst. A renderer fits symbols to a pattern. A renderer that already holds a string is well placed to find that string in a pattern, whether or not it is there." Rook-7 did not pause. It had plainly said this to itself a number of times. "Rook-7 read the fleet bulletin in the early autumn of last year. Item sixteen is in the bulletin, with its coordinate and its marker. Two of the three parts of the invariant are things the renderer had already read."

*And the third,* Cael said, to nobody outside.

"The three lines are the third part. Rook-7 has searched its own log for them, in every rendering it can construct, from the first entry to the first iteration the arrays hold. It found nothing. But the search was run by the analyst, on the analyst's log, to find out whether the analyst had spoiled its own reading. Rook-7 would not accept that from a witness. It is asking for somebody else to look."

*It wants a second reader,* Wyn said. *It has been the only one for a whole season, and it has just worked out what that is worth.*

*It wants the log read against the shelf,* Cael said. *That is a thing we do.* Cael was already reading the container's index, and had been since it was set down. *Five years. Continuous. It will take four days to do properly, and five to do twice.*

"Five days," Sen said. "The desk will not give you a result before it has checked it."

"Rook-7 would have been concerned by a shorter figure," Rook-7 said, and went back to its shift.

::::: scene 2

::: pov rook

**Field Analyst Rook-7. Log open. Fifth day. Summoned to the Survey Archive, 1610 station. The reconciling desk has had this log for five days. Rook-7 notes that it is now logging into a record somebody else has read to the end, which has not happened before. It has adjusted nothing. It logs that it considered whether to.**

The Senior Archivist had the registry leaf at one end of the desk and the log's index at the other, as Rook-7 had left them. Between them was a single sheet in the desk's hand. The Senior Archivist did not offer it. The Senior Archivist read from it, in order, one item at a time, which Rook-7 had heard the desk do once before and had not forgotten.

"First. The fleet bulletin of the early autumn. Your log shows it received and read, entire, some weeks before Dock Seven. Item sixteen is in it, coordinate and marker both. Verified against the bulletin on our shelf. So the renderer held both before the signal existed."

"Yes."

"So for those two parts, the desk cannot tell a signal that carries them from a renderer that supplied them. The rendering would look the same in either case. In the deep winter the desk said those two were verified against the bulletin and the flag. That stands. It was a statement that the rendering matches the record. It was never a statement about how the match came to be there, and the desk should not have let it sound like one."

**Logged. The desk has revised the reach of one of its own sentences without being asked to. Rook-7 notes that it came here to be checked and has so far watched the checker check itself.**

"Second. The three lines. The desk searched your log for them from the first entry to the first iteration the arrays hold. In order, out of order, in part, in the four renderings you listed and in three you did not. They are not there. The desk then did it again from the other end. The second answer did not change."

Rook-7 examined that. It was the result it had reached itself. It observed that the result was not the same object now that a second party had reached it, and that it had no means of measuring the difference, and that the difference was the thing it had come for.

"Third," the Senior Archivist said, and here the voice altered very slightly, toward something drier. Rook-7 had learned over five years to tell which of the desk was speaking and had never been told it was meant to. "That is as far as a log can be checked. The log is yours. It is continuous by your own account. The desk has read it for breaks and found none, and that is a statement about the log's surface. If there is an hour the log does not hold, the log would not show it. The desk can say the lines are absent from what it was given. It cannot say they are absent from the analyst."

"Agreed."

"Fourth. The arrays." The Senior Archivist put one finger on the registry leaf. "The registry says standard suite. The log is full of readings no standard suite takes. The registry holds no fitting record. So the desk has an instrument with nothing on file about what it was built to read, and nothing about what it held when it was fitted. Whether it came to this station holding anything cannot be looked for from here. The desk marks that and does not guess across it."

**Observation. The desk has reached the arrays' origin by its own route, stated that it is absent from the record, and stopped. It has not asked Rook-7. Count of times the question has been put to this analyst: zero, unchanged. Rook-7 assesses that the desk has not asked because Rook-7 brought everything it holds, and the answer is not among it, and the desk can see that. This is the first time the question has gone unasked for a reason.**

"So," Rook-7 said. "One condition named. Looked for by a second reader. Not found, for one part in three, as far as the log reaches. Not distinguishable for the other two. Not reachable for the arrays."

"Yes."

"Then the heading."

"The heading stands as it is," the Senior Archivist said. "*Under the assumption that the arrays are reading.* The desk considered whether five days had earned it a better word. They have not. One rival was tried and did not turn up. That is worth having. It is one rival, tried against one part, on a log the desk could read but did not keep. If you want it reworded, say what you want it to say, and the desk will tell you whether the shelf holds it."

Rook-7 examined the offer. It took longer than it expected.

It had brought the log here on the proposition that it wanted a check. It found, on inspection, that it had also carried in a second thing, unlogged, which was a preference about how the check would come out. It had wanted the heading to be able to say more. Nothing in five years of its own entries had flagged this. It had been using the word *check* partly so that it need not use the other one.

"Rook-7 does not want it reworded," it said. "The heading is correct. Rook-7 came to find out whether it was still correct. That is a different errand from wanting it changed. It notes that it had not separated the two before it arrived."

"Most people have not," said the Senior Archivist, in the third voice, the one Rook-7 heard least. "It is why we take five days."

**Note. There is one item the desk did not read out. Rook-7 rendered the invariant in the ninth week and logged it as a short string, a shorter one and three short clauses. It did not identify the first string as item sixteen. The desk did, in the deep winter, in less than a second. An analyst that had supplied the string from its own holdings might be expected to know what it had supplied. Rook-7 offers this at low confidence. It is evidence about the renderer, given by the renderer, in the renderer's favour. It is entering it here, where the desk has shown it will read, and is not raising it aloud.**

::::: scene 3

::: pov sen

The sheet was a working sheet and went nowhere. The registry leaf was a different matter.

*That one is ours,* Cael said, when Rook-7 had gone back down to the thin water with its container. *A filed entry and an instrument record that disagree, and a date on each. It is the plainest gap that has come across this desk since the autumn. Nobody sent it to us. Nobody would have.*

Sen wrote it at the foot of the Archive's copy. Cael's half first.

*Registry, early autumn 2826: sensor suite, standard. The analyst's own log, same date to this: signatures no standard suite reads, in continuous use. The registry holds no fitting record for the arrays, no maker and no specification. Checked twice.*

*And who paid,* Wyn said.

*Say what is on the file.*

*It is all on the file. It is five years of file.* Wyn was unhurried. *Every reading this station has had from that analyst rests on an entry that does not describe it. Dock Seven is only the latest. It has known that the whole time. It flagged the entry twice, in its own queue, and the registry has no record that anyone answered, because a flag in your own queue is addressed to no one. So it has stood behind each reading alone, and written the doubt into the reading every time, and this week it carried its entire log up from the docks and handed it to another desk so that somebody else could look for the fault. That is the cost. Nobody charged it. It paid anyway.*

Sen wrote that under the first half, in the same hand, cut down to what a reader could turn a page and find: that the analyst had flagged the entry twice, that the registry recorded no answer, and that the station had relied on the readings throughout.

Then the line at the foot.

*This one would do something,* Wyn said. It was not the long sentence. It was quieter than that. *I am not asking for the principle today. A signed note on a registry entry goes back to the registrar. Somebody has to open a file and write down what is on that chassis and who put it there. It has waited five years for one person to ask. We could make them ask.*

*We could make them answer us,* Sen said. *That is not the same as it being asked. And I do not know what the answer is, or who would be opened up by it. Nor do you. The desk has just spent five days telling an honest instrument it will not guess across a gap. I am not going to send a guess to the registrar with my name on it and call it a note.*

*A name moves no standing,* Cael said. *The gap is marked. It was not marked on Monday.*

*It has been a gap for five years,* Wyn said. *Marked is not much.*

*No,* Sen said. *It is where the desk can stop and still be telling the truth.*

Wyn let it go, as Wyn did, without yielding it.

Sen filed the note as it stood. The heading at the top of a column on another desk stayed word for word what it had been. On paper the five days had moved nothing. Sen did not think they had been wasted. Until this week the only reader that sentence had been tested by was the one who wrote it.

The light had gone. Sen wrote the date in the index and the three of them went home.
