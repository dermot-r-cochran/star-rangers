The notice arrived on a Tuesday. Wender noted it, as she had noted the other one, and found nothing in it this time either.

It was a form. The Oversight Council reviewed its open incident files once a year, and a file that had been open a year came round whether or not anyone had thought about it since, which was the purpose of having a form. *Dock Seven. Status: open. Holding officer: High Captain K. Wender.* Two lines for her to fill. *Grounds for continued holding.* *Anticipated date of closure.*

Nobody had written it at her. That was the difference a year made. The citation had been written at her, and the submission had been written at an officer who was not present to mind, and each had cost what it cost and she had paid it. This was only the Council's calendar doing what calendars did. She found she preferred it. A form with a line on it was an honest instrument. It asked for a date because it had a place to put one.

Under the notice was the boundary desk's audit, which had come up at midday, and she read that first.

Forty-one lines. She went to nineteen, because Syra had set it on top. A Cadet's finding, in a level hand: grounds stated, *use: none shown*, recommended struck by the desk's own test. Under it the Analyst's decision that the line stayed, and the Analyst's name.

She read it once for content and once for what it was for.

The content was that a reading had been taken at the failure point every shift for a year, a thousand times and more, and that no order of hers or anyone's had ever required it. She had assumed one existed. She had read the sheet every day it had come up to her and had not once asked on whose authority it was kept, because it had not occurred to her that the answer might be *nobody's*. Syra had written a line on a sheet the morning after the account went, with nothing beside it, and had then taken the reading, or seen it taken, on every shift since. And in the spring she had come up to this office to report that she had acquired a preference about the result, in the tone she would have used for a fault in a gauge.

What it was for was plainer. It was a Cadet telling his High Captain, through the proper channel, that the watch on Dock Seven had no demonstrated use. He was right. Wender checked it the way she checked anything she expected to dislike, looking for the place where it stopped being right, and there was no such place.

She had asked one direct question in a career built on not asking. *Let her tell us what she wants, and let that be the order.* It had gone at 0610 in the deep winter, in her own hand, and a year of shifts had come back with the same entry.

There were two things a commander could do with a year of that, and she had been offered both, by people who meant well. She could send again. The channel had carried one account; it might carry a second, shorter and kinder, that asked whether the first had been received. She had drafted that message more than once and had never finished a draft, because every version of it said the same thing under its words, which was *answer me*, and a question asked twice was not a question any longer. Or she could read the silence. A year without an order was surely itself an order; that had been put to her more than once, and not unkindly. It was not. Unanswered was not refused, and it was not granted. It was unanswered, and the officer it concerned had taught a whole station how much weight that word could carry if nobody leaned on it.

So neither. She took a blank order sheet and wrote.

*Dock Seven, failure point. The reading is taken each shift, and the arrays stay on.*

*Nothing further is sent from this side.*

*The file is not closed from this side.*

*Grounds: the account sent 0610, Deep Winter, 2831; the question asked in it; no answer detected.*

She read it over. It gave the watch no use. She had none to give it that was true, and she was not going to invent one so that a line on a sheet could look like the other forty; a Cadet had declined to do that this morning with rather less rank to protect him. What the order gave the watch was a name. For a year it had been one analyst's habit, and an analyst could be reassigned, and a habit could be audited away by an honest junior on a quiet afternoon. Now it was the High Captain's, and anyone who wanted it struck would have to come and say so to her.

She added one line beneath the grounds. *The sheet comes to the High Captain. It is not delegated.*

The order form had a space at the foot for a review date. She looked at it for the length of a breath and went past it.

Then she took up the Council's form. Under *grounds for continued holding* she wrote *see standing order attached*. The line for the date she left as she had found it. She did not think of it as a refusal. She did not have a date, and the form had not asked her to make one up.

She signed both, and sent the copies down for the binders.

The copies reached the reconciling desk late in the shift, and the desk did what it did with everything.

Cael went first. *Review notice, Council form, current issue. Standing order, High Captain's hand. The order cites an account sent at 0610, Deep Winter, 2831. Verified; the binder copy has the time on it. It cites a question asked in that account. Verified, last paragraph. It cites no answer detected.* A pause. *Verified against the boundary desk's sheet, line nineteen, one thousand and ninety-six entries. No entry records an answer.*

*And the form,* Sen said.

*The form has two lines. One is filled. One is empty.* Cael did not like the next part and gave it anyway. *The Council's own instruction sheet says a date is required. That is a discrepancy between a document and the rule it was filed under.*

*It is an empty line,* Wyn said, *at the foot of a true one. I would be careful, at this desk, about what we say that is.*

Cael let it go. There was also the review date on the order, which was blank too, and Cael counted it without being asked and did not give the count.

Sen turned to the sheet, because the order had cited it and a citation was an invitation to look. Line nineteen, in two columns, the arrays' column headed with the assumption the desk itself had stated a year ago. The entries were the Analyst's hand and the Cadet's, turn about. Sen read down the year. Then the three of them stopped at the same place, which did not happen often.

*The order says no answer detected,* Cael said. *The sheet agrees. But the order does not say how it knows. Four words. A reader who had only the order could not tell whether anyone looked once in a year or every shift of it.*

*And the sheet says.*

*The sheet says every shift. One thousand and ninety-six readings, none missed; I have counted the dates and not the entries. Two columns. One of them is carried under an assumption, and the assumption is written at its head, in our wording.* Cael was certain of this one. *"None detected" is only as good as the looking. The looking is in one document and the claim is in the other, and nobody has put them side by side.*

That was a note. It was exactly a note; it was what the desk was for. Sen wrote it at the foot of the binder copy as Cael gave it. *The order states no answer detected. It rests on sheet line nineteen: one reading each shift for a year, none missed, the arrays column carried under the assumption stated at its head. Neither document contradicts the other. The order does not say how it knows. The sheet does.*

*And the cost,* Wyn said.

*Say what is on the file.*

*All of it is on the file. I checked before I opened my mouth; I have been at this desk as long as you have.* Wyn did not hurry. *The reading was kept for a year by one analyst who put it on a sheet in her own hand with no order behind her and nothing to write beside it. She reported her own leaning to her commander rather than be found out by her figures. That is in the boundary desk's drift log, under the instruments, in her hand. And this morning a Cadet tested the line, found no use in it, wrote that down, and was told his finding stood. That is what a year of this cost and who paid, and none of them was asked to, and not one of them has said what it is for.* Then, more quietly: *Three things. She wrote three things and then the grounds.*

Sen waited for the rest of that. It did not come. Wyn had looked at the order's three plain clauses, and had plainly set them beside three other clauses the desk had read aloud once, and had decided that the adjacency could stand without an archivist in it. Sen did not ask. Sen wrote the cost under the note, in the same hand: who had kept the line, and on what authority, and what the Cadet had found.

Then the hand reached the line at the foot, and stopped.

*She signed hers,* Wyn said.

*She did.*

*A year. And the one person in this binder who has put a name under an open thing is the one it costs most to have there. Now somebody at the Council has to write back to her about a date, and they will, and she will have to answer it, and that is what a name does. I am not saying it to win. I am saying I noticed.*

*I noticed too,* Sen said.

*Sign it.*

*Probably not.*

*I will want it tomorrow.*

Wyn had wanted it tomorrow on every working day of a year. Cael had the figure, and had been asked not to give it.

Sen fronted, and held the desk, and filed the note as it stood: both halves, no name. The citation was in the same binder, and the submission with three lines of a four-line flag, and the account, and now an order and a form. Five documents, and no two of them were for the same thing. The desk had read each against the instruments, and had no note to add about the five together. A binder that did not agree with itself was not a gap. It was what the binder held.

Sen wrote the date in the index. The three of them went home by the one door.

::::: scene 3

::: pov wender

The sheet came up at the end of the shift, as it had every day for a year, and for the first time it came up because she had ordered it to.

Entry one thousand and ninety-six. Desk column: warm, within the band. Arrays column, under its heading: variation continuing, invariant held, nothing addressed, none detected. The Cadet's hand. It was the hand of the audit and it had not changed between the morning and the afternoon, though something had been put to him in between; there was a small mark in the margin, of the kind the desk used for a thing raised aloud and not written.

She did not ask what it was. It had been raised to the person it was owed to. If it had been owed to her it would have been on the sheet.

She read the entry once for content and once for what it was for, and for the first time in a year the second reading gave her something. It was not for Shepherd. Shepherd could not read it. It was not for the Council, which wanted a date. It was a record made every shift by people who would each have liked it to say more than it did, written so that it did not, and that was the only kind of record the officer in question had ever agreed to stand behind. If she never read a line of it, it was still kept the way she would have kept it.

Wender initialled the entry.

The window gave on the thin water, and at the far end of the harbour wall the lights were on along Dock Seven's outer gallery, where a maintenance crew was finishing. She had gone down there a good deal in the first winter. She went less now. It had stopped being the place where the thing was. The thing was on a sheet, in three hands, at every change of shift, and she found that she could think of the woman it concerned without the old arithmetic starting up. She thought of her as she had been on an ordinary morning over a forty-page bulletin: reading a thing twice, flagging it in four lines, and going on to the next item. Wender had thought that was composure. She understood it better now, having tried it for a year.

The next item was a berthing dispute between two cutters, and it was owed the same reading as anything else. She took it up without a change of pace. There was nobody in the office to notice the pace. She kept it anyway.

It was still Tuesday. She expected there would be another.
