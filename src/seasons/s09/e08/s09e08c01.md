:::pov iklix-varn

The order was on the desk when Varn came through the hatch, and the officer was standing beside it.

"Varn," Smith said, as a fact.

It was one leaf, printed, because an order on a reader is an order until the reader fails. It lay square to the edge of the desk, facing the side a Deputy stands on. In the early spring Varn had carried a leaf like it onto this ring in a kit bag, folded once, and had read it standing on the dock. That one had said *fold-approach watch, Eden Space Habitat, under Field Officer Smith*, and had given a hatch number. Nothing in it had said what the posting was for.

Varn had since found out. It had taken most of a year, and the answer was short. Six instruments read the fold approach, each on a different principle, and filed their agreement with each other every ninety seconds. The watch did not watch them. It stood in a compartment behind a hatch that the residents of the ring walked past without a glance, and it was the nearest qualified officer to the event if the event came. At the end of each shift a Deputy wrote down what the instruments had done and who had stood there. On the first day Varn had written *Nothing to report*, and had been told that it was an opinion. The event had not come.

Varn went to the desk and stood where a person stands, and read.

*Deputy Officer Varn, from the fold-approach watch, Eden Space Habitat.* Then a watch that was not Eden's, given by its station and a number. *Under Field Officer*, and a name that was in no log Varn had ever read. A date to be relieved here. A later date to report there. A reference.

That was all it gave.

Varn read it a second time, which a Deputy is allowed.

It did not say why. It did not say why now, or why there. Somewhere in the second reading Varn noticed that the question had not come. In the early spring there would have been three readings of a leaf like this, gone through one after another on the way down the page. A Deputy had done well and was wanted. A Deputy had done badly and was being moved. It meant something about the officer. None of the three could be supported from what was printed. What was printed said where, and by when.

The date to be relieved was not close. Varn counted it against the week, from habit. It fell on a sixth day, the next but one, and the soil in the pot outside the door after the hatch would be dark that morning.

"It came with the morning's notices," Smith said. "A Field Officer reads a Deputy's order first. I have read it."

"Yes, Field Officer."

"It is the form the fourteen had."

Varn waited for the rest of the sentence. There was no rest of it, and there did not need to be. She did not tell a Deputy what a thing meant. She had said what this one was, and it could be checked: the leaf was on the desk, and it was like the leaf in the kit bag.

Varn folded it once, the way a document is folded to be carried, and set it down at the Deputy's end of the desk.

The six lights were the colour they were. The shift began, and was the shift.

Somewhere in the middle of it Varn asked the one thing the page had left that an officer could answer.

"Does the watch get another?"

"The watch gets what the Corps sends. I have had fifteen." She did not look up. "Nobody has told me there will be no sixteenth."

The order had said nothing of her. It had said nothing of the hatch, or the six, or the log. *The hatch is not the Corps that came,* Varn had told a resident in the spring, for his mother, who wanted to know. *It is the one that stays.* Varn had thought at the time that it was a sentence about the Corps. It had also been a sentence about a Deputy, with the Deputy left out.

At the end of the shift she turned the log toward the Deputy and said, "Enter it."

Varn wrote the instruments. The hatch, opened once and closed once. The officers, named. Then the pen stopped over the page.

An order had been read in the compartment. It was the Corps' own, and the Corps kept it under its own reference, so somebody who was not here could check it without this log. That was a reason to leave it out. A resident's name had been left out, in the spring, and a path between two beds of beans, and a good deal since. But those had happened in a corridor and on a terrace, and this had happened on the desk. After that sixth day the entries in this column would be in one hand and would name one officer. Somebody reading back down it would come to the place where that changed, and would find nothing to say when the watch had known.

Varn wrote: *Order received for Deputy Officer Varn, posted on. Read by the Field Officer and by the Deputy.* Then the date of relief, and the reference. *No event.*

Smith read it as though it had been longer, and initialled it.

Varn took the leaf from the desk. In the late autumn, on a wall on the far side of the ring, Varn had told a resident what could be supported: that nobody stays a Deputy on a first posting, that the Corps had not said, and that when it said he would be told the same day.

It was still the same day. Varn went out through the hatch at the hour.

:::pov zoe-smith

She had read it at the start of the shift, standing, before the Deputy came.

It was the fifteenth such leaf to cross a desk of hers, and it was on the form the others had come on. The Corps did not write them differently for anybody. A name, a watch, an officer, two dates and a reference. She had seen Deputies read one for a verdict. There was none in it to read.

What she noted, since small things were what she had, was that this one had read it twice and asked nothing about it. Then folded it once, and set it down, and stood the shift. In the early spring the same Deputy had needed a week to ask why a log could not say that nothing had happened. She did not write that the two were connected. She could not have supported it.

Under the order in the morning's notices there had been a form. The Corps sent it when it moved a Deputy on, as it had sent one at the end of the first month, and it asked the same thing: what the Deputy had done on the watch. She would write it after the last shift, when the last shift had been stood.

She knew most of what would be in it. A log kept, in a clear hand. A line carried up the ring that belonged to nobody. A relieving officer's shift entered with the hatch closed to the seal, because that was what had happened. One interval in the autumn, stood, with the weight coming forward again and nobody telling it to. She would not write that the Deputy was ready for anything. No assessment of hers had said that about anyone, and nine of the fourteen now held rank above hers. The boards had learned long ago to read what she left out as left out.

The Deputy went at the hour, with the leaf. Through the latch she heard somebody at the next door stand up, not too fast.

She left the hatch on the latch for a reason, and had given the reason to nobody. She got up and crossed the compartment and closed it, as she had once before, in the late autumn. It closed without a sound.

It was the Corps' news as far as the hatch. Past the hatch it was the corridor's. She went back to the desk, and wrote nothing down.

::::: scene 2

:::pov noah-adeyemi

The hatch sat a finger's width off its frame, which was right, and he was down by the plant with nothing in his hands.

He had been here at the hour on most days since the early spring. The hatch opened, and the Deputy came out and stopped one step further off than there was any reason to stop. That was where the Corps stood. He had been told so across his own table, and had stopped minding it some time before the summer. In a shirt, three stops along the line, there was no step at all.

The handle turned. Three bars, grey-brown. The eyes went to his hands, as they always did, and then to his face.

There was a leaf in the Deputy's hand, folded once.

He had never seen one. He knew what it was the way he knew a spoke car had stopped. He stood up, not too fast, since it seemed like a thing to be standing for.

"Noah," the Deputy said. "The Corps has said."

He had had this ready since he first looked up how long anybody kept three bars. He had found out in the summer, in front of a shut hatch, that having it ready was not the same as having it. So he did not reach for any of that. He asked what there was to ask.

"When did it come?"

"This shift. It was on the desk when I went in."

"So it's the same day."

"It is the same day."

He nodded. On a wall on the far side of the ring, in the late autumn, he had been promised exactly that. People said *soon*, and *we'll see*. This had had a day in it, and he had thought then that he would be able to check it. Now he had checked it. It was a strange thing to be glad of, and he was.

"Go on, then," he said.

"I am relieved on a sixth day. Not this one. The one after."

"A sixth day."

"The soil will be dark."

That nearly undid him. He looked at the pot until it had passed. "Where?"

The Deputy said the name of it.

He had never heard it. It was not a habitat, he could tell that much. It had a number in it, like the hatch. He would look it up. He looked things up.

"Is it a watch?"

"It is a watch. It is not Eden's."

"What does it—" He stopped. He could almost hear a full can being set down on the deck. "No. It doesn't."

"It doesn't," the Deputy said, and something in the face let go, without a sound.

"How far?"

"A ship from the dock. I do not know how many days. That will be on the travel order, and I have not had it." It was the voice that said what could be supported. Then it went on, which it did not always. "Further than the far side of the ring. That I can support."

He laughed. It was not much of one. Nobody took a step back.

"You came out in it," he said. He meant the grey-brown, and the tab. "You could have gone along and changed, and knocked."

"I considered that. It is the Corps' news. It should be said where the Corps stands." The Deputy looked at the deck between them. "And it would have been a quarter of an hour later. I said the same day. I did not want to find out how much of a day I had meant."

He put his hands in his pockets, having nothing to hold.

His mother had said it once, in this corridor, to this collar. They come along the ring with a bag. A while after, they go back along it with the same bag, and there's a different walk at the hour, and nobody tells us. She had lived behind that door since before he was born, and she had said *nobody tells us* as flatly as she would have said which day the plant was watered.

Somebody had told them. It had taken one shift.

"I'll tell her tonight," he said. "She'll want to have been told."

"Yes."

"The one after this."

"There are evenings before it. I am off the watch for every one of them. That is on no order. I counted."

He went red, and did not mind.

"The squash is over," he said. "It's all leeks now. He leaves those on the wall as well."

"I will need a quarter of an hour."

"I'll be here."

"I know," the Deputy said, and went off along the ring toward the room the Corps kept, with the leaf in one hand.

He stayed by the plant. The soil was pale, two days off its sixth. On his other side the hatch was flat to the wall with no line of light round it, which at this hour it almost never was. He did not look at it. Somebody behind it had shut it, and he thought he knew for whom.

He waited the quarter of an hour.
