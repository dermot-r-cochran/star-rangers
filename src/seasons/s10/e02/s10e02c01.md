On the twenty-seventh day the watch went back to six.

The senior engineer came to the hatch in the morning with the case already closed. The reference was in it, unbolted from mount five at the end of ring's night, and the watch's name was on the request that had put it there, so somebody from the watch signed for its going. Smith signed. Varn stood where Deputies stand and watched a thing the size of a kit bag leave the ring, having told six instruments what they had been agreeing with.

"It goes back to the Passage oversight," the engineer said. "It is the oversight's. If you want it again, you ask again."

"We know how," Smith said.

Then the hatch closed and the compartment was what it had been on the first day: a rack of sealed grey housings, six small lights the same colour, an agreement filed every ninety seconds into a log nobody read. Varn found that it was not what it had been on the first day at all. On the first day the six had been six principles, so that no one fault could fool all of them. Varn now knew of a fault that had fooled all of them for three years, by the simple method of holding them up.

And there was the sentence.

It had been said once, under the ring, on the twenty-sixth day, by the Corps' engineer, in the voice of someone stating a load case. If the mount let go under an approach, the six would read normal until the moment they read nothing. It had gone into the inspection report. Varn had carried a slate up the ring that day and had not carried the sentence, because it was not on the slate, and a Deputy says nothing that cannot be shown on paper. It had come up anyway. It was in the compartment now, somewhere between the rack and the desk, and Varn did not know whether the officer knew it was there.

The mount was not shored yet. The habitat's engineer had written *remedial, immediate* and underlined it, and immediate turned out to mean a notice. It came that afternoon by the Certification Office's hand, addressed to the watch as a party of record.

*Temporary works, approach mount three, root: shoring, to take up load at the deformed bracket. Jacking four days from this date, at ring's night, between the second hour and the fourth. A displacement of the mount is to be expected in that window, and monitoring carried by the mount may register it. The jack will be taken up weekly until the permanent works, and each taking-up notified. Temporary works are notified, not certified.*

Varn read it standing, and then read the gap in it. Four days. For four days more the mount would carry a third again over its record under every approach that came up the Passage, as it had since 2825. Nothing about the mount had changed since the eighth day. What had changed was that two people in a compartment with no window knew.

Varn held the question for most of a shift, and then asked whether approaches could be held until the shore was in.

"By whom?" Smith said. It was the ninth day's question, turned round.

Varn thought about it. "The dock is the habitat's. The route is the Passage oversight's."

"And the oversight has the report we have. So has the Commissioner. Two pairs of engineers stood on that mount and agreed one word about when, and the word was *immediate*, and four days is what immediate costs when a jack has to be made to fit a ring. If either pair had thought the member would not last four days after lasting three years, they would have written a different word, and somebody with standing would have acted on it." She looked up from the trace. "We do not add urgency to an engineer's report from in here. That is not caution. It is saying more than we know."

"They said a thing, under the ring." Varn heard it come out and let it. "About what the six would read."

"Normal, and then nothing." She said it the way she said a figure. "It is on the fourth leaf. It is the best sentence in the report. They said it once and they wrote it once, which is the right number of times." She went back to the trace. "You have been carrying that since yesterday as though it were yours. It is in a report, Deputy. Everybody who needs it has it. Put it down and stand your shift."

Varn stood the shift. It was the same shift, and that was the difficulty with it. For four days nothing in the compartment could be made to look like what was known about it.

:::pov zoe-smith

She let the Deputy count.

Varn had stopped counting the six lights somewhere in the third week, as they all did, and on the twenty-seventh day had started again: the eyes going along the rack at each agreement, one to six, and back to the desk. She did not remark on it. A Deputy who learns what a posting is standing next to counts for a few days. The ones who never counted had worried her more.

What she did with the four days was write, before the thing and not after it. She entered the notice in the habitat's words. Under it she entered what the watch could add: *Window noted. Reference withdrawn on the twenty-seventh day; the six are unreferenced from that date. A displacement inside the notified window will be attributable to the works on the notice. It will not be verifiable by this watch.* An attribution written afterwards is a story. One written beforehand is a test, and she preferred to have set it while she still did not know the result.

There had been notified works before. Four, in her tenure: a conduit, a hatch frame, a housing re-seated twice. Each time the trace had moved in its window, and she had written *as notified* and thought no more about it.

She thought more about it now. Four times she had taken an office's word for what was moving her instruments. It was the same office that had since written *n/a* beside a line. The word had been good all four times. She had not known that. She had assumed it, which goes in a different column, and she had been entering it in the wrong one for years.

She did not write that down. It was not a finding. It was only a thing she would do differently, starting with this notice, and the way to start was to say in the log exactly how far a notice went.

::::: scene 2

:::pov iklix-varn

Ring's night, the thirty-first day, and the ring carrying only itself.

Varn was looking at the trace when it moved. Varn had been looking at it since the window opened, which a Deputy had not been told to do and had not been told not to.

It was not dramatic. A line that had lain flat for thirty-one days, and by the log for years before that, leaned. Over the length of two agreements it went from where it had always been to somewhere else, and there it lay flat again. The six lights did not change colour. Six instruments on six principles filed their agreement on schedule, and it was a true agreement. They had all moved together.

"Field Officer," Varn said. It came out level, which was something.

"I have it."

She had not looked at the trace. She had looked at the clock, and then at the notice, and only then at the rack.

Every part of the training had the words ready. *Displacement on the approach monitoring, all instruments, in agreement.* That was, to the syllable, the first line of the thing the watch existed for. The event would look like this. Varn knew of nothing in the six that could tell this from that, and stood very still, as though stillness were a contribution.

"What do you see?" Smith said.

"A step. All six. They agree." A breath. "I cannot tell from the trace what moved. The mount, or what the mount is there to read."

"No. Nor can I. Nor can they; they hang from it." She turned the log round on the desk. "What else do you have?"

Varn looked at what else there was. "The notice. The window opened forty minutes ago. And the approach board is empty until the morning."

"Then enter it."

Varn wrote the hour and the minute. *Displacement, all six, in agreement.* Then the pen stopped, because the next word was the entry. *Event* could not be supported. Neither, Varn found, could *shoring*. Varn had not seen a jack. Varn had seen a leaf of paper from across the ring that said a jack would be turned in a certain hour, and a line that had moved in that hour.

*Within the window notified by the habitat for temporary works, approach mount three. Attributed to those works. Not verified by this watch. Not an event.*

Smith read it as though it had been longer, and initialled it.

"How much?"

Varn read it off the trace against the slate from the sixteenth day. "Most of what the reference found. The other way. Not all of it."

"Then a jack has most of it and the bracket still has some. That is what a shore is." She wrote the figure under the entry. "From tonight the six read from a floor that is off by that much. We know the amount, and we have been told the cause. Do we zero them?"

It was a real question. Varn took it as one, and took a while.

"If we zero them against each other, we have set a zero with a prop in it. That is the certification date again." She waited. "If we ask for the reference, it sets a true zero on a jack, and the notice says the engineers take the jack up every week until the bracket is replaced. It would be out of date in seven days."

"So?"

"So normal is not zero. Not until the mount is mended. Normal is the number."

"On the first line of every shift, with the date of the notice it rests on, so that whoever reads the log knows what the floor is standing on." She capped the pen. "A number you know the reason for is a better floor than a zero somebody handed you. We have had the other kind."

:::pov zoe-smith

The Deputy did not go back to where Deputies stand. She noticed, and left it.

"On the first day," Varn said, "you told me that if they ever stopped agreeing, the watch would know before anyone could read anything."

"I did."

"They did not stop agreeing tonight. They did not stop for three years."

"No." She had wondered which shift that would arrive on. "It is still true. It is not the sentence you took it for, that is all. It says what the lights are for. It has never said what they are about."

Varn was quiet for the length of one agreement. Then: "They read something."

She looked up.

"The engineer's sentence. Normal, and then nothing. Tonight the mount moved, and they read it." The Deputy was working it out aloud, and she let that happen too. "If the instruments had been moved to a sound mount on the sixteenth day, they would be reading true now, and a jack would have gone in under mount three tonight and nothing on this ring would have known it but the two people turning it. We would have a notice and a flat trace."

"And we would have believed the notice," she said. "As I have believed four before it."

She had written, on the sixteenth day, *Instruments to remain on mount three*, and had told the Deputy that the number was the only witness the member had and that she would not take it off the stand. She had meant the bad night. She had kept six instruments hanging from a damaged mount so that if it went, there would be a record that it had been going. She had not once thought about what else a witness is present for.

"I kept it there for the other thing," she said. "I did not know it would also be the only one to see the member get better. Nobody tells you that. It is not in the doctrine, because it is never an event."

"Was it a good step?"

"It was a step, in a window. Ask me on the day the reference comes back."

The trace lay flat in its new place. Past the hatch the corridor was dim and empty. Somewhere under the dock two of the habitat's engineers were taking their hands off a jack, and they would never know that a compartment on the far side of the ring had written down, to the minute, that something happened when they said something would.

The lights held. They would have held either way. Both of the people in the compartment knew that now, and stood the rest of ring's night with a number on the first line.
