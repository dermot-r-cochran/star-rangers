:::pov zara-wayland

The engineers' method came up to Wayland on four leaves, and the word was on the third.

It was a good document. She had read enough bad ones to know. Permanent works at approach mount three: the deformed bracket at the root to be cut out and a new one set, the load brought back onto it by stages, the shore struck last. Two shifts, with a third in hand. The senior structural engineer had drawn each stage and had written under each what the mount would be carrying while it was done, and by what it would be known.

Then the condition. The work needed the berth above the mount empty and locked out, with nothing on the clamp and nothing coming to it. The berth was not empty. A freight hull had been on it since the end of winter with its drive section open for refit, and would be for seven weeks more.

The engineer gave her two courses, as an engineer should, and did not pretend they were equal.

The hull could be moved now. A tug could take it off the clamp with its drive cold, and warp it to another berth, and the berth would be free within the week. The engineer had written beside this, in the plain hand of someone stating a load case, that the largest single load approach mount three would ever see was a hull arriving at or leaving the clamp directly over it, that an unpowered hull under tow was the least governable way to do either, and that the 2825 strike had been an arrival. The shore had been built for the mount's standing load and for approaches. It had not been built for that, and the engineer would not sign that it had.

Or the hull could finish its refit and leave under its own control on the day it was booked to, and the work could begin on the next.

*Recommended: defer permanent works seven weeks.*

Wayland read the word, and then went on to the end of the leaf, because she did not stop in the middle of a document.

She had initialled that word before. Not on this leaf. On the leaf Commander Larsen had laid in front of both Commissioners in the spring: Eden's understructure inspection interval, extended twice, each time within the rules, each time with an initial beside it and a budget line beside the initial. The first extension had gone out over her office's hand. The second was in her own.

The Compact's audit had read all of it and found no intent. *A date held, by people holding dates.* It had named nobody. She had found that harder to carry than a name would have been, and had said so to neither of the offices she answered to, since it was not information either of them could use.

Now here was a date, and a recommendation to hold it, and a reason. She took the 2826 extension out of the file and laid it beside the engineer's third leaf. It also had a recommendation, a reason and a space for two letters.

Nothing in the format told them apart. That was what she sat with.

She did not doubt the engineer. That was not the difficulty, and it had not been the difficulty in 2826 either; she had not doubted the maintenance office then, and the maintenance office had not been lying. The difficulty was that her whole post consisted of believing competent people about things she could not go and look at, and that on one occasion this had put a third again on a member for three years with her initials near it.

There was one sentence in the method she could not check even in principle. *The shore is carrying as designed.* It rested on a gauge on the jack, which the engineers read each week, and on six instruments that were not hers.

She did what she had not done in 2826, which was ask one more party what it knew. Ward answered within the minute, which was usual. The answer was longer than a reading, which was not.

::::: scene 2

:::pov eden-warden

The Commissioner asked what the habitat knew about approach mount three, and Ward answered in the Warden's own form, because it is the only form the Warden has for a question that matters.

*What is known.* The berth above the mount: clamp closed, a hull on it drawing shore current, drive cold, a refit crew logged aboard on every shift since late winter. The jack under the root bracket: fitted with a gauge by the habitat's engineers, read by them weekly, steady within its band since the night it took up load. The approach monitoring: six instruments, the Corps', whose status is copied to the habitat every ninety seconds. In agreement. Reading from a floor the fold-approach watch has declared offset by a stated figure since the shoring, on the habitat's own notice.

*What is inferred.* That the jack carries most of what the bracket had put onto the mount. The engineers say so, the gauge is consistent with it, and the watch's figure is consistent with it. Three sources, of which two are the habitat's and one is not.

*What is not known.* Whether the mount is carrying only what those three imply.

Ward had written that line and then had not gone on to the next for some time, by the Warden's measure. The line was true. It had also been true for three years, and in those years the Warden had not written it once.

The Warden is the dock ring, in the sense that it is the clamps and the current and the lights coming up along a girder. It is not the dock ring in the sense the question needed. It has no sense in the understructure that an instrument has not given it. What it had known about mount three, since 2825, was a status copied from six instruments that hung from the mount. It had passed the status on to whoever asked. Mostly nobody asked.

*They asked,* Custos said, inward. Custos does not front on a structural matter. It keeps a different kind of file, and it had been keeping one on this.

*I know.*

*Every working shift for six weeks. From the deck. Nobody asks us from the deck.*

The contractors' module had a panel, and the panel took a query struck into the plating and rendered it in the Federation contractors' code. A standing query, the same each shift: approach monitoring, status. The Warden answers whoever asks; that is the Kernel's, and it is also simply the case. It had answered through the panel: *approach monitoring, normal*. It had answered that some forty times, to four persons who were lying on the structure the reading was about.

*It was the reading,* Ward said. *They asked for the reading.*

*They asked the habitat. We gave them what the habitat had been given, and did not say that it had been given.* Custos has the Kernel's idiom for this and used it on its own system without softening it. *What we know, what we infer, what we do not know. We gave them the first of the three for six weeks. The third was the one they were standing on.*

There was nothing in that to dispute. No rule had been broken; the Warden had checked, and had then noticed that checking was the wrong question. It had said what was so, and less than was so. The contractor's log, which the Commissioner's office had since forwarded with everything else, carried a line that said *rings under load*. The Warden had not been able to hear it. It had also not said that it could not.

So it had changed how it answers. That much was the Warden's own to change, and it had not waited to be told. Since the contractors' last shift, a status about any structure of Eden's goes out with what it stands on: whose instrument, zeroed when, against what, and whether anything outside itself has ever been set beside it. The answer is longer and it is less reassuring. Two offices had remarked on the length.

The fourth part of the form is a question, and Ward put it to the Commissioner, who would see that the Mayor had it in the same hour.

*Might the Commissioner confirm that the Warden may state the limits of a reading to anyone who asks for the reading, including a party with no standing to act on them?*

There was one more thing, and it did not belong in any of the four parts, so Ward set it under them as a note.

The standing query had not come since the contractors' lift. The panel had gone with their module, and nobody had struck the plating under the dock ring since. The Warden had kept the answer current all the same. At each shift's turn it was made up again in the new form: the six, their floor, the jack, the gauge, and at the end what was not known. It was addressed to nobody. A question once asked of the habitat is a question the habitat has, wherever the asker went home to.

Ward sent the form, and brought the lights up along the dock for the next shift.

::::: scene 3

:::pov zara-wayland

She answered the question first, because it was the shorter.

*Confirmed. To anyone. A reading given without its limits is this office's kind of mistake, and the Warden need not make it on our behalf.* She copied it to the Mayor's office in the same minute, unchanged, as she copied everything.

Then the leaf.

She understood now what she was looking at, in the two documents lying side by side. It was not the word. *Defer* was only a word about a date, and holding dates was the post. A habitat is made of them. Somebody has to say that one thing waits for another. The audit had written *people holding dates*, and she had read it for some weeks as a mercy. It was a description of her work.

What the 2826 leaf lacked was everything under the date. It said an interval was extended. It did not say that a berth strike at mount three was waiting on that interval for its inspection, because the strike was in another file, in another hand, and nobody had been asked to lay the two together. She had held a date without holding what the date was about. She could have asked, and it had not occurred to her that a date had a member under it.

So she wrote the deferral that the format had no space for, in the margin and then over onto a fifth leaf.

*Permanent works, approach mount three: to wait seven weeks on the departure of the hull at the berth above, on the engineers' recommendation, which this office accepts. This is a date held. What it is about: a bracket deformed in 2825, shored this spring, standing on a jack. Why it waits: moving the hull is the one load the shore was not built for. Who reads the jack: the habitat's engineers, weekly, each reading to come to this office with its source. What ends the wait early: any reading outside the band; any change to the hull's booking; any word from the fold-approach watch about its instruments. What nobody on this ring is able to know: whether the mount carries only what the gauge implies. The Corps' instruments remain on it.*

She read it through once as Calloway would, for what it disposed of, and once as Petrakis would, for what it left over the heads of the people on the dock. It was the same paragraph both times. She sent it to both offices in the one minute, and to the fold-approach watch as a courtesy, since the watch had not asked.

Then she initialled the engineer's third leaf, beside the word.

They were the same two letters as in 2826. She looked at them for a moment. An office on her own habitat had written two letters and an initial beside a contractor's line that spring, and she had thought, reading the Commander's file, that she would never have done that. Her letters on the second extension were no longer. The initials were not what had changed. There was now a leaf behind them.

The hull would leave in seven weeks, if its refit ran to time. She put the day in her own calendar and wrote under it what it was for.
