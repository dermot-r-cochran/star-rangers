:::pov iklix-varn

For two shifts the six instruments were going to mean nothing, and the habitat had said so in writing.

The notice came on the eighty-seventh day, by the Certification Office's hand, addressed to the watch as a party of record. Varn read it standing. The hull at the berth above approach mount three had left the clamp on its booked day, under its own control. The berth was locked out. Permanent works would begin at the next shift's turn and run two shifts: the deformed bracket cut out at the root, a new one set, the load brought back by stages, the shore struck last. *For the duration, monitoring carried by the mount will register the works and nothing else. No approach is booked to the dock ring's fold side for either shift, and the Passage oversight has been told.*

Somebody had chosen those two shifts for what was in them. Varn noticed that, and noticed having learned to look for it.

Under that there was a last line, in smaller type, which Varn read and did not stop on. *Monitoring to be re-zeroed on the certification date per standard practice, unless otherwise advised by the watch.*

Smith read the notice after the Deputy had. She read it the way she read everything, and then she read the last line again, and then she put the leaf down on the desk very square.

"Stand your shift," she said. She did not say anything else for some time.

The trace began to move a little after the turn.

Varn had seen it move once, on the thirty-first day: one step, inside a window, and flat again. For fifty-six shifts since, the first line of the log had carried a number, the size of that step, with the date of the notice it rested on, and under the number the six had lain as flat as they ever had. Varn had come to like the number. It was a floor with its reason written on it.

This was not a step. The line wandered. It went up as something under the dock ring took the load off the mount, and held, and sagged, and jumped a hair when, presumably, a thing was struck. The six lights did not change colour. The six agreed with each other every ninety seconds, wholly, about a member that was being taken apart.

The pen was in Varn's hand and the first line of the shift was empty.

"The number does not hold," Varn said.

"No."

"Then I have nothing for the first line."

"You have what the six say and what the notice says about what the six say. Enter both." She was looking at the rack and not at the Deputy. "A reading that is known to be of the wrong thing is still a reading. It goes in, with what is known about it. Leave it out and whoever reads this log will think the instruments were off. They are not off. They are telling us in great detail about a bracket."

Varn wrote it. *Six in agreement. By the habitat's notice, reading the permanent works at approach mount three for this shift and the next, and not the approach. No approach booked. Not verified by this watch.*

It was the strangest entry Varn had made. It said, in a form that could be checked, that the watch could not at present see.

Under the ring, on the twenty-sixth day, the Corps' engineer had said what the mount letting go would have meant: the watch as the nearest qualified officer to an event it could no longer see. That had been a load case. This was a schedule. It was the same compartment either way, and Varn stood in it.

"If it came tonight," Varn said. It was not quite a question.

"Then it would come to two officers and a rack that is busy." Smith did turn round then. "The watch is still the nearest. That was true before there was a rack to stand beside. Not on Eden. But it was."

She did not say what she meant, and Varn did not ask. Varn thought of a girl at a well, who had had no instruments either and had said what she saw and what did not fit. Varn did not know whether that was what the account was for. The officer who had handed it across the desk did not know either. It stayed where it was.

What Varn did do, for want of a trace, was listen.

The hatch was on the latch, as it was on most days. Varn had never asked why and did not now. Through it came the corridor. A door. Two children negotiating. A trolley going by with one wheel that had started, sometime that week, to tick. Varn had stood eighty-eight shifts in this compartment and could not have said before today what the corridor sounded like at this hour, having always had six lights to look at instead.

Nothing came up the Passage. The trolley went by twice more. The line on the trace wandered about under the hands of people Varn would never meet, and Varn entered it every ninety seconds, in agreement.

:::pov zoe-smith

She waited until the Deputy had gone at the hour, and then she took down the file.

The watch keeps its own notices, in order. Hers went back to her first week, and behind hers were other hands, three Field Officers of them, on leaves that had gone soft at the edges. She did not read the notices. She read the last line of each.

*Unless otherwise advised by the watch.*

It was on the conduit and the hatch frame. It was on both re-seatings of the housing. It was on the notice for the contractor's run of members under the dock ring, which had reached her some weeks before a Deputy arrived, and which she had read, and filed. On the certification date for that run the six had been set to zero against each other with a bent bracket's load already in them. The Deputy had called that standard practice on the twelfth day, reading it off the certification, and she had let the words stand as the habitat's.

It had not been only the habitat's. The habitat had asked. It had asked every time, in small type, in a clause so standard that it had stopped being a question some time before she was posted here, and no Field Officer in the file had ever answered it.

She had sat at this desk on the twelfth day and counted the parties. The habitat, which certified what its monitoring read. The Compact, which deferred what its rules allowed. The crew, who cut what they were asked to cut. Not one of them wrong; she had thought it in those words. She had made a list of everyone who could have stopped it and had left off the desk she was sitting at.

There was no reason she had known of, then, to advise otherwise. That was true. It was also exactly what was true of every initial in the Commander's file.

She did not write anything that night. She wanted the trace to finish first.

:::pov iklix-varn

At the end of the second shift the line stopped wandering.

It did not go back to where it had been. Varn had half expected that, without having any ground to. It settled, over the length of three or four agreements, at a place it had not lain before, neither the old flat nor the number's, and stayed there. The lights were the colour they were. Somewhere under the dock a shore had been struck, and a mount was standing on a new bracket by itself.

The six agreed. The reading, taken from the place the line now lay, was steady.

Varn wrote the hour, and *Six in agreement*, and then, the pen moving a word ahead of the Deputy, *reading normal*.

Varn looked at it.

Then Varn struck the two words with one line, so that they could still be read, and stood there with the pen up.

"Say it," Smith said.

"It reads normal against itself." Varn had said that sentence once before, in a borrowed room on the Compact side, with one finger on a certification and a raven on a bracket. "It is lying flat, and I called the flat place normal. Nothing has told me where zero is. On the certification date the standard practice will tell them that zero is wherever they happen to be lying."

Smith turned the notice round on the desk so that the last line faced the Deputy. Commander Larsen had turned a leaf that way once, to show two letters and an initial.

"*Unless otherwise advised by the watch*," Varn read.

"Advise them."

It took four drafts, which was three more than the sentence deserved. *The fold-approach watch advises otherwise. The six instruments at approach mount three are not to be re-zeroed against each other on the certification date. Zero to be set against an independent reference, requested this day of the Passage oversight under the watch's own competence.* Varn wrote the request to the oversight under it, in the form of the one from the spring, which was in the log to copy.

Smith read both, and signed both. It was her name and her competence. The hand was the Deputy's.

Then she drew the log to her own side of the desk, which she did not often do, and wrote under the Deputy's entry for some time.

Varn did not read an officer's entry upside down. Varn read it when she turned it round.

*The clause "unless otherwise advised by the watch" stands in every notice of works in this watch's file, under four Field Officers. It has not been exercised before today. It stood in the notice for the contractor's run certified earlier this year. The watch did not advise otherwise, and its instruments were zeroed with the load in them. The watch had no ground it knew of. It had the means. Practice of this watch from this date: no zero is accepted on a date; a zero is set against a reference or it is carried as a number.*

Under it, where the three words always went, there was one.

*Finding.*

Varn read it twice.

"You said a finding is something the watch finds."

"I did."

"You said we had found nothing. That a contractor heard it, and an office struck it, and a task force—"

"All of that is still so. None of that was ours." She capped the pen. "This is. It was in my own file, in my own tenure, and it took a Deputy striking two words to send me to read the small type. I have written *not a finding* under every entry since the ninth day so that I would know one when I had it. This is the one I have."

"It will go to the Starwarden."

"It will, and the Starwarden will write *noted* on it. Then it is on the Corps' record that a watch let its zero be set by the calendar, for as long as the Corps keeps one." She looked at the trace, which lay flat in a place nobody had yet asked about. "I told you that a referral noted is the opposite of nothing. I did not expect to be the one it was noted about. It is the same sentence."

The Deputy was standing nearer the desk than the first day, as on every day since the thirtieth, and had the sense not to say anything kind.

"Four days for the oversight to send the case," Smith said. "Then you go under the ring and stand where the request is carried out. Until then that line is flat and that is all it is. Put a number on the first line."

"There is no number. Nothing has been set beside it."

"Then say so on the first line. *Floor not yet asked.*" She pushed the log back across. "It is as good an entry as a number, provided you know which of them you have."

Varn wrote it. Past the hatch the trolley went by, ticking.
