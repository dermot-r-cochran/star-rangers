:::pov iklix-varn

On the ninety-sixth day the job was a form, and it was the same form.

Varn knew it before reading it, by the weight. Structural work on Eden that touches the approach monitoring is certified twice, and the work at the root of mount three had touched nothing else: the habitat's own engineers, under the original skin, taking out a bracket that had bent in 2825 and gone on carrying its load from the wrong place, and putting in one that had not. They had shored it within the week of the inspection, and then waited most of a season for the berth above to come out of service, and then done the work itself in two shifts. *Immediate*, Varn had learned, is a word about when a thing starts. Nobody had been invited for it. There had been no letter, no annex and no bird. The ring was the habitat's and the habitat had mended it, and for the whole of that time the Corps had been what it was on the Compact side every other day of the year, which was a courtesy with a hatch.

The watch had done the one thing that was the watch's. Two nights back, at ring's night, the senior engineer had come down from the Passage oversight with the case, alone this time, and Varn had gone under the ring to stand where the watch's request was carried out. The reference went onto mount five. Ninety seconds. The six agreed with each other and said normal, and the reference said normal, and the engineer wrote one figure in two columns and turned the slate round.

Varn had stood on the plating with both feet for the whole of it and felt nothing come up through them.

It was the same nothing. That was what Varn had carried back up the ring that night and not found a place for in the log. On the sixteenth day there had been a third again in that mount and a Deputy's feet had reported a floor. Now the slate said there was not, and the feet reported the same floor, in the same cold, under a mount that looked like the other five and had looked like the other five all along. Whatever had changed under the dock ring, Varn had been present for it twice and had perceived it neither time. The difference between the two nights was two columns that had disagreed and now did not.

So: the form. Varn took it from the tray standing, and it was heavier than a certification copy, because there were two documents in it.

Nobody had sent for the second one. Varn looked at the hatch, a little foolishly, as though the errand might still be waiting on the other side of it.

The certification said what certifications say. *Structural, approach mount three: root bracket replaced; load as certified. Approach monitoring: re-baselined against independent reference; reading normal. No items.* The log was the habitat engineers' own, in the habitat's hand, and it was longer, the way a log is. It agreed with the certification about everything the certification asked. Then it had one more line.

*Mount three, after remedial: whether it still rings, not known to this crew. Not a certification item.*

There was no initial beside it. Nobody had written *n/a*.

Varn read it twice, which a Deputy is allowed, and found that the sentence had changed while nobody was looking at it. On the ninth day *not a certification item* had been the sentence the whole ring was built out of, a way of saying that a true thing belonged to nobody, and Varn had carried it round the curve feeling the weight of that. But that had not been what lost the line. Two letters and an initial had lost it. The sentence itself had been written by someone who had no column for what they knew and wrote it down anyway, under the only heading that was honest, and here it was again in another crew's hand, about the same member, written by people who had read a file and had no better words than the ones in it.

They could not hear the mount. They had mended it and they could not hear it, and they had said so.

Varn set both documents on the desk, side by side, the way things were set on that desk.

"They both came," Varn said.

"So I see," Smith said.

:::pov zoe-smith

Smith read the log the way she read an approach trace: for what it matched, and for what it did not.

It matched. She wrote that first, as she had on the ninth day, though the Deputy at the desk no longer needed to watch an officer write down that most of a thing was nothing. Then she wrote the line that the certification did not carry, in the engineers' words and not hers, and beside it what the watch could truthfully add, which was shorter than last time: *observed by the habitat's engineers; not struck.*

There were three things she could have done with a line on the ninth day, and she had declined two. Today she could find only the one.

She looked for the others, because looking was the job. She could not withhold: there was nothing to clarify. The note said plainly what it meant, which was that the people who had mended the member did not have the means to know one thing about it. She could not refuse: the zero was the Corps' own and had been asked properly twice, and the second time it had answered what the certification said. And there was nothing to refer. A referral wants a discrepancy, and the two documents on her desk agreed about everything, including what neither of them knew.

Varn asked, from the desk, whether anyone could find out. Whether it still rang.

"Yes," Smith said. "There is an engineer at the Passage oversight who stood on that mount for one breath on the twenty-sixth day and said *root*. They could tell us in another." She did not look up. "Nobody has called them. Nobody will. The Corps does not come uncalled, and Five-O does not issue an invitation because a Field Officer would like to be surer than her record. There is nothing wrong with this record. It says what was done, what was measured, and what nobody on this ring is able to hear. That last is not a gap in it. That is the best line it has."

She had said, to herself, on the ninth day, that a thing which disappears under an honest signature is the one kind the Corps has never learned to find again, and had kept her pen off the leaf for seventeen days on the strength of it. She thought about that now with the pen in her hand. The signature would be honest today as it would have been then. The difference was not in the signature. The line was above it this time, in the open, unstruck, and a countersign that attests a record attests all of it, the part that says *not known* with the rest.

So she countersigned. Same hand, new reference number, and under it the sentence: *Not a finding.*

It occurred to her, writing it, that she had been writing the engineers' kind of sentence for most of a career. A thing stated by what it was not, so that what it was could stay on the page without anybody claiming more for it than they had. She had learned it from an officer on a station that no longer stood. She did not know where four contractors from the Skarn System had learned it, or two of Eden's engineers, and it was not the watch's question, and she left it.

"The distribution," Varn said, reading it upside down, which she noted and did not remark on, since it was the countersign's own leaf and the Deputy had carried it.

The countersign closed an item. The corrected certification, the one the habitat had issued on the twenty-sixth day for the contractor's run of members, had carried *remedial, immediate*, and a certification with an item open on it is not final. It was final now. The final copy went to every party of record, and the parties of record were printed at the foot in the Compact's order: the Commissioner, Eden; the reconciliation desk, Compact Administration; the fold-approach watch; the contractor of record.

"They will be told," Varn said.

"They will be sent a certification." Smith turned the leaf round. "It is what the Commissioner will be sent, and what we have been sent. It says the mount carries what it is certified to carry, and it is signed by a habitat and by a watch they have never met. They will have to decide what that is worth without standing on it." She capped the pen. "Which is a thing I would not wish on a Chthonari, and is what you and I do every shift. Take it round."

:::pov iklix-varn

Across the same ring, again, with a leaf.

The gallery over the dock was doing what it did. Varn looked up at the rail, from habit, and there was no raven on it and nobody else looked, and that was correct on both counts: no notification was filed downstairs, so there was nothing for a bird to mean. At the end of the quarter the desk at the Compact would lay five habitats' lines of structure side by side to see whether they agreed with themselves, and Eden's would, and a man who did not shake hands would read past it to the next thing. Varn found that this was not a disappointment. It was the ladder still standing where it had been left, with nobody on it.

The clerk at the Certification Office was the same clerk. He took the countersigned leaf across the long counter, checked the reference number against the wall of leaf-sets behind him, and apologised.

"For what?" Varn said.

The clerk considered it. "I thought something would be missing," he said. "It usually is, when you come."

"Nothing is missing. The log came up with the copy."

"Yes. I put it in." He said it as a fact about filing, not as a claim, and hung the leaf-set on its rail: the certification, the countersign, and the log behind them, in one file, where the next person to open it would not need a second. "The engineers asked whether their note wanted striking. I said I didn't know whose question that was." He looked, for a moment, like a man who has heard himself quote somebody. "So it went up as it was."

Varn thanked him, as on the ninth day, and went back out onto the ring.

On the way round, at the hatch on the Compact side that the residents walked past without seeing, the one that went down, Varn stopped. There was no reason to. A Deputy has no standing to go under the dock ring without the watch's name on a request, and Varn did not try the handle. Below it, and below that, a mount that looked like the other five was carrying what its record said, or was carrying something else and telling the truth about it to nobody. The record said the first. Six instruments and a reference said the first. Two crews of engineers said the first, and one of them had added, in writing, the exact extent of what they could not vouch for.

Varn could not hear it and would never be able to. On the sixteenth day that had been the reason for the case. It was still the reason for the case, and for the form, and for the hatch with the six lights behind it, and, Varn supposed, for the Deputy.

The watch does not watch anything. Varn had been told that on the first day and had taken it for a description of the instruments. It was a description of everyone on the ring, bar four people who had gone home. Nobody up here in the air could watch a mount. They could read what had been written about it by the last person who knew something, and decide how far to believe that, and sign their names to exactly as much.

Varn walked on round the curve to the side where the tab meant something, carrying nothing at all, and stood the rest of the shift.
