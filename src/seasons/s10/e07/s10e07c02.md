:::pov kattik

The certification came with the transit's other documents, and it came to Kattik, because the contractor of record is whoever keeps the log.

There were two leaves. In the Belt a thing from the air is struck out before it is anything else, line by line, in the flat register kept for other people's words, and Kattik lay along the wall of the contract hall with two limbs on the reader and took them in the order they were dated.

The first was a correction. It was the habitat's, and it replaced the certification the crew had left Eden with, the one that had said *within tolerance* and *reading normal* and *no items*. This one had an item. *Structural, approach mount three: load as measured, root bracket deformed, 2825 strike; remedial work immediate. Contractor's members: sound.* And below that, in the place where the first had carried nothing, a countersign from the fold-approach watch, and under the countersign three words that were not part of any form Kattik had been shown: *Not a finding.*

Kattik stayed on that leaf for some time.

*Root.* Under the original skin, then, below where the crew had been asked to cut, and older than the crew by three of the humans' years. Tikket had been right about the mount and wrong about what it meant, and Kattik had said so once and would not again, and now a habitat had said what it meant in its own hand. Something had struck a berth before any of the four had stood on a deck that was not home. A bracket had bent and gone on carrying. Nobody had cut anything too far. It was what the mount had been saying for six weeks to the one of them who could not get it into the air, and here it was, in the air, on a leaf, in a stranger's book.

*Rings under load. Noted. Not a certification item.* Kattik had memorised the line, as Kattik memorised everything. It was a certification item now. Somebody had decided whose.

The log's last entry still said by whom, *not known to this crew*, and the leaf did not alter that. It named offices. A habitat; a watch. No strike that was anyone's name. But the three words under the countersign were a hand, in the way a cut is a hand, and Kattik knew the cut. One says what a thing is not, exactly, so that what it is can be left standing with nothing claimed for it. The crew's own line had been made that way. Kattik had thought, writing it, that this was a thing a cutter does and the air does not. Somebody on that ring kept a log the way a log is kept. Kattik did not know who, and found that this was no longer the same as knowing nothing.

Then the second leaf, which was the difficulty.

It was the final. The item was closed: bracket replaced, the monitoring asked again against its second ear, the watch's countersign once more and the three words once more. And above all that, the plain statement the whole document existed to make. *Structural, approach mount three: load as certified. Approach monitoring: reading normal. No items.*

Kattik knew those words too. They were the first certification's, to the stroke.

The first time they had been less than the structure was doing. Kattik had stood on the mount and been told so by the feet. Now the same words had come back across a transit, about a member in a ring a long way down a gravity well, and there was no deck under them. Nothing in the Belt's rock carried Eden. Kattik could lie on this wall until the hall was recut and learn nothing about mount three that the leaf did not say.

The log was open. Its last line read: *Mount three line: read. By whom, not known to this crew. Mount still rings.*

That had been true when the deck gave it. Whether it was true now, the deck could not give, and a log does not carry what the log-keeper would like. It also does not stop one line short of the job. Kattik had never in a working life entered a thing that had not come up through the limbs. Tekka, who had felt the reader going and come along the wall to be near the argument, said that the words were the words and that settled it; then stopped, and said through the rock that this was the Eden answer and Tekka withdrew it, and waited to be given a better one.

Kattik wrote slowly, in the crew's hand, dated in the Belt's reckoning and the humans' both.

*Mount three: certified sound, final. Not stood on by this crew. Taken as read.*

Not *sound*. *Certified sound*, and how the crew came by it, and what the crew had done with it. A record that says more than the keeper knows has been cut as badly as one that says less. This one said what it had. Then Kattik struck the line through the wall for the other three, and for the youngest in particular, who was two tunnels off and would want to stand on it.

:::pov tikket

The wall said the mount was fine, and Tikket had no means at all of disbelieving it.

That was the first thing, arriving with Kattik's strikes through two tunnels of tuned iron: not relief, which would have been the easy note, but the lack of anywhere to put a limb. On Eden the deck had said *fine* and Tikket had stood on the third mount and known otherwise by the second day. Knowing otherwise had been lonely and had also been simple. One was in contact or one was not. Here was *certified sound, final*, clean as a cut, and under it nothing. No plating. A ring Tikket would not stand on again in this life, and a word about it.

Home was loud. Tikket had not expected that, coming back. Six weeks on a habitat nobody had tuned, and then the Belt, where every member was left to sound with every other and a question struck into the rock came back as the rock's own; for the first days it had been like being answered before one had finished asking. Nothing here needed a log line. If a member in this cluster carried more than it was left to carry, the whole cluster would know it before the shift turned and would argue about it in the walls. Nobody would have to write *noted*. Nobody would have to carry it anywhere.

Tikket had thought, on Eden, that this was what the humans lacked, and had been sorry for them, kindly, the way one is sorry for anyone working without a deck.

Lying in the home tunnel with Kattik's line still settling, Tikket understood where the humans had been standing the whole time. Here. Exactly here. With a word about a structure, and no way onto it.

Everybody who could decide anything on Eden had lived in the air, and Tikket had taken that for a kind of deafness that stopped them deciding well. But something had happened in the air, after the line went in. The leaves said so, in order. A line struck out. Then, by some route the crew would not learn, not struck out. A second ear set on the ring. A countersign refused, which was the humans' word, and Tikket remembered Tsikk reading it off the panel on the last shift. Engineers, two crews of them. A plate lifted that had not been lifted since the ring was built. A habitat writing *deformed* about its own member, in its own hand. And then, at the end, the same quiet words as at the beginning, set down by people not one of whom could hear a mount ring if they lay on it for a year.

They had done all of that from here. From where Tikket was now.

So Tikket did with the leaf what the burrow does with a member before the cutting stops, there being nothing else to do it with. *What is this still doing?* Not the mount. The record. What had it carried, and what had it been left to carry?

The first certification had been asked nothing. Six instruments agreeing with the thing they hung from, and a date. Tikket had been right not to believe it, and had been able to say why with the feet.

This one had been asked. It had been asked by something that could hear, set where the mount could not lean on it. It had been refused once by a hand that then signed it, and a hand that will refuse is worth more under a line than one that will not. It had been opened to the root. It said *as certified* in the same strokes as before and it was not the same record, any more than a member is the same member after the load comes off, though it lies in the same place and looks as it did.

That was a good deal. It was not hearing. Tikket laid the two beside each other, as far as two such things will lie together, and did not let either stand for the other.

"I believe it," Tikket said, through the wall, to Kattik. And then, because a log-keeper is owed the whole of a reading: "As far as it was asked. Not as far as standing on it."

"That is what I wrote," Kattik said.

"I know. I am saying I would have written it."

There was a silence in the rock, of the kind a tuned cluster does not often have. Then Tsikk, from somewhere further in, struck the old question into the wall, the one for Eden's mind, asked every shift for six weeks and answered every shift. It was not addressed to anything that could receive it. The Belt took it and gave it back as the Belt's own, a clean returning note with no reading in it, and Tsikk said that this was understood, and that one asks before one leaves, and that the leaving had only now finished.

Tikket lay and listened to home, which told the truth about itself all day to everyone on it. It was a great thing to be standing on. There was a mount a long way off that did the same, to nobody, and for that one there was a leaf, and people who would go on reading it and refusing it and signing it in the air.

The deck had said the habitat was fine, once, and Tikket had stopped believing it. The leaf said so now. Tikket went back to the cutting.
