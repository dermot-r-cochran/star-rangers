::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof, and it is about the second time a thing happens, which hardly anybody tells. The first time gets the telling. The second time gets *and then again*, at the end, in a smaller voice.

It was the working time after the two stones.

The warm had not settled. A name had grown in it, and that takes some sleeping on, and nobody had slept much. The small ones were saying *Stone-First-Who-Waited* to each other, very fast, to see who would trip. The old ones were telling the two stones to the ones who had been asleep. It was already a good telling. It had the light, and the kneeling, and a stone set down late, and a stone moved back.

And it had an end. That was the trouble with it.

"Will they do it again?" said the larger of the two who had tried the squeeze.

Nobody answered, so the small one asked Carried-It-Sleeping, who kept them, and who had stood nearer the up-people than anyone but Stone-First.

Carried-It-Sleeping thought about it properly, because it was a proper question.

There are two kinds of thing below the roof. There is a thing that happened. The first warm seep happened. The coming of the up-people happened. A thing that happened is told, and it has a beginning and an end, and nobody waits for it to come round. And there is a thing that is done. You move a stone first. You sit with a sleeping one. A thing that is done is not told at all, mostly. It is manners. It has no end, because you are going to do it next time too.

The up-people had moved a stone. Once.

"I don't know," said Carried-It-Sleeping, because saying so is also manners.

"Stone-First knows. Stone-First has gone up to wait for it."

"Stone-First has gone up to the line."

"That is not what I asked."

"No," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "It is the answer, though. I think. Go and trip over the name some more."

The small one went. Carried-It-Sleeping lay in the amber and felt the shape of the warm, with one piece out of it, the oldest piece, gone up the shelving the slow way before anyone was properly awake. It went up every time. Nobody had felt it go for so long that nobody felt it at all.

Above, a long way up, the loud came down the long passage.

::::: scene 2

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First was on the line.

Not waiting. Stone-First would have said so to anyone who asked, and nobody was there to ask. Waiting is for a thing you have been promised. Nothing had been promised. A stone had been moved, and a stone had been moved back, and that was a beginning, and a beginning does not tell you how long the telling is. So Stone-First kept the line, which from the outside looks the same as waiting and is not.

The two stones lay where they had been left. The up-people's, on the silt, a hand from where Stone-First's feet went. Stone-First's own, at the edge.

The loud came down the passage like water and reached the junction.

It did not settle. One piece of it came on.

Then the crack, and the scraping in it, one person's. Then the light, low, close to the ground. Then the small one out onto the silt, breathing hard, with a stone held to the front in both hands.

A different stone.

Stone-First had not thought about what the second time would look like. That was on purpose. But if Stone-First had thought, it would have been this: the up-person coming to the stone that was already there, and moving it. That is how it is done. You do not go looking for a stone to move. You move the one that is there, at the edge of the place. There is always one. The ground is made of them.

The up-person had brought their own. Again. All the way down the passage and through the crack, held to the front so it would not scrape.

They looked at Stone-First first. Feet, and then higher, quickly. Then at the stone from the time before, lying on the line. They looked at that for a while. Stone-First watched them look, and could not tell what the looking was. It might have been *that is mine*. It might have been *is that still here*.

Then they knelt, and set the new stone down beside the old one. Not on it. Not in its place. Beside.

The ground took it and said *someone*.

Late. They had been seen before the stone, the same as the first time. Stone-First noticed, and put it aside, the way Stone-First had once put aside a small one's stone that came late at the bottom of the squeeze. That one had learned. It had taken some tellings.

The up-person stood, and stepped back, and said the thing. It went up at the end. They waited.

Stone-First bent, and put a hand to the rock at the edge of the silt. The same rock. It was the stone that was there. And moved it, well.

The up-person went still, for as long as it takes to say a name. So that had not been only the first time either.

Then back through the crack, light low, and up.

Stone-First stood on the line with three stones on it now, and kept two things, without finishing either. The up-people had brought a new stone when the old one would have done. And they had not touched the old one. They had left it lying exactly where it was, as if it were not theirs to move.

Stone-First knew a rule that went like that. It was not possible that they knew it too. Nobody had told them anything.

::::: scene 3

::: pov went-round

The warm had heard it. *Someone*, from the line, small and late. Then *someone* again, properly. Everybody had stopped what they were doing, all the way to the far end of the amber, where Went-Round was.

Then everybody started talking, and the small ones were loudest.

"Again! They did it again!"

"So it is every time," said the larger one, to Carried-It-Sleeping, as if a thing had been won.

"It is twice," said Went-Round.

The small ones turned round. Went-Round is a long way to turn to, in the warm, and worth it about half the time.

"Twice is not every time. Twice is *again*. I have gone to sleep in the wrong place twice. Nobody calls me for it."

"Three, then," said the small one. "If they do it three."

"Is three every time?"

The small one thought. "Four."

"I am not laughing at you," said Went-Round, and was not. "I am going round it, because I cannot find the front. Listen. How many times has Stone-First moved a stone first?"

Nobody knew. An old one near the seep said there was no counting it.

"And when did we begin to say so? Which time was it, that made it *every time*?"

The old one thought for as long as a short telling takes, and said that nobody could remember Stone-First not doing it. As a small one, Stone-First had done it. In places nobody else was in.

"There," said Went-Round. "That is the far side. It is not a number. You do not get to every time by going up one at a time. It is what we say when nobody can remember the time it was not." A turn, the slow kind. "And with the up-people, everybody can remember the time it was not. It was all the time since they came below the roof. It is in a telling. The small ones know it by heart."

The warm was quiet. It was a hard thing to find at the back of something that had been so good from the front.

"Then it can never be every time for them," said the small one. "However many."

"I did not say I liked it," said Went-Round.

"It can be every time *since*," said the old one by the seep.

Went-Round turned that over. Nobody below the roof had ever needed to say *every time since*. For the Told there had been no *since*. The manners had no first time that anybody could tell, which was what made them manners.

"That is a new thing to say," said Went-Round. "I will keep it. I do not know yet whether it is a small thing or a large one."

Several people made the small sound that means *that is a thing worth saying*. The warm did not take it further. Twice is not enough to argue about, and everybody knew where the next piece of the argument would come from. It would come down the long passage, or it would not.

::::: scene 4

::: pov teddy

So the third time, the warm waited.

Carried-It-Sleeping had not known a whole people could wait at once. It is not like one person waiting. Nobody says anything about it. The old ones went on with what they were doing, slowly, so as to be doing something. The small ones did not even pretend. They sat in a heap in the amber with their heads up, facing the shelving.

The loud came down, a long way up.

It reached the junction. Carried-It-Sleeping could tell by the way it stopped getting nearer. And then there was a time, not long, when it had not settled and had not come on, and that was the time the whole warm was waiting in.

*Someone*, said the ground, from the line. Small, and late.

The small ones made a noise all together. Then they stopped, because there was a second part, and they wanted to hear it.

*Someone*, said the ground. Properly. The way a thing is said by a person who has said it all their life.

"Three," said the larger small one, and looked across the warm at Went-Round. Went-Round held up nothing, and said nothing, and was plainly keeping a number of things.

One of the littler ones got up out of the heap. Carried-It-Sleeping watched, ready to say *no*, because a littler one getting up usually means the squeeze.

It was not the squeeze. The littler one walked into the middle of the amber, and stood, and then knelt down. Slowly, both knees, the way Stone-First told it, the way the up-person knelt. And put both hands out and down to the ground, and set down nothing at all. There was nothing in the hands. Then stood up, and stepped back one step, and waited, looking very pleased.

Nobody had shown them. They had only been told. The other small ones laughed, and then three of them tried it too, and did it worse.

Carried-It-Sleeping let them. And lay back, and found the thing that had been underneath the waiting all this time.

The whole warm had been asking one question. Would the up-people come again? The small ones had asked it out loud. Went-Round had gone all the way round it. Carried-It-Sleeping had lain awake on it. Three working times, and everybody below the roof with their head up, wanting to know whether a stone would be moved at the line.

And all three times, before anybody knew, one of the people had already gone up the shelving to stand there.

Nobody had asked whether Stone-First would. Not the small ones, who ask everything. Not Went-Round. It had not come into anyone's head to ask. If the light had not come, the second time, there would have been one of the Told on the line in the open with nobody looking, the same as on all the times before the stones, and Stone-First would have come down to sleep and gone up again.

So that was what every time was. Went-Round was right that it is not a number. But it is not only a thing you say when you cannot remember. It is the question nobody asks about you.

Nobody was asking it about the up-people yet. They were asking the other one, the loud one, with their heads up.

Carried-It-Sleeping did not say any of this. It was not finished, and Went-Round would only have gone round it.

::: pov stone-first

On the line there were four stones now. Three of them lay side by side on the silt, each a hand from the next, each where it had been set. Stone-First had moved none of them and was not going to.

A long way below, the warm was loud, and then less loud. Stone-First could tell. Somebody down there had been counting.

Stone-First had counted once. The askings, one a working time, said to the dark. It had been the only count in a whole life, and it had been let go when the thing counted stopped being nothing. Stone-First did not take it up again now. There was no need. The stones were there on the silt for anyone who wanted a number.

What Stone-First had instead was smaller. The up-person, coming out of the crack, looked at Stone-First's feet first. Every time so far. Before the stone, before the kneeling. As if that were the thing they had come down the passage not knowing.

Stone-First stood where the feet went, so that it would be there to find.
