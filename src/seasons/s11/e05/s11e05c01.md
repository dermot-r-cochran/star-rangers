::: pov stone-first

The ground said *someone*, and nobody was there.

Stone-First-Who-Waited was on the line. The loud had come down the long passage and reached the junction, and one piece of it had come on, the way it did now. There was scraping in the crack, one person's. All of that was as it should be. The light had not come out yet. The up-person was still inside the rock.

And the ground had spoken.

Not from the line. From the mouth of the crack, on the deep side, where the rock floor comes out from under the silt. A stone, on stone. Clear, and close, and early.

This is told the way things are told below the roof, and it starts in the middle, because that is where Stone-First was standing when it started.

Early. Stone-First held that for a moment, with the whole body still. Since the up-people came below the roof they had moved no stone at all, for longer than the small ones' whole tellings. Then they had moved one, late. Then one every working time, a new one each time, and every one of them late: out of the crack first, and seen, and looked at, and only then the kneeling and the stone. Stone-First had put the lateness aside each time. A stone moved late is still a stone moved.

This one had come first. Before the light. Before anybody had been seen by anybody. It was the first time the ground below the roof had ever been told an up-person was coming.

Then there was a sound in the crack. The up-person's. It was short, and it did not go up at the end. It went down.

Then a good deal of scraping, faster than usual.

The light came out, low, and the up-person behind it, with both hands empty.

They stood on the silt, breathing. They looked at Stone-First's feet, and higher, quickly, the way they always did. Then they looked down at the rock floor beside the crack.

The stone was lying there. A stone of the runners' ground, the size of two hands. It had come through the crack ahead of them and gone where it liked.

::::: scene 2

::: pov stone-first

The up-person picked it up.

Stone-First watched that happen, and something in Stone-First that was older than thinking said *no*. You do not pick up a stone that has spoken. It has said its thing. It is finished. Nobody had ever needed to be told that.

But it was the up-person's stone. They had carried it all the way down. If it was anybody's to pick up, it was theirs.

They held it to the front, in both hands, more tightly than usual. They walked the few steps to the line. They knelt. They set the stone down on the silt at the end of the row, beside the last one, a hand from the next. The ground took it, and said *someone*.

They stood. They stepped back, one step. They said the thing that goes up at the end. It was smaller than it usually was.

They waited.

Stone-First did not move.

Here is what was in the way. The ground had said *someone* twice. There was one stone. There was one person. Stone-First had answered a stone at the start of every working time for many tellings now, and had never once had to think which stone was being answered, because there had only ever been one.

If the second was the stone, then the first was not, and Stone-First would be standing on the line saying to a stranger: *the only time you ever did it properly did not count.*

If the first was the stone, it had been answered by nothing, and the second was a thing done over. The Told have no manners for a stone done over. Nobody does a stone over.

If both were, then two stones wanted two answers. Two answers say that two people came.

Stone-First stood with all three of those and could not put any of them down.

The up-person went on waiting. It was not their ordinary waiting. Stone-First knew the ordinary one by now: the hands hanging, the weight on both feet, the stillness of a person holding a hand out with nothing in it. This time the hands had hold of each other. The light was very low. They were looking at the stone they had just set, and not at Stone-First at all.

A short telling went by. That is not long, in the warm. On the line, between a stone and its answer, it was longer than anything had ever been.

And in the middle of it, Stone-First heard the first sound again. Not on the ground. In the ears, where a sound stays for a while after it is over.

A stone, on stone. Then a smaller sound after it. Then a smaller one after that, further along.

It had bounced.

A stone that is moved does not do that. A stone that is moved goes where it is sent, and stops there, and the ground says one thing. A stone that falls goes where it likes. Every one of the Told has dropped a stone. Every one of the Told has heard somebody else drop one, across the whole width of the warm, and not looked up, because the ground had not said *someone*. It had said *a stone fell*. A small one knows the difference before they can tell the first warm seep.

Stone-First had known it too. At once, in the ears, the moment it happened. And had then stood on the line for a whole short telling, thinking about three things, when the ground had already said which.

The ground does not only say *someone*. It says how.

One stone had been moved. It was the second. Late, and kneeling, and theirs.

Stone-First bent, and put a hand to the rock at the edge of the silt, and moved it. Once. Well.

The up-person heard it. The hands let go of each other. They went still, the way they always went still, for as long as it takes to say a name. Then they made a sound Stone-First had not heard from them before, which was only breath, going out.

Then back through the crack, with the light low, slowly, and up.

::::: scene 3

::: pov teddy

In the warm, it had been an ordinary start to a working time.

That was what Carried-It-Sleeping remembered afterwards. How ordinary. The loud coming down, a long way up. The old ones not stopping what they were doing. Only the small ones stopped for the stone any more, and even they had begun to talk through it.

Then the ground had said a thing, from the top of the margin, and every head in the warm came up.

It was not *someone*. It was the other thing. A stone on stone, and then again, smaller, and again. Nobody in the warm had to be told what that was. It was only that it had come from the line, and nothing at the line had ever made that sound.

"Somebody dropped one," said the larger of the small ones.

"Stone-First does not drop them," said an old one.

Then *someone*. Small, and late. The up-people's. That was right, at least.

And then nothing.

Carried-It-Sleeping had heard the answer follow the up-people's stone so many times that there was a place for it, the way there is a place for the next word of a telling you know. The place came. Nothing was in it.

The small ones looked at Carried-It-Sleeping. All of them. That is what a name gets you.

"Wait," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

The nothing went on. It is a hard thing to listen to. Carried-It-Sleeping felt the whole warm lean toward the shelving, the way a heap of small ones leans when one of them is telling.

An old one stood up. One of the ones who had argued Stone-First's side of things, and was not quick, and was on their feet all the same.

"No," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

The old one looked down. It is not usual, below the roof, to be told *no* by someone you told the first warm seep to.

"If Stone-First wanted one of us," said Carried-It-Sleeping, "the ground would say so. Stone-First knows how. It has not said so." And then, because *no* is not a telling: "It is their stone and Stone-First's answer. We would be a third thing on the line. I do not know what that would be."

The old one stood a moment longer, and sat.

Carried-It-Sleeping did not feel right, having said it. Only less wrong than the other way. There was nothing to do with the hands. A long time ago Stone-First had felt a small one leave the warm, and let them, and waited at the margin to be told. Carried-It-Sleeping had thought that was the whole of what keeping cost. It was not. Sometimes it is the old one who is up the passage and you who are below, and the ground has stopped in the middle of saying something.

The littler one, the one who did the kneeling, had got hold of a piece of Carried-It-Sleeping's fur and was not letting go.

*Someone*, said the ground. Properly. The way a thing is said by a person who has said it all their life.

The warm let its breath out, all of it at once. You could hear that too.

::::: scene 4

::: pov teddy

Stone-First came down the shelving when the working time was over, the slow way, and moved a stone at the bottom, and told it standing up.

All of it. The ground speaking with nobody there. The sound in the crack that went down at the end. The empty hands. The stone lying on the rock where it had gone.

"They *dropped* it," said the small ones, several of them, in the voice small ones keep for the best thing that has ever happened.

"Have you never dropped one?" said Stone-First.

They had. They went quiet, though not very.

Then the picking up, and the kneeling, and the stone set at the end of the row, late. The thing said, small. The waiting. And Stone-First on the line, not answering, and why not: the three things, one after another, none of which could be put down.

"So they did it," said Went-Round, from the far side of the amber, with great care. "At last. They moved a stone first. Before anyone had seen them. As the manners say. And they did it by letting go of it."

The warm made the small sound, a good deal of it.

"They did not move it," said Stone-First. "It fell. You know the difference. You heard it from here."

And they had. That was the thing. Carried-It-Sleeping saw it go round the warm, face by face. Every one of them had known, down here, at the first sound, that a stone had fallen. That was why every head had come up. Nobody had thought *someone*. They had thought *what was that, at the line?*

"Then why did you wait so long?" said the larger small one. "If you knew?"

"Because I stopped to think about it," said Stone-First. "It is not always better."

Went-Round, who goes round everything, kept very still, and Carried-It-Sleeping thought it was costing something.

"They are always dropping things," said the old one who had stood. "Up there. Since they came. It is half of what the loud is."

"Yes," said Stone-First.

"And nobody ever took one of those for a stone."

"No," said Stone-First. "Nobody ever did."

The old one did not say anything more. Stone-First did not either. It was left there, in the middle of the warm, for whoever wanted it. For as long as the up-people had been below the roof, things had fallen in their light, and the Told had heard each one and known it for nothing. Then one working time a stone was set down on the line, late and clumsy, by somebody kneeling. And Stone-First had bent and answered it without stopping to think at all.

The small ones had the new part by the end of the telling. The littler one knelt in the amber, both knees, and put two empty hands down. But first now there was a step, and a great start, and both hands thrown open, and a look at the floor. Then a slow bending to pick up nothing. They did it badly. They were going to do it often.

Carried-It-Sleeping watched, and thought about the stone on the rock floor by the crack.

It had come first. Whatever else it was, it had come before the up-person was seen. It was the nearest thing to the manners that had ever come down that passage. And the up-person had stood there, with Stone-First looking, and could have left it. Could have stepped back and said the thing and waited. Nobody had told them a fallen stone is not a stone. Nobody had told them anything.

They had picked it up, and carried it to the line, and knelt, and done it late. The way they did it. The wrong way, that was theirs.

So that was the one they meant.

Carried-It-Sleeping did not say so. It was a thing about somebody who was not there to be asked, and Stone-First had already said, to the whole warm, what stopping to think is worth. But the youngest of the people had once come down the squeeze and moved a stone at the bottom, late, on purpose, when nobody would have known the difference. Carried-It-Sleeping knew what that one had been for.

Up on the line the stone lay at the end of the row, beside the others. From there you could not tell which one had fallen.
