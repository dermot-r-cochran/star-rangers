::::: scene 1

::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof: by someone who was there, to someone who was not, until the one who was not can tell it too. And this time it is about that last part, which is the part nobody watches happen.

It was many tellings after the two stones.

Here is what the warm was like by then. At the start of every working time the up-people's loud came down the long passage, a long way up, and settled. Then, small and late, the ground said *someone* from the line. That was their stone. Then, before the ground had finished saying it, the ground said *someone* again, properly, the way a thing is said by a person who has said it all their life. That was Stone-First-Who-Waited, answering. The warm heard both. At first everybody stopped what they were doing to hear it. Then only the small ones stopped. Then nobody did, which is how you know a thing has stopped being news and become the people's.

Carried-It-Sleeping kept the small ones. There were more of them than two now who wanted keeping, and they had all been told the two stones so many times that any of them could do the up-person kneeling, which they did badly, and often.

So they wanted a different one.

"The first one," said the smaller of the two who had tried the squeeze, the one who had stood on the line. "From before. With the lights."

"*Nobody looked up*," said another.

"That one."

Carried-It-Sleeping had told that one more times than anybody had counted. It was short. It had been made short on purpose, standing in the amber in front of Stone-First, a long time ago, and it had been the first thing in the deep that was Carried-It-Sleeping's own.

"I will tell it," said somebody.

It was the other one. The larger of the two who had tried the squeeze: the one who had been caught at the foot of it every time, so that the smaller could get halfway up the margin. Who had never been past the squeeze, and never stood on the line, and had never been anywhere first.

The small ones looked at Carried-It-Sleeping, because it was Carried-It-Sleeping's telling.

But it was not. That was the thing about a telling. It was yours when someone had heard it, and after that it was the people's, and the people were allowed to tell it. Carried-It-Sleeping had known this for a long time. Knowing it had never yet been tested by a small one standing up.

"Tell it," said Carried-It-Sleeping, and lay down in the warm, which is where you go to be told.

::::: scene 2

::: pov teddy

The small one stood in the amber, with the whole body still, the way the old ones stand. It was a good stillness. Somebody had been watching Stone-First.

"The youngest went up," said the small one, "and moved no stone."

Several of the small ones made a sound. It was not the small sound that means *that is a thing worth saying*. It was the other one, the one small ones make when somebody in a telling does a thing they have all been told not to do.

Carried-It-Sleeping lay very still.

That was not how it began. It began with the squeeze. It had always begun with the squeeze, and the runners' air, and the light that moves. The stone that was not moved had never been in the telling at all. It had been in what Stone-First said afterwards, *you did not move a stone*, once, and never again.

But it had happened. A telling can begin anywhere in what happened. And to a small one who had moved a stone, loudly, at the foot of the squeeze, every time they were sent back from it, there was only one place this telling could begin.

"Through the squeeze," said the small one, "and along the long passage, to the light that moves. There were three of them. They were small and loud. They looked at their lights. One knelt by the humming stone. One laughed. The youngest stood on the open floor, at the edge of the light, for the first warm seep, twice. The light went over the youngest's feet. And nobody looked up."

A breath.

"Then the youngest came down, and moved a stone at the bottom. Late."

That was the end. The small one stayed standing a moment longer, to be sure it was, and then sat down very fast.

It was shorter than Carried-It-Sleeping's. There was no squeeze scraping in it and no cold air with the roof in it. Carried-It-Sleeping had loved those parts, and found that the telling stood up without them.

"Why did nobody look up?" said one of the littler ones, the one who did the kneeling most.

Everybody looked at Carried-It-Sleeping, who had been there.

"They cannot hear the ground," said Carried-It-Sleeping. It was the finding. It had been not theirs, and then theirs, and they had carried a humming stone down the squeeze on the strength of it.

"The small up-person can," said the one who had stood on the line. "Stone-First moved the rock, and they heard it. They went still. Stone-First said. It is in the telling."

"From a hand away."

"How far away were you?"

Carried-It-Sleeping thought of the light that moves, swinging across the floor, touching their feet and going on.

"Not far," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

"Then if you had moved a stone —"

"I don't know," said Carried-It-Sleeping, because saying so is also manners. And then, because *I don't know* is not a telling: "I did not move one. So nobody was there for the time I did. There was not a time."

The small ones considered this. It is a hard thing to be given, a hole in a telling the shape of a stone, by the one person who could have filled it. The littler one took it best, and went back to kneeling.

::::: scene 3

::: pov went-round

Went-Round had been listening from the far side of the amber, which is where Went-Round listens from, and had already started round.

It was a good one to go round. It had been sitting in the warm for a whole season, in plain sight, being told over and over, and nobody had walked to the back of it.

"They came below the roof," said Went-Round, "and moved no stone. All three. Loud. And what did we say of them?"

"That they have not been told," said an old one.

"That they have not been told. And we left them the place. Good." Went-Round turned, the way Went-Round turns when the far side of a thing is coming into view. "And then one of us went up into *their* place. And moved no stone."

Nobody said anything.

"And that one had been told. Many times. By somebody who is called what they are called for a reason."

The warm made the small sound, a good deal of it. Went-Round looked to see how Carried-It-Sleeping was taking it. Carried-It-Sleeping was taking it lying down, with the whole body still, the way you take a true thing said of you in front of everybody. It was not said unkindly. Went-Round did not know how to say a thing unkindly; it would have meant going straight at it.

"So here is what I find at the back," said Went-Round. "There has been one time, in all the tellings, when one of us went to them the way they come to us. Walked in. No stone. Stood in the open. One time. And what did they do about it?"

"Nothing," said the small one who had told it. "They looked at their lights."

"They went on working," said Went-Round. "Which is, if you go round to it, what we did when *they* came. We went on. We left them to it. We did not go and stand in front of them and say, *you did not move a stone.*" A pause, the length of going round one more corner. "I do not say it is the same. I say I cannot find the side it is different from."

It was a long way round, and Went-Round was pleased with it, and waited for the warm to take it up.

The warm did not. An old one at the edge of the amber said that the up-people had not left anybody anything, they had only not turned round, and not turning round is not manners, it is having your back to a thing. Another said that you could not know what a people meant by a thing they did not do. That was two old ones on Stone-First's side of a question with Stone-First not even in the warm, and Went-Round, who liked a thing to be argued, let it lie where it fell.

Carried-It-Sleeping got up.

"Where are you going?" said the small ones, all of them, because that is what you say to the one who keeps you.

"Up the shelving. I will move a stone at the top. You will hear it." Carried-It-Sleeping looked at the small one who had told it. "It is yours now as well. Somebody should say so to the one who heard it first."

Went-Round watched them go, and felt the warm change shape, the way it does. The youngest had gone up once with nothing to tell and no stone moved. This was the same one, going up the slow way, over ground that announces you, to carry a telling they had not even told.

That was a far side too. Went-Round kept it. Not every one needs saying.

::::: scene 4

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First-Who-Waited heard the stone at the top of the shelving. A good one, moved well, and by somebody not small any more. You can tell.

It was between working times. The up-people's loud had gone away up the long passage toward the cold some while before, and the line was as the line was. The two stones, one on each side of the place where the feet had stopped. The shapes of feet in the silt. And along the line, each where it had been set down, the others.

There was one for every working time since the first. The small up-person brought a new one each time, held to the front, and knelt, and set it down a hand from Stone-First's feet, and left it. They never took the last one away. Stone-First did not know why they brought a new one every time, as if every time were the first. Perhaps where they came from a stone could only speak once. Stone-First had not moved any of them. A stone that has been moved for you is not yours to move. So they lay where they lay, side by side along the silt, and the line, which had only ever been a place where feet stopped, was turning into a thing you could see.

Carried-It-Sleeping came onto the silt and stood, not on the line, a step below it.

"A small one told the first one," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "Mine. Standing up. The larger of the two."

"Tell me how they told it."

Carried-It-Sleeping told it. It did not take long. *The youngest went up, and moved no stone.* The three. The lights. Twice through the first warm seep. *And nobody looked up.* The stone at the bottom, late.

Stone-First listened with the whole body still.

"Is it shorter?" said Stone-First.

"Yes."

"Then it is getting true."

Carried-It-Sleeping looked at the row of stones for a while.

"You asked me a thing, the first time," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "I said they cannot hear the ground. You said, that is a thing you know now, it is not a thing they know. You asked if I saw the difference. I said no."

"You said so. That was manners."

"I thought you meant I was cleverer than them." Carried-It-Sleeping made the small sound, at their own expense, which is the only expense it is ever made at. "I had found a thing out about them and they had not found it out about themselves."

"And now?"

"Now a small one asks me why nobody looked up, and I say, they cannot hear, and the small one says, did you move a stone? And I did not. So I do not know what they can hear from where I stood. I only know what they do when nobody has told them anybody is there." A breath. "It was never a telling about their ears. It had a stone missing from it, and the stone was mine."

Stone-First did not say *yes*. Stone-First hardly ever did. A thing agreed with is a thing you have been told.

"They do not hear the ground from far," said Stone-First. "That is still true. You carried their stone down on it, and you were right to. Keep it." Then: "What does Went-Round say?"

Carried-It-Sleeping said what Went-Round said, all the way round.

Stone-First thought about it for as long as a short telling takes, which for Stone-First is quick.

"We know what they did," said Stone-First. "They looked at their lights. We do not know what they did not do. Nobody can tell a thing that was not done; there is nobody who was there for it. Went-Round has gone round a place with nothing standing in it." And, after a moment, because it was fair: "It is a good place to have gone round. Somebody should know where it is."

"I will say so."

"Say it from me. Went-Round will enjoy that less." And Stone-First made the small sound, which Carried-It-Sleeping had heard perhaps three times in a whole telling.

Carried-It-Sleeping went down, and moved a stone at the bottom.

::::: scene 5

::: pov stone-first

Then the working time began, the way it does.

The loud came down the long passage and settled at the junction, and one piece of it came on, down the third passage, to the crack. The scraping, one person's. The light, low, coming out onto the silt close to the ground, moving the way a person moves who is trying not to throw anything.

The smallest of the three. Stone-First knew them now the way you know the shape of a warm place: by where they stopped to get their breath after the squeeze, and how they held the stone, to the front, and that they looked at Stone-First's feet first and then, once, quickly, higher.

They knelt. They set the stone down a hand from Stone-First's feet, beside the last one. The ground took it and said what the ground says. They stood, and stepped back, and said the thing that goes up at the end, and waited, the way you wait for a hand to be filled.

Stone-First bent and moved the rock at the edge of the silt. A good one. Well.

The up-person heard it. They always went still for it, for about as long as it takes to say a name. Stone-First had wondered, the first time, whether that would wear off. It had not.

Then back through the crack, with the light held low, and up.

Stone-First stood on the line.

There had been a shape to things, once. Stone-First had thought about it on the night the youngest came down with a first telling, a long time ago. The people knew where the up-people were. The up-people did not know where the people were. That was the shape, and it had held since the up-people came below the roof, and when a small one stood close enough to be touched by their light it was still the same shape, only closer.

It was not that shape now.

It was not very different. That was the thing Stone-First noticed, standing there. The up-people were a long way up, being loud, with their backs to the dark. The people were below, in the warm. Nobody had crossed the line. Nobody had told anybody anything. If you had come up the shelving on most of the tellings of a working time you would have found one of the Told standing in the open with nobody looking, exactly as the youngest once stood, and you might have thought nothing had changed below the roof at all.

But once a working time, for as long as it takes a hand to be filled, somebody came to the edge of the place and said so first. And somebody was there to hear it. And each of them knew where the other was standing.

That was all. It was one place, and one short time, and a row of stones that were nobody's to move.

Three small loud people who looked at their lights and never turned round. It had been a true telling, and it still was. Stone-First had asked for it twice, the first night, and had it by heart. It was only that it had turned out to be the telling of the time before a stone. Every telling has one of those in front of it. Mostly nobody was there.

Below, a long way down, the warm had settled. Stone-First could tell. Somewhere in it a small one who had never been past the squeeze was telling *nobody looked up* again, standing up, to whoever had been asleep the first time, and getting it a little shorter.

By someone who was not there. To someone who was not there.

That is how you know it is the people's.
