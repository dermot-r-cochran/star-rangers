::::: scene 1

::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there. It is about a thing everyone was there for, and it begins with two small ones who could not agree about it, which was not supposed to be possible.

"It was looking at me," said one of the small ones.

"It was looking at *me*," said the other.

This was in the warm, in the middle, on a time when nothing was happening. They meant the bright one. That is not a word. It is only what the small ones call it, because it was bright: the time the amber went out of the near wall and stood in the open floor in the shape of a person, made of light, and did nothing, and went back into the wall. The whole of the people had been in the warm for it.

Carried-It-Sleeping was keeping the small ones, lying down, and did what you do with an argument below the roof.

"Who was there?"

"I was," said the first small one.

"I was," said the second.

Carried-It-Sleeping waited for the rest. There was no rest. They had both been there. Everyone had been there. An argument is settled by who was there, and it always has been, and Carried-It-Sleeping had never once heard the question come back with two answers and both of them true.

"Where were you lying?" said Carried-It-Sleeping, to have something to say.

One had been against the near wall. The other had been across the open floor, by the old ones. They had been on opposite sides of it.

"Then one of you saw the back," said another small one, pleased.

"It was not the back. It was looking at me."

So Carried-It-Sleeping moved a stone, a small one, for Went-Round, on the far side of the amber, because Went-Round had done the one thing nobody else had. Went-Round had walked all the way round it.

Went-Round came and listened to both of them, and took a while.

"It had no back," said Went-Round. "I went round it. It was the same from behind as from in front. I said so at the time and nobody thought it mattered."

"So it was looking at both of us."

"Or at neither of you. I saw every side of it there was, and I cannot tell you it was looking at anyone. I cannot tell you it was looking." Went-Round thought. "Say what you saw. Not what it did. *The side toward me was the same as the side toward you.*"

The two small ones tried it. It was true all the way through, and neither of them liked it as much.

::::: scene 2

::: pov teddy

The smallest had been listening.

The smallest had been quiet for a good many tellings, and was not quiet now.

"When do we tell it to the ones not born yet?"

That was Went-Round's saying, from the time itself. Nobody could tell the bright one, because a telling goes from the one who was there to the one who was not, and there was nobody who was not. So it would be told to the ones not born yet. Everyone had heard Went-Round say it. Nobody had worked out how.

"When they are here," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

"Where are they now?"

"Nowhere yet."

The smallest thought about people who were nowhere yet. You could see it was hard going.

"Then who tells it, when they are somewhere?"

"Whoever is here."

"That will be me," said the smallest. "I am the smallest. I will be here the longest."

It was true, in the way things the smallest says are true, which is from a side nobody else was standing on. And the smallest got up, right there, in the middle, to find out whether it could be done, and told it standing up.

"The amber went out of the wall and stood in the middle of the warm..."

"The wall went dim first," said an old one, from the edge.

"It was not all amber. It was blue-green down one side."

"You have left out the ring. We left a ring."

"It stood for half a telling."

"Less."

"You were asleep when it came."

"I was *not*."

It went on like that. The smallest stood in the amber with the mouth open and the telling in pieces all round, and every piece was somebody's who had been there, and every one of them was putting theirs in.

Carried-It-Sleeping saw what was wrong, lying there, and it was not that the smallest told it badly.

Nobody was listening. They were all checking. When you tell a thing to someone who was not there, they hold still and take it, and that is how you find out whether it stands up by itself. People who were there do not let it stand. They hold it up for you, from every side, without meaning to, and you never find out.

The smallest sat down.

"I cannot tell it to them," said the smallest. "They keep having been there."

A stone moved, at the bottom of the shelving. Good, and plain. The working time was over, and the loud had gone up the long passage toward the cold, and Stone-First-Who-Waited was coming down to sleep.

Stone-First came into the amber and into the noise.

"What is being told?"

"The bright one," said Went-Round. "By everybody."

"The wall went dim first," said the old one at the edge, again, so as to have it heard.

Stone-First looked at the old one for as long as it takes to say a name.

"What wall?" said Stone-First.

The warm went quiet.

::::: scene 3

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First-Who-Waited had been on the line when it came.

Everyone knew that. Stone-First stays on the line long after the loud has gone, and comes down late. But nobody had thought about what it meant, and neither had Stone-First, until an old one said *the wall* as if there was only one thing a wall could have done.

Stone-First had felt the warm go thin in the middle, from the line, and had come down. When Stone-First came into the amber the thing was already standing. Stone-First had looked at it, and at the people, and at nothing else. The near wall had been on the other side of it the whole time, and Stone-First had not once looked.

"I was not there for the wall," said Stone-First. "Tell me."

Nobody spoke. Then Carried-It-Sleeping, who listens lying down, got up, and told it standing.

"It was the time after the working. Went-Round was telling the two stones. I looked at the near wall, where the small ones sleep, and the amber on it had gone thin. Like a telling going quiet near its end. The thin part had a shape. It was the wall with something taken out of it. Then I looked at the middle, and the something was standing there."

That was all of it. It was short.

And nobody put anything in. Stone-First noticed that. Most of the warm had been there for the wall, and a breath ago every one of them had a piece to add. Now they lay still and let it be told, because it was being told to someone who had not been there, and you do not help a telling on its way to someone like that. You wait to see whether it gets there.

It got there. Stone-First could see the wall go thin, who had not seen it.

"Who was there?" said Stone-First, because somebody has to say it.

"I was," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "Everyone was. I looked at the wall first."

"Then that piece is yours, and now I have it." Stone-First stood a while. "I have one too."

Stone-First hardly ever tells a thing. The warm lay very still.

"I was on the line. From the line you feel the whole shape of the warm, all the pieces, the small ones thick in the middle. And in the thick of it there was a place with nothing in it, the size of a person. I came down the shelving faster than I go. At the bottom I moved a stone. A good one. The ground said *someone is coming*, to all of you."

Stone-First stopped.

"And not one of you turned."

Nobody said anything for a long breath.

"I heard it," said the old one at the edge, slowly. "I heard the stone. I knew whose it was." The old one seemed to be looking at something a long way down. "I did not turn. I have never not turned for a stone."

"No," said Stone-First. "None of you ever has. I came into the amber and saw the whole of the people with their backs to a stone. Nobody has seen that before. And I was the only one placed to see it, because all of you were facing the other way."

"Were you cross?" said a small one.

"No. I was careful."

::::: scene 4

::: pov went-round

Went-Round lay on the far side of the amber and went round the thing they had all been saying.

*Everyone was there.* They had said it since the wall. It was the reason nobody could tell it. And it was true, and Went-Round had gone round it more than once and found no other side.

It had one. Stone-First had walked in and asked what wall.

Everyone had been there. But Carried-It-Sleeping had been there for the wall, and Stone-First had not. Stone-First had been there for a stone and a people's backs, and nobody else had, because the backs were theirs. Went-Round had been there for the far side of the thing, and for how near the gap in the warm felt from a step away, and nobody else had gone to look. The small ones nearest it had been there for looking straight up at it. The old ones across the floor had watched it go back into the wall from behind, with the wall in front of them the whole time.

So nobody had been there for all of it. Not one. Each of them had a piece, and for every other piece they were someone who had not been there.

Went-Round got up and said so, to the warm.

"We can tell it. We could always tell it. Not all at once to nobody. A piece at a time, each piece from the one who was there for it, to everyone who was not."

"That is just telling," said the smallest.

"Yes," said Went-Round. "I went a long way round to get back to it."

They did it then. It took a long while, and nobody minded. Carried-It-Sleeping told the wall again. Stone-First told the line, and the stone, and the backs. Went-Round told the going round, and that it was the same from behind. A small one who had lain nearest told how big it was from underneath. An old one told the light going back into the wall, and the wall brightening by just that much.

And each time, the ones who had been there for that piece wanted to put something in, and the ones who had not lay still, and so did they, in the end.

One of the small ones wanted a piece that said it was kind.

"Whose piece is that?" said Stone-First. "Who was there when it was kind?"

The small one looked round the warm. Nobody had that piece. Nobody had one that said it was unkind either. So it was not put in. Not because it was not so. Because nobody had been there for it.

"And what it was," said Went-Round, mostly to the amber. "I went nearer than anyone and I have no piece of that. Nor has anybody. So the telling has its edges now. How it came, how it went, what it looked like from the line. It still has no middle."

Nobody made the small sound. It was not that kind of thing. It was only put together, so that it could be carried.

The smallest had listened to every piece with the whole body still, the way the old ones do.

::::: scene 5

::: pov teddy

Afterwards, when the warm had the shape of people lying down, the smallest told it.

Not standing up. Lying beside Carried-It-Sleeping, under the breath, which is not how a telling is told. It was only trying it, to see whether it would hold.

The wall going thin, like a telling near its end. The shape standing in the open floor, amber down one side and blue-green down the other, in nobody's place. The gap in the warm, the size of it. The ring the people left. A stone at the bottom of the shelving, moved well, and not one of the people turning. Went-Round going round it, and the same from behind. The light going back, and the wall the way it had been.

Carried-It-Sleeping listened. For the wall, Carried-It-Sleeping checked every word, and could not help it. For all the rest, Carried-It-Sleeping was someone who had not been there, and held still, and took it.

It stood up.

"*Not one of us turned,*" the smallest said again, at the end, the way Stone-First had said it. It was the piece the smallest liked best. It was a thing about the smallest that the smallest had needed to be told.

"When they come," said the smallest, "the ones not born yet. Will they ask who was there?"

"They will be small. They will ask everything."

"What do I say?"

"Everyone. And then who was there for which piece. It is a longer answer than usual."

"I can count," said the smallest, with dignity.

Across the middle, two small ones were still at it, very quietly, half asleep. *The side toward me.* *The side toward me.* It had stopped being an argument some time ago. It was just a thing they both knew.

"If it comes again," said the smallest, "I am going to look at the wall first. I have only got the wall from you."

Carried-It-Sleeping did not say whether it would come again. Nobody could say that. The ground had not said when it came, and Carried-It-Sleeping did not see how the ground would say that it was not going to.

The smallest slept. Carried-It-Sleeping counted the pieces of the warm, from habit, and they were all there. Two of the old ones at the edge were lying facing out, toward the passages. Carried-It-Sleeping had not seen old ones lie that way before, and put it aside, to think about on a time when nothing happened.

On the near wall the amber was where it had always been. The smallest had not been born when the up-people set their first stone down, and had no name yet, and was going to be the one who told this to people who were nowhere so far. The warm would be there to check, for as long as the warm was the ones who had been there.

After that it would have to stand up by itself. That was what the pieces were for.
