::::: scene 1

::: pov stone-first

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by the one who was there. It is about a thing the one who was there did not do, and that is harder to be there for.

A stone moved, at the top of the shelving.

It was the middle of a working time. The up-people's knock had come and been answered, and the loud was a long way up at its working. The stone was a small one's stone: careful, and proper, and a little too hard.

Stone-First-Who-Waited bent and put a hand to the rock at the edge of the silt and moved it. Once. Well. The ground had not finished saying *someone* before Stone-First had answered it.

Remember that. It is what the rest is about.

The small one came up to the top of the shelving and stopped there, and did not step onto the silt. It was the one who goes straight at things. The same one who had come up, many tellings ago, while Stone-First was down in the warm telling the line, and had seen a shape standing in Stone-First's place. Older now. Not much bigger.

"I have a thing to ask," said the small one. "I moved a stone for it."

"I heard. I answered."

"Yes," said the small one. "You did. That is the thing."

Stone-First waited.

The small one looked along the line. Four stones, each where it was set. The up-people's first, with the silt worn smooth round it. Stone-First's answer beside it. The up-people's newest, a hand from Stone-First's feet. And the other one, at the edge of the silt, lying beside the mark of where it used to lie.

"Nobody's stone," said the small one. "In your telling of it. It was moved well. You said that twice. The ground said *someone is coming.* And you stood still and waited to be told who."

"Yes."

"You did not answer it."

Stone-First stood with the whole body still.

"No."

"Went-Round said a thing, about the shape in your place. I was there. Went-Round said a thing standing where someone always stands looks like them, until it does what they do. And it did not answer my stone. That was how we knew it was not you. Stone-First always answers a stone."

"Went-Round said that."

"But you did not answer nobody's stone." The small one took a breath, the way you do before you go straight at a thing. "So on that working time. Was it you on the line?"

::::: scene 2

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First did not answer that at once either, and was aware of it.

"I was there," said Stone-First. "Nobody else was. It is mine to tell."

"I know. I am not saying you were not there. I am saying you did not do what you do."

That was fair. It was also the hardest thing anyone below the roof had said to Stone-First in a whole life, and it had been said by somebody small, with a stone moved first.

"Go down," said Stone-First. "I will come at the end of the working time and say it in the warm. A thing asked with a stone is answered where everyone can hear."

The small one went, and moved a stone going, and the ground said *someone is leaving*, and Stone-First was alone on the line with the four stones.

Stone-First is slower than Went-Round at going round a thing, and stands in front of it instead, until it has been looked at. So Stone-First stood, and looked at the working time of nobody's stone, from the start.

A stone had moved, at the edge of the silt. Moved well. The ground had said *someone is coming.*

And here was the part Stone-First had told, every time, in the same few words, and never once looked at: *I stood still and waited to be told who.*

Why?

Nobody waits to be told who. A stone moves, and you answer it, and then they come, and then you see who it was. The answer is first. It has to be. The answer is how the one coming knows that you know. The ground never says who; Went-Round had found that out for everyone, in the warm, the same telling. You answer *someone.* That is the whole of the manners, and Stone-First had taught them to every small one who ever asked.

But on that working time Stone-First had known where everyone was. The up-people were far up the long passage. The people were below, all of them; from the line you can feel the shape of the warm with every piece in it. Stone-First had felt it, without trying, before the ground had finished. Everyone was somewhere else.

So Stone-First had not answered. Stone-First had counted who it could be, first, and found nobody, and stood still to see what came.

Nobody has ever had to do that before answering a stone. Stone-First had done it.

The stone lay there, by its mark. Stone-First looked at the rock at the edge of the silt, the good one, the one that answers the up-people before every working.

It could be moved now. One bend. One hand.

The hand did not go. An answer is for someone on their way. It says *I heard you; come.* This was more tellings late than Stone-First could count, and nobody was on their way, as far as anyone knew. A stone moved now would not be an answer. It would be a new stone. And a stone is moved for someone, and Stone-First would be moving this one only to find out what happened.

A stone is not for finding out.

So the time for answering had gone by, and it was not coming back. Stone-First stood on the line with that for the rest of the working time. Nothing came. Then the loud went away up the long passage, toward the cold.

::::: scene 3

::: pov teddy

The small one came down and told the others what had been asked, and the middle of the warm stopped being a place anybody could sleep in.

"You said Stone-First was not Stone-First."

"I did not. I asked."

"You asked the *line.*"

"I moved a stone."

Carried-It-Sleeping let it run for a while. That is part of keeping. You cannot tell what a noise is about until it has gone on long enough to have a shape.

It had two shapes. Some of the small ones thought it was the bravest thing they had ever heard of. The others had gone very quiet, and it was one of the quiet ones who said it.

"If Stone-First does not always answer a stone. Is the name wrong?"

And then, smaller: "Do names come off?"

"No," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "A name grows. It is never taken off and it is never put on new. It is the first true thing said about you that the warm went on saying. It was true when it was said."

"Is it true now?"

Carried-It-Sleeping did not know. Carried-It-Sleeping was named for one thing done once, a long time ago, and had never been asked whether it was still true.

"Then it should grow," said the one who goes straight at things, who had been thinking. "If a truer thing is said. You told us that. *Stone-First-Who-Waited-And-One-Time-Did-Not-Answer.*"

There was a silence.

"That is true," said the smallest.

"It is very long," said somebody else.

The small ones tried it. That is how a name is made; you cannot decide one. It gets said, and somebody else says it, and it goes round. They sent it round the middle. It got as far as the fourth small one, who dropped the end off it. The fifth put the end back on in the wrong place. By the time it came round again it was *Stone-First-Who-One-Time-Waited*, which was not true at all, and they stopped.

None of the old ones had picked it up. The warm had heard it, and had not gone on saying it. That is the only way a name is ever refused, and nobody has to be rude.

From the far side of the amber came a sound Carried-It-Sleeping had not heard in a long time. It was Went-Round, being pleased.

"It is not the name that has been caught," said Went-Round. "It is me. *Until it does what they do.* I went all the way round that, when I said it, and I thought I had seen every side. And a small one has just walked through the middle of it."

"Are you cross?" said the one who goes straight at things.

"I have been round six things this season and not one of them had another side," said Went-Round. "This is the first that has. It is mine, and it is wrong. I could not be less cross."

A stone moved, at the bottom of the shelving. Good, and plain.

The whole of the warm turned for it.

::::: scene 4

::: pov teddy

Stone-First-Who-Waited came into the amber and did not sit.

"A thing was asked me on the line, with a stone," said Stone-First. "This is not a telling. It is the answer."

The warm lay with the whole body still.

"On the working time of nobody's stone, a stone was moved well, and I did not answer it. Before I answered, I counted who it could be. Nobody has to do that. Nobody ever has. You answer, and then you find out. I found nobody to count, and I stood still, and standing still is not an answer." Stone-First let that be heard. "That is not what I do. I did it. It was in the telling the whole time, in the words *waited to be told who*, and nobody asked what they were doing there, and I did not either."

Nobody in the warm had ever heard the oldest of the people say *I did not do what I do.* Carried-It-Sleeping felt the small ones go still in a new way. Not afraid. It was the way you go still when the ground under a thing turns out to go down further than you thought, and to be ground the whole way.

"The small one asked whether it was me on the line," said Stone-First. "It was. Who was there? I was. That is how you know. You do not know it by what I always do."

"Then how do I know it was *not* you," said the one who goes straight at things, "the other time? In your place?"

"Where was I?"

"In the warm. Telling."

"Who heard me?"

The small one looked round. "Everyone."

"Then that is how. It was always how. Went-Round's saying is a good one for a thing you cannot ask. For a person, you ask who was there."

"I have been told," said Went-Round, from the far side, and was still pleased.

"Will you answer it now?" said the smallest. "Nobody's stone?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"An answer is for someone on their way. This one is a great many tellings late. If I moved a stone for it now, I would be moving it to see what came. A stone is not for that."

"Then you owe one," said the smallest.

"No. You owe a thing to someone. I do not know that there is a someone. Nobody came." Stone-First thought. "I missed one. That is different. A thing you missed stays missed. You do not get to make it up with another stone and feel better."

"For how long?"

"For as long as I tell it," said Stone-First. "It is in the telling."

Somebody small said the long name then, out loud, the true one, before it had gone wrong. Carried-It-Sleeping had hoped nobody would.

Stone-First heard it through to the end, which took a moment.

"That is true," said Stone-First. "If the warm goes on saying it, it is mine."

The warm did not. Stone-First waited long enough to be sure, the way you wait for an answer, and then lay down at the edge of the amber and slept.

Nobody made the small sound. But the one who goes straight at things lay awake a long time, looking pleased and worried at once. Carried-It-Sleeping knew that look from the inside. It is the look of having moved a stone and been answered by more than you asked for.

::::: scene 5

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First was on the line, and the loud came down.

It settled at the junction. Then the light came into the squeeze, low, and the smallest of the up-people behind it, with the stone held to the front and the small hard thing on the chest. The stone was set down, a hand from Stone-First's feet. The step back. The thing that goes up at the end.

Stone-First bent and moved the rock at the edge of the silt. Once. Well. Before the ground had finished.

The up-person heard it, and went back through the crack, not running.

That was every working time. It had been every working time since the up-people's first stone, and Stone-First had not once thought about it while doing it. Stone-First thought about it now.

Nobody had been keeping that. Not the name. The warm says *Stone-First always answers a stone*, and the warm is right to, and it has been said for so long that Stone-First had been standing inside it without looking, the way Stone-First stands in the place in the silt. Went-Round had stood in that place once and said it looked like Stone-First even with nobody in it. A name is like that too. It goes on looking like you on the time when you were not doing it.

The saying does not move the stone. The hand does, each time, or it does not. On one working time it had not. The warm had gone on saying *always* straight through that, for more tellings than the small ones can count, until somebody came up the shelving and asked.

Stone-First was glad of the asking, and would not have said so in the warm. It would have been heard as a rule.

The four stones lay where they were set. Stone-First looked at the one at the edge, by its mark. It had not been answered, and now it would not be. Stone-First did not know what to do with a thing missed that could not be put right, and did not make a rule about it, and did not move the stone back, and did not look away from it either.

Then Stone-First looked at the squeeze, which is what you look at, and waited. That is in the name as well. It was not going to do the waiting for anybody.
