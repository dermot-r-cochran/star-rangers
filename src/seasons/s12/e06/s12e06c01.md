::::: scene 1

::: pov toma-egede

This is logged the way things are logged on Fliade: by someone who was there, into a unit that was there too, and read afterwards by someone who was not. This one is about an instrument that was there when nobody else was, and about what was written on its case.

The first imager was on the bottom shelf of the rack, in its case, where it had been since the seven weeks.

Egede found it by tidying. The turn of the year was coming, and with it the manifest and the summaries, and a summary wants every instrument on the station to have a status. Egede liked to have the rack in order well before anyone asked.

The case had a tag on it, in Egede's own small square hand.

*Imager, first. Artefact, third passage mouth. Withdrawn for investigation.*

Egede looked at that for some time.

The survey lead had struck *artefact* out of the log, seven times, with Egede standing there. That was a long time ago now. The log had been right ever since. But nobody had struck the tag. Nobody had looked at the bottom shelf. The first imager had been sitting in the dark with that word tied to its handle the whole time, and the spare had been down at the junction doing its work.

Under the tag was the investigation sheet. Egede read it through. It was not long.

*Recalibrated. Six shifts clear. Resolved.* Then, in different ink: *Recurred.*

*Recalibrated again. Recurred.*

*Lens changed. Recurred.*

*Test card, one evening, all blocks. Within tolerance, every block. No fault found.* And directly under that, same evening, same pen: *Fault therefore intermittent, or in the mount.*

*Spare substituted. Spare records the same.* And under that: *Therefore not the imager. Mount, or wall.*

Egede read the two *therefores* again.

Nothing on the sheet had ever found anything wrong. Egede had asked the instrument, one way after another, whether it was broken. Each time it had answered no, as clearly as an instrument can. And each time Egede had written the answer down, correctly, to the block, and then written a reason not to believe it.

Egede had never once falsified a reading. That was the unpleasant part. It was all there, honest, in order.

::::: scene 2

::: pov toma-egede

Closing an investigation is one line. Egede took a clean sheet and wrote *Investigation closed*, and then the line that goes under it.

*No fault.*

The pen stopped.

Egede waited a moment, the way Egede had learned to lately, and looked at the two words.

Some weeks back the survey lead had written *second shape not present* in the log, and Egede had said, standing at that table, that *not present* was a reading. It had not been pleasant to be right. Egede had gone on thinking about it for days. And here was the same thing, in Egede's own hand.

*No fault* said something about the whole inside of an instrument, for good. The test card had not seen that. The test card had seen every block within tolerance, on one evening. That was what it saw. It had looked for a fault and had not found one.

Egede struck *No fault*, one thin line, so it could still be read, and wrote under it:

*Fault not found. Test card: all blocks within tolerance. The return at the third passage mouth was recorded by this instrument and by the spare alike. See log.*

Then the tag. Egede struck *Artefact*, the same way, and left it on the handle.

That was the paper done. It should have felt finished, and it did not.

Nakagawa was at the table, reading ground samples.

"I want to change the imagers back," said Egede. "The first one, on the stand. The spare in its case."

Nakagawa looked up. "Is there something wrong with the spare?"

"No."

"Is the first one better?"

"They're the same model. They always were." Egede waited, and then said it. "The first one's in a case with a tag on it. For something it didn't do."

Nakagawa was quiet for a moment.

"Is that an instrument reason?"

"No."

"Then do it," said Nakagawa, "and put it in the maintenance file as what it is. Not as an instrument reason."

That was fair, and it was awful. Egede wrote it out, slowly. *First imager returned to service. No technical ground. At the specialist's request.* In two years of summaries and manifests Egede had never written *no technical ground* about anything. It sat on the sheet looking like somebody else's handwriting.

Ferrant, on the floor at the third shelf, had not looked round once. Egede was grateful, and suspected it was on purpose.

::::: scene 3

::: pov aravena

Egede carried the case down.

Aravena noticed, because carrying things down was Aravena's job, and had been for three years. The unit, the handsets, the light. Aravena had offered, at the top of the long passage, and Egede had said "I've got it," and had gone on having it all the way down, awkwardly, with the case held in front in both arms.

"Seven weeks," said Aravena, after a while. "I kept trying to say it wasn't broken."

Egede walked for a few steps before answering. That was the new thing.

"You did say it. I've got it on a sheet. Every test passed, and under every one I wrote *therefore.*"

"Therefore what?"

"Therefore something else must be wrong with it."

Aravena thought about that. "That's quite a lot of work to go to."

"Yes," said Egede. "I was very thorough."

At the junction the knock came first, because it always did. Aravena picked the stone. Down the third passage, sideways through the squeeze with the handheld pressing its square into the breastbone, and out onto the silt with the light held low.

The one on the line. Still, grey, large, in the open.

The light found the feet, and went an arm's length to the side, and found silt, and came back.

Kneel. Stone down, a hand from those feet. Stand. Step back. Say the thing, going up at the end. Wait.

The hand on the rock. The ground saying so.

Back through, not running.

"Log," said Aravena, kneeling to the unit. "Shift one-nine-six. Upper junction. Party of three. Knock answered."

The unit hummed, once, low.

Egede had not started. Egede was standing at the far wall with the case at Egede's feet, still shut, looking at Aravena on one knee in front of the unit.

"You kneel to that," said Egede. "Every shift. I've never asked why."

"It's on the floor."

"You could pick it up."

Aravena had not been asked before, and had to look for the answer. "It was there for all of it. It went through the squeeze and stood at the line by itself, and woke up, and went back to sleep. It was there before any of us were. I don't like talking down at it."

Egede did not say that was not an instrument reason. Egede opened the case.

::::: scene 4

::: pov toma-egede

The spare came off the stand and went into its own case. Egede had written it a tag the night before. *Imager, spare. In service seven weeks and after. Recorded the same.*

The first imager went onto the stand. The mount took it with the small click it had always made.

Then Egede's hand stayed on it.

It had always faced the junction. Egede had set it that way when it first stayed down between shifts, because Egede had wanted the room: the whole working floor, wall to wall, to see what it did when the party was gone. The mouth of the third passage was only at the edge of that. A dark place at the side of the frame that nobody had chosen.

A hand's turn to the right would put the passage mouth dead in the middle.

Egede knew what that was. For seven weeks Egede would have given a good deal for the imager to look anywhere else. Now it would be very easy to make it look nowhere else. That was the same thing done backwards, and Ferrant had a word for it, which she had said at the table once about her own trade. You do not point an instrument at what you are hoping for.

Egede took the hand away. The imager faced the room.

"It wasn't looking for it," said Egede.

Aravena looked up from the unit.

"The shape. The imager wasn't pointed at it. It was pointed at the floor. I only left it down here because I wanted to know what the room did with nobody in it." Egede was working it out aloud, which was the old habit, but slower. "That's the only reason anybody on the roof ever saw the thing at all. We'd gone. We were up at the station eating. This was here. It was the only one of us that was."

"The unit was here."

"The unit was asleep."

"It usually is, when something happens," said Aravena, kindly enough.

Egede did not hear that, or did and let it go.

"It was the only one here. It kept what it saw. It said so, that same evening, as plainly as it could, on the one channel it's got. And I was up a long passage at a table, and I told it what it had seen. For seven weeks. I wasn't even *there.*"

Nobody said anything. The first imager sat on its stand and looked at the room.

Egede ran it through its first frame, and read the frame back, and took out the maintenance file, and wrote the day's line. Egede's pen went to the place where an instrument specialist writes *nominal*, and stopped, a little before the paper this time.

*First imager: on stand. Recording.*

It was what Egede could check.

::::: scene 5

::: pov aravena

Nakagawa read the channels that evening, at the table, as every evening.

"Thermal: the party. The Anstolik, passing, twice. Sound: nothing. Imager: nothing at the third passage mouth."

"First imager," said Egede.

Nakagawa looked up.

"For the file. It's the first imager again, from today. If something's read off it, it ought to say which one read it."

"It ought," said Nakagawa, and wrote it in.

Ferrant, on the floor at the third shelf, held out a hand without turning round. "May I see the tag?"

Egede had brought it up, off the empty case. Egede gave it to her. Aravena could see it from the berth: the word *Artefact* with one thin line through it, and under that, small and square, *Fault not found.*

Ferrant read it for longer than five words take.

"You kept the struck one."

"You said those were the best thing in a file."

"I did." She turned it over, and there was nothing on the back, and she turned it over again. "I'll say another thing, since I'm the one here whose job is grades. *Not found.* That's exactly what that test card was good for, and not a hair more. I've had analysts nine years in who would have written *no fault* and gone to bed."

Egede went red, starting at the ears.

"I did write it," said Egede. "First. It's on the sheet. I struck that too."

"Then you've a very good sheet," said Ferrant, and handed the tag back, and went on with her handset.

Egede tied the tag to the empty case, where a tag goes, and put the case on the bottom shelf. It was only a case now. It did not seem to trouble Egede the way it had that morning.

Later Aravena lay in the dark and thought about it.

Two years to learn to knock. Then most of a year to learn to write what an instrument saw, and not what it was. This was a smaller thing than either. It was Egede telling an instrument that it had been right.

It could not hear that. Egede knew it could not; nobody on Fliade knew better. Egede had said it anyway, in the one place the imager is ever read, which is its file, and had carried it down the long passage in both arms to do it.

Aravena said a thing at the line every shift, to someone who heard it, and it was in no log and never would be. Egede had said a thing in a log, to something that would never hear it. Aravena tried to decide which of them was the odder, and could not. Both were for someone who had been there.

At the junction, a long way down in the dark, the first imager went on looking at an empty room. Nobody had asked it to look for anything.
