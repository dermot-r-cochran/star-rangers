::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there, and for most of it what there was to be there for was nothing, which is harder to tell than you would think.

Nothing had come for a long time.

No shape at the seep. No stone moved at the edge of the silt with nobody's hand on it. Nothing in Stone-First's place but Stone-First. The amber had stayed in the wall. The up-people's loud came down at the start of every working time, and the stone, and the answer, and the loud went away up the long passage toward the cold, and that was all, time after time after time.

You would think the warm would be glad. Carried-It-Sleeping had thought so too.

But here is the thing about a shape that moves no stone. The ground did not say when it came. So the ground cannot say that it has stopped coming. A stranger who leaves moves a stone, and the ground says *someone is leaving*, and then you know. Nothing had said that. The warm had been given the beginning of a thing, more than once, and then no more of it, and no end either.

So the warm was waiting. All of it, old ones and small ones. And a warm that is waiting does not sleep well.

Carried-It-Sleeping knew, because Carried-It-Sleeping lay in the middle and counted the pieces, the way you do when the small ones are yours to keep. The pieces were all there. But they were not the shape of people asleep. The old ones lay facing out. The small ones woke before the telling and said, each of them, the same thing, *did it come?*, and Carried-It-Sleeping said *no*, and they lay down again and did not sleep either.

One of them had asked Went-Round to go round it.

"Go round what?" said Went-Round.

"Nothing coming."

Went-Round had gone off to the far side of the amber and come back after a while and said that it had been tried, and that nothing has no sides at all, which is different from having no other side, and worse. The small ones had liked that. It had not helped anybody sleep.

Then the smallest moved a stone.

A small one, properly, right there in the warm, a hand from where Carried-It-Sleeping lay. That is how you ask a thing of someone when it is a big thing.

"I want to go to the seep again," said the smallest.

"Anyone may go to the seep."

"I want Went-Round to come. Last time I was there and nobody else was, and so nobody could settle it. If two are there, and it comes, it can be settled."

Carried-It-Sleeping looked at the smallest for as long as it takes to say a name. It was not how the rule went. Nobody had been there for the part that would settle it, and the part was not at the seep, and Carried-It-Sleeping did not know where it was. But the smallest had gone round the thing as far as a small one can go, and had moved a stone first, and had asked.

"Ask Went-Round," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "With a stone."

The smallest was already going.

::::: scene 2

::: pov went-round

Went-Round went, because a small one had asked properly, and because Went-Round had only ever been to the seep after.

They moved a stone at the top of the last passage. The smallest moved it. The ground said *someone is coming* to whoever was at the seep, and nobody answered, and they went down.

The seep was the seep.

The warmth came up out of the ground on its own, where it always has. The amber was thick and still. Went-Round felt the shape of the warm in the place, and it had two pieces in it, one large and one small, and no gap anywhere.

"Now we wait," said the smallest.

"For how long?"

"The up-people's coming. That is what I counted last time."

So they stood, a little way back from the seep, and the smallest counted the story of the up-people's coming through, under the breath, from the light in the long passage to the two stones. Went-Round did not count. Went-Round looked at the place a little way out from the wall where the smallest said the shape had stood, and it went on being a place a little way out from the wall.

The story ended.

"It did not come," said the smallest.

"No."

"Then we will stay." The smallest sat down, right there, in the thick of the amber, the way you sit when you mean it. "We will stay until it does. We can sleep here. Then whenever it comes, two are there."

"You do not sleep on a beginning," said Went-Round.

It came out the way it comes out of everyone. Every small one is told it. It is in the telling of the first warm seep, near the start, in with the way down and the amber: you may go to it, and you do not sleep on it.

"Why not?" said the smallest.

Went-Round opened the mouth to say why.

And there was nothing in it. Went-Round stood at the bottom of the warm with the mouth open and went round the telling of the seep, the whole of it, start to end, the way you feel round a place in the dark, looking for the reason. The warmth coming up. The way down. The amber. *You may go to it, and you do not sleep on it.* It was not there because of anything. It was just there, a thing in a telling full of things, and nobody had ever asked it why, because everyone is told it when they are too small to ask.

"It is too warm," said Went-Round, which is what the old ones say.

"I do not mind warm."

"No," said Went-Round. "I do not have a reason. It is in the telling, and the telling does not say why, and I am not going to make one up for you at the seep, of all places."

The smallest thought about that, sitting down.

"But we may come."

"We may come. We came."

"And then we go back up."

"And then we go back up."

The smallest stood, slowly, and looked once more at the place a little way out from the wall.

"It is not much of a telling," said the smallest. "Two went to the seep, and the seep was there."

"Who was there?"

"I was. You were."

"Then it is settled," said Went-Round. "All the way through. Nobody below the roof can say it did not happen. When did you last have one of those?"

The smallest made the small sound, by mistake, and then looked pleased to have made it.

They moved a stone going out. Went-Round was quiet all the way up the last passage, going round something, and the smallest, who knows what that looks like by now, let Went-Round be.

::::: scene 3

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First-Who-Waited was on the line, and the loud came down.

It settled at the junction. And Stone-First looked to the side.

An arm's length along the line. Silt. The fungus-light on it, the kind that has no source and throws no shadow. Nobody.

Stone-First had done that at every knock since the light went round it. The loud settled, and the eye went to the side, and found nobody, and came back to the squeeze. It had not felt like doing anything. It had felt like keeping the margin, which is being there.

Then the light came into the crack, low, and the smallest of the up-people behind it with the stone held to the front and the small hard thing on the chest. The light found Stone-First's feet, the way it does. Then it went to the side, an arm's length along, and lay on the silt there for about as long as it takes to say a name, and came back.

It did that every time too.

The up-person knelt, and set the stone down a hand from Stone-First's feet, and stepped back, and said the thing that goes up at the end. Stone-First bent and moved the rock at the edge of the silt. Once. Well. The up-person heard it, and went back through the crack, not running.

So that was a knock: a stone, an answer, and two looks to the side at nothing, one from each. What the up-people looked for there was not the people's to know. Stone-First only saw that they looked.

A stone moved at the top of the shelving. Good, and plain, and a little impatient. Stone-First answered it without needing to think who.

Went-Round came out onto the silt and did not come near the place, and told it standing up: the smallest's asking, the two at the seep, the story counted through, the seep being the seep. The smallest wanting to stay and sleep there. *You do not sleep on a beginning.* The *why not*, and no reason.

"There is no reason in it," said Stone-First. "There never was. It is a thing, like the amber."

"I know that now. I had to be asked by somebody small." Went-Round looked at the four stones, and the row of the up-people's older ones, and not at Stone-First. "Here is what I went round, coming up. You have said it since the up-people's first stone. A stone moved for you is the beginning of a telling, and you do not tidy a beginning away."

"I have said that."

"Then that is what we have. Not tellings with no middle. Beginnings. A shape at the seep, and then no more. A stone moved well, and then no more. The light round a thing on this line, and then no more. Every one of them began, and none of them has gone on, and nobody can say whether they will." Went-Round stopped, the way Went-Round stops before the far side of a thing. "And we have always known what to do with a beginning. It is in the first telling anyone is told. You may go to it. We went, to every one. We told them and went round them. That was right."

"And you do not sleep on it," said Stone-First.

"And the whole of the warm has been lying down on them, every telling, since the light went round. Waiting for the rest. Asking *did it come.* That is not going to a place. That is living there."

Stone-First said nothing for a while.

"That is in the seep," said Stone-First. "It is about the seep. You cannot take a thing out of the seep without making a different telling about less."

"I am not taking it out. It stays where it is and gets told whole. And I am not making it a third of the manners; the manners are two, and a rule made from one season is a rule I would have to go round later, and I am getting old for it." Went-Round nearly made the small sound. "I am only saying we were told how, before we were big enough to ask why. All of us. That is all I have."

It was a good deal. Stone-First did not say so.

What Stone-First thought, when Went-Round had gone down, was about the side.

The margin is Stone-First's to keep. Keeping it means being there. It does not mean waiting for a thing; Stone-First had stood here a whole life without knowing what would come, and that was the whole skill of it. But since the light went round, every time the loud settled, Stone-First had looked an arm's length along the line to see if it was back. That was not being there. That was keeping a second place, beside the first one, for something that had never asked for a place and had never been asked.

Nobody had told Stone-First to keep it. Stone-First had lain down on it standing up.

Stone-First did not decide never to look to the side again. A thing you decide never to do is a rule, and there was no rule for this and there was not going to be. But the next time the loud came down and settled at the junction, Stone-First looked at the squeeze, which is what you look at, and went on looking at it, and was there.

The light came through, and found the feet, and went to the side, and came back.

That was theirs.

::::: scene 4

::: pov teddy

The smallest told it standing up, in the amber, and it was the shortest telling Carried-It-Sleeping had ever heard anybody stand up for.

"Two went to the seep. A stone was moved first. The seep was there, and nothing else was. A story was counted through. Two came back."

The warm waited for the rest.

"That is all of it," said the smallest.

"Who was there?" said an old one at the edge of the amber, because somebody has to say it.

"I was. Went-Round was."

"Then it is settled," said the old one, and stopped, as if the word had felt strange in the mouth. It had been a long time since anyone below the roof had got to use it.

And several people in the warm made the small sound, the one that means *that is a thing worth saying.*

Carried-It-Sleeping had not heard the warm make it since before the seep. Not over the shape with no warmth, or nobody's stone, or any of the ones after. There had been nothing in those to make it over. You do not make that sound at a thing with no middle. And here was the warm making it over a telling about a place with nothing in it, told by the smallest person there was.

"It is not about anything," said one of the small ones.

"It has a middle," said the smallest, with great dignity.

Then Went-Round, from the far side of the amber, said the other thing. That they were beginnings. That a beginning is a thing you may go to and do not sleep on, and that everyone in the warm had been told so before they could ask why, and that there was no why, and that Went-Round had looked. That it was not a rule and not a word and stayed in the seep where it lived.

"Then what do we do with them?" said the old one.

"What we did. Tell them the way they were told the first time. Nothing out, nothing in. Keep them for the ones not born yet. And if one of them goes on, somebody will be there, or nobody will, and we will find out which."

"And if none of them goes on?"

"Then they are beginnings," said Went-Round. "There are worse things to be left."

Nobody settled it. It was not that kind of thing. The warm just lay with it for a while, the way it lies with something Stone-First has said, to see whether it turns out true later.

Carried-It-Sleeping counted the pieces, from habit. They were all there. The old ones had turned over, some of them, and lay facing in. The small one who had said it was not about anything was asleep already, which is the fastest a small one has ever lost an argument. The smallest lay awake a little longer, holding the telling, the way you hold a thing that is yours and has an end on it.

The warm had not settled on what to call the smallest. It would. Not this telling.

Nobody asked *did it come.*

Carried-It-Sleeping thought about the seep, down at the bottom of the warm, with the warmth coming up out of the ground on its own and nobody at it. Nobody is ever at it. Carried-It-Sleeping had been told that as a thing about the seep. It was a thing about the people. They had been leaving a beginning alone, every sleep of their lives, since before the long dark, and going back to it whenever they liked, and nobody had ever once lost sleep over it.

Then Carried-It-Sleeping slept, in the middle, with the small ones, which is a thing Carried-It-Sleeping is named for, and had not done properly in a long time.
