::: pov anne-de-courtney

*…the Krilzat return being, on its face, an optical signature with no thermal or mass component, sustained under local illumination only, the desk considers it consistent with the class the Corps files as a membrane caustic. A cross-membrane gravimeter co-located with the present unit would resolve the class in a single pass of the relay. The desk therefore asks that the delegation put to the court a request for the placement of one such instrument, on the terms already granted for the first.*

*Pending which, the desk notes that the delegation's own rendering of the Kingdom's term, "photographic", independently anticipates the class, and invites the register's keeper to raise the second of her three filed readings accordingly.*

Anne de Courtney read it standing, in the working room, with the shutters open on the town.

It was a good request. That was the first thing she noticed about it. Most of what came down from the Science Corps was reasonable people wanting soil. This one was better than reasonable. Somebody at the desk had read la Chapelle's filing properly, all three readings and the reasons she could not choose among them. They had seen that the first and the second came apart on one measurement, and had named the one instrument that made it. If the strain was there, the thing in that room was the second reading, and the Corps had a class for it. If it was not, it was the first, a trick of old stone and lamplight. One pass of the relay would say which.

She would have liked very much to know.

She put the reader down on the table. On the wall by the door, where it had been since her first week, was the clause: *by invitation only, and must leave when the invitation is withdrawn.* The paper had gone the colour of the plaster. She did not need to look at it and did, most days.

Three years. One request. She had made it herself, in the court's own forms, with Strozzi checking every word: that a thing of the delegation's might go to Krilzat with a house's child, and be set wherever the keeper of the place decided, and nowhere the keeper did not. The court had taken a season to answer. Then it had said yes in a handful of words, which Strozzi had rendered four different ways before he would let her read them. *The keeper decides.*

And now there was a log, and a desk that wanted a second thing set beside the first.

"Strozzi," she said.

He was at the other table with the week's translations. He looked up as he did at everyone, as if she might be about to correct him and he would be glad of it.

"Read this. The second paragraph is yours."

::::: scene 2

::: pov ernesto-strozzi

He read the second paragraph three times. The first time for what it said, and the second for what would have to be true for it to be right. The third time he read it because he recognised it.

*The delegation's own rendering independently anticipates the class.*

It was courteous of the desk to say *independently*.

Ernesto Strozzi had chosen the word *photographic* in the delegation's first year, on a wet afternoon, with a court clerk who was tired of him. The clerk had used a term of the figures in the chronicle's first lines. Strozzi had asked what it meant when it was used of anything else, and the clerk had said that it was not used of anything else. So he had taken it to pieces. It came apart into a root for light and a root for a mark or a drawing, and a small worn particle that he thought, at a low band, meant *by no hand*. A likeness drawn in light by nothing. No register the Corps kept had a word the court would have known for that. He had reached back into the oldest stratum of his own language and found one that meant, at the root, light-written, and filed it at the band such choices get, with a note that it was a rendering and not a reading.

That was two years before anybody set an instrument on a floor on Krilzat.

And here was the desk, reading his word back to him as evidence. *The Kingdom calls them photographic. A caustic is an image drawn in gathered light. The fit is striking.*

The fit was striking. He had made it. He had chosen, out of everything available to him, a word that a reader in the Corps would find natural. That was what a rendering was for. The reader had now found it natural and concluded that the Kingdom agreed with the Corps.

The Kingdom had said a word of its own, to a clerk, of some names in a list. What agreed with the Corps was Strozzi.

He knew this error from the inside, as a man knows a stair he has fallen down. A cognate that looked secure. A fact beside it that was real. The first allowed to do work the second had not earned. *Tribute.* Nine months. He could name the three postings that had read it.

He took up a pen. The desk would have its answer in his hand and under his name.

*The rendering "photographic" is the delegation's, not the Kingdom's. It was chosen by this officer to be legible to the Corps. Its fit with a Corps class is therefore evidence about this officer's choice of word and is none about the room on Krilzat. I do not concur in raising the band. The register's keeper re-derived her filing some weeks since and withdrew a sentence of her own on similar grounds; I attach it, as the better statement.*

He read it over for softness and found none. Then he put the question to himself the other way, as he was obliged to. What would have to be true for the refusal to be wrong?

The desk might be right about the class. He thought, privately, that it very likely was. But a band was a claim about the claimer. The delegation's register said what the delegation had been told and had seen: a log, a lamp, two records and a word. The desk could file the class at whatever height the desk could defend. It would not do it standing on him.

"Mine is declined," he said. "With thanks. Yours is harder."

"Yes," said de Courtney. She had not moved from the table.

::::: scene 3

::: pov anne-de-courtney

"Argue it," she said. "For the desk. You do it better than they do."

Strozzi folded his hands. He never minded being asked to make the other side's case. She had sometimes thought it was the only form of rest he took.

"One instrument," he said. "Smaller than the first. It emits nothing, touches nothing, and is carried by the same boat. We ask for it on the terms the court itself chose: where the keeper decides and nowhere else. If the keeper sets it in a ditch, we have lost nothing. If the keeper sets it beside the other, la Chapelle drops a reading inside a week. And the Archive holds a finding about this world in place of three candidates at the bottom of a register." He considered. "And it is not soil. I think the desk would wish that noted. It is not a sample of anything the Kingdom owns."

"All true."

"I have tried to make it so."

"And when the court says yes," she said, "what has the court learned about us?"

He did not answer at once. It was one of the reasons she kept asking him things.

"That the first thing was a first thing," he said slowly.

"That we asked for one instrument, and were given it more generously than we had any right to expect, and that the moment it spoke we were back at the gate with its brother." She went to the shutters. The afternoon lay along the roofs, and the palms beyond them leaned as they always did. "They took a season to grant it. They have not once asked us what it heard. Have you thought about that?"

"Often."

"Nor have we offered. So the court has let a strangers' thing sit in the oldest place it has, and does not know what the thing has said, and has not sent to ask. And I am to answer that with a second request." She turned round. "What did we ask for, Strozzi? The words."

"That it be set where the keeper decided."

"And what do we know of what the keeper decided?"

He opened one hand, as he did when he was laying something out for the juniors.

"The log's first word is *Placed*. Interior. Floor level, against a wall. After that, a lamp comes, and there is a figure for as long as it stays." He stopped. "That is all. We do not know who the keeper is. The court did not name one. We do not know if the hand that placed it is the hand with the lamp. We do not know why that room. La Chapelle heard the court's reciter say at the Abbey that a door was *given*, and the Abbess say that it was always there, and we do not know which of those the keeper would say, or whether the keeper would say either."

"So someone on that rock was handed our instrument and told to choose," Anne said. "And chose a room with that in it. Out of a place that la Chapelle tells me the Abbey keeps a whole count of, door by door. That was the decision. It is the only thing anyone on Krilzat has ever said to us, and it was said by putting a case down on a floor."

"And we have not read it."

"We have read the instrument. We have not read the placing." She sat down at last. "I asked for one thing. I do not think it was the log. I think the log is what we sent. What we were given is a keeper's decision, and I cannot tell you the first thing about it."

::::: scene 4

::: pov ernesto-strozzi

La Chapelle came in off the evening boat with salt on her collar and more questions than sentences, which was the ratio he had recorded for her in three assessments.

He gave her the desk's request and watched her read it. He had watched a great many juniors read a document that offered them something. They sat a little straighter at the second paragraph. It was not vanity. It was only that a filing at the bottom of a range is a lonely thing to have made, and here was a desk a long way off, saying *we think you may be right*.

La Chapelle did not sit straighter. She read to the end and then went back, he saw, to the first paragraph, the gravimeter, and stayed there.

"It would drop one," she said. "The first or the second. One pass."

"It would."

"I have wanted to drop one since the day I filed them." She said it flatly, as a measurement. "Three is too many to hold. I re-derive them on the boat and they come out three every time, and every time I think, there is one instrument that would make this two, and it is in a store somewhere, in a crate."

"So you would send the request," said de Courtney, from the shutters.

"No, Chief."

Strozzi kept his face as it was. He had found, over the years, that the moment before a junior gives her reason is the one worth attending to, and that most seniors talk through it.

"The third reading doesn't move," la Chapelle said. "Whichever way it came back. The Kingdom has two records with that room in them. One has a name for what's standing there and one has a place where a name isn't. The instrument has a wavelength. A gravimeter tells me which kind of thing the wavelength is. It tells me nothing about what either of their records is a record *of*. I'd go from three readings to two, and feel a great deal better, and know exactly as much as I do now about the only part that's theirs."

"That is an argument that the instrument is insufficient," Strozzi said mildly. "It is not an argument against asking for it."

"No." She looked at the request again. "That one's the Chief's. But I'd have asked the same question before I spent it. What would we have to already understand, for the answer to be worth what it cost?" A pause. "And I'm not raising the second reading."

"I have told them so," he said. "I attached your struck sentence."

She was, briefly, appalled. Then he saw her work out that it was the correct thing to have attached.

"There was a novice," she said, to neither of them in particular, putting her reader down. "On the steps, some weeks ago. She asked me whether I write down who told me a thing. I said always. I've been thinking since that there's one line in that log I can't do it for, and it's the first one."

De Courtney turned from the shutters and looked at her for some time.

"Write that up," she said. "At whatever band it gets."

::::: scene 5

::: pov anne-de-courtney

She wrote the reply after they had gone, under the lamp, with the town gone quiet below the compound and the wind doing what it did.

She had written this letter before. Not to this desk, but the letter. She had a paragraph for it that she had not much varied in three years: *the Kingdom gave one thing, and a delegation that asks for a second before it can reliably read the first is teaching a court that a grant is a lever.* The people who received it thought she had confused restraint with policy. They were probably half right. She wrote the paragraph out again, since it was still so.

Then she stopped, with the pen up.

*Before it can reliably read the first.* She had always meant the language by that. The first thing was the delegation itself: one compound, one standing permission, a tongue nobody had heard in longer than the Archive could measure. Do not ask for more until you can read what you were handed. She had been saying it for three years as a rule she had brought with her.

She thought about what la Chapelle had told her of that day at the Abbey. An old man had climbed the steps and said a line. He had asked one question, whether the book had the room. He was answered. He was told there was a name as well, and that it was not offered, because the court had not asked. And he went down again. He did not ask.

She thought about the Abbess, who answered what the Accounting states and nothing it does not, to the court and to the delegation alike.

She thought about a court that took a season to say *the keeper decides*, and had not sent since to ask what the strangers' thing was hearing.

She had believed she was being careful at these people. It had not occurred to her until this evening that there might be nothing foreign in it. They had lived longer than either record could count between a chronicle and a book that disagreed, and nobody had pressed. A delegation that sat still and asked for nothing might look to them less like restraint than like manners. She might have been three years learning to say *good morning* and taking it for a policy.

It was a reading. Nobody had told her. She had it from the outside of other people's conduct. Anne de Courtney had sent back more of her own people's sentences than the court had ever refused her, and for exactly this fault: a sentence that claimed to know something the delegation was not told.

She did not write it. She let it stand where la Chapelle would have put it, at the bottom, under her own name, and went back to the letter.

*The delegation will not forward the request. The class may be as the desk supposes. The delegation asked the court for one thing and was given a keeper's decision, made on ground no Ranger has seen, by a person the court has not named, for reasons nobody has told us. It has not yet understood what it was given. It will not ask for a second thing.*

She read it through for anything it claimed to know. It claimed nothing. It was the plainest account of three years' work that she had ever sent.

On a floor she had never seen, the first thing went on listening. The delegation did not come.
