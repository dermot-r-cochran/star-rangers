::: pov anne-de-courtney

The town had gone to the Grove. From the working room Anne de Courtney could see four streets, and there was a dog asleep in one of them.

The clerk had come a fortnight before. He had stood at the compound gate and given the day, in the form the court uses when the room requires it, with the hour the garden quarter would be closed to carts. Then he had stopped. She had waited, as one waits for the second half of a sentence. There was no second half. He had not said *come*. She had thanked him in the words Strozzi had taught her for being told a thing, which are not the words for being given one.

She did not know what it meant. She had spent an evening establishing that she did not. In four years the court had offered her audiences, in rooms, and she had gone to those. She had never been inside the Elder Grove. It was possible that only the Kingdom's own ever went under it, or that the court had simply forgotten her. It was possible that telling her the day was the invitation, in a form she could not yet read, and that the whole Kingdom was at this moment noting her absence. Nobody had told her which. She had on the table a day that she had been told, and nothing she had been asked.

So she had ruled on what she had. The delegation would keep an ordinary day. It was one of la Chapelle's three on Aduine, and la Chapelle would take the boat. Strozzi had the week's translations. She herself had the quarter's filings, which were late.

On the wall by the door the clause had gone the colour of the plaster. *By invitation only.*

She had not expected the sound to carry.

It came over the roofs in the middle of the morning, when the wind dropped. It was faint. It was a great many voices saying one thing at one level. At that distance it was less like speech than like a mill a long way off. But it had lines in it, and she could hear them close, one after another.

She looked at the shutters. To shut them would be a thing done about it. To stand at them would be attending. She turned her chair so that her back was to the window and took up the filings. The Kingdom said its descent to the back of her head.

After some while she noticed the other table.

"Strozzi. You are not writing."

"No," he said.

::::: scene 2

::: pov ernesto-strozzi

He had not been translating for some minutes. He had been trying not to listen, which is the harder task, and he had never been good at it.

He was glad of the distance. No word of it reached him. Had the wind stood a little further round he would have had one word in five. He knew exactly what he would have done with one word in five, because he had done it once with a cognate.

What reached him was the shape. He knew the voice that is not a speaking voice. A clerk had shown it to him in the first year, and la Chapelle had brought it back from the Abbey's steps with the pauses marked. He had heard it only from one mouth at a time. Nobody had ever told him that it was said by so many together. He could have written that down. It was not something he had been told. It was something he had overheard, through a window, at a ceremony to which he had not been asked. He took a clean sheet and headed it *As heard*, so that it could not be taken later for anything else.

*From the working room, with no instrument. Many voices, together or near it. Level. Lines audible as lines.*

Then it stopped.

It had not ended. He knew what an ending sounded like in that voice, and this was not one. It stopped in the middle of its own pulse, as a clock does. He looked up. The Chief had looked up as well, with her back to the window, and had looked down again at once.

He counted. He did not decide to. A linguist times a silence as a carpenter eyes a length, and he had nothing to time it with but his own wrist. He passed ten beats. He passed twenty, which was longer than he would have allowed any pause in any language he had worked.

Then it all came in at once. For the space of one line it went quicker than anything before it, and then it was as it had been.

He wrote it. *One break. Something over twenty beats by this officer's pulse, which is not an instrument. Resumed together. The first line after the break quicker than the rest. Not read.*

He put the question to that last pair of words, as he was obliged to. What would have to be true for *not read* to be wrong, and had he looked? He would have had to know what the break was. He looked. He found that he could think of five things it might have been. A custom of silence. A death remembered. A name that is not said aloud. A fault, in somebody very important. A single voice too small to carry up a hill.

He was pleased to find that he did not prefer any of them. Four years ago he would have preferred one by noon.

"There was a break," he said. "I have put it down as heard. Do you want to know how long?"

"No," said the Chief, to her filings. "Put it in the file. I'll read it there."

::::: scene 3

::: pov anne-de-courtney

La Chapelle came in off the evening boat with salt on her collar. She did not sit down.

"I have it, Chief. The thing you asked me for, on the first line of the log. I think I have. It's short."

"How short?"

"It was four pages in the storm season."

Anne had asked for it once. *Write that up, at whatever band it gets.* She had not asked again in all the months since, and that had cost her something on a number of evenings. A thing asked for twice comes back the size the asker wanted. She had watched la Chapelle carry it on and off the Aduine boat for a season, and had asked after her translations.

"What does it want?"

"Today's line from the instrument. The relay's over within the hour." La Chapelle put her reader on the table. "And whatever you and the Principal Officer have of the morning. I've one thing of my own. A novice told me what the boats said."

"And the Abbey?"

"Kept its day. The offices at their hours, and the correctors at the shelves." She said it flatly, as a count. "They enter the day's line after the evening office. I'll read it when I'm next there."

"You did not wait for it."

"The boat doesn't. And it'll say then what it says tonight."

Strozzi handed his sheet across without a word. La Chapelle read the heading, and something went over her face that Anne could not place.

"As heard," la Chapelle said.

"It seemed the honest heading," said Strozzi.

"It's the one I've been using." She turned to Anne. "And yours, Chief?"

"I heard what he heard."

"For how long? The break."

"I did not count." She considered whether that was a thing to be ashamed of, and decided that it was only a thing that was so. "Put that I did not count."

The relay chimed while la Chapelle was still standing. She sat down then, at the end of the table, and wrote for something under an hour. Anne did the quarter's filings, and did them badly, and did not look up.

::::: scene 4

::: pov ernesto-strozzi

She gave it to him first. He supposed that was because he asked the question in the same tone every time.

*FIVE ISLANDS DELEGATION. Note by the keeper of the register on the Krilzat instrument, asked for by the Chief Ranger in the third year. Filed on the day of the crowning, in the fourth. No band. This is not a reading.*

*What the instrument had today. One optical return: standing figure, same position, lamp wavelength, 00:03:16. Thermal nil. Sound nil. Absent thereafter. It is the two hundred and fourteenth return since placing, and all are of this kind. The count is given as a count. It supports nothing, for the reason filed in the third year.*

*What the instrument has not had. The log does not describe who brings the lamp. For six evenings together in the middle of the last storm season there was no return. The instrument reported nil at each pass of the relay, and was sound. The delegation does not know why no lamp came. The instrument registers nothing in that room without a lamp. It cannot say whether anyone came without one, or what was in the room.*

*The first line. The log's first word is "Placed". The delegation asked that its instrument be set where the keeper decided, and it was. The delegation has not been told who the keeper is; who placed the instrument; whether the hand that placed it is the hand that brings the lamp; or why that room. None of these is a fault in the instrument, and no instrument would mend one of them. Each is a thing that somebody on Krilzat knows. The placing could be read by being told, and in no other way known to this officer. The delegation has not been told. Whether it asks is not this officer's to say.*

*The day. A crowning was held under the Elder Grove. The court told the delegation the day a fortnight before, and did not ask it to come. The delegation did not ask. Beyond the return above, it has of the day:*

*As heard, by the Chief Ranger and the Principal Officer, from the working room, with no instrument: the chronicle said by many voices, in the morning. One break, something over twenty beats by the Principal Officer's pulse. The Chief Ranger did not count. The first line after the break was quicker than the rest. Not read.*

*As heard, by this officer, on the Abbey's steps, from a novice, who had it from the boats: the heir is crowned, under the tree, this morning.*

*The Long Accounting's line for the day is entered after the evening office. This officer had left. It will be read on her next day at the shelf and filed then, as read.*

*So for the one day the delegation holds an instrument's line and two things overheard. It holds neither of the Kingdom's two records of the day. It has no reading of either.*

*Proceed carefully.*

He read it three times. The first was for what it said. The second was for what would have to be true for it to be wrong.

That took longer than the reading. A filing is wrong by claiming. He went down it for claims and found very few to test. There was a duration from an instrument, and he had the log open beside him and the duration was there. There was a count, and he made it two hundred and fourteen. There were six evenings, and they were the six he remembered her showing him, in the storm season, with nothing in them. There was his own break, in his own words.

Everything else on the half page was the delegation saying what it had not been told. He could test that. He had been here four years, and he went back through them. Nobody had told them who the keeper was. Nobody had told them why that room.

The third time he read it as he had read the desk's letter in the storm season, because he recognised it. Since the nine months of *tribute* he had taught juniors that a band is a claim about the claimer. He had not thought to tell one of them that a file could be mostly that, and be sound.

"It is correct," he said. He did not say it was good. She had never been one who came for that.

"It hasn't a band."

"There is nothing in it to put one on."

La Chapelle looked, for a moment, as she had looked when he told her he had attached her struck sentence to his reply.

::::: scene 5

::: pov anne-de-courtney

She read it after they had gone, under the lamp, for the one thing she read everything for.

It claimed that an instrument had returned what it returned. It claimed that two officers had heard a silence, and that one of them had not counted. It claimed that a novice had said what some boats said. She went through it for a sentence that knew more than the delegation had been told. She had found that sentence in nearly everything that had crossed this table in four years, in her own letters as often as anywhere.

There was not one. There was a list, and she read the list twice. *Who the keeper is. Who placed the instrument. Whether the same hand brings the lamp. Why that room.*

She had said in this room, in the storm season, that they had read the instrument and had not read the placing. She had thought of that afterwards as a thing to be put right. She had supposed, without ever saying so, that la Chapelle's write-up would be the beginning of putting it right: a first careful reading, low in the register, that could be built on.

It was not a reading. It said where reading stopped. Past that there was only being told, and nobody was obliged to tell them. She thought of a door on a rock she had never seen. A lamp had come to it two hundred and fourteen times, and on six evenings had not, for a reason that was somebody's.

She did not send it back.

Below the compound the town was coming home under lamps, and it was saying a name. She had known the name for four years. A clerk had given it to Strozzi in their first season, as the heir's. It came up the street now in a great many mouths, with other words round it that she could not follow. Nobody had told her what had been said under the tree. She had been told a day.

She took a clean sheet for the covering line to the Archive. She wrote: *Filed as it stands. The delegation was told the day of the crowning, and kept it as a day.* She read that through for anything it claimed to know.

Then she filed the half page under its date, and left the shutters as they were.
