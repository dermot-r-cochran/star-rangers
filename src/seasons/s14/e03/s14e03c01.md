::: pov went-round

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there, and this time the someone went looking for a far side and found a person sitting on it.

Went-Round had lost something, and it was not a thing you can go and look for.

It was the far side. There had always been one. A blocked passage has another way in. A hard thought looks different from behind. That is not a thing Went-Round believes. It is a thing Went-Round had found, every time, by going, for a whole telling of a life. Then Stone-First brought a thing down the shelving that was too short to be a telling and too long to be a hand, and Went-Round went all the way round it and came back to the near side, and the far side turned out to have been lying in the middle of the warm the whole time, under a small one's hand. An old one who does not say much had seen it there and said so, in an ordinary voice, without getting up.

That was some tellings ago. Since then the telling had gone up, and the up-person had stayed, and the warm had said *they stayed* until it was worn smooth. Everything had come out well. Went-Round was glad of it, and went on being bothered anyway, the way you go on feeling a stone in the place you lie after you have moved it.

So Went-Round did what Went-Round does with a bother, which is take it round the warm.

Past the middle, where the small ones were playing. Past Carried-It-Sleeping, lying where the small ones could be counted. Out to the edge of the amber, where the ones who do not say much lie.

There was a gap at the edge.

A warm place has a shape, and the shape had a piece out of it, and the piece was exactly the size of an old one.

"Where?" said Went-Round, to the next old one along.

"The shelving. The foot of it. Went when the working time began. Has done for a few now." And then, kindly, because it was Went-Round asking: "You could ask *them* why. Everyone asks them everything else."

::::: scene 2

::: pov went-round

Went-Round moved a stone first, at the turn of the passage, so that the ground said *someone is coming*, and then came on. That is manners, even for somebody you have lain a few places along from for your whole life.

The old one was sitting at the foot of the shelving, where the broken rock begins, beside the small ones' first line.

It was still there. A row of small stones across the passage, from wall to wall, where the game had begun before it moved down into the warm. Nobody had cleared it away. A stone moved for you is not yours to move, and nobody had ever settled who those had been moved for, so there they lay.

The old one has a name. It is a good one, short and old. It is not said in this telling, and you will hear why.

"You found me," said the old one. "You went round."

"I asked the one who lies next to you."

"That is going through," said the old one, and made the small sound, and Went-Round sat down.

They sat. Up the shelving, out of sight at the top of it, was the line, and Stone-First on the line. Above that, a long way up, the up-people's loud had come down the long passage and settled, the way it settles. It was a working time like any other.

"I came to ask how you found it," said Went-Round. "The hand. *As long as the hand is on it.* I went all the way round that thing and there was nothing on the other side. You did not even stand up."

"I did not find it."

"You said it."

"I said what I was doing." The old one looked at the row of small stones. "I was lying at the edge, waiting for a small one to move a stone. The same as you. The same as everybody. And I noticed that I was. That is all of it."

"Nobody else noticed."

"Nobody else had been teased. I made the small sound in the middle of nothing, the first time the smallest did that with the hand, and I was told about it every telling afterwards by people who were doing the same thing and did not know. So I was watching myself. To catch it before it came out again." The old one thought about that. "You should thank the ones who teased me. I do not."

Went-Round turned it over, looking for the side on which it was cleverer than that.

There was not one.

"You cannot go round a thing you are lying in," said the old one, not unkindly. "You were lying in it. So was I. I only looked down."

::::: scene 3

::: pov went-round

"Why here?" said Went-Round, after a while. "It is not warm here."

"No."

"There is nobody to lie beside."

"No," said the old one, with some feeling. "There is not."

Then it came out, a piece at a time, the way things come out of people who do not say much.

In the warm, now, when the old one breathed in, it went quiet. Not the whole warm. The pieces nearest. They thought there might be another one coming. The old one had lain at the edge of the amber since before the up-people came, and had breathed in a great many times, and nobody had ever stopped a telling for it.

And the small ones asked things. How long would the up-people wait next time? Would they wait for nothing, if a hand was put on the rock and no telling came? Were they cold, standing there?

"I have never been asked whether an up-person was cold," said the old one. "I have never seen one. I have been *told* one."

"What do you say?"

"That I do not know."

"And?"

"And they think that is me keeping a hand on it."

Went-Round laughed, out loud, which is a different sound from the small one, and it went up the shelving and came back.

"It is all very well for you," said the old one. "You like being asked." And then, lower: "There is a thing going about. I have heard it twice. *The one who said we are doing it.*"

Went-Round stopped laughing.

Everyone below the roof knows what twice is. Twice is a thing said about someone. The third time, and the times after, are how it stops being that.

"I have a name," said the old one. "It was said of me when I was smaller than Keeps-A-Hand-On-It is now, and it is still true, and it is short. I should like it left short."

"A name grows when a truer thing is said. It is not for wanting or not wanting."

"I know how a name grows. I have listened to more of them grow than you have. It grows by somebody saying it a third time, in the warm, where it can be heard." The old one looked at Went-Round. "And when a thing gets said in the warm, it is very often you."

That was fair. Went-Round did not pretend it was not.

Went-Round did not promise, either. A promise about what you are going to say is a rule, and a rule made out of one telling is a rule you go round later. But Went-Round has not said it. Not then, and not since. And that is why the old one's own name is not in this telling. Put a name in, and somebody hearing it will try the other words after it, quietly, to find out how they sound. This telling is not going to be where that happens.

::::: scene 4

::: pov went-round

Then the knock came.

Went-Round had heard the knock on every working time for two years, lying in the warm, and had never heard it from here. In the warm it is one sound with two parts. The up-people's stone, small and late, and Stone-First's answer on top of it before the ground has finished. The small ones who were born since think it is all one thing.

From the foot of the shelving it is two.

The up-people's stone. *Someone.* Small. Theirs.

Then a gap. Not a long one. You could put a breath in it.

The old one did not put a breath in it. The old one had gone still, with the whole body, the way you go still for a hand on a stone, and Went-Round saw it, and understood what the foot of the shelving was for.

Then Stone-First's answer. A good one. At once.

The old one breathed out.

"You come here for that," said Went-Round.

"I come for the gap. You cannot hear it from the edge of the amber." The old one was quiet for as long as it takes to say a name. "I said they would wait as long as the hand is on it."

"You did."

"I said it in an ordinary voice. Like a thing I knew. And the warm took it, and Carried-It-Sleeping took it up the shelving, and Stone-First bent to that rock and kept a hand on it in front of an up-person. On my saying." The old one's hands were flat on the broken rock. "I did not know. Nobody knew. It came out. And I lie at the edge of the amber, with everyone going quiet round me, and I think about the one where it did not come out. The up-person waits a hand. And goes. And Stone-First is bent on the line with a hand on a rock and a telling not begun. That one is mine as well. It is as much mine as this one is."

Went-Round did not answer quickly.

There are things you go round. This was not one. It had no far side to be easier from, and Went-Round knew that without setting off to look, which may have been the first time. So Went-Round sat in it, beside the old one, for a while.

"I was there," said Went-Round, at last. "When you said it. So I can settle one piece. You did not say they would stay."

"I did."

"You said *nobody knows how long they would*. And then you said *and I say* they would wait as long as the hand is on it. *I say.* Everyone heard that. The warm did not take a thing you knew. It took a thing you said, and it knew which one it was taking. It had been wrong with Stone-First's thing for tellings and tellings. It took yours because yours was the first one that could be carried up the shelving and tried. Not because it was sure."

"That is going round."

"No. That is what was said. Ask anyone who was there. It was all of us."

The old one thought about it.

"And Stone-First?"

"Stone-First put a hand on that rock once before. With the wanting to find out. And moved it at once, because a thing you do to see whether it works is not manners. Carried-It-Sleeping brought that down; you heard it. Stone-First did not keep the hand there to see whether you were right. Stone-First kept it there because that is how long a telling is." Went-Round went on, slowly, not going round, going along. "If they had gone, Stone-First would have moved the stone behind them. A good one. And come down, and told it standing up. *They went.* It would have been a telling with a middle in it. The warm would have made the small sound, and started again on how. And nobody would have said your name any differently." A breath. "Nobody would have said anything about you at all. You would have liked that better."

The old one made the small sound. It was a short one, and it was a real one.

"Do you think they would wait again?" said Went-Round. It came out before Went-Round could stop it.

"I do not *know*," said the old one. "I had one. I have said it."

::::: scene 5

::: pov went-round

They could have gone down then. Neither of them did.

The old one put a hand on a stone. Not one of the row. A loose one, lying beside it, that was nobody's.

"What are you doing?"

"Seeing whether the hand still knows."

The old one moved it. Once. There was a scrape in it.

"That was you, not the ground," said Went-Round, before thinking, in exactly the voice the small ones say it in.

The old one looked at Went-Round for a moment. Then moved it again. Once. Well. And the ground said what the ground says.

*Someone is coming.*

Nobody came. There were two old ones at the foot of the shelving, and a row of small stones, and a great deal of broken rock.

Went-Round looked at the small ones' line. There was nobody on it. There had been nobody on it for a long time.

Went-Round got up, and stood on it. Facing the shelving. With the whole body still, which is not a thing Went-Round is good at, and the old one watched Went-Round try.

"Again," said Went-Round.

"You know it is me. There is nobody else here."

"Then I will be right."

"It is not a good game with two."

"No," said Went-Round. "It is not."

The old one knocked. Once, well. *Someone is coming.* A step back, on old legs, with a hand out to the wall. Went-Round answered with a stone, a good one, and said who had knocked. Said the name. The short one, the old one, the only one the old one has.

"Right," said the old one. "Stay on."

The next time, the old one put a hand to the stone and did not move it.

Went-Round waited. There was nothing to go round. There was a line of small stones underfoot, and the shelving ahead, and a hand on a stone somewhere behind and below, and the stone had not spoken. You could not get to the other side of that. You could only be in the middle of it, with the whole body still, for as long as it takes to say a name, and then for longer.

Went-Round had lain in exactly this, in the warm, on every telling for a season, and had not seen it, and had been told so by a person who looked down.

It was easier to see standing up.

Then the stone moved, and the ground said so, and Went-Round answered it, and said the name again, and was right again, and was more pleased about that than there was any reason to be.

::::: scene 6

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First-Who-Waited heard it from the line.

The up-people's stone was down, a hand from Stone-First's feet, small and plain. The up-person had waited a hand, and gone. The four stones were where they were, and the long row. It was a working time when nothing happens, which is most of them.

And from below, from the foot of the shelving, the ground said *someone is coming*.

Nobody came.

Stone-First did not move. Stone-First had learned that sound twice already: once as the worst sound below the roof, and once as small ones. This was neither. A stone has the hand in it that moved it, the way a step has the foot. These were not small hands. They were slow, and heavy, and one of them had a scrape in it that a small one would have been told about.

Old hands. Two of them. Stone-First knew whose, the way you know who is coming up the shelving before they are on it. One who goes round things. And one who has lain at the edge of the amber since before the up-people came, and who said a thing, some tellings ago, that Stone-First had carried on the inside of a hand ever since.

*As long as the hand is on it.*

Stone-First had not weighed that, when it came up the shelving in Carried-It-Sleeping's mouth. You do not weigh what the warm sends up. You carry it. It was only afterwards, on the line, on the times when nothing happens, that Stone-First had thought how much had been done on it, and how little anybody had known. Somebody had said a thing they did not know, and said that they did not know it, and a whole people had put a hand on a rock.

It was a great deal to have been given. Stone-First had not said so to the old one. Stone-First hardly ever says so.

Below, the ground said *someone is coming* again.

Then nothing, for a while. For longer than a knock takes. For as long as a hand might be kept on a stone by somebody who had watched a small one do it all season and wanted to know what it was like from the other end.

Stone-First waited for it. There was no reason not to. It was a stone that was going to speak.

It spoke. Nobody came.

Stone-First stood on the line, in the light that never changes, with the whole body still, and listened to two old ones at the bottom of the margin move stones for nobody, and did not go down to see who was winning.
