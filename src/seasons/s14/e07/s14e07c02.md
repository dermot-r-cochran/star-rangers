::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof: by someone who was there, to someone who was not, until the one who was not can tell it too.

It began with the small ones' game going wrong, which it had never done before.

The game had grown. It grows the way a telling does, by somebody adding the next true thing. First it was a knock and an answer and saying who knocked. Then, after the smallest was named, it was a hand kept on the stone and the whole warm waiting. And now there was the newest thing to put in, the biggest thing that had ever happened on the line, and the small ones had been trying to put it in for longer than anyone had patience for.

One on the line. One to knock. The knocker moves a stone and steps back and waits a hand. The one on the line puts a hand on the answering stone, and keeps it there, and tells. And the knocker *stays*.

That was the part that would not play.

Carried-It-Sleeping lay in the middle of the warm and listened to it not playing. The one on the line told the first warm seep, because it is the telling every small one has, and told it badly, bent over, with a hand on a stone. And the knocker stood there being an up-person. And the knocker knew the first warm seep. Every word. You could see the knocker knowing it. When the one on the line left out the part about the smell, the knocker said, "You left out the smell," and the game fell down.

They tried it again with a different knocker. That one hummed all the way through, so as not to hear.

"That is the humming stone," said the one on the line. "You are being the wrong thing."

They tried it with the knocker's back turned. They tried it with the one on the line telling very fast. A small one suggested telling a telling nobody knew, and was asked which one that was, and could not say, which was the trouble exactly.

In the end they came to the warm with it, the way they come with a thing they cannot get round, all one piece, and the one who did the asking was the one who had been knocking.

"We cannot do the staying," said the small one. "You have to not know it. Nobody does not know it."

Several of the old ones made the small sound.

"The up-person stood for all of it and did not know a word," said the small one. "Stone-First said. We want to know how you stand for a thing when you do not know a word. Who has?"

"You have," said an old one. "All of you. The first time you were told the seep."

"We do not remember that."

That was true, and the old one let it be. Nobody remembers the first time. It is the only telling everyone was there for and nobody can tell.

"Who has, and was big enough to keep it?"

The warm thought. You could feel it think. And then you could feel it turn, which is a different thing. Nobody moved. But a warm place has a shape, and the shape of a warm full of people all thinking of the same person leans, the way the heat leans toward a seep, and Carried-It-Sleeping felt it lean, from the edge of the amber inward, old ones first, to the middle.

Carried-It-Sleeping had not thought of that telling in a long time. It was the first one. It was from before the name.

"I have," said Carried-It-Sleeping, and stood up.

::::: scene 2

::: pov teddy

It is a short telling. Stone-First made it short, once, by asking for it again with one thing left out, and it has been that length ever since.

Carried-It-Sleeping told it the way it goes. The squeeze, which fitted then. The runners' air. The light that moves, at the end of a long passage, and three of them in it, small, and wrapped in something that was not fur, and loud. Not loud like the runners. Loud like a stone that never finishes falling. They looked at their lights. One of them laughed. One of them knelt by the humming stone and said things to it, and it hummed. And the youngest stood at the edge of their light, on the open floor, for as long as the first warm seep takes to tell, twice.

And nobody looked up.

The small ones knew it. Everyone below the roof has been told it. It is one of the old ones now, the way the coming of the up-people is, told by people who were not there. Carried-It-Sleeping had heard small ones tell it to smaller ones and get the laugh in the wrong place.

But Carried-It-Sleeping had not told it since the telling went up. And a telling you have told many times can still say a thing to you that it has not said before, if something has happened in between. The old ones say so. Carried-It-Sleeping had not believed it, quite, and was standing in the middle of the warm believing it.

*For as long as the first warm seep takes to tell, twice.*

That was how long. Carried-It-Sleeping had counted it, at the time, to have a length to bring home. And had always told it as how long nobody looked.

It was also how long somebody stood.

::: pov keeps-a-hand-on-it

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had been told that telling before, as a small thing among the other told things, somewhere between the long dark and the coming of the up-people. It had always been a telling about how strange the up-people were. They do not look. They cannot hear the ground. A whole one of the people can stand at the edge of their light and they go on with their lights.

This time it was not about that.

This time Keeps-A-Hand-On-It was hearing it with a hand still warm from a stone, after a whole season of finding out what waiting is. And what Keeps-A-Hand-On-It heard was somebody standing very still for a long time while a thing went on. Keeps-A-Hand-On-It knew about that. It was the one thing Keeps-A-Hand-On-It knew more about than anyone in the warm.

So when it was done and nobody had made the small sound yet, Keeps-A-Hand-On-It asked. Small ones may. It is one of the good things about being one.

"Did you stand with the whole body still?"

"Yes," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "All of it."

"Did you know a word of it?"

"No."

"Did they stop? To look if you were there for it?"

"No. They did not stop. They said it the way they were saying it, all the way through."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It thought about the hand. It was always, in the end, about the hand.

"The one who knelt to the humming stone. They said a thing, and it hummed."

"Yes."

"Once?"

Carried-It-Sleeping was still for a moment, the way you are when a thing you have told a great many times has a piece in it you have never been asked for.

"Twice," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "They said a thing to it, and it hummed. Then for a while they did not. Then they said a thing to it again, and it hummed again. I had forgotten it was twice. It is not in the telling. It was in the being there."

"Did they keep a hand on it? In between?"

And that was the question. The whole warm knew it was the question, old ones and all, because the whole warm had spent a season learning what a hand on a stone is: a stone that is going to speak, and a thing you wait for, for as long as the hand is there.

"I do not know," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

"You were there."

"I was there. I was counting the first warm seep, to know how long it was. I was not looking at the hand. I did not know, then, that a hand was a thing to look at. Nobody did. You had not been born."

Nobody else had been there. So that was the end of it, by the oldest rule there is, and Keeps-A-Hand-On-It lay back down, not minding. A waiting does not have to come out to be a good one.

It was Went-Round who could not leave it, which surprised nobody.

Went-Round went round. You could hear it happen. "A telling is from one who was there to one who was not," said Went-Round, slowly, to the amber. "There were three of them and they were all there. So whatever they were saying, it was not a telling to each other. If it was a telling, it was for someone who was not there." A pause. "And I do not know who that would be. And if it was a telling, then somebody has heard it, and we know who, and a telling is not yours until somebody has."

Then Went-Round stopped, and was quiet for longer than Went-Round usually is.

"I have come out where I went in," said Went-Round. "I do not say it was a telling. I do not know what the loud is. Nobody below the roof does. I say that I went all the way round it and that is what is on the far side: the same thing, facing the other way."

The warm did not make the small sound. It did not settle anything, either. It lay in the amber and held the thing, the way it holds the shapes, without a word for it and without a rule, and after a while the small ones went back to their game and played it the old way, with a knock and an answer, because that one works.

Carried-It-Sleeping went up the shelving and moved a stone at the top.

Stone-First-Who-Waited was on the line, in the open, facing the squeeze, with the four stones and the long row. Stone-First did not turn. The ground had already said who.

"The small ones asked for nobody looked up," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "I told it. The one that is mine."

Stone-First waited to be told.

"There were two, the first time. You asked for it again with a thing left out, and you said that one is yours, and the other one is not yet." Carried-It-Sleeping had not known, coming up the shelving, that the words were still there, every one, in the order they had been said. "You said I would know when it was."

"I said you would know."

It was said the way Stone-First says most things, with nothing leaning on it. Carried-It-Sleeping stood on the broken rock and heard, a great many tellings late, what had and had not been promised.

"It is not going to be mine."

"No."

"It was that they cannot hear the ground. It was the finding. I was so pleased with it." Carried-It-Sleeping looked at the newest stone, a hand from Stone-First's feet, small and plain. "One of them sets that down at the start of every working time and waits for yours. And goes when it speaks. Not before. They hear that one. So it was true when I found it, and now it is not, and in between it was never anybody's, because nobody had heard it but you, and you would not have it."

"I heard it," said Stone-First. "I asked for the other."

"Why?"

Stone-First did not answer that. Stone-First hardly ever does. A thing explained is a thing you have been told, and Carried-It-Sleeping had been told a great many things by Stone-First and had been explained almost none, and was only now, standing at the top of the shelving, beginning to see how much room that had left.

So Carried-It-Sleeping answered it instead.

"What they cannot do was mine to guess. What they did, I was there for." A breath. "And what they cannot do, they can stop not doing. What they did stays done."

Stone-First made the small sound. Carried-It-Sleeping had heard that perhaps twice in a whole life, and neither time for anything Carried-It-Sleeping had said.

That would have been enough to come up for. It was not what Carried-It-Sleeping had come up for.

"There is a thing in the one that is mine. I did not know it was in there. I stood still for all of theirs, twice through the seep, and did not know a word. And they did not look to see." The line was very quiet. It always is. "When you told, up here, with the hand on the rock. Did you look to see?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"You do not, with a small one. You tell it."

"I thought nobody looked up was a thing about them," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "A thing they could not do. I told it that way to everyone, the first time, and you would not have it, and I told it short, and I still thought it. Under the telling. That they did not look because they could not." A pause. "What if it was the other thing? What if they were only saying what they had to say, properly, and not stopping to see who was there for it?"

Stone-First was still for as long as it takes to say a name, and then for longer.

"I was not there," said Stone-First-Who-Waited. "You were."

"I do not know."

"Then it is not settled."

"No."

"Some thoughts are for keeping," said Stone-First, "not for finishing." And then, because Stone-First leaves nothing out, not even after all this time: "You did not move a stone."

"No," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "I did not."

"So they did not know you were there."

"No."

It had been a scolding, the first time. It was the same words now and it was not a scolding, and Carried-It-Sleeping could not have said what had been taken out of it. Only that if a stone had been moved, that first time, properly, the way the manners say, and the ground had told them, and three heads had come round, there would have been nothing to stand for. There would have been three up-people looking at a youngest. That is a different telling, and a much shorter one.

Carried-It-Sleeping did not say that. It was not a thing to make a rule of. You move a stone first. That is manners, and it was manners then, and the small ones are told it every telling by the one who once did not.

Carried-It-Sleeping moved a stone at the top of the shelving, going, and went down to keep them.

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First keeps the margin.

Keeping the margin means being at the place where the deep stops, on all the times when nothing happens, so that you are there on the time when something does. Stone-First had been doing it since before the youngest had a name, and the youngest had one now and was not the youngest, and had just gone down the shelving moving a stone, the way you are supposed to.

Nothing had happened, in the sense that mattered. Nothing had come up the squeeze or gone down it. Nobody had been told anything they had not been told before. A telling that everybody knew had been asked for by the small ones and told by the one whose it was, and it was the same telling. Not a word of it had moved.

It was only facing the other way.

Stone-First did not say that to anyone. It was Went-Round's, from the warm, and it had come up the shelving in Carried-It-Sleeping's mouth without being told, the way a true thing sometimes does. Stone-First set it down on the inside, beside the thought about the asking, which was also not finished, and left it there.

Then the working time came, and the loud came down.

Down the long passage, all at once and then steadily. Settling at the junction, the way it settles. Stone-First had heard it settle on every working time since the up-people came, and had waited through it. It was what came before the thing you were there for. It was the sound of them being where they were. A stone that never finishes falling; the youngest had said so first, and everyone below the roof had said it since.

Stone-First stood on the line with the whole body still.

That is how Stone-First stands. Nothing about it was different that anyone could have seen, and nobody was there to see. Four stones. The long row. The crack, dark. The light that never changes, amber on one wall and blue-green on the other.

And the loud, coming through the squeeze. The squeeze takes whatever is in it and leaves the shape. Up, and along. A gap the length of a breath. More of it. One of them laughed; that comes through whole. Then, under the rest, one voice by itself, low, saying a thing and stopping. And after it, so small that you would have to have kept a line for a very long time to know it from the fungus and the rock, a hum.

Stone-First did not know a word of it.

Stone-First did not go up to see who was saying it, or to what. The line was where Stone-First had put it, and it was still the right place. Stone-First did not lean toward the crack, or turn the head, or do any of the things you do when you want a sound to know you are there for it. You stand with the whole body still, so that nothing you do gets into it by mistake. Stone-First had taught that to more small ones than there were stones on the line. It had not once crossed Stone-First's mind that it could be done in this direction.

Whether it was a telling, Stone-First did not know. Nobody below the roof was there for the part that would settle it. It might be only what it had always been, the sound of the up-people being where they were. A thing can be stood for without being for you.

It went on for a while. Then it changed, the way it changes, and one of them was coming on, down the third passage, to the crack.

The light, low. The smallest of the up-people out onto the silt, breath not back, with a stone held to the front. The looking, which is short now. The kneeling. The stone set down a hand from Stone-First's feet, and the ground saying what it says, late, and theirs. The step back. The thing said that goes up at the end.

Stone-First bent, and put a hand on the rock, and moved it. At once. A good one.

So that they would know someone was there.
