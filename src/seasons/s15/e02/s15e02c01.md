::: pov wender

The form came in the deep winter, as it had each year since the first, and this time there was a letter under it.

Wender took the form first, because she knew it. *Dock Seven. Status: open. Holding officer: High Captain K. Wender. Grounds for continued holding. Anticipated date of closure.* It was the file for the failure point, where an officer of hers had held the boundary and had not been recovered, and where a signal had persisted ever since that the station's certified instruments filed as noise. The form had not changed by a word in any of its returns. She had thought well of it the first time, as a thing that asked for a date only because it had a place to put one, and had found no reason since to think worse.

The letter was a single leaf. Nobody at the Council had written to her on this file since the reply to her account, the one she had sent in place of a signature. They had written about the service in the hall, which was the ceremonial record's business and was filed there. On Dock Seven there had been the form each winter and nothing with it.

She read the leaf once for content.

*At this review the Council has read the Dock Seven file whole.*

*1. The anticipated date of closure has been returned empty at each review. The Council's instruction to holding officers requires a date. The Council has not entered one on a holding officer's behalf, and will not. It asks whether a date is held.*

*2. The file holds the report of counsel to a party to the inquiry. It quotes whole a statement of the holding officer: that she knows more of the flagged coordinate than she did when she declined to answer the claim, that she did not learn it by asking the officer concerned, and that she will not say what it is, since it is that officer's. The report was placed on the file when it was received, as counsel's. The Council made no finding upon it. It makes none now.*

*3. The file holds the Council's draft citation. It is unsigned.*

That was the whole of it. She read it a second time for what it was for, and went slowly. The last letter from that room had taught her not to trust the first place she arrived at.

The first item was a question, and it was not the form's question. The Council's instruction sheet said that a date was *required*. The letter asked whether one was *held*. Somebody had seen that those were different, after a line had come back empty winter upon winter, and had put the second. She looked for the demand under it and did not find one. *Has not entered one, and will not.* She had not known that anyone had proposed to. A body did not as a rule write down that it would not do a thing unless the thing had been put to it. She could not support that, and she left it.

The second item took longer.

Counsel had said she would quote all of it, and she had. It was there in three clauses, with nothing dropped for either side's convenience. Counsel had also said that the room could make what it liked of a High Captain who volunteered that her position had got worse. What the room had made of it was a paragraph. *Placed on the file. No finding.* It was the rung the account stood on.

And the room had asked nothing. She went through the item again to be certain the question was not in it, because it was the question she would have put in the room's place. The Council could not command a Ranger. It could ask, and what it asked the Corps was bound to answer. It had had on its file, ever since counsel sent the report, a High Captain's own statement that she was keeping something back. It had only to write *state what you hold*. It had not written that when the report came. Now it had read the whole file, on purpose, and had not written it again.

She had an answer for that question. It was one sheet, in the drawer at her right hand, written the evening counsel left. She had taken it out at each review and had never been able to improve it. It said that the Corps held nothing, that she held something which had reached her unasked and was another officer's, and that she would not produce it. There it stopped, and left the Council to decide what a High Captain was who wrote that to the one body she answered to. She had never thought it a good answer. It was the one she had.

She did not know why she had been spared it. The letter gave no reason, and she was not going to supply the room with one, a kind one or a tired one. Not asked was not excused. It was not asked.

The third item she took last, as it came. In the reply to her account the citation had stood alone at the foot, a line to be left with: *remains before the holding officer*. Here it was the third thing in a list of what the file held, in the same flat order as the other two. She looked for a withdrawal in that, and there was none. The draft was still the Council's, still on the file and still unsigned. The room had only stopped saying where it was waiting.

The draft lay at the left of the desk. She did not look at it.

There was an analyst on the file the Council had read whole, graded where the account was graded. The last time the room wrote, she had put the leaf in front of that analyst herself. It ought not to learn from a binder copy that it had been read again and passed over. She keyed the internal line.

"Rook. My office, when you're free. The Council has written on Dock Seven. Nothing in it is asked of you, and I would still rather you read it here."

::: pov rook

**Field Analyst Rook-7. Log open. Summoned to the High Captain's office, 1105 station. Stated reason: the Oversight Council has written on the Dock Seven file, and nothing in the letter is asked of this analyst. Rook-7 has not previously been summoned in order to be shown that a document does not concern it. Logging the reason as given.**

Rook-7 did not sit. The High Captain turned the leaf on the desk so that it could be read from where Rook-7 stood. She had done the same with the Council's reply, at this desk.

It read the letter once, and filed the assessment first.

**Assessment: the letter is consistent with the file as Rook-7 knows the file. Three items. Each states what the file holds. One asks a question. The arrays are not mentioned. Their certification status is not mentioned. This analyst is not mentioned. Rook-7 has read the leaf a second time for all three. It did not take long.**

"It asks one question," Rook-7 said.

"Whether I hold a date."

"Rook-7 requests one fact before it logs the letter. Is this the first letter the Council has written to the High Captain on the Dock Seven file since its reply to the account?"

She did not ask why it wanted to know. She seldom asked that of anyone.

"On the file, yes. The form has come each winter. They wrote to me about the service in the hall, and that went to the ceremonial record. Otherwise nothing."

"Then this is the next letter."

**Log opened at the entry for Deep Winter, 2831, the ninth day after the account was sent, 1020 to 1034 station. On that day the Council asked the certification status of the arrays, and was told that they are uncertified and cannot be certified, since no maker's specification is held. Prediction located. Text as logged: *a body that has been told an instrument cannot be certified for want of a specification will ask, in its next letter, who made it. Confidence moderate.* A note is attached. It refers the reader to an adjustment in the weight Rook-7 gives its own predictions about the Council.**

**Terms. *Its next letter.* The Council's review form has reached this station each deep winter since. Rook-7 did not count a form as a letter when it made the entry, and it is not going to begin now that a result is available. It notes that the stricter reading is the one that has kept the prediction open longest, and that an analyst should be slow to trust a reading with that property. On the looser reading the prediction failed at the first form. On the stricter it is tested today. The result is the same on both.**

**Result. The next letter asks whether a date is held. It does not ask who made the arrays. The prediction did not hold.**

"You have gone to your log," the High Captain said.

"Yes. Rook-7 logged a prediction on the day the reply came. It was logged so that it could be checked later. It has now been checked."

"What was it?"

"That the Council's next letter would ask who made the arrays."

She looked down at the leaf. She had certainly read it more than once already.

"It doesn't."

"It does not. Rook-7 has entered that."

"What did you rest it on?"

"On what a reader does. A reader who is told that an instrument cannot be certified because no maker's specification is held will want the maker next. Rook-7 would. The Council does not read in that way. It has written to the High Captain twice on this file since the account went. Each time it has asked for what the document in front of it could not be filed without, and for nothing beside. The account rested on the instruments, so the reply asked their status. The form has a line for a date, so this letter asks whether there is one. No document on the Council's file needs the maker in order to be filed."

"That is two letters."

"It is two letters. Rook-7 would not found a rule on two. It is describing them."

The High Captain was silent for a time. Rook-7 had learned that she was seldom idle when she was silent.

"If they had asked," she said. "Would you have had an answer for them?"

"Rook-7 would have had what it gave on certification, at more length. No specification is held. The station registry lists a standard suite and holds no fitting record. That is a statement of what the record holds. It is not a maker's name, and Rook-7 would have asked that the answer not let it sound like one."

"It wouldn't have."

She said nothing further. Rook-7 waited through the interval in which a further question would ordinarily have come. None came.

**Note. The High Captain asked what the prediction was, what it rested on, and what Rook-7 would have answered. She did not put the question itself. Count of times it has been put to this analyst by anyone: zero. The count is unchanged by the Council's letter and by this conversation.**

**Adjustment. Predictions about the Council logged by this analyst: one. Checked: one. Failed: one. An earlier expectation was never logged as a prediction and appears in this log only as surprise. In both cases Rook-7 expected the room to go further than its document required: the first time to say more than it could test, the second to ask more than it needed. The weight is lowered again, and the direction of the error is entered beside it. An error with a direction can be corrected for.**

"Is there anything you want in my answer?" the High Captain said. "The last time, there was."

"Nothing in this letter rests on Rook-7. An analyst that added itself to the answer would be supplying the question."

"Yes," she said. "Then that is all. Thank you for coming up."

**Observation, not sent. On the day of the reply the High Captain had three folded sheets at the right of her desk when Rook-7 came in, and at 1034 she put them in the drawer. Today the right of the desk was clear when Rook-7 came in, and is clear now. Rook-7 has no instrument for preparation. It logs the surface.**

**The arrays' column is due at the boundary desk at 1400. Nothing in the letter bears on it. Log held open.**

::::: scene 3

::: pov wender

She wrote the answer after Rook-7 had gone.

*1. None is held.*

It was three words, and she could stand behind each of them.

The second item had asked nothing, and for some minutes she meant to answer nothing to it. Then she thought of how the statement would read to whoever next took the file whole. It had been made in a summer, in this office, to a claimant's counsel, and it had stood since with nothing under it from the person who made it. A statement left like that came in time to read as a thing an officer had once said. It was not a thing she had once said. She would say it this afternoon, in the same words, to anyone with standing to hear it.

*2. The statement is quoted accurately. It stands.*

That gave the room no more than it had. She did not think the line would help her. She was fairly sure it was owed.

The third item asked nothing and stated a fact, and the fact was correct. She wrote nothing under it.

Then the form. Under *grounds for continued holding* she wrote *see standing order attached*, as she had each winter, and attached it. The order had not been amended. The reading was taken each shift and the arrays stayed on. Nothing further was sent from this side. The file was not closed from this side.

The line for the date she left as she had found it.

She had considered that, with the letter beside her. The room had asked whether a date was held, and she had told it. But the form's line asked for a date, and *none* was not a date. Written there, it would be read as *never* by everyone who had read *no finding* and heard *no*. She did not hold *never* either. She had an empty line in one document and an accurate sentence about it in another, and between them the two said what she knew.

She signed both, and sent the copies down for the binders.

After that she drew the draft citation across the desk and read it, as she had read it at each review. It had not changed, which had always been the difficulty with it. Every sentence was true. The sum still said *this is finished*. She put it back where it had been.

Then she opened the drawer at her right hand. The one sheet was where it was kept. She did not take it out.

The room had asked her one question this winter, and it had been a question she could answer. She did not expect that of every winter.
