::: pov tissadelle

The book was open.

It had lain on the kitchen table, closed, for more mornings than she would put a figure to, and she had not opened it. Nobody had said it was for her. It was not in her hand and she had not put it there, and a record is not offered by being left within reach. Her mother laid the table round it each morning, as she laid round anything left by somebody who would be back for it.

This morning it lay open at the first leaf, squared to her chair.

"Did you open that?" she said.

"I did not," said her mother, at the kettle. "It isn't mine. It was like that when I came down."

Tissadelle looked at it from the door. She had been on this hill since Dock Seven, where she had held the boundary from a ship that did not come back, and she did not have the number of the mornings since. It was spring, or the hill was showing spring: the high field had its green and the flock was up on it. She had been to the edge of the clearing first, as on most mornings, and had stood where the air was warm, and had come down with nothing to enter.

Now there was this. She set it out as she set out anything on the hill that had changed between one morning and the next. *Closed on the last morning held. Open today. By whom: not established.* The third course of the wall had been reset once in the same way, and she had never got further with that than the fact.

She did not look round for him. He was Tobble. He had been in her head for longer than she had known how to file a report, he was a cat because that was what he was, and he did every sum twice. If he was there, he was watching without appearing to, and in all her life she had not caught him at it.

*Good morning,* she said, inward, as on every morning.

Then she sat down in the chair the book was squared to. In every office she had worked in, a file turned to face a reader had been handed over, and she took it that the custom held here.

It had no title. The boards were plain, and it was about the size of a watch log. At the head of the first leaf, where a title would have stood, he had put down what the book was.

*What was there. Entered from what is held. Each figure got twice, or marked.*

She read the line once for content and once for what it was for, and went on to the next leaf.

It was an account of the world she had kept. She had never itemised that for anybody and had never needed a better name for it. There had been named places, each with its rule. There had been offices, held as a party holds them, and work that was taken up and carried and not set down unfinished. None of it was on the hill. She had known that for a long time, as she knew that the ship was gone. What had come across was a slope and a wall and a house with a kettle in it. Somewhere in the first mornings she had stopped looking over the wall for the rest, and had not entered the day she stopped.

He had it all down. Each place had its name at the head of an entry, and under the name where it lay from the next place and how far. Then the rule of the place, in one line. Then who had held office there, and from when, and what had been taken up there, and who had carried it, and the day it was finished. Where he did not have a thing he said so. *Reason not held.* She met that under the fourth rule and again under the ninth. She knew both reasons, and could have given him either on any day he cared to ask. He had not asked. He had marked the place and gone on.

It was correct. She checked it as she read, against what she had, and found nothing to amend. A door that had opened to a word was entered as having opened, with the occasions. A light that had come when it was called was entered as having come. Everything in the book had done something once and was written down as having done it, and the page did none of it. It read as a return reads for a sweep she had flown herself, with each figure right and none of the weather.

She looked up from the leaf, to find the weather where she had always kept it.

She had the names. She had the order of the places, and the rules, and the two reasons. She went past those for the rest, for what one of those rules had been like to stand under, and what came was the entry. It came in his words, since his were the last she had read.

She kept her hand on the leaf. It was a finding, and she put it as one. What she held of that world was what the book held of it. She could check the account and supply two reasons. She could not make any of it do what it said. She had lost nothing this morning that she had possessed last night. She had only not opened it before.

"You'll want this while it's hot," her mother said, and put the cup down beyond the book, well clear of it.

Tissadelle drank some, and turned the leaf.

::: pov tissadelle

Some way in, she came to a leaf that had been smoothed flat.

She took it first for one that had never been written on. But the entry on the facing leaf stopped at the foot in the middle of its business, with the rule given and the offices still to come, and the leaf after took up partway through another place. And it was wrong to the hand. A written leaf in this book carried his pressure, a faint ridging that she had been reading through her fingers without noticing. An unwritten one would have had the tooth of the paper. This had neither. It was level, as a doorstep is level, and nobody had walked on it.

So it had been written, and then put away by the one who wrote it. Nothing was struck through or inked over. He had not hidden that there was something. He had kept back what.

She looked for what belonged there. She had the place before it and the place after, and she went between them as she had gone for the weather. This time nothing came at all, in his words or in hers. Whatever the leaf had held, she did not hold it.

She sat back.

She had once spent three mornings on the wall looking for exactly this: a question whose answer she did not have. Everything she could think to ask him then, she could think to answer, and what came back in his voice was no use to her, because she could have written it. She had given the test up and started a list. The heading of the list was still blank.

Here was the question. *What is under it.*

It would not have been proof. She could not have checked what came. But it would have been an answer she could not have written herself, and on this hill she had not yet had one.

She had the words ready, and she did not put them.

He had smoothed the leaf. That was his answer, given before she reached the question, and given in the book, where he put things he had checked. To ask now would be to tell him she had read his answer and required another. And he would be the one to pay for it, out of the single thing in the account he had put away, so that she could be surer of her company. Her mother had said it more shortly, on a morning long past: he did not owe her a sum.

So the heading would stay blank. She had kept the list under a blank heading for a great many mornings, and it had not stopped her keeping it.

She laid her hand flat on the leaf, since it was a leaf of the book and she was reading the book, and gave it its time. Then she turned it.

::: pov tobble

Tobble was somewhere warm, and from there he could see the table.

He had opened the book before the grey came, and squared it to her chair. She would not have opened it otherwise. She did not open what had not been handed to her, and he had known that about her longer than he had known most things. The book had been in order for some time. He had found that a record in order and a record ready to be read were two states, and that only the first could be checked.

She read it as she read anything. He knew the pace. He could have said which leaf she was on by how long she had been at it, and privately he did, and was right each time she turned one. He did not count that as a result, since he had written the leaves.

There were people in it. She took those leaves at the pace of the others. He noted it, and put no meaning beside it.

Then she came to the first of the smoothed ones, and he held still.

He had smoothed it himself, as he had smoothed each of them, and he knew what was under it. That is not in this record, since it is not in his.

He had an answer ready. He had had it since the day he smoothed the first, because a keeper who puts a thing away ought to be able to say so when asked. He had worked it down to two words. *It's kept.* He did not think well of them. They would shut a door on somebody who had never shut one on him, and they would have had to go into the book afterward, in full, since a refusal is an entry too.

She sat back from the leaf, and he waited.

She did not look up. She put her hand flat on it, and kept it there for as long as a written leaf of that size would have taken her, near enough. He was in a position to know. Then she turned it, and went on at the pace she had come in at.

He stayed as he was. The two words were where he had left them. Nothing had been asked.

He did not enter it. One reading is an opinion, and she had never read anything once.

::::: scene 3

::: pov tissadelle

There were more of them further in. She gave each its time, and did not go looking between the entries again.

She read to the last entry that morning, or over several mornings that she held as one. It was not on the last leaf. After it the leaves were unwritten, with the tooth still on them.

She turned back to the first leaf and squared the book to the chair, as she had found it. A thing handed across a desk goes back the way it came, unless a note comes with it, and there had been no note.

Then the morning's entry. She made it inward, as she made all of them, to whoever kept the count now.

*Morning. Gold on the table. Book found open at the first leaf, and read to the last entry. Some leaves level. Asked: none. Quality of the quiet: as before.*

She considered the last three words. They had come without her deciding on them, and they were accurate. There were prior entries now to hold a quiet against, which was all she had wanted of the list when she began it. She did not know at which entry it had started to serve.

*Thank you,* she said. That was for the book. She did not wait on it.

Her mother took the cup away and brought it back full.

She had read it once, for content. What it was for she would have at the second reading, and she was in no hurry about that. The book would be on the table.
