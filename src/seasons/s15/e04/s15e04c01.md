Tobble had the watch. Nobody had given it to him, and there was nobody he could have handed it to.

It was kept in the stores, below the hill. The hill was the floor above: a slope, a wall, a house with a kettle in it, and a woman at the wall each morning who did not have the number of the mornings. He had the number of the nights, got twice. It is not given here.

The stores were the ship's. He had used them once for a purpose no manual covered, and they had gone on standing afterward in their ranges: the aperture telemetry, the station's discrepancy, a log with three conditions in it each made to stand alone, and every certified reading the *Sufficient Cause* had taken, in order and cross-referenced. The last range ended at 0551 in the middle of a line. No instrument down here had taken a time since. It had gone on being 0551, and when he needed an hour he had it from above.

Past the end of that line was the break.

He had never called it anything else. The hill had been built on a record, and this was where the record stopped, and it had not closed. It was not a wall. It was not a door either, since nobody had made it to be opened. On the floor above there was an edge where the air was warm, and whatever stood at that one asked for nothing. He had that from her entries. Whether her edge and his break were one place seen from two floors, he could not say. Hers was warm. His was not, and it was not warm anywhere near it, which a cat notices.

Things came to it.

They came one at a time, and they did not show. They called. He kept the log of them on the reading shelf at the end of the last range, where he could sit beside an open book and have the break in the corner of his eye without appearing to watch it. Each night had its leaf. The form was the one a desk uses for a call: what came, in what manner, what was asked, what was given. He had left the heading blank. She kept a list upstairs under a blank heading, and had kept it for longer than he would have thought a blank could be kept, and he had found that it served.

Tonight there had been two so far.

The first was a hail. It came in the form the ship's shuttles had used, correctly, with the pause where a shuttle waits for the ship to answer. It said it was standing off and could take one aboard. It asked for a name to put against the seat. He had heard it on other nights. It had never altered what it asked according to what it got, and what it got was nothing. A pilot who was told nothing would have asked a second question by now.

*First. A hail, shuttle's form, standing off. Asked: a name. Given: none.*

The second was smaller, and he liked it less. Something at the break cried as a young animal cries when it has been left. It was pitched for a cat. That was good aim or it was an accident, and he could not tell which. He did not go to it. He listened to how it went on, and it kept time: the same cry at the same interval, on through the hail when the hail came round again, with neither of them giving way to the other. A hurt thing does not keep time. Two creatures after one door would have noticed each other.

*Second. A cry, as of young. Asked: to be fetched. Given: none. Overlapped the first, twice. Neither altered.*

He did not know what they were. The log said what each one did and when, and that was the whole of what he could stand behind.

Nor could he say what kept them at the break. None had come further in than the end of the last range. He had given nothing on every night of the log. He could not support that the one fact was the cause of the other. He had a great many nights that agreed, and upstairs she had her mornings, which agreed in the same way and proved as much. He made no more of his than she made of hers.

At the foot of each leaf he had ruled two lines that no desk's form carried. *Admitted.* *Sent up.*

He would fill them in when the grey came. He knew already what the second would say. It had said the same on every leaf, and it was the only line in the log that he had decided and not observed.

Asteria came down before the grey. She did not come every night, and she sent no word ahead. There was nobody at the foot to take it but Tobble, and he would have entered it.

He was on the reading shelf beside the log, and he did not look round. She had known him a long time and had stopped expecting him to.

*Tobble,* she said.

*Asteria.* It was as much as anybody had from him before he had finished a count.

She sat where she usually sat, on the step of the last range, with the break on her right. She had no work down here, and nobody had asked her to come. The first time, she had come because she thought a watch kept alone for so long ought to be seen by somebody, and she had told him so. He had said the log was the witness. She had said a log could not sit up with a person. Neither had moved the other, and she had gone on coming.

The shuttle was hailing. Under it the small thing cried. She had heard both on other nights, and her account of them was shorter than his. Neither was speaking to anybody. A voice that is meant for you shifts when you shift, and these went on as rain goes on. She had not needed the intervals timed to know it. She did not think the less of him for timing them.

Then there was a third.

"Good morning," it said.

It was not yet morning, and that was the least of it. The voice was the voice of the woman at the wall, in the manner she kept for Tobble and for nobody else: two words set down level and left.

Asteria looked at Tobble. He had not moved. One ear had gone back and stayed there.

*That one is new to me,* she said.

*It has come before. Never on a night you were here.*

"Good morning," it said again. And then, where the woman at the wall would have waited: "You'll have counted the ranges. You'll have got it twice."

So it had his half as well. Asteria found that she was angry, and looked to see at what. The woman at the wall said good morning and then kept still. She left what came after it for him to end. She had gone whole seasons unable to tell whether he had ended it, sooner than say his answer for him. This had her two words, and had filled in the rest itself.

It could be seen from the step, if she turned her head. Tobble had the angle and had not used it, and she understood why. She turned her head all the same. She was not Tobble, and it was not her log.

What stood at the end of the range is not set down here. She knew the face. It was correct in everything she could check.

She was better placed than most to say what that was worth. Her own face was correct too, to a woman she had never met. Karla Wender had once described her teacher to a cadet. The cadet had formed the picture, and the picture was what Asteria wore. She had worn it a long time. In all of it she had not answered to the other woman's surname, or let it be supposed that she had the other woman's years. A face could be come by honestly. It could not make the claim for you.

She got up and went along the range until she stood in front of it, and was plainly not a cat.

"Good morning," it said, to her. "You'll have counted the ranges."

It had brought its listener with it, and it was speaking to him through her.

That was enough, for Asteria. There were more people in that head than the form had boxes for, and in all the years Asteria had been one of them she had never been taken there for another. Tobble would want the interval and the overlap. She had what she needed. She came back and sat down.

*She ought to be told,* she said.

*No.*

*It has her voice, Tobble. It is at the foot of her own house, saying her words. I think that is hers to know. I have thought so since the first night I came down.*

*I know. It's entered.* He turned a leaf back, and forward again. *What would she do with it?*

*That is hers as well.*

*She would take it up. She can't check it. Whatever I send from here arrives in my voice, and she has established that she can't tell my voice from what she holds of it. It would be a report she could neither confirm nor set aside, of a thing she can do nothing about.* He stopped, and went on more shortly. *She has never put down anything unfinished. This doesn't finish. I watched her carry more than there was of her, once.*

It was the longest speech she had had from him in some while, and it was an argument. She gave it its due. She did not agree with it.

*You are deciding what she carries,* she said.

*I'm deciding what I send. The log is here, and it's in order. The day she comes down for it, it is hers, every leaf.* The ear came forward again. *Those two aren't the same. I grant you they're close.*

They sat with that. The thing at the end of the range said good morning to the place where she had been standing.

She had come down more than once meaning to go back up and say it at the wall herself. She could have. She needed nobody's leave for it, his least of all, and he would not have tried to stop her. She found now that she was not going to, and the reason was not his.

It was the telling. Her face had come into this mind by one. Nobody had ever brought Asteria Wessex up the hill. A woman had been described, well, by somebody who had learned from her, and that had been enough to give a member what to look like. Asteria did not know the whole of how she had come, and nobody had asked her. She knew that part. In this mind a face described well enough had found somewhere to stand.

She would describe this one well. She would have to, to be believed: the voice, the two words, what she had just looked at. She would be carrying it up the hill herself, by the one road she knew for certain a face had travelled.

She told him so.

*I can't support that,* Tobble said. *You have one case.*

*I have one case, and it is me. I don't say I'm right. I say I won't be the one who finds out.*

He entered that too. She watched him do it and did not ask for the wording.

*I still think she is owed it.*

*Entered.*

*You needn't enter everything I think.*

*You came down,* Tobble said. *It's a watch. What's said on it goes in.*

She took that to mean that she had been put on the watch, and that he did not intend to say so.

It was coming up to the grey. There was a thing she said at that hour. It had come to her with the hill, and it was older than the wall. What she said is hers, and it is not entered. Tobble kept still while she said it.

Then she went up, ahead of the morning. She did not want to be at the foot of the house when the real one came down.

::: pov tobble

The grey came. He had it from above, as he had every hour.

*Good morning,* she said.

It came down from the floor above in two words, level, and nothing came after it. She was at the wall. She had said it there every morning, and after it she kept still.

"Good morning," said the thing at the end of the range. "You'll have counted the ranges. You'll have got it twice. It's all right. You can stop now."

He had both in the one moment, and he did not need the angle. One had said its piece and was waiting on him. It had waited like that through mornings when it could not tell whether anything answered. The other had answered itself and gone on.

He had counted the ranges, as it happened, and had got it twice. He did not see that it was any business of the break's.

In this house a greeting was what you gave a threshold. He had given one to the hill when the hill was a few minutes old. She had given him one every morning since, across a floor. He had the one to give, and he knew where it was owed. He had nothing to show that an answer at the break would have let anything in. He was not going to establish it with her upstairs.

*Good morning,* Tobble said, to the floor above.

Whether she had it he could not tell from here. She could not tell from there. That had not altered, and he had stopped entering it as a fault.

Then her entry came down, as it did each morning, made inward to whoever kept the count now. He kept the count.

*Morning. Grey, then gold on the wall. The flock down off the high field; autumn, I think. Name said. Quiet after it.*

There was a line more. It was her finding on the quiet, and it is hers.

He filed it under the one before. He had all of them, in order, and he had seen for some time what she was making of them: a level, to hold a morning against. A level wants a floor under it that does not shift. He did not enter that the quiet was his work. He could not support it. He could support what he sent.

He turned back to the night's leaf.

*Third. A greeting, in her voice and manner. Showed, at the end of the range. Not looked at by the watch. Looked at by A. Supplied the watch's answers. Asked: to be answered. Given: none.*

*Admitted: none.*

*Sent up: none.*

Under those, since it had been said on the watch: *A. holds that she is owed it. A. will not carry it up, on a ground of her own. One case. Stands.*

He read the leaf through twice. Then he ruled the next.

The hail had left off at some point, in the night or between two nights. The cry had not. "Good morning," said the thing at the end of the range, to the step, where nobody sat.

He would go up presently. There was a place on the floor above that was warm, and from it he could see her without appearing to, and she had never been able to establish whether he was there. He would be down again before the dark. The leaf was ruled.
