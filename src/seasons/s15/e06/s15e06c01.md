The binder copies came down to the reconciling desk in the forenoon, and the line's notice was on top of them.

Sen had been in the signals room at four, for as long as it took to set the returned account against the binder copy and say that the two agreed. The High Captain had thanked the desk and had not asked it to stay. This was the desk's ordinary work at its ordinary hour. A station had filed something, the station's instruments had logged something, and the desk read each against the other.

The notice first, since it had already gone. It was on the line's form, and it was short.

*Threshold Station, Dock Seven. Final phrasing received 0400, arrays' log, confirmed. Founding profile logged at the failure point: jurisdiction Telearch, standing referred, classification not casualty. Line Captain Shepherd, T.: at her own order, file closed complete. Cause of closure: founding. Standing order of the holding officer met at its third clause. No further entry.*

Cael went first. *Final phrasing, 0400. Verified. The arrays' log has it entire, with the time. Confirmed: that is us, and it is accurate as far as it goes. We confirmed that the account in the phrasing is the account in the binder, mark for mark. We did not confirm the arrays.*

*The notice says the arrays' log,* Sen said. *It does not say the arrays are sound.*

*No. It is careful.* Cael seldom found a careful document restful, since care was also what a tidy one looked like from outside. *Founding profile. I have had the boundary manual down. The plate is in the back pages, and the log agrees with it at every point the plate gives. I can go that far. Nobody on this station has seen one, so nobody on this station can say the plate is right.*

*Then say that.*

*I have. The closing sentence, in the holding officer's hand, timed. Verified. The third clause of the standing order is that the file is not closed from this side. Verified, and met. It was closed on an order that came from the other.*

That left the sheet. The desk had cited line nineteen once before, in a deep winter, as the place where a claim's looking was kept, and Sen turned to it without being prompted.

The entry before the phrasing was the Analyst's, at 2200: warm, within the band, and the four phrases the arrays' column had carried at every change of shift. The entry after it was Toval's, at 0600. The desk column was in his level hand, and it had changed.

*Failure point: not resolved at the logged position. Figure: ground. Band: none applies.*

*That is the certified column,* Cael said, slowly. *Those are the instruments everything on this station is allowed to rest on. They never carried the signal. They filed it as noise for the whole life of the file. Two hours after the arrays log an ending, the certified instruments cannot find the place.*

*So the closing sentence has a leg that is not the arrays.*

*It has one. It is in the hand of the reader on that desk who once found no use in the line and wrote that down.*

That was a note. Sen wrote it at the foot of the binder copy as Cael gave it.

*File closed complete, in the holding officer's hand. The closing sentence rests on three records. The final phrasing: arrays' log, 0400, carried under the assumption stated at the head of the arrays' column; the account in it compared with the binder copy and found the same. The founding profile: arrays' log, the same hour; agrees with the manual's plate at every point the plate gives; no earlier instance is held on this station to set it against. Sheet line nineteen, desk column, 0600: certified instruments no longer resolve a failure point at the logged position. The third does not rest on the arrays. Between the file and the instruments the desk finds no discrepancy.*

*We have not written that last sentence on this file before,* Cael said.

*We have not written it often on any,* said Sen.

*And the cost,* Wyn said.

*Say what is on the file.*

*The line is on the file. Two readers, turn about, at every change of shift, from the morning after the account went until six today. I have not counted the entries, and I will not ask Cael for the figure.*

Cael had the figure, and did not give it.

*The draft citation is on the file as well,* Wyn went on. *It is in the binder where it was at the last review, and nobody has signed it. A file closed complete, with that in it.*

*The closing does not sign it,* Cael said. *It does not withdraw it either. The Council's draft is the Council's.*

*Then mark it.*

Sen wrote it under the note. *The binder holds the Council's draft citation, unsigned. The closing of the file neither signs it nor withdraws it. The citation, the account and the closing sentence do not say the same thing. The desk has read each against the instruments and has no note on the three together.*

*And one thing that is on no file,* Wyn said. *She kept that watch herself, every night of it. We offered.*

*We did.*

*I am not asking to put it anywhere. I know what it cost and who paid, and for once the desk cannot write that down, because she arranged that it could not.* Wyn was quiet for a moment. *I think it was the right arrangement. I would not have said so at the time.*

Then the hand reached the line at the foot, where the form asked for the archivist's name.

Sen waited. Wyn had wanted a name on that line on every working day for longer than any of the three would have cared to total, and the wanting had its hour. The hour passed.

*You have not asked,* Sen said.

*No. I ask so that what the desk finds has to be answered. This time it found nothing.* Wyn considered that. *And there is nobody left on this file to do the answering. She saw to that too. I will want it tomorrow, on something else.*

Sen filed the note as it stood, both halves, with no name. It was the last the desk would add to that binder. In the index, under the file's status, the desk had written *open* at every pass. The index allowed a short list of words there, and *complete* had been on the list the whole time.

Sen wrote it, and the date. The three of them went home by the one door.

On the day after, the Dock Seven line was still on the sheet.

Syra came in at ten to two and read the sheet before she spoke to him. Every line was taken and entered, and she reached nineteen in its order.

It had four entries since the one she had written at 2200 on the night of the closure. Each was in two columns, and each read as the one above it read. In the desk column: *Failure point: not resolved at the logged position. Figure: ground. Band: none applies.* In the arrays' column: *No variation. No invariant. Nothing addressed. None detected.*

The first of the four was Toval's, from six yesterday. Beside it was a mark of the kind the desk used for a thing to be raised aloud. He had made it while she was handing him the desk, and had said that it would keep a day. She had agreed that it would.

"You may raise it," she said.

He closed the sheet first.

"Two things. The first is the audit. *Use: none shown.* I wrote that, and I have written it at every audit since, because it was true. I want to know whether it still is."

"State what you found."

"The file was closed a few minutes after four, by the High Captain. I took the line at six. Nothing waited for my entry. By the test you gave me, if the line had been struck last winter the file would have closed at the same minute." He stopped. "So, none shown. But the reconciling desk's note came up with the binders this morning. It rests the closing on three things, and one of them is my entry. It says mine is the one that does not rest on the arrays."

"It is."

"A note is not a decision."

"No. A note is what a later reader has in place of having been in the room." Syra considered what she could support, and gave it in order. "A final phrasing came through arrays that nobody can certify. It was read by the officer it was addressed to, who had asked for it, and confirmed by an archivist who has kept that binder since it was opened. I do not say that either of them leaned. I say that a reader who doubted them would be entitled to. Your entry was made on certified instruments, at the hour the sheet required, by a reader who had recommended in writing that the line be struck. It says that the place is no longer there to read. A doubting reader can check that."

"That is what you told me it was for. In the winter, after I asked you what you wanted."

"I told you it was for that if anything came. Something came."

"Then it has been shown."

"By which test?"

He took the time he needed. "Not by mine. By yours. Once."

"Then at the audit you will write what you find to be true in the deep winter. It may be that."

He did not write it down. The audit was not open, and he had learned not to draft a finding into a book that had not asked for one. She noted it.

"The second thing," he said. "You told me the line was the place on this station where an answer would first be written down."

"I did."

"It wasn't. It came at four. You were on this desk at four."

"I was."

"You didn't take the line."

"It was not the change of shift. I knew within the hour what had come. I took nothing from line nineteen until you took it at six, because the sheet takes it at six."

"Did you want to?"

"Yes. That is the reason there is a rule about when."

He accepted that. He looked at the place by the wall where the Field Analyst would stand, and back at her.

"So you were wrong about *first*."

"I was wrong about *first*," Syra said. "I gave it to you as grounds, and it was the best statement I had. I did not allow that an answer might be addressed. It was written down first by the officer it was sent to, in a room I have never been in. The line had it second, from instruments that had not carried a word of it. I would not have designed that. I think it is sounder than what I designed."

"And the grounds?"

There had been three, and she went through them as she would have gone through the tolerances of a gauge. A process was running at the failure point: ended, by the desk column, four entries together. A person might be in it: she had never been able to speak to that, and could not now, since the notice gave an order, a classification and a cause, and none of the three was a position. She had been asked something: discharged.

"The grounds have ended," she said. "All three."

"Then by the desk's rule it is struck. A line that cannot say what it is for."

"By the desk's rule it would be. It has not been the desk's line since the High Captain wrote it into standing orders. *The reading is taken each shift, and the arrays stay on.* That order was met yesterday at its third clause. It was not withdrawn. I read it again this morning, to be certain that nothing in it ends the first."

"So we take it."

"We take it. Anyone who wants it struck may go up and say so to her. You are free to. You found against the line before anybody else thought to test it."

Toval thought about that while he collected his bag. He did not say which he would do, and she did not ask him.

"I'll take it at ten, then," he said. It was his turn. He went.

Rook-7 came in at the change and stood at the end of the desk with one hand flat on the wall. Syra began at line one. She reached Dock Seven at nine minutes past two.

The desk column first. The instrument swept the logged position and returned the ground figure for that reach of the approach, as it returned it for the open water on either side. She wrote the three short entries. The place had not cooled to this. A failure that ends on its schedule leaves weeks of figures going down, and a reader can lay a rule along them. Between her own entry at 2200 and Toval's at 0600 there were no figures at all. One entry was warm, and the next had no place to read.

"Arrays' column," said Rook-7. "No variation. No invariant. Nothing addressed. None detected."

She wrote the four phrases under the four above.

Two of them were new that week and two were not. *Nothing addressed* and *none detected* had stood on this line at every change of shift she had taken, over a signal that phrased and returned. They stood today over nothing, and they were exactly as accurate as before. For all that time most of the station had read those two as the news.

"Rook-7 has a question about the column," the Field Analyst said. "It is not a complaint. The arrays stay on, by order. The column may read as it reads today at every change, for as long as the order stands. Does the Analyst wish it given in full each time?"

"In full. An unchanged entry is still an entry. You told this desk so."

"Rook-7 did. It notes that it is being quoted, and has no objection." It took its hand from the wall, and went.

The preference was where she had left it. She looked for it, as at each of her turns. It pointed as it had always pointed, toward an answer from the failure point. There had been an answer, and there was no longer a failure point, and the lean had taken notice of neither. The book had no word that she knew of for a drift whose reference had gone out of range. The drift log would put that to her at the quarter, and it was not the quarter.

She finished the sheet, and took up the first of the afternoon's gauges.

The year's rosters were due at the turn of the season. Kane was at them, longhand, when the clerk put a relay form on the corner of the desk.

He knew the heading before he had read it through. It gave a post by its number, on a line he had never served. *Lindahl, E., Chief Ranger, general operations.* He had one sheet from that officer in his files, from another autumn, and had answered it in three words.

He looked for a crossing-out. A relay form has none. He still held that against them.

The Chief had written four lines. The post's log held one notice sent on to this office, and its acknowledgement. He did not know whether the office had since been put on his line's distribution. The notice was below as it had reached the post, with nothing changed. The post had not written after her.

Kane read the notice through. Then he read the Chief's four lines again, and then he took down his own book and found the other one. He had entered it under its date, in the notice's words, and had given it no number. *Eleven taken off in forty-one minutes, by shuttle and by courier under charter. All eleven fit. Line Captain Shepherd, T.: position logged at the failure point at the moment of closure. No further entry.*

He put the two side by side on the desk, as he would have put two statements of the same morning.

The first had given eleven people a condition and the twelfth a place. He had thought at the time that it was exact, and that he could not have written it. The second did not give her a condition either. It gave her an order, and said whose. It gave her a classification, and the classification said only what she was not. And it gave a cause.

*Cause of closure: founding.*

He could not read what a boundary station meant by the word. He held no certificate for that work, and *jurisdiction Telearch* was on no roster he had ever set. He knew what his own corps meant by it. His corps kept its cases for courts that were not yet founded, and the first book such a court opened was its founding docket. He had sealed cases into three of those. In his trade the word named a morning. On that morning there was an authority that had not been there at breakfast, and at the back of the hall a Ranger who had stopped being one at the end of somebody else's sentence.

He did not suppose the two corps meant the same thing. He noted that they had reached for the same word.

The sheet was still in the book, where he had left it. *Shepherd,* and the comma, and the rest of the leaf clean. He had stopped there because the next thing a letter wants is somewhere to go. He had not torn it out. At every turn of the season since, he had come past it on his way to the rosters.

She had asked him one question, by pouch, in her first winter as a Starwarden. *When is leaving the correct order?* He had written back that he was the wrong man. His corps left by a clause and not by an order, and a clause he trusted was made of three things. It was set down beforehand. It was set down by somebody other than the one who would have to go. And when it happened, the whole hall could see that it had. He had told her to ask again when she had a case in front of her. *I answer at the ends.*

She had not asked again. He had a case in front of him all the same, and the notice said it was at its end.

He tried the three against it. *At her own order.* That was the one kind of leaving he had never trusted, and he had a file of his own in the library to distrust it by. Then he came to the last clause but one. *Standing order of the holding officer met at its third clause.*

He sent for it. A station's standing orders went to any officer who asked, in clear, as a closed file did. *Wender, K., High Captain. Threshold Station, standing order on Dock Seven. As it stands.*

It took two days. He did rosters.

When it came he began at the end.

*The sheet comes to the High Captain. It is not delegated.*

Above that were the grounds: an account sent, a question asked in it, no answer detected. He did not have the account, and he did not send for it. It had been addressed to her.

Above the grounds were three clauses, and he read them upward. *The file is not closed from this side. Nothing further is sent from this side. The reading is taken each shift, and the arrays stay on.*

There was a space at the foot for a review date. It was empty.

He read the sheet for some while. It was in a High Captain's hand, over her name, and it was dated in a deep winter. He counted the autumns between that date and the notice's, and wrote the figure in his book. It is not given here.

He had written to Shepherd once that her corps would not have given her a clause. One officer of it had. She had set down beforehand, in three lines, what would not be done from her own side of the thing, and so who alone could end it. She was not the one who would have to go. And she had sent the ending down a line in clear, to every post and hull on it, and one of those had passed it to a desk the line had no reason to tell. That was as much of a hall as a line had.

It had also been an order, and nobody's to give but Shepherd's own. The notice said so in four words. He had always taken those for two kinds of leaving. He had fifteen and more of the one in his book, and a single file of the other in the library, and it had not occurred to him that a leaving could be both. He was not certain his corps would have allowed it. He doubted that hers had. Two officers had arranged it between them, with a great deal of time between the one's part and the other's.

And the High Captain had stood where she could be seen. *It is not delegated.* With the first notice he had gone through everyone it counted and had found nobody standing there.

He took up the pen. The sheet had her name on it, and a comma.

*Shepherd,*

*It had the three. I looked.*

*Kane*

He read it through. Nothing in it was crossed out.

It still wanted somewhere to go. He had a cause now, and a jurisdiction, and neither was an address. He did not tear the sheet out. He left it in the book between the first notice and the second, which he entered under its date in the notice's own words. He gave that no number either. The numbers were for halls he had sat at the back of.

To the Chief he wrote *received as sent*, and his name. They were the words he had used before. He could have signed to more this time, and it was not the Chief he owed it to.

Then he drew the rosters back across the desk. The long passage was still to do.
