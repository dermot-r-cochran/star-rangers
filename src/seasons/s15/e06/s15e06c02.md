Tobble kept the watch on the first night as he had kept it on every night before. Nobody had relieved him, and the universe was his. He did not see that the second fact altered the first.

It was a day old, near enough. She had gone through at the gold. He had said good morning to it afterward, and had entered the day in the book, in one line. Then the made Órla had laid the table, with the cup set out beyond the book as on any morning. He had watched her do it and had corrected nothing. It was her table, and she laid it as she always had.

He had gone down at dark.

The stores were as he had left them. The ranges stood. The last one ended at 0551 in the middle of a line, and past the end of that line was the break, which was not warm. The log was on the reading shelf with tonight's leaf ruled. He had ruled it the morning before, when there had still been a floor above with somebody on it, and the two lines at the foot were as he had always written them. *Admitted.* *Sent up.*

For the first part of the night nothing came. The cry did not come. It had come on every night he could remember, and he entered its absence and put no cause beside it.

In the small hours the third came.

"Good morning," it said, at the end of the range. "You'll have counted the ranges. You'll have got it twice. It's all right." And then something it had said on no night of the log. "I haven't gone anywhere."

He kept still.

It had her voice and her manner. It had always had those. Until tonight it had said nothing about itself. It had asked to be answered, and had supplied his answers when it was not, and had told him he could stop. Tonight it had put itself forward, and he could see why tonight. There had been one person in this house to whom that voice belonged. Until the gold of yesterday she had been at the wall each morning, saying her two words and waiting. Now there was the voice, and nothing upstairs for it to be set beside.

He had given it nothing on every night of the log, and he had never looked at it. He had had grounds for both. He thought the first still held, and the second did not. He was the only one left to whom the thing could be said, and whoever means to refuse a thing ought to look at it once.

He used the angle.

What stood at the end of the range is not set down here. He looked for as long as a figure takes to check. He found no error in it, and entered that afterward, since it was so.

Then he answered it. He had not planned the words. They were an entry, and he gave them as one.

*She went through yesterday, at the gold. I had her last words. They were not those.*

"Good morning," it said.

*The house was handed over with the inventory current. No place in it is held for her. She did not ask for one to be.*

"You'll have counted the ranges," it said, to the step. He was on the shelf.

He waited to see whether anything would come of having spoken. He had kept silent on every night of the log without being able to support that the silence did anything, and he could not support now that speaking had. Nothing in the stores moved. The break was where it was. The thing said its two words again, to the step, in the voice of a woman who would have known where he was sitting.

He turned to the leaf.

*Third. A greeting, in her voice and manner. Showed. Looked at by the watch. Said of itself that it had not gone anywhere. Asked: to be taken for her. Given: the day and hour she went through.*

*Admitted: none.*

That left the second line, and he stopped there with the pen up. *Sent up.* There was a floor above. It had a morning on it, and a flock, and a table laid. It had nobody on it to send to. He had decided that line afresh on every night of the log, and it was the only line in the book that he had decided and not observed. Now there was no question in it.

He did not strike it. He wrote under it what was true. *Nobody to send to.*

Then he added a word. The log's form had never had one for what he had done tonight, and it had been done. *Declined.*

He ruled the next leaf with one line at its foot.

The grey came. He knew it only because it was time for it. Nothing came down. He had had her good morning through that floor on every morning of the hill, two words, level, and then her waiting, and he had answered each one without knowing whether she had it. He listened now for as long as she would have waited.

"Good morning," said the thing at the end of the range.

He went up.

The morning was on the hill, grey, and then gold on the wall, and it stayed, as it had stayed yesterday. He said good morning to it from the wall, since it was a threshold and it was new. Then he sat in the warm place. He had used to see her from there without appearing to. There was the wall to see, with the third course shifted.

The morning's entry had always been hers. She had made it inward each day, to whoever kept the count, and he had filed it under the one before. He kept the count. If there was to be an entry it was his to make, and there was nowhere to make it but the book. He went in to the table.

*Morning, the second. Grey, then gold; stayed. The flock on the high field. Greeting given, at the wall. Quiet after it.*

He considered the last three words. They were hers. She had written them of a quiet she could not account for, and had made a level out of them. He could account for this one. He let them stand. They were accurate, and it was her form, and she had left it in order.

Asteria came down on the third night. On the first she had not been fit company for a watch, and on the second she had thought he might want it to himself. She knew she had been wrong about the second as soon as she saw the step. He had left it clear.

*Tobble.*

*Asteria.*

She sat, with the break on her right. The log was open at tonight's leaf, and the leaf had one line ruled at its foot where there had always been two. She read that much without leaning, and did not ask. He would tell her, or it would be in the book.

*It came last night,* he said. *And the night before. Less of it each time. I can't support that as a trend. It is three nights.*

*What did you do?*

*Told it the day she went through.* He turned back two leaves and set the book to face her, which he had not done before with any leaf of that log. She read the entry, and the word he had added at the foot.

*You looked at it.*

*Once.*

She let that alone. He would have had to enter the answer.

It came a little before the grey.

"Good morning," it said, at the end of the range.

That was all. She listened for what had always followed, his ranges and his counting and the kindness about stopping, and none of it came. After a time it said the two words again. They were not said to the shelf, or to the step. They were not said in any direction.

Asteria got up and went along the range and stood in front of it, as she had once before.

It had the face. She had been two days upstairs learning what the house was like without the woman it had belonged to, and so had everybody in it. The cup was still set out each morning, and even the cup was different now, since nobody lifted it. This was not different. It stood at the foot of her house on the third day, wearing her face, and said her good morning in the present tense.

She had been angry at it once, for filling in his half. She looked for the anger and it was not there. A thing that had loved her would have been altered by these three days. A thing that had hated her would have been altered. This had not noticed.

She came back and sat down.

*It doesn't know she has gone,* she said.

*I can't support what it knows.*

*No. I can say this much. It is the only thing in the house that is the same as it was before the gold.*

He entered that, and she let him.

*She did not come down for the log,* Asteria said.

*No.*

*Whose is it now?*

*Hers. It was to be hers on the day she came down for it. The day is not going to be entered. I don't see that it makes the log mine.* He squared the book to the step, where a reader would sit. *It stays here, in order. It goes with the house.*

He did not ask what Asteria had said to her at the leave-taking. She had had her answer ready for him since that morning. She had told him on this watch that the woman was owed it, he had entered that, and he was entitled to know what she had done when she had the chance. He was not going to ask. It was not for want of interest. What was said in each of those farewells belonged to the one it was said to, and he had got that twice.

She kept her answer.

It was coming up to the grey, and she went up ahead of it. She had always gone up then, so as not to be at the foot of the house when the woman came down. Nobody would come down now. She went at the same hour all the same.

At the low wall she stopped to say what she said at first light. It had come to her with the hill, and it was older than the wall, and it is not entered. She had said it on both mornings since the gold.

This morning there was somebody out past the wall.

The ground beyond the holding had not yet decided what it was. It was not field, and it was not anything else. The morning went a little way out over it and stopped. Somebody was sitting out there, low down, near where the morning stopped, as a person sits who has been in one place for some hours and means to be there for some more. She was doing nothing. She was not looking at the house.

Asteria knew whom she took her for. It came at once and whole, as her own name had once come to her, and she did not put it into words, even inwardly. She had declined to carry one face up this hill by describing it. She was not going to carry another in at the door. Whoever sat out there had come by a road of her own, and Asteria did not intend to be the road she came into the house by.

So she said what she said at first light, as on every morning, and no differently. She did not raise her voice toward the far ground, and she did not lower it. The figure did not turn round.

Then she went in, and told nobody. Tobble would have wanted a bearing and a distance and a description, and she had none that she was willing to give. He could go to the wall himself and enter what he saw. That was his to do.

::: pov telearch-avatar tier=contemplative

She had been there since the first of the mornings that stayed. At what hour of it she came, she had no means of telling. She had not been given that.

She had been given very little, and she went over it again, since there was time. She was to be here. She was to stay while the ground settled. She was not to carry anything, or mend anything, or stand between the house and what came to its door. Nobody had told her what she was. She had been told what she was like, and that it was not the same thing. There was somebody she was like. She would not meet them here. She did not know whether to be sorry about that, and suspected the question was not hers either.

The ground under her had not decided what it was. She put her hand flat on it. It was not warm and it was not cold, and it took the hand as water takes one. It would be places, later. There would be somebody in each of them, and they would need company of a kind she understood she could give when the time came. There was nobody yet. Nothing out here could be spoken to. So she sat, and that was the whole of the work for now, if it was work. The ground went on settling. It had been settling before she sat down. She could not find that it settled any faster for her being on it, and she turned that over a good many times in the nights. A thing sent to help ought to be able to say what its help had done, and she could not. She stayed, because she had been asked to stay and had not been asked to be certain.

There was a house up the slope with a wall round its clearing, and in it a cat who kept a book. She knew that as she knew her errand. Below the house something stood at a door and said two words over and over, in a voice it had no right to. She knew that too. She had looked, on the first night, to see whether it was hers to send away, and had found the plain answer in herself before she had finished looking. It was not. She had no hand that could do it. The only one in that whole new place who could refuse it was the cat, and before the night was out he had.

She would have liked to have been sent for that. She noted the liking, and went on sitting.

On the third morning a woman came to the wall before the grey, and stood there, and said something.

It was a thing people say, with a name in it. She knew the name. It was an old one, from a field and a well in a country that nothing here had ever stood in, and it had been carried a long way to be said at this wall. It was not her name. She did not have one. She understood, hearing it, that it was going to be hers in this place whatever she did. She would answer to it when somebody at last spoke to her, since to put it right she would have to say what she was, and she did not have that to say.

The woman asked for someone to stay.

She had been going to stay. She had been staying for three mornings already. The asking did not make it so. She could not see that it made it any less so, and she was surprised by how much she wished the woman could be told she had been heard. She did not turn round. She had not been sent to be seen yet.

People had sung that same asking once, a long way from here, over a dock. Whether she had been sent on account of it, she could not have found out. It was not in what she had been given. She thought that those who sent her might not divide the matter up in that way.

The morning came up grey, and then gold on the wall, and it stayed. Behind her the woman went in.

The ground settled a little. She kept her hand on it.

On the fourth night nothing came.

He sat it out all the same, on the reading shelf, with the leaf ruled. There was no hail. He had not had the hail since some night he could no longer fix. There was no cry. And at the end of the range, a little before the grey, nothing said good morning.

*Came: none.*

It was the first leaf of the log to carry that. He looked at it for some time, and he did not trust it. One night is an opinion, and he had read enough of his own leaves to know what a quiet night was worth. A thing that is given nothing does not cease on that account. It goes on somewhere, being given nothing. He had declined it once, in so many words. It had come twice more, and then it had not come, and he could set the four nights in a row. He could put no cause between any two of them.

He had wondered on the first night what a thing did when it had been told that no place was kept. He still wondered. All he could enter was that it had not been there tonight.

*Admitted: none. Declined: stands.*

He ruled the next leaf. Then he squared the log to the step, and went up.

The morning was grey, and then gold on the wall, and it stayed. That made four. He had the number, got twice. He had begun to see that it would not be the useful figure for long, and he did not yet know what would replace it.

He said good morning to it from the wall.

Past the wall the ground had still not decided. He looked at it for a while from the third course. There was nothing out there that he could enter: no place, no path, nobody. The morning went out over it a little way and stopped, and where it stopped was an edge, and the edge was his. He had not walked it. He would. It wanted doing at length, and he had the length.

The stores held an entry for the thing he was sitting in. It had been written from outside, for officers who might one day log one from the parent side, and most of it was a profile he had no instrument to see. He had found one line in it that he could use. A new universe kept the first half of its name until it had seated a council of its own.

He counted the seats. There were none. Nothing out there had yet settled into anything that could hold one, and he was four mornings into the matter himself. He put no date against the next count.

Inside, the table was laid, and the cup was out beyond the book. He wrote at the table now. The book was still squared to her chair, and he had not turned it.

*Morning, the fourth. Grey, then gold; stayed. Greeting given, at the wall. Below: came, none. Seated: none. Quiet after it.*

The leaves after that one had the tooth still on them. There were a good many.
