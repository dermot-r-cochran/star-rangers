::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there. It is about a telling that had been kept for somebody, and about the somebody, who had turned up and then slept through nearly everything.

The newest one was awake for longer now.

Carried-It-Sleeping knew without looking. A warm place has a shape, and a sleeping piece of it does not feel the same as a waking one, and the piece in the middle of the middle had begun to be the waking kind for a good while at a time. It looked at the amber on the near wall. Its hands opened and shut on nothing. That was all it did. It did it for longer than it used to.

Somebody else had noticed.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It came across the warm with a stone moved first. It was three places. Carried-It-Sleeping had given up saying that it was not needed. With Keeps-A-Hand-On-It a stone meant a proper asking, and it was better to know.

"The bright one," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It.

Carried-It-Sleeping waited to be told.

"We put it together. A piece at a time. We kept it for the ones not born yet, with nothing taken out and nothing put in." Keeps-A-Hand-On-It looked at the middle of the middle. "That one got born. It has had the first thing. It has had the waiting, from me. It has not had the bright one."

"No."

"Why not?"

Carried-It-Sleeping thought about it properly, because it was a proper question, and found that there was no reason at all. Nobody had decided anything. The telling had been put together so that it could be carried. Then everyone had gone on lying where they lay. The one it was for had arrived, and been looked at, and counted, and told the first thing, and not one of the people had got up.

"Nobody has asked," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

"I am asking."

"You said once that it would be you."

"I said so when I was the smallest." Keeps-A-Hand-On-It said it plainly. It had stopped being a sore place some time ago. "I have every piece from the one who was there for it. I have not lost any. I go through them."

"Then it is yours to tell. What did you come across the warm for?"

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It sat down.

"It is long. It is longer than the waiting. It has everybody in it. And a telling is not told to a sleeping one."

"It is awake for longer now."

"For as long as that?"

Carried-It-Sleeping did not know.

"What if it goes to sleep in the middle?" said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It. "A telling that is stopped has a hole in it. And you do not tell a telling twice to the same one. So if it sleeps halfway, has it been told? Or half told? Can you go back to the beginning?"

That was three questions, and Carried-It-Sleeping did not have an answer to one of them.

"Who was there?" said Carried-It-Sleeping, because that is where you start. "Has anyone told a small one a telling and had it go to sleep partway?"

They both thought. Nobody had ever told of it. If it had happened, it had happened to people who did not think it worth a telling.

"Then it is not settled," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It.

"No."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It thought for as long as it takes to say a name. Keeps-A-Hand-On-It is good at that length.

"I will wait until it has only just woken. That is when there is the most awake left in it."

Carried-It-Sleeping had been keeping small ones since before this one was born, and had not thought of that.

::::: scene 2

::: pov keeps-a-hand-on-it

So Keeps-A-Hand-On-It waited again.

It was easier the second time. You lie beside a sleeping one, close, because the people do not leave a sleeping one alone, and you do not try to make it shorter. Keeps-A-Hand-On-It went through the pieces instead, in order, the way you feel along the wall of a passage. They were all there.

It was the time after the working. The loud had gone up the long passage toward the cold. A stone had moved at the bottom of the shelving, good and plain, and Stone-First-Who-Waited had come down and was standing at the edge of the amber, not yet lying. The whole of the people were in the warm. An old one had just told the long dark to its end, not very well, and the warm had made the small sound for it anyway.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had not planned that part. But everyone had been there for the bright one. It seemed right that everyone should be there for this.

One of the small hands opened, and did not shut.

The newest one was looking at the near wall.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It stood up.

It went out across the warm the way it had gone the last time, faster than any telling goes. A small one was standing up in the middle.

"This is the light that came out of the near wall," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It, bent toward the newest one. Then straightened, because a telling is told standing up, and told it whole.

The time after the working, and the whole of the people in the warm, and Went-Round telling the two stones. The near wall going thin, like a telling going quiet near its end. The thin part having a shape. The something standing in the open floor, in nobody's place, the size of a person, amber down one side and blue-green down the other, brighter than any wall. How big it was from underneath. The gap in the warm, the size of it, and not cold. The ring the people left round it, without being told to. From the line, a place in the thick of the warm with nothing in it. A stone moved at the bottom of the shelving, a good one, and not one of us turning. Went-Round going all the way round, and the same from behind as from in front. The side toward the near wall the same as the side toward the old ones. How long it stood, which was about half of the two stones. The light going back into the wall, and the wall brighter by just that much. And the wall afterwards, the way it had always been.

Somewhere in it Keeps-A-Hand-On-It wanted to look down, and did not. Keeps-A-Hand-On-It looked at the near wall instead. It was a help. It is easier to tell a wall going thin when you are looking at it not doing so.

And nobody put anything in.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It noticed that at about the ring. The last time, standing in the amber with this, there had been a piece coming from every side before the first one was out. *The wall went dim first. You have left out the ring. You were asleep when it came.* Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had the whole body ready for it.

It did not come. The warm lay behind and all round, every one of them who had been there, as quiet as it had ever been for a hand on a stone.

So Keeps-A-Hand-On-It went on to where the pieces stopped.

There was one more thing. Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had it, and had had it for longer than any of the pieces.

*It was the same as the shape at the seep, with light in it.*

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had said that at the time. The warm had kept it, as Keeps-A-Hand-On-It's. But it was not a piece. A piece is what you were there for. This was what Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had thought about it, and that is a different thing, however sure you are. Nobody had a piece that said what it was. If the newest one wanted to think something about it, the newest one could, later, when it had the words. There ought to be room left.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It left it out, and stopped, because that was the end.

::::: scene 3

::: pov teddy

Carried-It-Sleeping heard the wall go thin.

*Like a telling going quiet near its end.* Those were Carried-It-Sleeping's own words. Carried-It-Sleeping had said them standing up, to Stone-First, on the time the telling was put together, and once more for the warm. And had checked them, lying down, the one time the smallest tried the whole of it under the breath. Every word. You cannot help it, with a piece that is yours.

Carried-It-Sleeping began to check them now, from habit, and stopped.

They were not coming this way. They were going past, over the small ones, to the middle of the middle, to one who had not been there. You do not help a telling on its way to someone like that. You hold still and wait to see whether it gets there.

So for the first time Carried-It-Sleeping only heard it.

It was short. It was shorter than it had ever seemed from inside. A wall, and a thinness, and a shape in the thinness, and then looking round. Somebody could have that who had never seen a wall do anything. Carried-It-Sleeping had always hoped so, and had never been in a place to find out. The one who tells a piece is the one person who cannot be told it.

And it was not only Carried-It-Sleeping.

You could feel it go round the warm as the telling got to each. The old one at the edge, who had said *the wall went dim first* to anybody who would lie still for it, heard the wall go dim first, in its place, and breathed out. Two small ones on opposite sides of the middle heard *the side toward the near wall the same as the side toward the old ones*, and each looked across at the other. Went-Round heard somebody else go all the way round it and find it the same from behind, and was still for the whole of that, which is not a thing Went-Round is good at.

Then the line. The place in the warm with nothing in it. The stone at the bottom of the shelving.

"And not one of us turning," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It.

Stone-First had told it as *not one of you*. It had gone to *us* on the way down, in a small one's mouth, a long time ago, and nobody had put it back. Carried-It-Sleeping looked across to the edge of the amber. Stone-First was standing with the whole body still, which is how Stone-First stands, and there was nothing to see. But Stone-First hardly ever tells a thing. So Stone-First had hardly ever had one come back.

Everyone had been there. Everyone had a piece. And not one of them had heard their own, until there was somebody to tell it to who needed all of it.

It was a strange thing to be given by a person who was looking at a wall.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It stopped. That was the end.

Nobody asked. The one who should have asked did not know yet that asking was a thing you could do.

"Who was there?" said Carried-It-Sleeping, because somebody has to say it.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It did not turn round.

"Everyone," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It, to the newest one. "Carried-It-Sleeping for the wall. The ones who lay nearest, for underneath. Went-Round for the far side. Stone-First for the line, and the stone, and the backs. The old ones across the floor, for the going back." A breath. "Nobody for all of it. And nobody for what it was."

"That is a longer answer than usual," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

"I counted," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It.

::::: scene 4

::: pov keeps-a-hand-on-it

The warm did not make the small sound.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had half thought it might, and was not sorry. It had not made it when the pieces were put together, either. It was not that kind of telling. Nobody could say it was a thing worth saying, because nobody could say what it was. It had been put together so that it could be carried. Now it had been carried, as far as one person.

Then a small one sat up. It was the same one who had asked the last time.

"It has still not got the piece that says it was kind."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It knew that piece. Everyone did. It had been asked for when the telling was put together, and Stone-First had answered.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It looked toward the edge of the amber.

Stone-First did not say anything. Stone-First was looking back, for as long as it takes to say a name, the way you look at the one whose telling it is.

Oh, thought Keeps-A-Hand-On-It.

"Whose piece is that?" It came out with more of Stone-First in it than was meant. "Who was there when it was kind?"

The small one looked round the warm, slowly, at every one of the people in turn. It took a while. Nobody had it.

"Or unkind?"

Nobody had that either.

"Then it is not in," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It. "Not because it is not so."

"I know why," said the small one. "I only wanted to see whether anyone had found it." And lay down again, quite content.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It understood that the small one was going to go on asking, whenever there was somebody new to ask in front of, and that this was all right. It was a good thing to keep looking for. It was only not a piece.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It sat down beside the newest one. Its hands had shut. It was still looking at the wall.

"Did it stand up?" said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It, low, to Carried-It-Sleeping. "By itself?"

"Nobody held it up. I was watching them. The old one at the edge had the whole mouth ready, and kept it shut."

"Then it stood up."

"Yes."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It thought about that for a while. It was a quieter thing than a name going round. It did not go anywhere. It stayed where it was.

::::: scene 5

::: pov teddy

Nothing was done with it afterwards.

That is the end of this telling, and Carried-It-Sleeping puts it in because it is the kind of end that gets left out. The bright one had been kept a long time for the ones not born yet. Then one was born, and it was told, whole. And then the small ones went back to the game, and an old one began the long dark again to get it right this time, and nobody said what the telling had been for.

No rule was made out of it. No word. Went-Round did not go round it. The light had not come again, and the ground had not said that it would, or that it would not.

It would not be told to the newest one a second time. You do not. The newest one would lie in the warm and hear it go round in pieces, with people putting things in, the same as everybody.

Carried-It-Sleeping counted the pieces of the warm, from habit. They were all there.

"It looked at the wall the whole time," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It, nearly asleep.

"It always looks at the wall."

"I know. So did I, telling it. I have only got the wall from you."

"Now it has the wall from you."

"Who had it from you." Keeps-A-Hand-On-It worked it out. "That is a long way for a wall to go."

"It is the same wall," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It slept.

On the near wall the amber was where it had always been. The newest one looked at it, and its hand opened and shut, and after a while it slept too.

Carried-It-Sleeping stayed awake a little longer, in case there was anything that needed doing with a telling once it had been told.

There was not.
