::: pov went-round

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there, and this time the someone began it lying on the far side of the amber, listening to the small ones not manage something.

The small ones had decided that the newest one should have a name.

Went-Round could have told them how that would go. Nobody asked. They had worked it out for themselves, the way they had worked out the game. A name is a thing said about someone that goes round the warm, twice and three times, until it is worn smooth. So you find a thing to say, and you say it three times, and there you are.

"Looks-At-The-Wall," said one of them, to the next. "Looks-At-The-Wall. Looks-At-The-Wall."

"That is three by you," said the next. "Three by you is one."

They did it properly then, one to another across the middle, and it got as far as an old one near the edge, who was meant to say it back and did not.

"You looked at the wall," said the old one. "For the whole of the seep. So did I, I am told. It is what a newest one does."

"Opens-The-Hand."

"So did you."

"Was-Told-The-Bright-One," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It, who was not too big to join in.

Went-Round thought that one might go. It was true, and it was true of nobody else below the roof.

"That is a thing you did," said Carried-It-Sleeping, lying down. "You told it. It lay there."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It thought about that, and gave it up, fairly.

It went on for most of a telling. Went-Round did not help and did not stop it. A name is not for wanting or not wanting. Went-Round had said that once to somebody who did not want one, and it is just as true for somebody who wants one for somebody else. You cannot push a thing round the warm. It goes, or it lies where you put it.

In the end the small one who had started it sat down, cross.

"There is nothing to say of it. It has not done anything. Everything it has, it has always had."

Several old ones made the small sound, kindly. The small ones went back to the game.

Went-Round did not go back to anything. *Always* had gone by, and Went-Round had felt it go, the way you feel a turning in a passage you had thought was straight.

::::: scene 2

::: pov went-round

What had the newest one always had?

Went-Round went round it, lying down. The warm. The amber on the near wall and the blue-green on the far one. Somebody close by when it slept. The tellings going over it. The game. And at the start of every working time, far up, from the line, the ground saying *someone*, small and late, with a good one on top of it before it had finished.

Went-Round stopped there.

That one was not *always*. Everyone below the roof knew it was not. It had a first time, and the first time was in a telling, and the small ones played it.

Went-Round had been round this before. It was a long while ago, on the working time after the second stone, when a small one wanted to know how many times made *every time*. And Went-Round had found that it is not a number. It is what the people say of a thing when nobody remembers the time it was not done. Of the up-people, everybody remembered that time. So the most that could be said was *every time since*. The old one by the seep had found those words, and Went-Round had kept them, without knowing whether they were a small thing or a large one.

*When nobody remembers.*

Went-Round had said *nobody* and had meant the people. All of them at once, as if the people were one thing lying in the amber, remembering. It had not come into Went-Round's head to go and ask them one at a time.

Went-Round got up.

The old one by the seep remembered. "They came down with their light and went about and moved no stone. For longer than you would think anyone could."

Carried-It-Sleeping remembered, and had stood nearer to it than anyone. The larger of the two who once tried the squeeze remembered, and was scornful about being asked, having told *nobody looked up* standing up in front of everybody.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It took longer.

"I was not there. I was born after the two stones. I remember being told it."

"Is that remembering it?"

"I can tell it. All of it. Without a mistake."

"I know you can. That is not what I asked."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It thought, with a hand flat on the floor.

"No," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It. "It is having it. It is not the same as it having happened to me. It is like the seep."

"Which did you have first? The stone from the line, or the telling that says there was a time before it?"

"I do not remember."

"No. Nobody remembers that far down. But I was lying a few places from you, and I do. You had the stone first. You had it on every working time before you knew a word. Then we told you *since*."

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It looked at Went-Round for a while, and did not say anything. It had the look of a thing being put aside to keep.

Then there was only the middle of the middle.

The newest one was awake. It was looking at the wall. Went-Round lay down beside it, close, and did not ask it anything, because there was no way to, and waited.

The working time came. Far up, the loud came down the long passage and settled. Then the ground, from the line. *Someone.* Small, and late. And on top of it the other. A good one. At once.

The newest one's hand opened and shut.

It did not look toward the shelving. Nobody in the warm did, any more. But the old ones did not look because they had got used to it, and could tell you when. The newest one did not look because there was nothing there to look at. It was the sound that comes at the working time. It had come on every working time the newest one had been awake for. And nobody had yet told it that there had ever been anything else.

So there was one.

Went-Round lay still and went round it again, to be sure. The newest one would be told. The coming of the up-people, and the two stones, and all of it; Keeps-A-Hand-On-It would see to that. And then it would have what Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had. A thing that had always come, and a telling that said it had not. The stone first. *Since* afterwards.

The people had been telling the small ones *since* for as long as there had been small ones born to tell. So nobody had ever looked at what was lying in the middle before the telling got there.

Went-Round was looking at it now. It was looking at the wall.

::::: scene 3

::: pov went-round

In the time after the working, when Stone-First had come down and the whole of the people were in the warm, Went-Round stood up and said so.

"I said a thing once about the up-people's stone. That it could never be *every time*. Because *every time* is what we say when nobody remembers the time it was not, and everybody did."

"Every time since," said the old one by the seep, who liked having found it.

"Yes. For you. For me. For everyone who was there before the stones. That will not change, however many stones." Went-Round turned to the middle. "It is not so for that one. It does not remember a time the up-people's stone did not come. There was not one, for it. And it has not been told one yet. For the newest one it is not *every time since*. It is *every time*."

The warm was quiet.

"It will be told," said an old one. "The coming of the up-people. Then it will know."

"It will know what Keeps-A-Hand-On-It knows. That there is a telling. Keeps-A-Hand-On-It had the stone before the telling, and so will this one, and so will any who come after." Went-Round went on, carefully, not round. "I am not saying anything has changed on the line. I do not know what the up-people do, or why. I never have. I am saying where I have been and what is on the far side of it. There is one below the roof now for whom it is a thing that is done."

A thing that is done. Everybody below the roof knows what kind of thing that is, and what the people call it. Went-Round did not say the word. Nobody else did either. You could feel them not.

"Then say it is every time," said a small one. "If it is."

"I cannot. I was there before. It is not mine to say." Went-Round looked at the newest one, which was looking at the wall. "It is that one's. And it does not know a word."

That got the small sound from a few. Not from everyone.

The old one by the seep said that a thing which is true for one who cannot say it, and cannot be said by anyone who can, is a strange sort of true. Went-Round said yes, it was. That was why it was being kept as Went-Round's and not handed to the warm. And the warm let it be kept. It did not settle anything. It had argued the up-people's stone once before, at the beginning, and had not settled it then.

The small ones tried it, of course. *Every-Time.* Across the middle, one to another, three times and four.

No old one said it back.

"That is a thing Went-Round found," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "It is not a thing the newest one did."

So it lay where they had put it. The newest one had no name, and went on looking at the wall, and did not appear to feel the want of one.

Stone-First had heard all of it from the edge of the amber, standing, and had said nothing. Went-Round had not expected anything else. But Went-Round looked, to see what the nothing was like, and could not tell.

::::: scene 4

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First-Who-Waited went up to the line.

Before the working time, the slow way, with a stone moved at the top of the shelving. As on every time. That was in the name, the way the warm said it, and Stone-First had never had to think what it meant. Other people said it.

Went-Round's thing had come up the shelving on the inside. Stone-First set it down beside the four stones and looked at it.

It was true. Stone-First did not doubt that. Nobody below the roof remembers Stone-First not moving a stone first, and so it is *every time*, and Stone-First knew from the inside what that is like. It is like nothing. Nobody asks. Nobody lifts a head. For the newest one the up-people's stone was like that: a sound in the warm, with another on top of it.

On the line it was not a sound in the warm.

On the line it was the loud, coming down, and settling, and one piece of it coming on. A light, held low. Feet looked at first. A stone carried to the front through the squeeze, so that it would not scrape, by somebody whose breath was not back. It did not come. It was brought. Each one had been brought, down the long passage, in somebody's hands. And Stone-First had gone up before every one of them without knowing whether the light would be there, and had not once thought of that as not knowing. It was only how a line is kept.

So it could be *every time* in the middle of the middle, and something else up here, and both be so. Stone-First did not go round that. It was not a thing with a far side. It was two places.

Four stones. The long row, each a hand from the next, each where it was set.

The loud came down.

All at once, and then steadily. It settled at the junction, the way it settles. Then one of them coming on, down the third passage, to the crack. The light, low. The smallest of the up-people out onto the silt with a stone held to the front. The looking, which is short now. The kneeling. The stone set down a hand from Stone-First's feet, and the ground saying what it says, late, and theirs. The step back. The thing said that goes up at the end.

Stone-First bent, and put a hand on the rock, and moved it. At once. A good one.

The up-person waited a hand, and went. The light went with them.

A long way below, where the warm is thickest, that had been one sound with two parts, the sound that comes at the working time. Stone-First had been told so.

Up here it had been this one.
