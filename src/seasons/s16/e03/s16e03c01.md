This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there. This time the someone had asked to be, with a stone, which is not how the same someone went about it the first time.

It began in the game, with Carried-It-Sleeping being told how the up-people kneel.

The small ones were short of a knocker. They had their line of stones across the floor, and one of them standing on it very straight, and the rest bunched below, all one warm piece. They wanted to play the first stone, the old one, with the kneeling in it. Nobody would be the up-person. The up-person has to come late and be forgiven, and nobody likes that part.

"You do it," said a small one. "You went up to them."

That was true. So Carried-It-Sleeping got up, and went through a squeeze that was two small ones standing close together, and knelt, and put a stone down a hand from the feet of the one on the line.

"Not like that," said the one on the line.

"Like what, then?"

"Lower. And you look at the feet first. And the stone is in two hands, held to the front, and it goes down slowly, so it is small and plain." The small one knelt, there on the line, to show. It was very exact. "Like that."

"Who told you?"

"Stone-First told it. Everyone knows."

"I saw one of them kneel before you were born. I stood as near to it as I am to the edge of the amber."

"That was to the humming stone," said the small one. "This is to the line. It might be different kneeling."

Carried-It-Sleeping opened the mouth to say that it was not, and found there was nothing to say it with.

A long time ago, when the squeeze still fitted, Carried-It-Sleeping went up it and stood at the edge of the up-people's light. No stone was moved first. Three of them worked and talked and knelt to their humming stone, for as long as the first warm seep takes to tell, twice, and nobody looked up. That was the first telling that was ever Carried-It-Sleeping's. Then the humming stone, carried down asleep and stood on the line, and that was the name.

Everything the up-people have done since, they have done at the line. A stone set down at the start of every working time. A step back. A thing said that goes up at the end. Then Stone-First's answer, at once. Stone-First came down and told it, and the small ones play it. Carried-It-Sleeping has told it to newer small ones than these, without a mistake.

And had never once been there.

It was an odd thing to find out at the foot of a row of small ones' stones. The one who went up to the up-people before there was a line to meet them on had the up-people's stone the way Keeps-A-Hand-On-It has the time before it. It was a told thing. Carried-It-Sleeping could tell it. That was not what the small one had asked.

"Who has been on the line for one?" said Carried-It-Sleeping. "Besides Stone-First."

The small ones thought. They are quick at who was there. It is most of the game.

"The one not asked," said the one on the line.

"Who else?"

There was nobody else.

"Then ask that one how they kneel," said the small one, very pleased. Several old ones at the edge of the amber made the small sound, because nobody below the roof can ask that one anything.

Carried-It-Sleeping did the kneeling again, lower, the told way, and was forgiven for being late, as the up-person is. Then lay down in the middle and counted the pieces of the warm, from habit.

They were all there. One of them was thinking about the shelving, and it was not a small one.

::: pov teddy

Carried-It-Sleeping went up between working times, the slow way, and moved a stone at the top of the shelving. A good one.

Then stopped at the edge of the silt. That is where you stop until the one on the line has answered you.

Stone-First-Who-Waited was on the line, in the open, facing the crack, with the four stones and the up-people's long row. Stone-First bent and moved the rock at the edge of the silt, and Carried-It-Sleeping came on, and stood a step below the line, not on it.

"I want to stand on the line," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "For one."

Stone-First waited to be told.

"I have their stone from you. Every one of them. I tell it to the small ones, and they tell it back to me, better. I have never been there for one."

That was the first reason, and it was true. There was another under it.

"And I went to them once. You know how. I moved no stone. I stood where their light was not, and nobody saw me, and I came home pleased with it. I have told that to every small one since, as the way it is not done." Carried-It-Sleeping looked at the crack. "I would like to have gone to them once the way it is done."

Stone-First looked at Carried-It-Sleeping for as long as it takes to say a name.

"Is it to see what they do?"

It was a proper question, and not an easy one. Everyone below the roof knows what Stone-First thinks of a thing done to see. Stone-First once had a hand on the answering rock, and the wanting to keep it there and find out, and moved the rock at once.

So Carried-It-Sleeping looked, properly, on the inside.

"A little," said Carried-It-Sleeping, because saying so is also manners. "Mostly it is to be there."

"The little stays at the top of the shelving."

"Yes."

"Then it is yours to find out, and nobody else's. I cannot find it out for you. I have been on the line for every one of them. I do not know what it is to come to one."

Carried-It-Sleeping had expected it to be harder than that. Once, when the up-people's stone fell before it was set and an old one stood up in the warm, Carried-It-Sleeping had said it plainly: when Stone-First wants someone, the ground says so. That was still true. Stone-First had not wanted anyone. This was the other thing, which is asking, and it seemed that nobody had tried it.

"Where do I stand?"

"On it."

"Where on it?"

"Not in my place. Not among their stones. Those are not ours to stand in."

That left one side. Carried-It-Sleeping looked along the line that way, at an arm's length of bare silt, and knew what had stood there once. Stone-First had told it standing up, and had said *the light went round it* three times.

Stone-First said nothing about that. Neither did Carried-It-Sleeping. Somebody has to stand somewhere.

"Do I answer?"

"Whose stone is it?"

Carried-It-Sleeping thought. "It is set down a hand from your feet."

"Yes."

"And two stones at once is noise."

"The small ones found that. I only keep it."

"Then I stand."

"With the whole body."

"I know how," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "I did it for the first warm seep, twice."

Stone-First did not make the small sound. It was a near thing.

Carried-It-Sleeping moved a stone at the top of the shelving, going, and went down to say so in the warm. The ground says *someone*. It never says who, and it was only fair that everyone should know whose stone the next one would be.

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First-Who-Waited went up to the line before the working time, the slow way, with a stone moved at the top. As on every time.

Then a second stone, at the top of the shelving. Good, and plain, and a little careful. Stone-First knew whose. Stone-First bent and moved the rock at the edge of the silt, and Carried-It-Sleeping came out across the silt and onto the line, an arm's length along it, on the side where the up-people's row does not run. And turned to face the crack, and was still.

Nobody had asked for this before. Stone-First had been turning that over since the asking. A small one came up the squeeze once, before the stones, and stood here for a while. Went-Round stood in the place itself, once, to see what it looked like. Neither of them asked first. And nobody had ever said that the line at a stone was for Stone-First alone. It had only been so, on every working time, for so long that it had begun to look like something somebody had said.

There was another thing, and Stone-First set it down on the inside and left it there. Somebody had stood at that arm's length before. It had moved no stone. The ground had said nothing of it, and Stone-First had not turned. This one had moved a stone at the top of the shelving and been answered. Stone-First did not turn this time either. There was no need. The ground had said *someone*, and Stone-First knew who.

The loud came down.

All at once, and then steadily. It settled at the junction, the way it settles. One voice by itself, saying a thing and stopping, and the small hum after it. Then one of them coming on, down the third passage, to the crack.

The light, low. The smallest of the up-people out onto the silt, breath not back, with a stone held to the front and the small hard thing on the chest.

The light found Stone-First's feet, the way it does.

Then it went to the side. It still does that. It goes an arm's length along the line, and lies on the silt there for about as long as it takes to say a name, and comes back.

This time it did not find silt.

Stone-First did not look to the side. You look at the squeeze. But the up-people's light is the only light on the line that moves, and you can tell where it has gone with the eyes held still. It had found feet. It went up from them, slowly, the way a person looks, and onto what was there, the way it goes onto Stone-First. Not round. And it stayed.

The looking has been short for a long time. This one was long. The up-person stood with the stone held to the front, and the small hard thing on the chest had come round with the light. The breath that was not back stopped coming for a while, and then came. Nobody on the line did anything. It was longer than a name. It was not as long as a short telling, and Stone-First did not count it.

Then the light came back, low, to Stone-First's feet.

The kneeling. The stone set down at the end of the row, a hand from Stone-First's feet, where the stone is always set. The ground saying what it says, late, and theirs. The step back. The thing said that goes up at the end, with the face turned to Stone-First, as on every time.

Stone-First bent, and put a hand on the rock, and moved it. At once. A good one.

The up-person heard it, and waited a hand, and went. The light did not go to the side again. It went with them through the crack. Then the silt was in the light that never changes, and there were two on the line.

The up-people had two on this line once before, and set the stone by the one who could answer. Stone-First had seen them do it, and had said in the warm that they told the two apart. Whether there had been any telling apart this time, Stone-First could not say. The stone had gone where the stone goes. A thing that is done every time does not show you whether it was chosen.

::::: scene 4

::: pov teddy

Here is what it was like. What happened has been told already, and it was the same as every time.

It was loud. Carried-It-Sleeping had said so first, a long time ago: loud like a stone that never finishes falling. Everyone below the roof says it now. And then it came down the long passage and through the crack, thinned, with only its shape left, and Carried-It-Sleeping thought, yes. That is what it is like. It is a strange thing to stand still while an old telling of your own goes by, and to find it was right.

Then the hum.

It is very small by the time it has come through the crack. Carried-It-Sleeping would not have known it from the rock, but for one thing. Carried-It-Sleeping had carried that hum through the squeeze against the chest, and held still for it, and knew it at once. It was awake up there. Somebody was with it.

Then the light was in the crack, and the little that was to stay at the top of the shelving had not stayed. It had come up after all. What would the light do? Carried-It-Sleeping was standing where the one not asked had stood, and everyone below the roof has been told what the light did then.

There was nothing to do about the wanting. So Carried-It-Sleeping did nothing about it.

The light came along the silt, and it did not go round. It went onto the feet and up, the way it would go up anybody. It was only light.

Carried-It-Sleeping stood with the whole body still.

That was the same as the first time, and it was not. The first time Carried-It-Sleeping stood still so that no head would turn. This time a light lay on Carried-It-Sleeping, with one of the up-people behind it, looking. There is nothing you can do about being looked at except go on being there. It is harder than the other way. Stone-First has done it on every working time and has never said so.

Then the kneeling.

It was the one who knelt to the humming stone. Carried-It-Sleeping knew them by it. Low, with the stone in two hands, and the feet looked at first. It was not different kneeling at all. It was the same, and now there was somebody who could say so.

Then the thing said. It went up at the end, and Carried-It-Sleeping did not know a word of it.

It was said to Stone-First. The face was turned that way, and the stone was at Stone-First's feet. Carried-It-Sleeping was only where it could be heard.

Carried-It-Sleeping had heard one of those before that was said to nobody in the light. That was from the side, at the edge of their light, at the end of a working time. The smallest of them had turned to the deep dark, and said a thing, and waited, and gone. That one went up at the end too. Whether this was the same thing said again, Carried-It-Sleeping could not tell, and does not say. They all go up at the end. A sound you do not know a word of has only its end to know it by.

So Carried-It-Sleeping stood for it, the way you stand for a telling, so that nothing of you gets into it by mistake. You can stand for a thing that is not for you. Carried-It-Sleeping had done it once before, for the first warm seep, twice, and had not known, then, that that was what it was.

And then the ground, from an arm's length away, up through the feet. *Someone.* A good one, at once.

In the warm that had always been the second half of a sound. Carried-It-Sleeping had never been standing beside it when it was made. It is a great deal bigger there.

The up-person waited a hand, and went.

They stood on the line, the two of them, in the light that never changes.

"You moved a stone," said Stone-First.

"At the top of the shelving. Before they came down."

"They did not hear it."

"No. They were not there to hear it."

"It was moved."

"Yes."

Stone-First was still for as long as it takes to say a name.

"And they saw you."

"Yes," said Carried-It-Sleeping. "They did."

Carried-It-Sleeping knew those words. They had been a scolding once. *You did not move a stone. So they did not know you were there.* Stone-First had only said them again, with what was different put in.

Stone-First waited to be told.

"It is the same kneeling," said Carried-It-Sleeping.

"Yes."

"I do not know what they said."

"No."

"I do not know why they looked for so long."

"No."

"I was there."

"Go down and tell it," said Stone-First-Who-Waited. "Move a stone at the bottom."

Carried-It-Sleeping told it standing up, in the warm, and it was short. It had only what the up-people did in it. Stone-First taught that once, by asking for a telling again with one thing left out.

The loud, and the hum. The light, low. The feet, and then the side. The looking. The kneeling, and the stone at the end of the row. The step back. The thing that goes up at the end. Stone-First's answer, from an arm's length. The going.

The small ones asked, because small ones may.

"Did the light go round you?"

"It went up me."

"Like Stone-First?"

"Like anybody."

"Did they ask you?"

"The stone was at Stone-First's feet. Whatever they said, they said it that way. I was where it could be heard."

"Did you know a word of it?"

"No."

Then the one who had been on the line in the game. "Was it different kneeling?"

"It was the same. I have been there for both now."

"Then I was right about how they kneel."

"You were right. You had it from Stone-First."

"And now from you."

"And now from me."

The small one lay down, satisfied. Another sat up.

"Nobody looked up, the first time. Did they look up this time?"

"The light did. With them I think it is the same thing."

"Why this time?"

Carried-It-Sleeping had come down the whole of the shelving with that one, and had not got to the end of it.

"The first time I was behind them. This time I was in front."

"Is that all?"

"It is all I was there for."

A few of the old ones made the small sound. Nobody said anything of Carried-It-Sleeping afterwards that they had not said before.

Keeps-A-Hand-On-It came over, later, with a stone moved first. It was two places.

"They were all here. Nobody asked me, so I counted them, for the whole of it. One went as far as the foot of the shelving and moved a stone. I waited to be told. That one came back, and said it was only to hear better."

"Was that hard?"

"Yes," said Keeps-A-Hand-On-It, with feeling.

"Good."

By the next telling the small ones had put a second one on the line. The second one stands an arm's length along and does nothing, with the whole body, while the knock is knocked. Anyone who moves is off. It is the hardest place in the game, and they take it in turns.

Carried-It-Sleeping was asked how it was done, once, and showed them, and was not corrected.

Then the working time came. Far up, from the line, the ground said *someone*, small and late, and another on top of it, at once. Stone-First was up there alone. One was what had been asked for.

Carried-It-Sleeping lay in the middle of the warm and counted the pieces.

They were all there.
