  <h1 class="lore-entry__title">The Life of Saint Aoife Set Down at Cill Aoife</h1>
  <p><span class="character-badge">1216 AD (1416 UCSD)</span> <span class="character-badge">Cill Aoife, Ireland</span></p>
</header>
<div class="lore-entry__body">

The monastic community at Cill Aoife, in Ireland, deputises one of its own to write down the life of the woman who tends its thorn well. [Brother Daire](/star-rangers/characters/brother-daire/) opens the document by naming himself unworthy and naming who asked for it — *"Written by the hand of Brother Daire, unworthy, at the request of the community"* — and those two clauses turn out to be the whole method. The account knows what it is and who is accountable for it, which is more than most of what survives the period can say.

The vision it records falls in the same year: spring, the eve of Saint Muirenn's feast, [Aoife's](/star-rangers/characters/saint-aoife/) thirtieth year, keeping her habitual vigil at the well alone. She would not describe it fully even to the one who heard her most private accounts, and Daire was not that person — he says so in the text, distinguishing the role the community gave him from the role of the one meant to hear what she could not otherwise say aloud. What he writes is assembled from the fragments she permitted to be written, and he marks it as assembled rather than filling the gaps with invention. He also preserves the distinction Aoife drew between having been *shown* something and having been *healed* by it, and states plainly that he did not, at the time, understand it.

Neither the manuscript nor anything else surviving records when Daire was born or when he died. The record that kept his writing did not trouble itself to keep his biography.

What it did keep is the transmission. [The Life of Saint Aoife](/star-rangers/codex/life-of-saint-aoife/) is copied and recopied through the medieval and early modern period; the [Celtic Union's](/star-rangers/lore/celtic-union-of-planets/) founding Currach Fleet scriptorium carries a nineteenth-century transcription off Earth in 2561–2589 UCSD, roughly eleven centuries after Daire wrote it, and the Ceridwen Archive on [Aethelrock](/star-rangers/lore/planets/aethelrock/) holds the copy currently cited. The devotion travelled with it as living observance rather than as archived text — feast kept, well-water blessing continued in symbolic form aboard ship — which is why the Union has never treated [Aoife](/star-rangers/lore/saint-aoife/) as a subject of antiquarian interest.

Fourteen centuries after it was written, the account is being read twice at once. Union cultural scholarship annotates it against [Levril](/star-rangers/lore/levrils/) taxonomy — a presence at every distance at once, air that folds without tearing, a well that runs warm for three days — and the Survey Archive's reconciliation desk lays its instrument anomaly beside a boundary signature logged at Threshold Station. Neither reading closes, and the Union's own devotional authorities have declined to treat the two as competing. Daire's editorial habit is the reason both are possible: he recorded what he could not explain as something he could not explain, and left it in.

**Canon facts:**
- *The Life of Saint Aoife* is written at Cill Aoife in 1216 AD (1416 UCSD), at the community's request, by Brother Daire. It is the earliest dated event this timeline carries.
- The vision it records falls in the same year — spring, the eve of Saint Muirenn's feast, Aoife's thirtieth year. Her feast is afterwards kept on the anniversary of the vision rather than of her death, a choice the community made without recorded debate.
- Daire records only the fragments Aoife permitted to be written, marks what is assembled as assembled, and preserves the *shown*-not-*healed* distinction while stating that he did not understand it.
- No birth or death date for Brother Daire survives in any form.
- Aoife dies in the winter of 1241 AD, having refused three offers to enter a formal religious house. Her sainthood comes from sustained local usage and from no formal process at all.
- The manuscript is copied and recopied through the medieval and early modern period; a nineteenth-century transcription leaves Earth with the Currach Fleet's founding scriptorium in 2561–2589 UCSD. The currently cited copy is held by the Ceridwen Archive on Aethelrock.
- The two later readings of the account — Levril encounter and the Dock Seven warm-edge correlation — are both filed in the Codex as their authors' accounts, not as the Archive's finding. Neither is settled.
- Whether the Brother Daire who keeps the river abbey near Dún Rí fourteen centuries later is the same man, a later namesake in the same tradition, or a title rather than a personal name, this Archive does not close. No record settling it has surfaced, and a shared name is not evidence of a shared identity.
- Dates in this era are given in AD, as the sources that produced them would write them; the UCSD equivalent is arithmetic (UCSD = CE + 200) and carries no claim that the standard existed to be kept. See [Universal Concord Standard Date](/star-rangers/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate/).
