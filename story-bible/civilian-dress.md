# Civilian dress — what the record already holds, and how a prompt says it

Planning note; not rendered on the site. Written 11 October 2026 at Dermot's
question, verbatim: *"Is Inspector Albercombe being deliberately retro when he
wears a 20th century suit? Add a new skill or artefact for 29th century fashion
and civilian dress codes."* The fifth sibling of `species-design.md`,
`mind-design.md`, `language-design.md` and `starship-design.md`, and the civilian
half of `uniforms-and-insignia.md`, which covers the three uniformed forces only.

**Nothing here is canon.** Each line is marked **DERIVED** (it follows from the
page cited) or **PROPOSED** (his to rule). Art is illustrative: an `image_alt`
records what a file shows, not what the world holds, and is cited only as

## The question that started it

**DERIVED.** The record does not say Albercombe dresses old. His page says
nothing about his clothes; the prose gives him a coat by the door
(`src/seasons/s02/e09/s02e09c01.md`). He is a Detective Inspector, and the bureau
jacket's obligation is on the lower ranks (`src/lore/eden-bureau-uniform.md`), so
civilian cloth is *the entry working, not the entry contradicted* (`images.md`,
19 August note). What reads as twentieth-century is the generator's: a lapelled
sports jacket over an open collar is the stock detective, and the portrait was
filed before the 7 October hint rule existed. Herringbone itself is a weave as
old as looms and need not date anything; the cut does. (Prompts still negate
it, because the generator draws it as a sports jacket; the standard negative
below is a prompt rule, not a claim about the cloth.) Whether to make it a
trait was open item 1, ruled *not retro* on 11 October.

The record does hold one deliberately dressy officer: Bertram Ashcombe, *"immaculately
dressed"*, who *"dresses for the job he believes he is doing"*
(`src/characters/bertram-ashcombe.md`), and whose prompt says *the formality is
his, not the room's*. That is the shape a deliberate choice takes: the page
says it. *(Superseded 12 October 2026: his page's* uniform *was ruled to
stand, so he wears the Threshold Station Constabulary's uniform, immaculate,
and the record now holds no character who dresses old. See* Ashcombe, ruled
*below.)*

## The principles the record already forces

1. **Clothes are built for the work and the place.** The uniform's reason is
   the hospital's: *built for the work and shed at the door*
   (`src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`). The bureau jacket is *cut for a long
   shift* (`eden-bureau-uniform.md`). New London holds 16–18 °C, *cool enough to
   keep the islanders' layered dressing a practical habit rather than a
   costume* (`src/lore/new-london-space-habitat.md`); Fliade's surface party
   works *in cold-weather clothing* (`src/lore/planets/fliade.md`). DERIVED: a
   civilian's clothes answer the place's temperature and the person's work
   before anything else.

2. **No names, no words on cloth.** *No Ranger's name is written on their
   clothing … the era has no use for them* (`rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`;
   `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`). The plainmark carries no letters or
   numerals (`src/lore/plainmark.md`). DERIVED: the habit is the era's, not the
   Corps' alone, so civilian clothes carry no names, slogans, logos or lettering.

3. **The technology in cloth does not announce itself.** *Smart-weave clothing
   that reads as ordinary fabric until it needs to be more*
   (`src/lore/environmental-technology-design.md`; `src/lore/planets/tir-na-nog.md`).
   *No human in the Concord carries an AI subsystem on or in the body* (same page;
   `src/glossary/smart-pet.md`). DERIVED: no glowing trim, no screens on a
   sleeve, no visible electronics; the century is in the cloth's behaviour, not
   its look.

4. **A uniform is a role, not a class of person.** A Ranger off duty is out of
   uniform, and *off duty is a state, not a role*
   (`src/lore/shore-leave-and-neutrality.md`); the Corps attends a swearing-in
   *in civilian clothes, unarmed* (`src/characters/anne-wender.md`); Tissadelle
   walks the uplands *in her own coat instead of her service one*
   (`src/seasons/s05/e02/s05e02c01.md`). In a bureau the garment follows *role and
   case* (`CLAUDE.md`). Five-O is uniformed, design unstated
   (`src/lore/orbital-habitats-compact.md`).

5. **Nothing military, no display, no ideology.** The Military Space Command's
   braid and ribbon order is the record's named anti-model
   (`rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`); the Union's military is *mostly ceremonial*
   (`src/lore/celtic-union-of-planets.md`); no page names a political ideology
   (`CLAUDE.md`). DERIVED: civilian dress has no epaulettes, braid or military
   cut, and no badge or colour declaring a side.

6. **The modesty bar.** *Dressed for the work or the place and never to be looked
   at* (`CLAUDE.md`). DERIVED: the default civilian frame is covered,
   high-necked or plain-necked, practical.

7. **Kept, not shabby.** Places and kit are clean and maintained; *honest marks of
   use at most* (`images.md`, 11 October). DERIVED: clothes are cared for and
   mended; *worn* means used, never neglected.

8. **Peoples dress in their own media.** A uniform's equivalent may be *a colour,
   a sound, a posture or a mark on the body* (`rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`);
   Rook-7 *has no garment at all*. DERIVED: a non-human civilian is never put in
   human clothes by default (`species-design.md`).

9. **Dress is a choice, and carries custom.** The silhouette clause lets an
   impression be carried by *dress, carriage, custom* and never physiology
   (`CLAUDE.md`). Aethelrock keeps *tartan-woven cold-weather cloth in
   clan-specific setts* (`src/lore/planets/aethelrock.md`), the record's one
   patterned-cloth custom; a whole habitat may choose an old register, as New
   London's neo-Victorian frontage does.

10. **The hint of the century.** A present-day frame *may look plain, worn and
    ordinary … but not period*; History is exempt (`images.md`, 7 October).

11. **Each place has its own wardrobe, and people do not dress alike.** RULED
    13 October 2026 (Dermot, *"Yes agreed"*, to option A of *One wardrobe for
    every human world*, with his uniqueness and diversity directions of the
    same day). The century lives in one detail of cloth (a closure, a seam,
    how the cloth behaves); cut, layering, garment kind, colour and pattern
    follow each place's climate, work and culture, and each person's age,
    build and taste. The vocabulary below is a range, not an outfit. Ruling 4
    stands: colour is personal, and no place has a colour custom unless a page
    names one. A dress line is added per setting as pages need it.

12. **Fashion is local and slow.** RULED the same day (option A). With no FTL
    communication a look can travel no faster than a ship, so there is no
    fashion across the settled worlds: each habitat and world has its own
    current look, styles move within it and are carried between worlds by
    people, and a ship's crew is recognisable as being from somewhere.

13. **Heritage may show in one chosen thing.** RULED the same day (option A).
    A person may carry their heritage in one choice of their own (a pattern, a
    garment form, a way of wearing it), never as costume and never as the
    picture's point, as Aethelrock's setts are a custom kept. Dress has not
    converged, and it has not frozen either.

14. **A Krenyi dresses plainly.** RULED 14 October 2026 (Dermot, *"Option A"*,
    after asking *"Krenyi dress style or just very functional?"*). PROPOSED from
    `src/lore/krenyi.md`, which describes their manner, *no ornamental mystery*,
    and clarity as *an absence of ornament*, and says nothing about clothes. A
    Krenyi's clothes are well made and unornamented, chosen for clarity, not
    utility: nothing is added that adds nothing, and that is not the same as
    workwear. The cloth may be fine and the cut exact. This holds wherever a Krenyi
    is, and needs no setting of its own: the Krenyi decline to be placed, so
    *Dress by place* carries no Krenyi entry, and this principle stands in for
    one. On duty a Krenyi Ranger wears the uniform like anyone else (Toval); off
    duty, Syra's plain fitted high-necked garment is the worked example.

## What follows, by setting

- **Habitat professional** (Eden, Threshold, the Compact). DERIVED: a plain
  jacket over ordinary clothes. A junior detective wears the bureau jacket,
  *plain single-breasted … issued in one colour*, rank as strokes at the
  collar; seniors, the undercover and Commissioners wear their own cloth.
  Analysts and clerks dress as for any long shift. Practice agrees: Oyelaran's
  plain grey work jacket, Voss's high-collared suit, Wayland's dark suit.
- **Domestic.** DERIVED from the terraces and galleys: knitwear, aprons, plain
  trousers, work clothes for growing things (Kate Ukrainka, Lev Saunders, Tomas
  Brennock in practice). Eden's air temperature is not stated.
- **Celtic Union worlds.** DERIVED: on biosphere worlds, smart-weave reading as
  ordinary cloth; on hard worlds (Aethelrock, Ynys Wydrin) weatherproof
  outerwear where *technology stays visible because pretending otherwise gets
  people killed*; clan setts on Aethelrock; Drithane exports wool
  (`src/lore/planets/drithane.md`). Ceremonial offices exist (Uachtarán, Seanad,
  Highland Guard); no ceremonial civilian dress is described. The Guard's coat is
  proposed in `uniforms-and-insignia.md`.
- **Human isolates.** The Kingdom of the Five Islands is a colony out of contact
  for longer than the record (`src/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands.md`);
  its people's dress is not described, and the portraits' wool tunics, high-necked
  dresses and the Sisterhood's habit are illustration. Saltmere grew up out of
  contact too but lives on engineered platforms (`src/lore/planets/saltmere.md`),
  so it is not a pre-industrial case. How the hint rule meets an isolate is open
  item 5.
- **Devotional houses.** A Fellowship chapter leader wears robes, worn *strong*,
  not as a costume for wisdom (Dermot, 12 August, `images.md`); novices, plain
  undyed tunics in practice. Martian hermitages keep open ground with *thin
  atmosphere, extreme temperature swings* (`src/lore/monasteries-of-mars.md`), so
  outdoors is pressure gear; their indoor dress is not stated. Cnoc na mBeach
  puts every guest to work at the hives (`src/lore/cnoc-na-mbeach.md`): working
  clothes.
- **Mars.** The founding-era service dress (Drey, Voss, Krast) is History and
  exempt. Present-day Martian civilian dress is not stated.
- **Ship crews off duty.** DERIVED: the uniform is shed at the door; a crew
  member off duty dresses as a civilian of wherever home is. An independent
  courier has no uniform at all (Lorien's unmarked flight jacket, in practice).

*Found in passing, since resolved:* two Survey Corps officers at work on Vellamar
are shown in what may not read as the olive Survey shell (Emma la Chapelle's
green field jacket over a leather vest, Ernesto Strozzi's khaki shirt), which
the no-plain-clothes rule would refuse, and Ashcombe's page says *uniform* where
his portrait shows a suit. Both resolved: la Chapelle and Strozzi were re-made
in the Survey shell in the century audit's batch 3 (11 October), and Ashcombe's
uniform was ruled on 12 October.


## Dress by place

## How a prompt says it — PROPOSED

**Positive.** *Well-made everyday clothing of the twenty-ninth century: simple
considered cuts, soft matte woven and knitted cloth, clean and cared for,
honest mends; nothing printed, nothing lettered.* Then choose, per person and
against the cast already filed (principles 11 to 13): the garment (not always
a jacket), the colour (the wearer's own, quiet or strong), the collar, the fit,
and any one heritage element. Name the place's reason (*layered for a cool
habitat*, *weatherproof for an upland*), and name the work.

**The hint, in cloth.** One detail of cut or closure no past century had: a
closure with no visible fastening, a seam that is a line and not stitching,
a cloth that drapes like wool and is not quite wool. Never a light. Use it on
one garment in a frame, or not at all where the room or the sky already
carries the century; it is not a description of every jacket (the 13 October
review found *band collar* in 27 prompts).

**Standard negative.** *No science-fiction costume, no skin-tight bodysuit, no
cape, no glowing trim or light strips, no visible electronics or screens on
clothing, no shoulder pads, epaulettes, braid or armour; no twentieth-century
business suit, necktie, lapelled sports jacket, tweed or herringbone, trench
coat or hat-brim detective look; no name tape, logo, badge or lettering on any
garment.* The period items are dropped from the negative only where the page
says the character dresses old (no page does, since 12 October) or the frame
is History.

## Ruled 11 October 2026

Dermot accepted all five recommendations below as put (*"Accept all five"*), so each item's option A is now the rule: Albercombe is not retro, and his next portrait dresses him in his own jacket in this note's vocabulary; neckties and lapels are absent from present-day dress, open only to a page that says a character dresses old, or to History; formality is the same clothes in a better finish; there are no colour customs except where a page names one (Aethelrock's setts); and an isolate's own dress is exempt from the hint-of-the-century rule, the hint arriving with the Survey's kit. On his question whether political leaders have a dress code of their own he chose **ceremony only**: no everyday leaders' dress anywhere; the Celtic Union's ceremonial offices (Uachtarán, Seanad) wear a ceremonial dress on occasions only, drafted when a page needs it, and leaders elsewhere dress as any senior professional does. His answer on detectives was the record's own: juniors wear the issued bureau jacket, seniors and the undercover their own clothes.

## As put — Dermot's decisions (ruled above)

1. **Albercombe.** A) Not retro: nothing changes on his page; the portrait
   stands as illustration, and its next re-make dresses him in his own
   civilian jacket in the vocabulary above. B) Make it a trait: one line on his
   page that he dresses older than his century (a canon change). C) Re-make the
   portrait now. **Recommended: A.** Ashcombe already owns the dressy-inspector
   note, and a second would blur both.
2. **Neckties and lapels.** A) Absent from present-day dress, available only to
   a page that says a character dresses old, or to History. B) They survive as
   formal wear somewhere (New London the likeliest). C) Leave it unsettled and
   keep negating them. **Recommended: A.** It makes the hint rule easy to keep
   and gives Ashcombe's choice its meaning.
3. **Formal dress.** A) No habitat-wide formal dress: formality is the same
   clothes in better finish, as the Ranger dress uniform *differs from field kit
   mainly in finish*. B) New London keeps a formal register to match its
   frontage. C) A Union ceremonial dress for Seanad and Uachtarán occasions,
   drafted when a page needs it. **Recommended: A**, with C left to a page.
4. **Colour customs.** A) None: colour is personal, and prompts default to quiet
   natural colours, since colour already carries meaning on uniforms and
   plainmarks. B) Name colour customs per place, as Aethelrock's setts already
   do. C) Mourning or occasion colours, marked as absent until a scene needs
   one. **Recommended: A**, B per page.
5. **Isolates and the hint rule.** A) An isolate's own dress is exempt like
   History, because a century it never had would be false to it; any hint comes
   in with the delegation's kit. B) The hint applies as in any present-day
   frame. C) Decide per page. **Recommended: A.**

**Read against the rulings of 13 October 2026** (`intake-2026-10-13.md`; the Benevolent Critic's pass the same day). Three directions of that day bear on dress, and none changes a rule above. *Bodies of the century:* bodies differ by training, work, age and heritage, a heavy working build is fit, and prosthetics rarely look like prosthetics, so clothing is cut for the person and never to hide or display a replaced limb. *Cleaner settlements:* kept clothes in kept places, as principle 7 already says. *Retro-futurist design passes when it looks intentional:* that allowance was given for furnishings and does not reopen ruling 2, so a lapelled jacket or a necktie still waits for a page that says a character dresses old.
