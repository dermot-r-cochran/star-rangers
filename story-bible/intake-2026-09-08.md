## First — "Shape (b) agreed": the thread keeps *Below the Roof*

Dermot raised it himself, as a question rather than a ruling:

> The Told might be unclear as the name of a thread?

The reading offered back, and the reasons, were these: *The Told* is a name
earned inside the story, when Stone-First explains in the second chapter that
the people are the ones who have been told, and cold, in a thread list or a
season index beside other titles, it reads as a fragment — told what, by whom.
*Below the Roof* is a place and a direction and reads without context. The two
names have different jobs: the door is an invitation on its own subdomain, and

readers who arrive knowing nothing. The thread now has two strands, and the
treatment rules that the survey-side strand must not explain the Told, so a
title that names the people tilts toward Strand A where *Below the Roof* holds

thread keeping its title.

## Fourth — "Most AI systems and robots are not drones"

Dermot, verbatim, unprompted, after the Pandoid rule:

> Most AI systems and robots are not drones unless there is a very good
> reason why a drone would be needed.

### Read as

A design principle for the whole record, on the same axis as *AI: Present,
Never Intrusive*: **embodiment follows the job.** A lawful system has the body
its work needs, and most need none — they are the building, the recycler,
the archive, the telemetry. Where a body is needed it is the right body:
Lumbarian's repair frame for open vacuum, Rook-7's analytical chassis, a
humanoid among people, a Smart Pet's subsystem in an animal. The small
free-flying machine is the reflex the ruling refuses: a drone is the
exception, and it has to earn its place with a reason a fixed system could
not meet. *Most* and *very good reason* are the ruling's own bounds — this
does not retire drones, it stops them being the default shape of a machine.

### Direction check

An **extension** of the AI texture already published (*there when something
needs doing and unremarkable every other moment*), and consistent with three
things the record already does: the Anstolik proposal argues against a drone
in so many words (*must be powered, is observable, has to be told where to
go*); the named machine characters are all built for a job, none a drone; the
image-prompt runbook already fights the generator's reach for hovering
hardware. Also consistent with the trope default: the drone-swarm and the
hovering-companion-bot are patterns, refused unless canon needs one.

**Existing drones, checked against the bound.** Each has a reason and stands:

- Remote-station maintenance drones on visible patrol routes
  (`environmental-technology-design.md`, Aspenar) — the outside of a
  station is the job, and legibility is the point of that register.
- The Subsea Cable Warden programme — an ocean floor no crew could work.
- CM-4471, the maintenance drone of *Docked Twice* (S04E01C01) — a hull
  that berths on a schedule because the work is at the berths; and the
  chapter's whole mechanism needs a registry identity on a powered-down shell.
- The Anstolik proposal's *a drone* — named only to be rejected.

**One line does not pass, and it is his.** The biosphere-world paragraph of
`environmental-technology-design.md` says *drones that hover at the edge of
notice rather than the center of it*. On a garden world, with no job named,
that is exactly the reflex the ruling refuses. Under the canon-change rule
this is a genuine dilemma between a published sentence and a ruling, so it is
put as choices rather than edited:

1. **Clarify the sentence** — the upkeep on a biosphere world is done by
   what is already there (the weave, the nanites, the ground systems), and
   the clause about hovering drones goes; nothing else on the page moves.
   *Recommended: it is a clarification in the rule's sense, one clause, and
   the rest of the paragraph already says the thing better.*
2. **Keep the drones and give them a reason** — name the job a biosphere
   world cannot do from the ground (canopy survey, say), which the sentence
   would then have to carry.

### Realized the same morning

- `src/lore/environmental-technology-design.md` — an **extension**: a new
  paragraph in *AI: Present, Never Intrusive* stating the principle, with the
  four reasons that stand as its worked examples. The biosphere sentence is
  untouched pending the choice above.
- `story-bible/images.md` — a standing rule for prompts: no drone in a scene
  unless the page it illustrates has one, and never as set-dressing.
- `CHANGELOG.md` — the lore extension recorded.
- The local novel list carries the biosphere-sentence choice.

### Still his

The biosphere sentence (above), and everything unchanged from the third
section.

## Fifth — the provenance of the drone ruling: Banks' Culture, and Star Wars

Dermot, verbatim, after the fourth section was recorded:

> I disliked the anti-gravity drones of Iain M. Banks Culture novels as well
> as the weak endings but did like his treatment of the advanced starship AI
> Minds

and, a few minutes later:

> The Star Wars robots are often too comical and childish for my liking

### Read as

Not rulings; influence notes, and the first the record has in his own words.
Four things, and they cut differently:

- **The Star Wars robots — too comical and childish.** The same objection as
  the Ewok line in the third section, turned on machines: a robot is not
  comic relief and not a toy, whatever tier is reading. It sits beside the
  drone ruling as its other half — the Culture's drones fail on *reflex*,
  the Star Wars droids fail on *register*. The record's own machines already
  hold the line (Rook-7 is scuffed and retrofitted, Lumbarian is built for
  the outside of things, neither is a joke), and the rule now has a name to
  check against: if a machine's scene would play as a gag, it is wrong for
  this record.

- **The anti-gravity drones — disliked.** This is where the fourth section's
  ruling comes from. It is a reaction against a specific reflex in a specific
  body of work: the floating, fussing, sentient machine that follows a
  protagonist about. The ruling's *very good reason* bound is the bound the
  Culture never applied.
- **The weak endings — disliked.** A craft note as much as a taste one. The
  chapters he has said he loved end on a quiet mutual recognition, a kept
  custom, or a plain-words aphorism, and land. The ending is not the place a
  chapter goes soft.
- **The starship AI Minds — liked.** The one element of that world he wants
  in the neighbourhood: large, capable, characterful made minds treated as
  persons. The record has the room for it and has not yet used it — the
  Kernel (*think freely; not act freely*), commissioned standing, the habitat
  collectives and Made Minds as a formtype are all in place, and no ship or
  habitat mind has yet been written as a character.

### Direction check

Consistent with everything ruled today and earlier: the trope default (the
hovering companion-drone and the comic droid are the refused patterns, named
now by their sources), the Pandoid rule of the third section (not cute, not a
mascot — and now, not a machine that plays for laughs either),
the tone guardrail (a Mind at Banks' scale, filtered through the Kernel, is
unsettling in the right way and never horror), and *what lands*. Nothing in
canon moves.

### What it opens, and does not decide

A large made mind as a person — a ship's, a habitat's — is now a direction
with his taste behind it, not a request. Nothing is queued. If he asks for
one, the Banks Minds are a fair reference for scale and personhood, and the
Kernel is the filter that makes such a mind this record's rather than his.

### Still his

Unchanged from the fourth section.

## Eighth — Star Trek: Data, and the aliens

Dermot, verbatim, continuing the influence notes of the fifth section:

> Also dislike the Star Trek TNG depiction of Lt. Cmdr. Data as either too
> emotional or clueless about human behavior. In general all of the Star Trek
> aliens are too stereotypical in behavior.

### Read as

Two more anti-influences, and both name the source of a rule the record
already runs on.

- **Data — neither too emotional nor clueless.** The objection is to a made
  mind written as a dial between two failure modes: a person who cannot read
  a room, or a person who wants to be human and overshoots. Neither is what a
  capable mind is like. This record's made minds are competent about people
  because competence is their job — Lucene closes a case from the chair
  beside the interview, Rook-7 is an investigative chassis, the Eden Warden
  keeps a welfare watch — and the Kernel gives them their real difference
  from a human, which is not feeling or its lack but the line between
  thinking freely and acting freely. A made mind's interiority in this record
  is a matter of what it may do, not of whether it has feelings it is
  puzzled by. Pairs with the fifth section: the Banks Minds are the model
  for scale and personhood; Data is the model for what to avoid on the way
  there.
- **The Star Trek aliens — too stereotypical in behaviour.** The planet of
  hats: one species, one temperament, one job, no internal variety. The
  record already names this as a trap (`species-design.md`, *Traps specific
  to this setting*) and builds against it (the cast principle against
  monoculture; *use them for texture, not for temperament*). The mind-design
  questions exist so that a people's difference comes from its sensorium,
  its medium of thought and its history — what it is certain of, what it
  cannot know, what was never scarce — and not from a single trait worn
  like a hat. The Told are the current worked example: they argue, they
  differ, and the argument is settled by who was there, which is a custom
  and not a temperament.

### Direction check

Consistent with the cast principles (no stereotypes; strength and
vulnerability orthogonal; antagonists misguided, never pure villains), the
species and mind design notes, and the Kernel. Nothing in canon moves. The
notes give two standing rules their sources, which is useful when a draft is
being checked: *is this Data?* and *is this a hat?* are now questions with
his name on them.

### Still his

Unchanged from the seventh section.
