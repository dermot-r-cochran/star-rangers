## First — "No branch is military": what the Rangers are, branch by branch

Dermot, verbatim:

> Star Rangers, except the Provost Corps  are not military police, and
> except the Frontier are not police. No branch is military.

### Read as

Three clauses, one institution, read by the plain-meaning convention (the
double space after *Provost Corps* is a typo and carries nothing):

1. **The Star Rangers are not military police, except the Provost Corps.**
   The Provost Corps is the one branch that does the work a military police
   does for an army: it polices its own service — internal investigation,
   counter-intelligence, protective security, the authority to investigate
   a Ranger and to restrict one. Nothing else in the Corps holds that
   function.
2. **The Star Rangers are not police, except the Frontier Corps.** The
   Frontier Corps is the one branch that polices anyone *outside* the
   Rangers — and only as caretaker, by a settlement compact's standing
   invitation, on a world with no law of its own, until that world's first
   sworn officer ends the mandate. Nothing else in the Corps polices at
   all.
3. **No branch is military.** Not the Provost Corps, whose function is the
   military-police function but whose service is not an army; not the
   Frontier Corps, whose posture is non-combatant peacekeeping; not the
   command ladder, whose upper ranks sound naval because their
   institutional ancestors were vessel commanders. The Rangers are neither
   an army nor a navy, hold no warfighting mandate, and answer to civilian
   oversight outside any military chain.

The reading that makes the first and third clauses agree: **"military
police" names a function, not a status.** The Provost Corps does what
military police do — polices the service it belongs to, with powers of
investigation and restriction and none of conviction — for a service that
is not military. That is how the record already had it (*"the closest Solar
System analogue is military police and intelligence work — investigation
and protection, answerable to due process — not combat arms"*), and this
direction confirms the analogy while ruling out the status.

Two names, both already in canon: *Provost Corps* is the specialist corps
(listed among the corps on the rank page and at the Academy); *Provost
track* is its certification track, the one a Chief Provost climbs. The
direction uses the corps' name, and the pages use both; nothing to migrate.

### Canon check

Nothing in the corpus contradicts any clause, and each was already stated
somewhere; the direction gathers three scattered lines into one rule.

- **The 2723 charter** (`src/lore/formation-of-star-rangers.md`): *"They
  do not govern, legislate, police, or administer"*; no jurisdiction on
  planets or large habitats; entry by invitation. Independent of *"any
  military command hierarchy"* (quoted on the rank page).
- **Rank and the Chain of Command**
  (`src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md`): *"The Rangers are neither
  an army nor a navy. They field no offensive capability, hold no
  warfighting mandate"*; the naval vocabulary explained as borrowed
  discipline, never mission; the Provost track's military-police analogue
  and its limit (*"the authority to investigate and to restrict; the
  authority to convict or to fight a war was never on the charter's table"*).
- **The Frontier Corps** (`src/lore/star-rangers-frontier-corps.md`):
  *"the police force of frontier worlds that do not yet have a local
  government or law enforcement of any kind"*; the caretaker clause as
  *"the principle's own completion"*, not an exception carved out of it;
  *"Provosts police the Rangers. The Frontier Corps polices, temporarily
  and by invitation, for colonies that cannot yet police themselves"*;
  non-combatant posture, *"no military formations"*.
- **The Safety Corps** (`src/lore/star-rangers-safety-corps.md`) holds
  investigative authority over incidents and certifies hulls; it holds
  *"nothing outside the Star Rangers"* — it cannot summon, compel or
  arrest. Not police. Consistent.
- **The Imperium** (`src/lore/the-imperium.md`): the Rangers *"stand
  institutionally farthest from its model: independent, civilian-overseen,
  and explicitly accountable to a structure outside military command."*
- **The chapters**: civil police are always someone else's — Albercombe's
  bureau, Oyelaran's, Five-O on the Governor's authority (S02, S04); the
  charter debate in S00E03C01 writes the mandate as *"No governance, no
  policing, no side in anyone's dispute."* Consistent.
- **The uniforms note** (`story-bible/uniforms-and-insignia.md`) already
  keeps three uniformed services apart — Garda, Highland Guard, Rangers —
  *"none of whom may do each other's job"*, and the Rangers' uniform was
  designed to reject the MSC's military display culture. Consistent.

Where a reader could still go wrong, and what the direction closes: the
ranks (*Line Captain*, *Star Captain*, *High Captain*) invite the naval
reading; the corps' name invites the frontier-lawman reading refused on
8 September; and the Provost track's *military police* analogue, read
without its qualifier, invites the inference that the service it polices is
military. The rule now says all three inferences are wrong, in one place.

### Direction check

- **Clarification** of the 2723 charter's non-political mandate and of
  *Rank and the Chain of Command*'s *"neither an army nor a navy"* — says
  in one rule, branch by branch, what those pages say across several
  paragraphs. Nothing reversed, narrowed or widened.
- **Consistent with the Frontier Corps entry** as published, which already
  framed the caretaker mandate as the charter's *"do not police"* completed
  rather than breached; the direction names the Frontier as the sole
  exception, which is what that page claims.
- **Composes with 8 September's** *no guns-and-spaceships, no space
  western* and with the shadow-shaped mysteries rule: a corps that is not
  military and not police is exactly the corps that arrives with an
  instrument, a page and a question. The 8 September ladder — habitat
  bureau, Five-O, Rangers by invitation — stands: the bureaus and Five-O
  are the police in that ladder, and the Rangers are the tier that is not.
- **Not a change to the Liaison Corps analogy** (28 July: *"like police
  officers reassigned from dangerous front line work"*). That was a
  comparison about career shape, not a claim that the Corps is police, and
  it stands as a comparison.

### Realized

- `src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md` — a paragraph added to *The
  Provost Track, and What the Rangers Are Not* stating the rule in its
  three clauses, with the function-not-status reading of *military police*
  written out.
- `src/lore/star-rangers-frontier-corps.md` — the *Provosts police the
  Rangers* paragraph extended by one sentence: these two are the whole of
  the Corps' police function, and neither is military.
- `CLAUDE.md` — a bullet beside the *no guns-and-spaceships* rule, since it
  governs the same register in prose and image prompts.
- `CHANGELOG.md` — one entry under *[Unreleased] › Changed*.

### Still his

- ~~**The reading of *military police* as function, not status**~~ —
  **ruled the same day, fifth section: *Option (a) for the Provost
  Corps*.** Function, not status; the paragraph stands as written.

## Second — "Compare with military and police roles in the BBC drama Vigil"

Dermot, verbatim, after the first section was recorded and the PR opened:

> Compare with military and police roles in the BBC drama Vigil.

### The comparandum

*Vigil* (BBC One, 2021; a second series in 2023) puts a Police Scotland
detective, DCI Amy Silva, aboard a Royal Navy ballistic-missile submarine on
patrol to investigate a death in the crew, while her sergeant works the case
ashore. The first series' engine is the seam between two institutions,
and every turn of it is a turn of that seam:

- **The civilian detective can enter but cannot command.** She is aboard by
  the Navy's leave, the captain runs the boat, and the patrol — the
  continuous deterrent — outranks her inquiry: the submarine will not
  surface, break patrol or signal for her.
- **The military has its own discipline and its own police, and is not
  trusted to use them.** A submarine carries a coxswain responsible for the
  crew's discipline, and the service has its own police and its own
  chain for a death aboard; the premise is that a service investigating
  itself under national-security cover is exactly the investigation nobody
  outside it can trust, so a civilian is sent in.
- **Secrecy is an actor.** The Ministry, the security service and an
  admiral each have reasons for the truth not to reach a civilian court,
  and the drama's pressure comes from the institution's secrets more than
  from the culprit.
- **The confined setting cannot pause.** A boat that cannot come up is a
  closed room with a watch to keep; the case has to be worked inside the
  institution's own routine.

The second series moves the same seam to an air base and a foreign
partner's territory — a detective inside a military and diplomatic
structure that outranks her — and adds the remote-weapon question the
first did not have.

### Against the record

Set the record beside it and every element of the seam is present, and
most of them run the other way.

- **The Rangers are not military, so the shield is missing.** *Vigil*'s
  Navy can close ranks behind a warfighting mandate and a state secret.
  The Rangers hold no warfighting mandate and no state; their charter puts
  their oversight in a civilian council outside any command chain, their
  Safety Corps publishes incident findings *"not subject to review by
  Star Rangers command"*, and the deterrent-patrol argument — the mission
  outranks the inquiry — has no Ranger equivalent. A boundary post keeps
  its watch, as a boat keeps its patrol; but nobody may invoke the watch
  against a question.
- **The Rangers investigate their own, and the record says that is
  credible.** *Vigil*'s premise is that a service cannot credibly police
  itself. The rank page's premise is the opposite: what the Rangers kept
  from naval discipline was *"a Provost function that investigates its
  own without needing an external authority to do it credibly"*. The
  record earns that claim by what the Provost track is not: it holds the
  authority to investigate and to restrict and never the authority to
  convict, and its findings meet the same publication rule as the Safety
  Corps'. So the *Vigil* shape — the outsider detective needed because the
  insiders cannot be trusted — is not this record's shape. Where an
  outsider comes aboard a Ranger vessel it is for jurisdiction, not for
  credibility.
- **Jurisdiction runs the other way.** In *Vigil* the state's police hold
  jurisdiction over a death aboard the state's own vessel and the service
  resists it. In the record the Rangers hold jurisdiction nowhere a
  polity does — no standing on a planet or a self-governing habitat, entry
  by invitation, departure on its withdrawal — so a Ranger ashore is under
  the habitat's law and the habitat's bureau, with the Provost track
  having no more standing there than the Rangers have. The friction *Vigil*
  gets from a navy refusing a detective, the record gets from a detective
  who cannot follow a case past a habitat line (Five-O's founding gap) and
  from a Ranger who cannot enter uninvited.
- **The one place the *Vigil* seam is real here** is a Ranger vessel or
  post that is the Rangers' own ground with a polity's people aboard, or
  a polity's ground with a Ranger vessel docked at it: a death or an
  offence there is the one case where a habitat bureau and the Provost
  track could both claim the file. The record does not settle it (see
  *Still his*).
- **The confined institution that cannot pause transfers whole.** A
  boundary post with a watch to keep, an outsider aboard who can ask but
  not command, a routine the case must be worked inside — that is a
  Sapphire & Steel room as much as a *Vigil* one, and it is the register
  the 8 September rulings want.
- **What does not transfer is the plot.** *Vigil* is a murder mystery with
  sabotage and a foreign agent behind it. The 8 September rule refuses the
  murder mystery and the trope default refuses the spy; a *Vigil*-shaped
  chapter in this record is a death or a disappearance at a post that
  turns out to be a shadow-shaped thing seen through a gap, worked by an
  outsider who was invited in and a Provost officer who did not need her,
  and closed marked rather than solved.

### Direction check

Not a ruling; a comparison he asked for. It is **consistent** with the
first section: *Vigil*'s whole engine is a military service's relation to
civil police, and the record has neither a military service nor a Ranger
police, so the comparison mostly measures a distance. It is consistent
with the 8 September rulings on register and trope. Nothing in canon moves.

### Still his

- **Who holds the file when the *Vigil* seam is real** — a death or an
  offence aboard a Ranger vessel docked at, or a Ranger post inside, a
  self-governing habitat. Three shapes: **(a)** the vessel is Ranger
  ground wherever it is, the Provost track investigates, and the habitat's
  bureau comes aboard by the captain's invitation — the charter's
  invitation rule mirrored, which is what makes it the recommended shape;
  **(b)** docked means inside the habitat, the bureau holds the file, and
  the Provost track assists as the habitat's guest; **(c)** a standing
  joint instrument, negotiated per habitat, so the answer differs by
  polity and the chapters can use the difference. Recommended: (a).

## Third — "Compare Orbital Five-O with French Gendarmes"

Dermot, verbatim, in the same message:

> Compare Orbital Five-O with French Gendarmes

### The comparandum

France polices with two national services side by side. The **Police
nationale** is civilian, under the Ministry of the Interior, and holds the
cities and larger towns. The **Gendarmerie nationale** is a branch of the
armed forces — its members are soldiers with military status, living in
barracks with their families — placed under the Interior Ministry for its
policing work since 2009 while keeping its military status and its military
missions, and it holds the countryside and the small towns: most of the
territory, about half the population. Both hold judicial-police powers,
and in both an investigation is directed by a prosecutor or an
investigating magistrate rather than by the service's own chain. The
Gendarmerie also carries the specialist formations a civilian police does
not — mobile squadrons for public order, an intervention group, the
Republican Guard, maritime and air and transport arms. Beneath both, most
communes keep a small **police municipale** of their own. The dual model is
a Napoleonic export and has cousins in Italy's Carabinieri, Spain's Guardia
Civil and the Dutch Marechaussee.

### Against the record

Orbital Five-O is the Governor's Investigative Task Force: commissioned by
Governor Petra Voss, tasked by her directly, reporting administratively
through the Chief Commissioner's office, covering the Compact's five
self-governing habitats and *"not one metre further"*, and existing for
one gap — a case that crosses a habitat line, which no habitat's own
Detective Superintendent can finish. Beneath it, each habitat's bureau;
above it, the Rangers, by invitation only. Beside the Gendarmerie:

- **The position matches.** Both are a second police body layered over the
  local civil police, answering to a different chain, and both hold the
  space *between* — the Gendarmerie the country between the cities, Five-O
  the lines between the habitats. The 8 September ladder — bureau, Five-O,
  Rangers by invitation — is the French three-rung ladder with its top
  rung replaced by something that is not police at all.
- **The dual chain matches.** The Gendarmerie answers to the Interior
  Ministry for its policing and to the armed forces for its status; Five-O
  answers to the Governor for its taskings and to the Chief Commissioner
  administratively. In both, the split is what lets the body move faster
  than the consultative layer it bypasses — and in both it is where the
  institutional friction lives (the Compact thread is *"the gap between a
  Governor's tasking authority and a Chief Commissioner's coordination
  office"*).
- **The friction matches.** France has a phrase for the rivalry between its
  two services, and the record has Albercombe *"not conceding it was
  joint"* in an hour of joint work. A bureau that sends a case up and a
  task force that takes it over are always going to argue about the
  moment of handover, and both systems keep that argument alive on
  purpose rather than merging the services.
- **The status does not match, and this is the load-bearing difference.**
  The Gendarmerie is military; that is the whole reason France has two
  services. Five-O is a task force of a civil administration, commanded
  by a former Survey Corps officer who was never a soldier either, and
  nothing in the Compact's chain is armed-forces. The first section's rule
  is about the Rangers, but its spirit reaches here: the record has no
  military policing anywhere on the ladder, and the Gendarmerie's
  defining feature is the one thing Five-O must not borrow.
- **The mode does not match.** The Gendarmerie is territorial — it holds
  its ground as first responder, walks a beat, lives in the villages it
  polices. Five-O holds no deck of its own; it is jurisdictional by
  *offence*, taking only the cases that cross a line, and every deck it
  works on is some bureau's. A gendarme is where you go; Five-O is who
  arrives when the case has left where you are.
- **Direction of an investigation is unsettled in the record.** In France a
  prosecutor directs the judicial police, so a gendarme and a police
  officer on one case answer to the same magistrate — which is how the
  system survives having two services. The record has warrants (Albercombe
  needs one *"naming a door his own habitat's registry insisted did not
  exist"*) and has never said who issues them or who directs a Five-O
  inquiry once the Governor has tasked it. That is a marked absence, not a
  defect: the chapters have not needed it yet.
- **The uniform question is open.** Gendarmes are always uniformed except
  in their research sections; Eden's bureau is uniformed with the
  obligation thinning going up (*The Eden Bureau Uniform*); Kai Larsen's
  portrait wears a working flight suit. Whether Five-O is a uniformed
  service, and whose rules it follows if so, is not on the record.

### Direction check

Not a ruling; a comparison he asked for. **Consistent** with the 8
September twenty-fourth section (Five-O as the intermediate tier), which
the Gendarmerie comparison confirms structurally, and with the first
section's rule, which the comparison marks the one thing not to borrow.
Nothing in canon moves.

### Still his

- **What, if anything, to borrow.** Three shapes: **(a)** nothing —
  Five-O stays a civil, offence-defined task force with no ground of its
  own, and the comparison is filed as a comparison (recommended: it is
  what canon says and it keeps the ladder free of military policing);
  **(b)** the territorial half — Five-O becomes first responder for the
  ground between the habitats, the transit corridors and the docks no
  bureau holds, an extension that would give it a beat; **(c)** the
  status half — Five-O with a service status of its own beside the civil
  administration, which would be a re-grounding of the Compact page and
  cuts against the first section's spirit; not recommended.
- **Whether Five-O is uniformed**, put as two shapes: **(a)** yes, on the
  Eden bureau's public-facing reasoning, with the same thinning going up
  (recommended, since it is a police body read by residents at their own
  doors); **(b)** no, since it arrives on other bureaus' decks and works
  beside them — in which case the flight suit in Larsen's portrait is
  working dress and not a service uniform.
- **Who directs a Five-O inquiry and who issues a Compact warrant** —
  kept as a marked absence until a chapter needs it, and then his.

## Fourth — "Star Rangers have uniforms for the same reason as hospital staff do"

Dermot, verbatim, in the same message:

> Star Rangers have uniforms for the same reason as hospital staff do,
> except there are no plain clothes ranks or roles. Aliens have uniform
> equivalents

### Read as

Three sentences, and the first names the reason the uniform page has been
circling since it was written.

1. **The hospital reason.** A hospital puts its people in uniform so that
   anyone in the building — a colleague from another department, a
   patient, a stranger in a corridor at three in the morning — can tell at
   a glance what a person is there to do and can hand them the right thing
   without asking; so that the garment is built for the work and shed at
   the door; and so that what it announces is a function, not a claim on
   anyone's obedience. That is a uniform read for *role* by *colleagues*,
   which is exactly what the rank page and the uniform page already say
   the Rangers' is (*"what a colleague across a corridor actually needs at
   a glance and cannot look up in time — rank, and certification"*) and
   exactly what *The Eden Bureau Uniform* contrasts it with (a police
   uniform is read by the public for what its wearer is entitled to do).
   The direction names the analogy the two pages were reaching for, and
   it lands on the right side of the first section: a hospital's uniform
   is the least military and least police garment there is.
2. **No plain-clothes ranks or roles.** The exception is the point. A
   hospital keeps some of its people in ordinary clothes — the consultant
   on the ward round, the administrator, the chaplain — and Eden's bureau
   does the same in its own way (the obligation thins going up; the
   undercover officer is exempt; Commissioners need not wear it). The
   Rangers keep nobody in ordinary clothes: a Ranger on duty is in uniform
   in every rank and every role, Novice to Triumvirate, the Provost track
   included, the Liaison Corps included. The only Ranger out of uniform is
   a Ranger off duty, which *Shore Leave and the Neutrality Constraint*
   already treats as a state and not a role. Two things follow. **The
   Corps has no undercover officers** — a Ranger who arrives is visibly a
   Ranger before a word is said, which is what the invitation rule needs:
   a service that could enter unseen would hollow out *enter by
   invitation*. And **the uniform never thins going up**: exempting the
   senior is the habit of a service whose uniform signals rank to the
   public, and this one signals function to colleagues, which a Line
   Captain needs read as much as a Cadet does.
3. **Aliens have uniform equivalents.** Two readings, both taken. The
   narrow one is already canon: alien Rangers wear the Rangers' uniform,
   which is a modular shell over a liner precisely so that it fits Krenyi,
   Pelagene, Veyr and Serephine bodies and bolts to Rook-7's hull. The
   wide one is new and is what the sentence says: other peoples, in their
   own institutions, have their own equivalents of a uniform — the mark a
   stranger in a working place reads to know who does what — and the
   medium is each people's own: cloth and a tab for a human, and for
   another people perhaps a colour, a sound, a posture, a mark on the
   body. What is universal is the job the mark does, not its form. The
   record describes almost none of them yet, and the Told chapters should
   be read before anyone claims one for the Told.

### Canon check

- Consistent with `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md` throughout: the
  uniform *"rebuilt from the certification up rather than the hierarchy
  down"*, marks that are shapes and not words so they read across a
  language nobody shares, no name on the garment, dress uniform differing
  in finish and not in insignia.
- Consistent with `src/lore/eden-bureau-uniform.md`, whose *Why Not the
  Ranger Answer* section is this direction seen from the other service:
  *"the garment that withholds is correct for one service and wrong for
  the other. The Rangers are a body that arrives; the bureau is a body
  that lives there."*
- Consistent with the corpus on plain clothes: no chapter or page has a
  Ranger working undercover or out of uniform on duty. The undercover work
  on Eden belongs to Tamsin Reyes (the bureau) and to the Undercover Pets
  Detective Agency (not the Rangers). The one phrase that could mislead —
  the Liaison Corps page binding a representative *"exactly as they bound
  her in uniform"*, which reads as though a representative is out of it —
  is clarified below.
- Consistent with the image conventions: every Ranger portrait on file is
  in uniform or off duty, and the 5 September regeneration of Tissadelle's
  portrait was run precisely to get the uniform right.

### Direction check

- **Clarification** of the uniform page (says the reason the page already
  runs on, in one analogy) and **extension** on two points it had not
  stated: that there are no plain-clothes ranks or roles, and that the
  other peoples have equivalents in their own media. Nothing reversed.
- **Composes with the first section**: a hospital's uniform is the
  clearest non-military, non-police garment there is, and a corps with no
  undercover arm is a corps that cannot be mistaken for a police one.
- **Composes with the Eden bureau page** rather than contradicting it:
  the two services answer different questions, and the direction sharpens
  the Rangers' answer without touching the bureau's.

### Realized

- `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md` — a new section, *Why a Uniform
  at All*, between *The Uniform* and *Corps and Branch Badges*: the
  hospital reason, the no-plain-clothes rule with its two consequences,
  and the other peoples' equivalents stated generally with the absence

- `src/lore/planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters.md` — *"exactly as they
  bound her in uniform"* clarified to *"on active service"*, since a
  representative acting as one is in uniform and the old phrase read
  otherwise.
- `story-bible/species-design.md` — a section adding the uniform
  equivalent to the questions that decide a people, with the medium put

- `story-bible/uniforms-and-insignia.md` — the hospital reason and the
  no-plain-clothes rule added to the Rangers' governing idea, as a prompt

- `CLAUDE.md` — a bullet on uniforms beside the images convention;
  `CHANGELOG.md` — one entry.

### Still his

- **The medium of each people's uniform equivalent** — put as shapes in
  `species-design.md`, none asserted.
- **The Liaison Corps reading** — that a Planetary Representative acting

## Sixth — "The Wrath of the Dragons happened because the Star Rangers could not physically force a rival organisation to respect the safety of meta-dimensional fauna"

Dermot, verbatim, in the same message (*Srar* read as *Star*):

> The Wrath of the Dragons happened because the Srar Rangers could not
> physically force a rival organisation to respect the safety of
> meta-dimensional fauna

### Read as

A cause, stated at the level of institutions — and the level is the whole
point. The record holds three readings of what *did it* and adjudicates
none; this says why it could happen at all, and it is compatible with all
three, because each of them starts from the same fact: a licensed concern
held an aperture open for four months off Dock Seven, rendering Class III
fauna into stabiliser, and nobody stopped it.

- **The rival organisation is the Hyperfold Yield Combine.** The one
  operator on the record working meta-dimensional fauna, and *rival* in
  the precise sense the Combine page already gives: the Rangers are the de
  facto regulator of fold-adjacent operations, the regulation is voluntary,
  and the Combine was the concern that declined it and was content to be
  conspicuous. A rival not for territory or trade but for the one thing
  the Rangers actually hold, which is the standard.
- **Could not physically force.** The Combine page has *"could not have
  been made to"* and *"the only body competent to judge it had no power
  over it"*, and leaves why unstated. The direction states it, and it is
  the first section's rule applied: a corps with no warfighting mandate,
  no police function outside the Frontier's caretaker worlds, and no
  jurisdiction in open space beyond a formal finding has no means of
  compulsion, and is built not to have one. What it had was a finding a
  named officer could make, and she did not come back.
- **The safety of meta-dimensional fauna.** The motive the Rangers had and
  could not act on. The Combine page already has the boundary analyst
  saying the fauna had no standing and instructing her junior not to
  soften it, and has the finding as one made *"on behalf of animals with
  none"*; the direction confirms that the fauna's safety was what the
  Rangers wanted respected, standing or no standing, and that wanting it
  was not enough.

### Read as

Not a ruling — *might be* — and the *although* is the useful half. The
fourth section's rule is *no plain-clothes ranks or roles*; the Liaison

- **Galen holds a Corps posting.** *Ranger Liaison Officer, Tír na nÓg
  Gateway*: one Ranger permanently stationed at the Union's shuttle gate,
  whose job is the handoff into and out of Corps jurisdiction. That is a
  Ranger on duty at a Ranger post, and her page has her in *"a plain grey

  uniform argument holds either way.)

officer at a Corps posting is on duty and in uniform like anyone else.**
