### Readings taken, flagged for confirmation

- **Bumble is a bumblebee.** The name is the plain-meaning reading in an
  English children's register, and the corpus's two other readings were
  weighed and set aside below (a dog; a small robot). It composes with three
  things already on the record: Drithane's *agriculture runs under glass*,
  which is where bumblebee colonies work in the real world; the planet's
  *cold, dry, and high* air, which is where bumblebees out-fly every other
  bee; and the portfolio's own two bumblebee frames, which under the
  3 September ruling (*anything a camera can photograph comes from Dermot's
  own frames*) make her the first character in the record whose portrait is
  a photograph of his rather than a generated image or a card. **Confirm the
  species.**
- **She lives in Gleann na gCaorach, on Drithane, in the wool-shed.** The
  valley is the pets thread's second home (S02E03–E04), Sorcha Nic Dhiarmada
  reads animals for a living and the chapters already give her a wool basket
  by the stove; a shed for the wool is the smallest possible addition. Eden
  was considered and set aside: nothing in canon has Eden growing anything,
  so a bee there would first need a grow deck, a larger assertion than the
  bee. **Confirm the placement.**
- **The name is the valley's, rendered.** *Bumbóg* is the Irish for a
  bumblebee; the page says the record renders it *Bumble* the way it renders
  everything else said in an Irish-speaking valley — the translation default
  the 7 August pronoun rule names, applied to a name. The Zhulik and Teddy
  precedent: a name is a fact about the namers, and the page says so on its
  first line. **Confirm that Bumble is the rendering and Bumbóg the valley's
  word, or rule that the valley says *Bumble* itself.**
- **"A character" read as one name for one nest's queen, whichever queen it
  is.** A bumblebee queen founds one nest and lives one season; the page
  makes that its subject rather than hiding it — *there has been one Bumble
  for as long as there has been a wool-shed, and there have been a great
  many* — on the Zhulik pattern (*on a habitat with one cat, every cat is
  that cat*). This is the reading the draft turns on and the one most likely
  to be wrong about what was meant. **Confirm, or choose one queen; see the
  second question below.**
- **Status *Active*.** Under the 11 September vocabulary, *alive and present
  in the story's present*. True of the line at every moment and of no
  individual queen for long; the page's reading makes it honest.
- **The tone rule.** A queen's season ending is on the page in one clause,
  said plainly and gently, in the register the pets thread's chapters use for
  a young reader. Nothing dies on the page. **Confirm that the lifecycle may
  be stated.**

### Canon check

Nothing in the corpus is contradicted; the page extends four settled things.

- **Drithane** (`src/lore/planets/drithane.md`): *cold, dry, and high — a
  thin but breathable-with-acclimatisation atmosphere*; *agriculture runs
  under glass*; exports *wool, preserved goods, precision cold-climate
  botanicals*; *Irish remains the world's working language*. The image alt
  already shows glasshouses. A bumblebee is the one bee that pollinates
  under those conditions, and the page's physiology is the real thing:
  bumblebees warm the thorax by working the flight muscles before flight and
  forage near freezing; bumblebees have flown in a chamber at air pressures
  equivalent to above the summit of Everest; honeybee colonies cluster below
  about ten degrees; red clover needs a long-tongued bee, which is why
  bumblebees were shipped from England to New Zealand in 1885 CE
  (2085 UCSD, on the *dates are absolute* rule); glasshouse bumblebee
  colonies are boxed commercial stock replaced on a schedule; bumblebees
  leave scent footprints on visited flowers that later bees read and avoid,
  fading at roughly the flower's refill rate; bumblebees sense floral
  electric fields through body hairs; carder-group bumblebees comb fibre
  over a surface nest. No new physics; nothing above the Material layer.
- **The valley** (S02E03C01–C03, S02E04C01): Sorcha's holding, her
  collies, the wool basket by the stove, the Survey delegation's crossing
  visit, the instrumented spring-2827 crossing with Thrum, and the survey

- **The crossing-interval trap** (`open-questions.md`, Drithane's orbit):
  the page commits to no interval, counts no crossings, and speaks of

- **Muffin.** One sentence, marked as an absence: the valley's file does not

## Second, same day — two craft questions

Dermot, verbatim:

> Two questions. Should I study the work of other photographers and writers
> or just develop my own unique style? Second should I include characters
> and settlements from other people groups or cultures even ones I am less
> familiar with?

Both were answered as false choices, and he agreed (*Agreed.*). Recorded
here because both answers name practices the record already runs on, so
that they can be argued with rather than operate tacitly.

### Study others, and keep making the work at volume

A style is not designed; it is what remains after enough work that its
habits can be seen. `prose-style.md` and the portfolio's `STYLE.md` are the
evidence: both were derived by reading the corpus, not prescribed to it.
Studying other people's work does not dilute that. It gives names to what is
already being done and to what is being avoided. The record's own borrowing
shows the method: Sapphire & Steel's register taken without its horror
(8 September), UNIFIL as the shape of the Federation's force (10 September),
the Bramble Storybooks as the in-world children's book. Take the mechanism
and refuse the surface. Read for mechanism, ask what the work refuses to do,
and close the book before writing. What kills a voice is imitating surfaces;
what starves one is never seeing how anyone else solved the problem. The
same holds for the photography: look widely, notice what is flinched at and
what is envied, and shoot enough that the envy becomes a choice. The
withholding treatment is already a position; study sharpens it.

### Write other peoples and cultures, including the less familiar ones

A record with one culture in it is a smaller lie than one with many, and
still a lie about a Concord of worlds. The question is method, not
permission, and the method is mostly already in use:

- **Write from the record's limits.** The Pandoid pages claim only what the
  survey observed, and *Teddy* is marked as the party's word. The same
  discipline applies to human cultures: a narrator who knows the edge of
  their knowledge is honest in a way an omniscient one cannot be.
- **Explain the epistemology, never invent the fact** (15 August). For a
  real culture: do not invent customs, rituals or beliefs and attribute
  them. Draw on what is documented, or let the culture's own voice carry it
  through the Codex, valid for its author. Kate Ukrainka's domovyk is a
  real folkloric figure written by a character from that diaspora; that is
  the safe shape.
- **Let people name themselves.** *Bumbóg* rendered as *Bumble*, the Told
  over *Pandoid*, Achebe's heritage stated in the prompt rather than left to
  a generator's default. Endonyms and the translation default are the tool.
- **Refuse the trope default there too** (4 and 8 September). With an
  unfamiliar culture the stock image arrives first, exactly as it does with
  guns-and-spaceships; the rule against it applies to a people as much as
  to a plot.
- **Get one reader from the culture before it binds.** For anything beyond
  a name and a dish, one informed reader catches more than any amount of
  research; the draft-and-stop tier exists for this kind of decision.

The opposite failure is the one to avoid: a Concord where everyone is Irish
or Ukrainian because those are the two that can be vouched for. The genre's
claim is that the future is wide, and the register built here —
observational, marked absences, names as facts about the namers — was made
for writing what is not fully known without pretending it is.

### Ruled — the same day

Dermot, verbatim:

> Agreed. No relevant informed readers yet but I will listen for comments.

So, as standing practice: both answers stand as written above. The
informed-reader step is **open until one exists**; until then the
observational register and marked absences carry the load, since a page that
claims only what its narrator could know has little to retract, and the
Codex gives a later correction somewhere to land — a new voice filed as
valid for its author, never a retcon of a lore page. The comment boards
(giscus, on the sciencefiction and church-space discussion repos) are the
likeliest channel. **A reader's comment that touches a culture the record is
unsure of is treated as an intake**: quoted verbatim and dated, read against
what was already decided, and ruled, so the reading and the ruling are on
record. The ask to record this here, verbatim: *Record this in the story
bible*.

## Third, same day — what a good comment could become

Dermot, verbatim, after the informed-reader ruling above:

> A well written comment could almost be used as codex with attribution or
> else treated as fanfiction.
>
> Did I have a nicer word for fanfiction?

### Answered from the record

**The word is *chartered work***, settled 26 July 2026 and written into
`CONTENT-LICENSE.md` (*Chartered Works*) and `story-bible-summary.md`'s
canon-status table: a derivative work by another hand that Dermot has
individually approved — above a fan work, below canon, governed by one rule,
*canon binds it; it does not bind canon*. Plain fan work keeps the licence's
own term, *fan work*; the About page's heading still reads *Fan fiction*.

The licence's ladder (`CONTENT-LICENSE.md`, the status table) already has
the two rungs a comment would climb: a fan work is **in circulation** —
known, not authenticated, not suppressed; a chartered work is **deposited**
— filed by another hand, registered here, kept as filed, attributed to its
author and not owned by this one.

**A comment cannot simply be filed as Codex with attribution.** Chartered
works are *registered, not hosted* (settled 26 July): another person's text
placed under `src/` would sit inside a CC BY-NC-ND scope Dermot cannot
license, and a Codex entry lives under `src/`. So the three shapes for a
strong reader comment are:

- **(a) Leave it in circulation** and link it as *referenced, not endorsed*
  — the standing rule, no grant needed, and referencing never makes a work
  chartered.
- **(b) Charter it** — an individual grant from Dermot and from its author;
  registered, pointing to wherever the author keeps it (the comment board
  included); the first entry on the chartered-works register, which becomes
  a published page when a first chartered work exists. This is the shape the
  policy was written for, and none exists yet. *Recommended if one arrives.*
- **(c) Bring it into the Codex** only with the commenter's explicit licence
  of the text to Dermot, and only if it passes the Codex's own tests: a
  named in-universe author it could plausibly have come from (`author` is
  required), and canon no less settled after reading than before
  (21 August).

### Direction check

A **clarification**: nothing new is settled. The chartered-work status, the
register-not-host rule, the referenced-not-endorsed rule and the Codex's two
tests were each already on the record; this section joins them to the case
of a reader comment, which none of them had named. It composes with the
second section above — a comment that corrects a culture the record is
unsure of is an intake first (verbatim, dated, ruled), and only then, if it
is also a work in its own right, a candidate for one of the three shapes.

Recorded at his ask, verbatim: *Record this in the story bible*.

## Fourth, same day — screen adaptations are chartered, never canon

Dermot, verbatim:

> Any TV or Video Streaming or Cinematic adaptation would be treated as
> Chartered but never as Canon.

### Read as

A ceiling on status, stated in advance for a kind of work that does not yet
exist: an adaptation of the work for television, a streaming service or
cinema is, at most, a **chartered work** — endorsed, registered, bound by
canon and binding nothing — and cannot become canon, whatever its scale,
whoever makes it, and however closely Dermot is involved in it. The
published text stays the only canon.

### Canon check

The chartered-work status already carried most of this: *a chartered work is
endorsed but not canon*, *canon binds it; it does not bind canon*, and the
worked list of forms — *a story, a comic, a game, a recording* — was open
to a screen adaptation by its shape. What the direction adds is the ceiling.
The 26 July policy said what a chartered work *is*; it did not say that
nothing derivative could ever rise above it, and a screen adaptation is the
one form where the pull toward "the film is canon now" is strongest. The
ruling closes that: the screen never becomes the record, the record never
adjusts to the screen, and an adaptation the published story later
contradicts is superseded rather than followed.

Two consequences already implied by the licence and now stated with it:

- **Permission is separate from standing.** A screen adaptation is
  commercial, so the non-commercial fan-works exception does not reach it and
  a real licence is required; that licence would grant the right to adapt
  and, at most, chartered standing, never canon force.
- **Registered, not hosted.** Like every chartered work, an adaptation would
  be listed on the register and would live with its own rights holder;
  nothing of it enters `src/`.

### Confirmed, in his words

Dermot, verbatim, on what the ruling means:

> This means that the show runner and director would have creative license
> but no influence on my own work

Read as the two directions of the rung, stated once each. **Their side:**
creative licence within the individual grant — a showrunner or director may
add, restructure, recast and invent, bounded only by consistency with the
published story as it stood when the grant was made; where the published
story later contradicts what they made, theirs is superseded rather than
followed. **His side:** no obligation flows back — the record never adjusts
to the screen, whether to a casting, a redesign or a plot the production
needed. One nuance recorded so it is not misread later: the rule stops the
*obligation*, not the *choice*. If he ever took something from an adaptation
into the published story, that would be his own act of writing canon, not
the adaptation binding it.

### Direction check

A **clarification** of the 26 July chartered-work status — the rule that
canon binds a chartered work and a chartered work binds nothing is applied
to the largest form it could take — and an **extension** in one respect:
the status is now also a ceiling, which the July policy had not said in so
many words. Nothing reversed. Composes with the third section above (a
reader comment climbs the same ladder and stops at the same rung) and with
the register-not-host rule.
