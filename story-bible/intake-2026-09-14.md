## The children's About page

Dermot, verbatim, first:

> Under Cover Pets no longer has a contact or comments section of any kind
> even for critics, parents, teachers and guardians - should there be a
> redirect to the main site?

then, before an answer:

> undercoves-pets.com, child reading tier, page-by-page comments not shown
> but should there be something for parents in the About page?

and:

> should there be a different About page for sites and editions within the
> child reading tier?

**What was found.** undercover-pets.com/about/ served `src/about/index.md`
verbatim: the author's job title, Eleventy and npm, Copilot and Sudowrite,
three engineering repositories, fan fiction on Archive of Our Own and Wattpad,
and a live link to the GitHub Discussions board — the comment system this tier
switched off on 4 September (participation, not protection). The footer's
*fan work* line pointed at the same page on every edition. No email address
anywhere on the tier, and none on the main site's About either, so the only
route to the author from any domain was a GitHub account.

**The three recommendations, and his answer** — verbatim, *Yes, agreed*:

1. **Not a redirect.** A redirect sends a child off the site with no
   explanation, and the main site's About is not a contact page either; it
   would move the problem rather than solve it. The one sentence an adult
   needs belongs on the page they are already on.
2. **A "For grown-ups" block at the foot of the About page**: who wrote the
   story, that it is the same world as the main site, why discussion is off
   here, and where an adult can reach the author.
3. **One About per tier, not one per edition.** The registry's own rule is
   that editions carry framing, never facts, and the About page is framing;
   but the two children's doors differ by page set, not by who is reading,
   so they want the same words with only the site name swapped. Two copies
   would be two things to keep true.

**Realized** as `about` on `CHILDREN_TIER` in `lib/editions.js` — the first
framing a tier constant has carried, because the tier *is* the register, and
a third children's edition inherits it by the same spread that gives it the
tier's filters — validated like the Reading Plan (`validateAbout`, with unit
tests), rendered by `src/about/index.md` in place of the site-wide page. Four
plain paragraphs for the child (who made it, what the pictures are, that the
bigger world is on the grown-ups' site, and that there is nowhere to comment
here so tell a grown-up), then the grown-ups block. The main site is named in
words and not linked in the child-facing paragraphs, on the 2 September
ruling that a child cannot judge the adult site; the grown-ups block carries
the page's only links. The footer's *fan work* sentence, whose anchor exists
only on the site-wide About, no longer renders on a tier with its own.

## The Grok safari games

Dermot, verbatim, on the same message as the agreement above:

> should I include links to the children's version of my photo tutorial at
> photo-safari.grok.me and photo-tutorial.grok.me both of which are more
> visual but much more simplistic than my tutorial repo

**What was found.** Both pages load without sign-in and carry no ads:
*Sundrift* (photo-safari.grok.me, "Field unit — East Range") and *Longwatch*
(photo-tutorial.grok.me, "Field station — Mara to the Zambezi"). Both carry
the host's *Created with Grok / Remix* bar at the top, and Remix opens an
account-gated AI product — on the one domain where comments are off because
a child cannot hold the account, the same problem by a different door. Their
hero art is generated (a painted savanna, a lion on a rock), not his
photographs, and nothing on those pages says what made it.

**The recommendation, and his answer** — verbatim, *Yes agreed*: link them,
in the grown-ups block rather than the child-facing part, worded for the
adult: two short games the author made with Grok, on a third-party host, with
a bar at the top that is not part of the game and pictures that are
generated. The parent decides; the child's own paragraphs stay inside sites
he controls. Realized in the third grown-ups paragraph.

The audience argument runs the other way from the tutorial repository's, where
the same Grok link was removed on 14 September as *more visual but too
simplistic* for a novice adult. For a child that is a fit, not a fault, and
the two decisions stand together.
