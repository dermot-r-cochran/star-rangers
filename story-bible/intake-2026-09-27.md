## The legend of Aoife the slave girl, champion and prophet — a direction

Verbatim:

> The legend of how a young slave girl Aoife becomes a levrilic champion and
> prophet, joining the secret Fellowship of Light.

### One line

A legend of Saint Aoife in which she is a slave, is chosen by a Levril as a
Champion, speaks as a prophet, and is taken into a Fellowship of Light that
keeps itself hidden.

### Measured: what the record holds

Aoife is canon, and closely bound. Four pages and a timeline entry, all
consistent, plus five chapter canon facts:

- **Character page** (`src/characters/saint-aoife.md`): born c. 1186 AD,
  *parentage and townland of birth not preserved*; came to the thorn well at
  Cill Aoife in her fourteenth year; took no husband and *accepted no dowry
  offered her*; the vision in her thirtieth year, c. 1216; three offers to
  enter a religious house refused; died winter 1241 AD; a saint by local usage
  and no formal process.
- **The *Life*** (`src/codex/life-of-saint-aoife.md`, Brother Daire, 1216 AD):
  *daughter of no house the record troubles itself to name*; the vision in her
  permitted words; *shown, not healed*; Daire was not the one she confided in
  most, and says so; the 2819 annotation reads the vision against Levril
  taxonomy without settling it.
- **The reader-school *Legend*** (`src/codex/the-legend-of-saint-aoife.md`,
  Dermot's direction of 12 September, PR #787): a Union teller's telling for
  the young-adult tier, ruled to assert nothing new about the world.
- **Lore page** (`src/lore/saint-aoife.md`): the two readings, Muirenn and
  Levril, which *Union cultural authorities have not resolved* and have *shown
  little institutional appetite* for resolving.
- **Canon facts that bind.** S06E01C03: the Union's scholars *read* the
  vision as an early, unrecognised Levril encounter. S07E01C01, twice: *what
  Aoife met, and whether it held any Cascade standing at all, has never been
  settled*, and the Dock Seven warm-edge signature matches the well. S07E01C02:
  the *shown*/*healed* distinction, and the Shepherd holding's discipline of
  letting two things be true.
- **The Fellowship** (`src/lore/fellowship-of-light.md`): an ancient,
  universal order, *open by constitution*, pre-modern and pre-scientific,
  whose comparative archive's *oldest holdings are Old-Earth scriptural
  accounts, carried off the planet as living observance* (`arilon.md`). A
  contemplative chapter, the river abbey on the Boyne near Dún Rí, is kept by a
  Brother Daire (S01E00C00 canon fact), and the abbey already has a leaf in the
  Codex (`two-in-the-one-currach.md`). Whether that Daire is the scribe of 1216
  is the open *Dún Rí question*, which the record declines to close.
- **Champions and prophets.** `champions-heroes.md`: a Champion is a sentient
  material being *chosen by Levrils* on constraint literacy and causal
  relevance, recognised and not manufactured. `arilon.md`: Arilon-pattern
  accounts are *the recorded footprint of Champions*; a Champion who looked
  like the people visited *is written down as a person: a stranger, a prophet,
  an outsider who spoke*; for a human community on Old Earth the pattern
  predicts a human Champion. Ruled 23 September: *Prophet is not a tier. The
  Arilon page files it as one culture's word for a Champion.*
- **Slavery.** The word appears in canon only of the Krenyi, who *cannot be
  enslaved* (S01E01C01). Nothing in the record says whether Aoife was free.
  The *Life*'s *daughter of no house* and *parentage not preserved* leave it
  open; *took no dowry offered her* implies standing at the time of the
  offer, not at fourteen.
- **The arc note** (`tissadelle-arc-s6-7.md`, 24 July): the protouniverse's
  Aoife is a rhyme, not an answer; the thorn-well question *still leaves
  Levril vs. something-higher open*. Nothing here touches it.

### Readings, flagged

Each is the interpretation the draft proceeds on. All six **confirmed the
same day** in the ruling below.

1. ***Legend* read as a telling, which is Codex work.** As on 12 September.
   A legend is what a tradition says; the Codex is where a named source's
   account lives, valid for its author. **Confirm.**
2. ***Slave girl* read as bondage in the legend's own word.** The telling
   uses the old law's *cumal*, a woman in bondage and the price of three
   cows, and has her paid over for a debt at fourteen and freed at the water
   before the dowry is offered, which is what lets the *Life*'s dowry line
   stand. The keeper's margin notes that bondage of that kind was dying in her
   century. **Confirm.**
3. ***Levrilic champion* read in the Cascade's sense**: recognised, not
   given anything; the house's word *chosen*; the Rangers' word *unsettled*;
   both kept. The telling does not say *Levril*. The keeper's margin says the
   house does not place what came in the Cascade either. **Confirm.**
4. ***Prophet* read in the Arilon sense**: one who says the thing the place
   already knows and the people set over it will not say. Not a foreteller.
   Her second saying (*it will come again where there is such a one, and not
   otherwise, and not to order*) is a statement of pattern, not of event, so
   it neither predicts Dock Seven nor rules it out. **Confirm.**
5. ***Secret* read as hidden in her century**, a house that could not say
   aloud what it kept vigil for, and not as a secret society in the record's
   present, where the Fellowship is open by constitution. **Confirm.**
6. ***Joining* read as the house entering her among its watchers**, with no
   ceremony: she takes the vigil every Muirenn's eve until she dies. The
   telling makes this the meaning of her third refusal (*I am already in a
   house. You cannot see it from the road*). **Confirm.**

### What was drafted

`src/codex/the-champion-at-the-thorn-well.md`, author *Traditional — the
river abbey on the Boyne, near Dún Rí*, read aloud on the eve of Aoife's
feast, with a keeper's margin signed *D.* and an Archive note. Its shape is
the Arilon pattern told from inside one house: an outsider arrives (sold),
is read and found already fit (the Champion rule), says what authority will
not (the three sayings), is opposed (the three askers), dies before the
vindication (the Union's scholars, fourteen centuries on). Its ending test:
the girl who was given to the well as property turns out to have been the
well's keeper from the day she was sold, and the only thing the vision
changed was who knew — which re-lights the opening.

The two-books device answers, from inside the story, why the *Life* carries
none of this: Daire wrote for a church that could read him, the house wrote
for itself, and the house has kept both. That is the 15 August convention
(explain the gap in-story) used on the gap this direction itself opens.

Two words of the Union's Irish, taught by context: *cumal* and *curadh an
tobair*; one more, *fáidh*, glossed in the same sentence. Dates absolute,
AD with UCSD beside. Tone: unsettling, not horror; the whip is named once
and nothing is shown.

**What it commits the story to.** Nothing in canon: a Codex entry is one
source's account. Three small in-universe facts it does assert by existing:
that the river abbey keeps a book read on the eve of Aoife's feast; that the
Ceridwen Archive shelves a copy beside the *Life* and the reader-school
*Legend*; and that the Fellowship's comparative archive files it among the
Arilon comparanda. One liberty of the teller's: the third asker *wanted a
saint on his land the way he wanted deer in his park*, which uses the
century the lore page's *Her Century* section supplies and puts no deer at
the well.

Also changed: a paragraph on `src/lore/saint-aoife.md` naming the abbey's
telling as a third that stands beside the two readings without joining
either, and a See Also link on the Fellowship page. Both are extensions.

### Validity for its author

A Fellowship house would say *chosen* and mean the Cascade's Champion
without saying *Levril*, because the Fellowship keeps its own vocabulary for
what the surveyors have since measured (the Overfold rule). It would keep
the surveyors' *unsettled* beside its own word and argue with neither, which
is the first of the Two Refusals. It would file its own certainty as one
more Arilon comparandum, since that is the archive's standing discipline
(*the shape is not a verdict*). It would withhold the name of the one who
kept the book, on Sen's rule. And it would read the whole on the eve and
tell you not to cite it. Canon is no less settled after reading than before.

### An inconsistency noticed, not changed

a separate change.

### Choices put

**1. The standing of the four claims.**

- **(a) A Codex telling, as drafted** — *recommended*. The S07E01C01 canon
  fact binds: the record cannot say in its own voice that a Levril chose her
  without changing a published chapter's canon facts, and the 25 August rule
  allows canon to change only by extension, clarification or genuine
  dilemma.
- **(b) Enter some of the claims into the lore and character pages.**
  *Slave* is an extension the *Life* leaves room for. *Joined a Fellowship
  house* is an extension too, but as the record's own statement it would
  need the record to place a Fellowship house in thirteenth-century Ireland,
  which no page does today. *Secret* is a genuine dilemma with *open by
  constitution*, resolvable as *hidden then, open now* on the Fellowship
  page if wanted. *Champion* is listed and not offered: it contradicts the
  canon fact above.

**2. The teller.**

- **(a) The river abbey's book, near Dún Rí** — *recommended, and drafted*:
  the Fellowship is what she joins, the abbey already has a Codex leaf, and
  the keeper's margin lets the Fellowship's own discipline mark the telling.
- **(b) A Currach Fleet ship-telling**, the Union's folk version — livelier,
  but it has no reason to know the Fellowship from inside.
- **(c) A comparative-archive note** in Sister Ione Marrec's hand — the
  right voice for the margin, the wrong one for a legend.

**3. The lore page.**

- **(a) One paragraph naming the third telling, as drafted** — *lean*: the
  lore page already names the two readings, and a reader should find the
  third from there.
- **(b) Codex only**, the lore page untouched.

**4. The image.**

- **(a) None, as drafted** — the reader-school *Legend* carries none.
- **(b) A designed title card** on the *Life*'s pattern, by
  `make-codex-cover.ps1`, which is his to run.
