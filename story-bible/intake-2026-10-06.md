## Lot, then vote, in the Celtic Union — DRAFTED, readings OPEN

Four directions in one evening, on a design he had worked out the same
day outside the fiction (a chamber whose candidates are drawn by lot and
then elected as normal; the real-world note is `docs/lot-then-vote.md` in
`dermot-r-cochran/Voting`, with its decisions marked):

1. *"Consider all this in the context of the Celtic Union and local
   planetary governments within, except that clans replace parties"*
2. *"status of inter-clan parties of intra-clan factions to be determined"*
3. *"Celtic Union upper house seanad representing the six planets"*
4. *"each planet has a Taoiseach or Priomh Aire but the Union has an
   Uchtarain"*

**What the record already held.** The Union is deliberately weak at the
centre, with no Union-wide executive and a rotating council that mediates
and does not overrule (`src/lore/celtic-union-of-planets.md`, *Structure*).
The Uachtarán and the Seanad are ceremonial, and the page said in so many
words that *how the Seanad is seated* the record had not established, a
marked absence this direction fills by extension rather than reversal. Six
worlds hold charter membership, five of them documented (Tír na nÓg, Ynys
Wydrin, Aethelrock, Kernowek Reach, Drithane). The Union confers and
recognises no titles, on the reasoning that an institution which cannot
decorate anyone cannot build a court around itself, which is the same
reasoning the draw runs on. Clans are established on Aethelrock (five
landholding Ridgemoot clans and the landless Clan Avalon), in the Boirinn
Uplands of Tír na nÓg (the Shepherd clan, `src/characters/sorcha-shepherd.md`)
and in the Union's clan archives generally (`src/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate.md`).
Aethelrock *has no planetary executive*; Drithane *never produced a capital*
and its valley chains govern themselves.

**The design, in the Union's terms.** The real-world note's decisions carry
over almost unchanged, and one of them becomes simpler. *Prior affiliation
only* (a candidate may state the party they belonged to before the draw,
and parties may not nominate, endorse or spend) becomes, with clans in the
place of parties, the clan of birth or adoption as the registry recorded it
before the draw, which is not something a candidate can acquire for the
occasion. So the clause that was the design's main defence against capture
is, in the Union, a fact about how clans work. The renewal rules (one
automatic renewal, then a ticket back into the draw that halves each term,
staggered seating, a college of former members who brief the new) carry
over whole. The pilot-first logic (a council before a chamber) is the
Union's history read backwards: the draw is the Seanad's, and which worlds
also use it for their own assemblies is each world's own record.

**Realized** as:

- `src/glossary/the-seanad-draw.md`, *The Seanad Draw*, in Union Irish
  **Crannchur an tSeanaid** (*the lot of the Senate*): the method, the
  clan clause, the renewal rules, and the reasoning in the founders' own
  register.
- `src/lore/celtic-union-of-planets.md`: the Seanad paragraph now says the
  chamber is seated by the draw, world by world, with the same number of
  seats for each of the six; the *Structure* section gains the heads of
  government (direction 4); the absence that remains (the Uachtarán's
  succession and term) stays marked.
- The canonical guide: the Uachtarán/Seanad entry's notes updated, and a
  new entry for *Taoiseach / Príomh-Aire* and *Crannchur an tSeanaid*.
- `story-bible/open-questions.md`: direction 2 and the readings below.

**Readings flagged, with recommendations.** Each is his; the PR stops on
them under the merge-unless-open rule.

1. **Aethelrock and the head of government.** Direction 4 says every
   planet has a Taoiseach or Príomh-Aire; the Aethelrock page says the
   world has no planetary executive and the Union seat rotates among five
   clan heads. Three shapes:
   - (a) **Recommended.** The style attaches, for Union purposes only, to
     whichever clan head holds the world's Union seat under the Ridgemoot
     rotation; nothing on Aethelrock changes. Drafted this way, as a
     clarification.
   - (b) Aethelrock is the one exception and the Union page says so.
   - (c) Aethelrock gains a Taoiseach. A reversal of the Aethelrock page,
     not drafted.
2. **Taoiseach or Príomh-Aire: which world says which.** Drafted as the
   charter's Irish for an office each world names in its own language,
   with *Taoiseach* the usual style on the Irish-speaking worlds and
   *Príomh-Aire* where a world's constitution calls the office a first
   minister. The alternative is one style for all six.
3. **Seats per world.** Drafted as *the same number for each world*, the
   number not established. Alternatives: name a number (seven a world,
   forty-two in all, is the shape that reads well); or weight by
   population, which the direction's *representing the six planets* reads
   against.
4. **Which worlds seat their own assemblies by the draw.** Drafted as a
   marked absence (*each world's own record*). Lean, if he wants it fixed:
   Tír na nÓg and Drithane's valley chains yes; Aethelrock's Ridgemoot no,
   since it is a gathering of clan heads and not an elected body; Ynys
   Wydrin and Kernowek Reach open.
5. **Stratifying by clan.** Drafted as a quota in the draw where a world's
   registry records clans, so that the drawn names carry the world's clans
   in proportion. Aethelrock would insist; whether Tír na nÓg does is a
   smaller question the page leaves to the world.

**Direction 2, deliberately not drafted.** *Status of inter-clan parties or
intra-clan factions to be determined.* The glossary entry marks the
absence in the record's voice: whether senators of different clans combine
into standing associations, and whether a clan divides on the floor, the
record has not established. Three shapes, when he wants them:

- (i) **Neither exists in law and both exist in fact**, unrecognised, like
  the titles: senators combine as they please, the record names no
  grouping, and the draw's clan clause is the only clan fact the Seanad
  keeps. Lean.
- (ii) Inter-clan associations are permitted and registered; intra-clan
  factions are a clan's own affair and invisible to the Union.
- (iii) Both are barred from the floor by the charter, which would make the
  Seanad the one Union body that polices anything about its members, and
  reads against the weak centre.

The crossing with the Voting repository is of the *Three Disciplines*
kind, deliberate and invisible to the reader: no real-world name appears

## Compare the Celtic Union with the European Union — ASSESSED, FILED

Verbatim: *"compare Celtic Union with European Union"*. Answered in the
session; filed here because the comparison is a design instrument and the
lore may never name the EU (the Archive names nobody from our century;
the Journal may, if he wants an entry).

**Where they rhyme.** A rotating presidency of the Council of the EU
against the Union's rotating council; heads of government as the real
seat of power (the European Council; the Taoisigh at the rotating council);
a language policy that is constitutional rather than incidental (24
official languages; the charter's Celtic working languages); no titles,
no court; a defensive clause (Article 42.7; the charter's mutual defence).

**Where they diverge, and it is the whole design.** The EU has law above
its members, a Commission with the right of initiative, a court whose
rulings bind, and a directly elected Parliament that acquired real power
over fifty years precisely because it was the one body the people chose.
The Union has none of the first three on purpose, and its directly chosen
body is the one with no power at all. The Union is the EU before
Maastricht at most, closer to an old defensive confederation with a
customs-and-navigation agreement, six members with no accession queue and
no exit clause on record.

**The seed the comparison plants.** Legitimacy flows to whoever the
people chose. The Parliament's history is the proof. A Seanad drawn from
the people and elected by them is the most legitimate body the Union has,
and the charter gave it nothing to do; over generations that is a pressure,
and a season could turn on the first time the Seanad is asked to decide
something because no other Union body can claim to speak for the people.
Not drafted; noted as the kind of institutional enigma the record likes,
a weak centre whose honest face slowly becomes its strongest claim.
