## The POV label: the bracket, the rank, and the type — RULED

**Dermot's first line, verbatim:** *"Usage of text in brackets after character name in POV pages"*.

What was found: a chapter's `povs[].label` is free text rendered verbatim in seven places (the "View from" buttons, the POV block header, and the scene-POV page's title, H1, breadcrumb, "Also in this scene" list and meta description), and nothing had ever said what the bracket holds. Across 94 chapters the 85 distinct labels carried species, augmentation, posting and time in four orders, with dashes beside commas, role sometimes before the bracket and sometimes in it, and Elvira with no bracket in five chapters.

**Three shapes, put to him:** (1) write the convention down and sweep; (2) derive species from the character page and compose the label in the renderer; (3) leave the labels and shorten only the titles.

**Ruled, his *Option 1*: the convention and the sweep** — merged as PR #1005. The shape: `<rank as held in this chapter> <name> (<frame>[, <one qualifier>])`, frame first with the augmentation the record marks, at most one qualifier, commas only, nothing after the name outside the bracket; `validate-content.js` warns on a label outside it. Recorded in `lib/content-schema.js` and the story bible summary's front-matter table. Elvira carries `(Unclassified)`, her own page's word, and that was named in the PR as the one bracket worth his glance; he has not asked for another word.

**His second line, verbatim:** *"Should the character name be prefixed by their current rank or role as in Line Captain Tissadelle Shepherd, and if so should it be in italics, or any part of the actual name in bold."*

The reading put to him: keep the rank, because it is time-bearing (Tissadelle carries eight forms across the corpus and the label is where a reader sees when in her career a scene sits), it matches the prose's own first mentions, and it is the uniform rule applied to the page. Against the typography: the block header is already uppercase, bold and letter-spaced, so italic has no form there and bold is already on every letter; the label is one string, and rank cannot be told from name without a rank vocabulary that the labels' *Dr.*, *Sir*, *Brother*, *Detective*, *Delegate*, *Governor* and *Administrator-Voice* would outrun; and a running head sets a name in one weight.

**Three shapes:** (1) leave it plain; (2) mute the bracket only, parsed from the trailing parenthesis, on the H1, breadcrumb and buttons; (3) full three-part styling on structured front matter.

**Ruled, his *Option 1*: the rank stays before the name as held in that chapter, and the label is set plain** — no italic on the rank, no bold on any part of the name, no muting of the bracket. Nothing built. Not to be re-proposed without a reader-facing reason the labels themselves do not supply.
