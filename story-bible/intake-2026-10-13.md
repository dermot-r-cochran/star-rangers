## Health in the 29th century — RECORDED; both readings RULED (*All recommendations accepted*)

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> Characters in 29th century have freedom from most medical conditions and
> ailments known in the 21st century, prosthetics rarely look like prosthetics
> and genetic disorders can often be treated and cured during pregnancy.
> Fitness is still a matter of personal training and discipline but obesity is
> no longer a risk.

**Direction check.** An extension. Nothing published says otherwise, and three
pages already lean this way without saying it: *What the Record Refuses* puts a
plain settlement's technology "in the roof's certification, the settlement's
medicine"; the Mnemari page names disease among the ordinary ways any body stops
working, which "most" leaves room for; and teleportation's
[recognised clinical condition](../src/lore/teleportation-limitations.md) shows
the century has conditions of its own that ours did not. The direction removes
ours, not illness as such.

**Canon check against the
[Heritable Modification Protocols](../src/lore/heritable-modification-protocols.md).**
That page builds its whole regime on one line: a **somatic** change ends with
the body carrying it and is treated as a matter of that one patient's safety; a
**heritable** change is closed to novel design, and restoration is the one
narrow road open. *Treated and cured during pregnancy* is read here as
**somatic treatment of the unborn child**: the child is cured, and their own
children may inherit the condition and be treated in their turn. That is the
reading that sits inside the protocols without touching them. *Flagged for
Dermot:* the other reading, that a known disorder is corrected in the line itself
on the protocols' restoration road, is also compatible, and is a stronger claim;
it is his to choose, and nothing is drafted that depends on either.

**What follows, without a new page yet.**

- **Prose and lore** do not show 21st-century chronic illness, obesity, or a
  visible congenital condition as the ordinary state of a 29th-century body.
  Injury, age, exhaustion, the century's own conditions and *most* (not all)
  still happen.
- **Bodies differ by training, work, age and heritage**, not by disease: a
  broad, heavy miner (Fergus Aonghas) and a lean pilot are both fit. Fitness
  is personal discipline, so a soft, unathletic build is allowed; obesity as a
  health risk is not.
- **Prosthetics rarely look like prosthetics.** A replaced limb or eye reads as
  the body's own; the exception is a choice or a story, as the cyber-revenant's
  one silver seam already is under the tone rule.
- **Image prompts** follow the same lines (`images.md`, *Conventions*).

**Nanobots and microdrones.** Dermot, later the same day, verbatim:

> We don't have nanobots in the sense of Andromeda Ascendant but advanced
> surgical microdrones do exist.

*Direction check:* a clarification. Three published pages already carry
*cosmetic nanites* doing *invisible upkeep* (`environmental-technology-design.md`,
`crisis-centuries-terran-wildlife.md`, `planets/tir-na-nog.md`). Read as
recommended: those stand, because *in the sense of* excludes a kind, not the
word. What the record has no place for is the *Andromeda* kind: self-replicating
swarms that rebuild a ship or a body, repair anything, and answer any problem.
Medicine works through **surgical microdrones**: built, counted, guided devices
doing bounded work inside a body, and leaving it. *Flagged for Dermot:* the
other reading, that *nanite* should leave the record altogether and the three
pages be revised to drones, is his; it would be a change to published lore, so
it waits on his word (`open-questions.md`).

A lore or glossary entry stating this in the Archive's voice is possible later
(a definition, so Opus's, not Fable's); not drafted until a page needs it.

  trail are not the only signs of 2826; each new place should find its own.

## Dates: the offset, the New Year, the Union's CE and the adoption year — RULED (*All recommendations accepted*)

Dermot asked whether he should say *27th century* for 2826 UCSD onward, then
whether the UCSD offset is exactly 200 or drifts, whether CE would diverge if
still kept, whether there was an overlap, and what the convention is for dates
before about 2126 CE. Answered from `src/glossary/ucsd.md` and
`src/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate.md`, with three readings and a convention
note put as choices; his reply, verbatim: *"All recommendations accepted."*

- **Century.** *The 29th century* stays the record's name for its present: it
  is the 29th in UCSD, the 27th in CE. Where a sentence sets it beside our own
  time, the other century is labelled CE or the gap given as six centuries.
  Recorded in `CLAUDE.md`'s dates bullet.
- **The offset is exactly 200, on every day.** The UCSD year's *midwinter* turn
  is the season's name for the old common-era New Year, not the solstice, so
  no ten-day seam opens each December. Recorded in the glossary entry. (What
  could drift, if CE were still kept by the sun, is the day's length against
  atomic time: minutes, never a year.)
- **The Union's CE is the common CE.** The clan archives reconciled the arks'
  own reckoning to the Solar System's count on renewed contact, so their CE is
  a second label, not a second clock. Recorded on the Count and Calibration
  page. The overlap continues: the Union and New London still print CE.
- **Adoption is the Concord's founding**, under the Tycho Accords, 2543 UCSD
  (2343 CE), which fixes the line the 6 September date rule left undefined.
  Earlier dates are written CE first with the UCSD year beside them; the sweep
  re-keyed 28 dates in 13 files (the Navigation and Trade Accords of 2137 CE,
  the USC's founding about 2140 CE, the coastal fragmentation of the 2130s and
  2140s CE, the Titan Water Rights dispute of 2289 CE, the Coherence Wars from
  2303 CE, New London's construction in the 2180s CE). The *about 2126 CE*
  in his question is not the line: the Concord did not yet exist then.

**Direction check.** Clarifications: no event moved, every date names the year
it named, and the notation now follows the rule already in force.

**Left open, put to him:** *Civilisation Comparison*'s three columns (13th,
21st and 29th century) and the Saint Aoife lines that set *a
thirteenth-century holy woman* beside *a twenty-ninth-century Cascade
taxonomist* mix CE and UCSD in one comparison; see `open-questions.md`.

## New London's measures, and why two hundred — RULED and drafted (*Yes, Option A and please draft the codex*)

- **Measures, Option A**, realized on `src/lore/new-london-space-habitat.md`

  pounds, yards and miles on the promenade), the Solar System's standard units

  beside the standard one in records, never instead. *Direction check:* an
  extension of the page's own principle; nothing published said otherwise.
- **Codex, drafted:** `src/codex/why-two-hundred.md`, a working paper by Sen in
  the *Standing Claims* series, grading three accounts: the USC misreading
  (refuted; the timeline page already says the Archive corrects it in print,
  and this is that correction), the Fragmentation folk reading (sole, the span

  desk writes the era). The commission's actual reason is a marked absence.
  Canon left settled: the rule, and that the zero means nothing. A working
  paper, so Opus's under the model rule.

## The Cerebraun view of humans — RECORDED and drafted (direction of 11 October, captured here)

Dermot's direction, 11 October 2026, verbatim:

> The Cerebraun see humans as the descendants of violent warlike vicious savage
> predators who moderated gradually once they achieve sentience and became
> aware of higher powers, albeit imperfectly, originally still attributing
> violent behaviors to their 'gods' and dieties.

*achieve* read as *achieved*, *dieties* as *deities*, by the plain-meaning
convention. Captured two days late; no intake of the 11th carries it.

**One line.** The Cerebraun hold an account of humans, and it is theirs: a
predatory ancestry, a late and slow moderation, an imperfect awareness of
higher powers, and an early habit of filing their own violence upward.

**What the record held before.** Three things about the Cerebraun, nothing
about what they make of humans. `src/lore/cerebraun.md`: nine centres and one
voice; governance as *a direct extension of their own neurology*; the Hegemony
stated *by the species' own account*, which is the attribution device this
direction needs. `src/lore/cerebraun-hegemony.md`: a force of its own,
Meridian administered over recorded objections, relations with the Rangers
correct not warm. `src/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`: the Hegemony's
rendering of the rank ladder, filed *attributed and revisable*, with the
record's rule that *a rendering is a courtesy* and never settles anything.
`src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`, *A Likeness Belongs to Whoever Sees
It*: *the other peoples' comparisons are just as local*, filed the same way as
human ones and weighing no more and no less. No page carries a Cerebraun
account of the Cascade, of their own past, or of any other people, and none
is added.

**Canon check.**

- *Humans not privileged* (28 September): a human culture is one case among
  the cases. A Cerebraun account of humans is therefore ordinary material for
  the record, on the same terms as the Chthonari's or Verdani's comparisons
  the taxonomy page already licenses.
- *War is never assumed of a people* (`what-the-record-refuses.md`, *War as a
  default institution*): humans hold war as an institution, and the record
  says so (the Coherence Wars, forty years, three billion dead). The Cerebraun
  reading of that history as ancestral is their reading; the record's own is
  that a people arrives at war from its world. The two do not collide because
  the record files the one and asserts the other.
- *The ladder is refused* (same page, *And the ladder itself is refused*):
  the account's shape, savage then sentient then aware, is a ladder, and the
  record holds that a people's past is a shape and never a height. The draft
  says so beside the account, as a note of where it is partial, without
  calling the account wrong.
- *Gods who answer, refused outright*; *no word religion in the record's
  voice* (9 October): *gods and deities* appears only as the account's own
  phrase, and the record's voice says *devotional traditions* and *the early
  traditions*. The record adjudicates nothing about what any tradition
  attributed; it says those traditions have their own accounts and ranks
  none.
- *Cast principles*: the Cerebraun are misguided, not villains. The draft
  derives the account from their neurology, a history read as a nervous
  system settling, and gives them the inside of it: a limb's behaviour taken
  for the voice's instruction is the confusion they know best, and their
  physicians already treat a limb acting against the central brain as
  ordinary (the species page's own bullet).
- *Tone*: the four hard words are quoted as the Hegemony's and the register
  stays calm; nothing is depicted.
- *No real ideology named.* None is.

**Direction check.** An extension. It adds a people's view to a published
species page and alters nothing that stood; the attribution device is the one
the ladder page and the taxonomy page already use. It sits beside the humans-
not-privileged rule, the war-not-assumed rule and the ladder refusal, and
re-grounds none of them.

**Where it lives, and why.** Two homes were weighed. (A) A section of
`src/lore/cerebraun.md`, *What the Cerebraun Make of Humans*: the Archive
reporting what the Cerebraun hold, attributed, not endorsed, with the record's
note of what the account leaves out and the human mirror beside it. (B) A
Codex entry by a named Cerebraun author, which under the 10 October ruling
needs a character page in the same PR. **(A) chosen**, settled by Fable under
*Who approves whom*, for three reasons: the direction is a species-wide view,
which a single author's paper cannot carry without the reader wondering whose
opinion it is; the record already files other peoples' accounts in lore on
exactly these terms, so no new device is needed; and (B) would put a new named
Cerebraun character into canon on nobody's instruction, which is his decision
and would stop the PR. (B) stays open as a later addition if he wants the
account in a Cerebraun voice, and Sohrel is not the author for it, since a
doctrinal account of another people is not a document Sohrel's three
centuries of procedurally safe drafting would produce.

**What it commits the story to.** That the Hegemony holds this account in its
diplomatic register; that the record has met no Cerebraun who put it
differently on the first channel, and has no transcript of the second; that
the account's shape is the Cerebraun body's; and that the record keeps the
account beside the human decade of filing three-part answers as evasion,
adopting neither. It commits nothing about human prehistory, nothing about
any devotional tradition, and nothing about the Cerebraun's own relation to
the Cascade, which stays unasked.

**Readings flagged.**

- *Species-wide, held as doctrine* rather than one faction's or one era's.
  **Confirm** — the draft hedges it to the first channel.
- *The record notes the account's partiality without ruling on it.*
  **Confirm** — the three notes (the Hegemony's own force and Meridian, the
  ladder, the Krenyi's name for the move) are each filed *beside* and the
  record applies none of them.
