# Intake — 2026-10-13

## Health in the 29th century — RECORDED; both readings RULED (*All recommendations accepted*)

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> Characters in 29th century have freedom from most medical conditions and
> ailments known in the 21st century, prosthetics rarely look like prosthetics
> and genetic disorders can often be treated and cured during pregnancy.
> Fitness is still a matter of personal training and discipline but obesity is
> no longer a risk.

**Direction check.** An extension. Nothing published says otherwise, and three
pages already lean this way without saying it: *What the Record Refuses* puts a
plain settlement's technology "in the roof's certification, the settlement's
medicine"; the Mnemari page names disease among the ordinary ways any body stops
working, which "most" leaves room for; and teleportation's
[recognised clinical condition](../src/lore/teleportation-limitations.md) shows
the century has conditions of its own that ours did not. The direction removes
ours, not illness as such.

**Canon check against the
[Heritable Modification Protocols](../src/lore/heritable-modification-protocols.md).**
That page builds its whole regime on one line: a **somatic** change ends with
the body carrying it and is treated as a matter of that one patient's safety; a
**heritable** change is closed to novel design, and restoration is the one
narrow road open. *Treated and cured during pregnancy* is read here as
**somatic treatment of the unborn child**: the child is cured, and their own
children may inherit the condition and be treated in their turn. That is the
reading that sits inside the protocols without touching them. *Flagged for
Dermot:* the other reading, that a known disorder is corrected in the line itself
on the protocols' restoration road, is also compatible, and is a stronger claim;
it is his to choose, and nothing is drafted that depends on either.

**What follows, without a new page yet.**

- **Prose and lore** do not show 21st-century chronic illness, obesity, or a
  visible congenital condition as the ordinary state of a 29th-century body.
  Injury, age, exhaustion, the century's own conditions and *most* (not all)
  still happen.
- **Bodies differ by training, work, age and heritage**, not by disease: a
  broad, heavy miner (Fergus Aonghas) and a lean pilot are both fit. Fitness
  is personal discipline, so a soft, unathletic build is allowed; obesity as a
  health risk is not.
- **Prosthetics rarely look like prosthetics.** A replaced limb or eye reads as
  the body's own; the exception is a choice or a story, as the cyber-revenant's
  one silver seam already is under the tone rule.
- **Image prompts** follow the same lines (`images.md`, *Conventions*).

**Nanobots and microdrones.** Dermot, later the same day, verbatim:

> We don't have nanobots in the sense of Andromeda Ascendant but advanced
> surgical microdrones do exist.

*Direction check:* a clarification. Three published pages already carry
*cosmetic nanites* doing *invisible upkeep* (`environmental-technology-design.md`,
`crisis-centuries-terran-wildlife.md`, `planets/tir-na-nog.md`). Read as
recommended: those stand, because *in the sense of* excludes a kind, not the
word. What the record has no place for is the *Andromeda* kind: self-replicating
swarms that rebuild a ship or a body, repair anything, and answer any problem.
Medicine works through **surgical microdrones**: built, counted, guided devices
doing bounded work inside a body, and leaving it. *Flagged for Dermot:* the
other reading, that *nanite* should leave the record altogether and the three
pages be revised to drones, is his; it would be a change to published lore, so
it waits on his word (`open-questions.md`).

A lore or glossary entry stating this in the Archive's voice is possible later
(a definition, so Opus's, not Fable's); not drafted until a page needs it.

## Diversity of future characters and settlements — RECORDED

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> consider diversity of future characters, and diversity of human settlements
> and outposts yet to be written

Given the same day as his rulings that every character look unique and that the
site's images be consistent with each other (`images.md`, *Conventions*).

**Direction check.** An extension, forward-looking: it asks nothing of a
published page. It sits beside the cast principles already held (no
stereotypes; antagonists misguided, not villains) and the record's refusal to
treat any one human culture as the baseline (the 28 September usage rule).

**What it asks of new work.**

- **People.** A new character's heritage, age, build, sex and look are chosen,
  not defaulted, and chosen against the cast already standing, so the record
  keeps a spread rather than drifting toward one face (the image pipeline's
  default, which the 13 October identity repair met in thirteen portraits). A
  page that gives a heritage is followed; a page that gives none still gets a
  deliberate choice in its prompt, recorded beside the prompt so it can be
  changed.
- **Settlements and outposts.** Not every human place is a rotating habitat
  with green terraces or a frontier yard of rounded composite modules, which
  are the two looks the century batches leaned on. New places vary in climate,
  ground, building tradition, density, wealth of materials, the culture that
  settled them and how long they have stood, within the standing rules: places
  are well kept, the century shows, no space western.
- **The century's hints vary too.** A habitat window and an ascent javelin's
  trail are not the only signs of 2826; each new place should find its own.

## Cleaner settlements — RECORDED

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> 29th century settlements are generally cleaner and more environmentally
> friendly than in the 21st century even when the technology is more
> advanced, or maybe because the technology is more advanced

**Direction check.** An extension of *Places are well kept* (11 October), from
rooms and hulls to whole settlements, and consistent with published lore:
*Environmental Technology Design* has technology *subordinate to the organic
setting*, and *What the Record Refuses* puts a plain settlement's technology in
its certification and medicine. The *maybe because* is taken as the record's
reading: the cleanliness is what the technology buys, so a clean frame is not a
low-technology one. Recorded under *Conventions* in `images.md`; prose follows
the same line. Nothing drafted.

## Dates: the offset, the New Year, the Union's CE and the adoption year — RULED (*All recommendations accepted*)

Dermot asked whether he should say *27th century* for 2826 UCSD onward, then
whether the UCSD offset is exactly 200 or drifts, whether CE would diverge if
still kept, whether there was an overlap, and what the convention is for dates
before about 2126 CE. Answered from `src/glossary/ucsd.md` and
`src/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate.md`, with three readings and a convention
note put as choices; his reply, verbatim: *"All recommendations accepted."*

- **Century.** *The 29th century* stays the record's name for its present: it
  is the 29th in UCSD, the 27th in CE. Where a sentence sets it beside our own
  time, the other century is labelled CE or the gap given as six centuries.
  Recorded in `CLAUDE.md`'s dates bullet.
- **The offset is exactly 200, on every day.** The UCSD year's *midwinter* turn
  is the season's name for the old common-era New Year, not the solstice, so
  no ten-day seam opens each December. Recorded in the glossary entry. (What
  could drift, if CE were still kept by the sun, is the day's length against
  atomic time: minutes, never a year.)
- **The Union's CE is the common CE.** The clan archives reconciled the arks'
  own reckoning to the Solar System's count on renewed contact, so their CE is
  a second label, not a second clock. Recorded on the Count and Calibration
  page. The overlap continues: the Union and New London still print CE.
- **Adoption is the Concord's founding**, under the Tycho Accords, 2543 UCSD
  (2343 CE), which fixes the line the 6 September date rule left undefined.
  Earlier dates are written CE first with the UCSD year beside them; the sweep
  re-keyed 28 dates in 13 files (the Navigation and Trade Accords of 2137 CE,
  the USC's founding about 2140 CE, the coastal fragmentation of the 2130s and
  2140s CE, the Titan Water Rights dispute of 2289 CE, the Coherence Wars from
  2303 CE, New London's construction in the 2180s CE). The *about 2126 CE*
  in his question is not the line: the Concord did not yet exist then.

**Direction check.** Clarifications: no event moved, every date names the year
it named, and the notation now follows the rule already in force.

**Left open, put to him:** *Civilisation Comparison*'s three columns (13th,
21st and 29th century) and the Saint Aoife lines that set *a
thirteenth-century holy woman* beside *a twenty-ninth-century Cascade
taxonomist* mix CE and UCSD in one comparison; see `open-questions.md`.
