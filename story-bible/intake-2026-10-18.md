## Quiet jokes, and the two registers refused — RECORDED; nothing open

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> Quiet jokes are allowed. I would never accept the sarcasm and mockery of Red
> Dwarf or the cynical horror of Dr. Who.

**Direction check.** An extension of the tone rule, and a clarification of
two earlier lines. It adds no fact about the world.

- **The tone rule** (`CLAUDE.md`, *Tone*) says only what the dark side may not
  do: unsettling, never horror. It said nothing about humour. This supplies
  the other side: humour is allowed, at a quiet register.
- **The 12 August line on Doctor Who** (the house tone guardrail) refused
  *horror rather than sci-fi*. The new wording names what kind of horror:
  *cynical* horror, dread that comes with contempt for the people it happens
  to. The two readings agree, and the new one is the sharper.
- **The 5 October ruling on the Archive** took *The Hitchhiker's Guide*'s
  device and not its register ("the device, never the register"). This is the
  same line drawn for comedy in general.

**What a quiet joke is, from the record's own examples.** It is a joke the
record does not signal, made by the structure or by a character's dry
precision, and it never costs anyone their dignity:

- *Ask and It Will Be Reviewed* (`src/lore/ask-and-it-will-be-reviewed.md`):
  prayer and magic filed as a permissions office. `ideas.md` records that the
  joke was kept because it is load-bearing.
- The frame-dependent beauty seed in `ideas.md`: two true survey entries,
  *gorgeous at ten kilometres, lethal at ten metres*, that read as a
  contradiction until the range column is noticed. That file calls it *very
  much this archive's kind of joke*.
- The paperwork in Eden's bureau: Barsik's file says *mascot* and does not go
  very far, and Bubochka's says *courtesy animal* (`src/characters/knopka.md`).
- Jeeves's *"sir"*, kept under the prefixes rule because it is load-bearing
  for the comedy (`CLAUDE.md`).

**The two registers refused.**

- **Sarcasm and mockery** (*Red Dwarf*). A joke at a character's expense, a
  put-down as a running gag, a crew who despise one another for laughs, or a
  narrator who mocks the people in the record. It breaks the cast principles
  as well as the tone, since nobody in the record is a figure of fun, and it
  breaks the rule that the record ranks no character.
- **Cynical horror** (*Doctor Who* at its darkest). Dread used to show that
  hope was foolish, or people treated as fodder for the frightening thing.
  The tone rule already refuses the horror. This refuses the cynicism with
  it: hope and serenity stay load-bearing, as the 12 August target says.

**The test.** Would the person the joke is about laugh at it too, or at least
recognise themselves in it without being diminished? If not, it is mockery.
And does a dark passage leave hope where it was, or argue it away? If it
argues it away, it is cynical.

**Scope.** Prose, Codex voices, Journal entries and image or video prompts,
at every tier. Undercover Pets keeps its own register (*cute, cool and
clever*), which is already a quiet one and is not changed. A Codex author may
be dry, or even tart about an institution, in their own voice, as a character
may; what is refused is the record's own voice taking that register, and any
page whose point is to mock.

**Recorded** as a clause of the *Tone* rule in `CLAUDE.md`. Settled at Opus as
following directly from the direction; nothing open.
