## Sen's page names the standing difference; three of his remarks on plural systems

**The extension.** `src/characters/sen.md`, *Character Notes*, gains one paragraph after the reopening line. **Three remarks, verbatim, made while it was being written.** *"Two viewpoints or POV from within the same plural system would also be valid"*; then *"However not all headmates are equal"*; then *"Some plural systems could be as large as 720,000 members or infinite and many headmates or members have never fronted or co-fronted"*.

1. **Two blocks from one system are a valid shape.** The two-viewpoint rule in `validate-content.js` counts `povs` entries, and Fable read it as forcing an outside witness into C04 (reading 3 above). By the first remark, a chapter may meet the rule with two members of one system, each in their own block. The engine names a block by a character id (`::: pov <id>`) and keys the scene-POV pages by it, so Sen's three share one id, `sen`. A per-member block therefore needs a way to name the member. That is engine work, built when a chapter first needs it and not before, per the engine rule. C04 stands as merged, because Wender's block earns its place on its own terms.
2. **Not all headmates are equal.** Read, by recommendation and his to overrule, as follows. A system's members are not interchangeable and need not hold equal standing, presence or say: one may front far more than another, hold the body's commitments, or speak rarely. Each system's arrangement is its own, stated where the record states it and marked absent where it does not. This bounds remark 1: a member gets a viewpoint block where the record makes that member someone who can carry one, never by symmetry. It never ranks members as more or less real (every member is real, 10 October), and it leaves the 21 October condition untouched. Sen's page already shows unequal roles (Sen fronts most, holds the desk and signs the timesheets), so it is consistent. The new paragraph says nothing about equality either way.
3. **Size, and members who never front.** A system may be vast: hundreds of thousands of members, or without number. Many members of any system may never front or co-front. So the record never assumes that a system is small, that its known members are all its members, or that a member who never fronts is less of a member. Sen's three are the three the record knows. Nothing here says whether there are more, and nothing is to be added without a page that needs it. The engine consequence of remark 1 is unchanged: a member who never fronts can still be a viewpoint only where the record gives a way in, and none is assumed.
