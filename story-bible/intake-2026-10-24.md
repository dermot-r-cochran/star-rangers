## Citations of scientific and academic papers in the Codex

Dermot, verbatim, after the session's answer on wormholes and chronology protection:

> Allow citations of scientific or academic papers in codex

**The reading.** An extension of the 23 September direction (`intake-2026-09-23.md`), which let an in-world Codex source name real physicists and cite the historical literature. That direction was read narrowly, as physics. This one widens it to **scientific and academic papers in any field**: physics, mathematics, biology, the philosophy of science and the rest, by title, author, venue and year. The same day's Codex papers by Yusuf Adair, Ilsabet Corren, Oren Halsey and Ansel Thale already cite Popper, Lakatos, Dicke, Carter, Gödel, Turing and the physics literature in that way.

**What does not change.** The 23 September condition stands: nothing cited may raise a copyright or privacy issue, so a citation names a published work and quotes it briefly, if at all. Lore and the glossary still name nobody from our century, and the Journal still carries the real-world notes. Fiction keeps its own stricter rule (17 October): it may be cited only when it is in the public domain on both sides of the Atlantic, and only as fiction. A pre-adoption year is written CE first with UCSD beside it, under the dates rule. And a citation is a Codex author's account like everything else in the Codex: valid for its author, never a claim by the record.

## In-story authors citing one another

Dermot, verbatim, in the same exchange that asked for a comparison with science fiction harder than this record's:

> Fictional in story authors may also be quoted or cited by later authors

**The reading.** An in-world author, whether of a Codex entry, a paper or report a character page names, or a document the record files, may be quoted or cited by a later in-world author. This gives the Codex a literature of its own that grows by citation, as a real one does. The day's worked case is Ansel Thale's *Coherence Debt as Quantum Interest* (Engineering Corps working papers no. FS-212, 2826 UCSD), written to be cited by proposition. Ilsabet Corren's 2827 paper already cites Yusuf Adair's review of 2815.

**The conditions, from rules already standing.**
- **Date order.** An author may cite only what existed at the time of writing, by the record's own dates.
- **Access.** An author may cite only what they could have read: a sealed file stays sealed, the Celtic Union's register stays the Union's, and a document carried by courier is transit-lagged. This is the Codex test, *could this person have known this*, applied to sources.
- **Standing.** A citation does not promote anything. A Codex entry quoted by a later author is still valid for its own author, and the citing author's use of it is valid for theirs. Neither becomes canon by being cited, and a citation may disagree with what it cites.
- **Reader test.** A chain of citations may not do what a single entry may not: after reading, canon is no less settled.
