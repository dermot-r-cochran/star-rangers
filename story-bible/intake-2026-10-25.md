## Elvira, Galahad, and the Krenyi rule on romance

Dermot, verbatim, after *The Word She Did Not Like* (S01E03C05) thickened the Krenyi reading of Elvira:

> If there is any romantic affection between Elvira and Gallahad then that would be very unusual for most Kyreni and this would be the singular exception

**The reading.** `src/lore/krenyi.md` states that Krenyi do not form romantic attachments to humans and will not consent to interbreed with them. This direction does not change that rule. It reserves one exception: **if** romantic affection ever forms between Elvira and Galahad Thorne, it is the only case of a Krenyi's romantic attachment to a human, and it stays the only one, under the 17 October rule that a singular exception stays singular (CLAUDE.md).

**What it does not do.**
- It asserts no romance. None exists in the published record. S01E03C05 keeps their regard to model-revision and a plain apology, and Galahad's page warns against the obvious inference.
- It does not establish Elvira's kind. Her kind remains a marked absence that S01E03C05 thickened without closing. The exception applies only if she is Krenyi, which the record has not said.
- It does not cover interbreeding. The Krenyi refusal to interbreed stands untouched.
- It changes no page now. `krenyi.md` keeps its rule as written. If a chapter ever shows the affection, that chapter carries the revision: `krenyi.md` gains a clause naming the one exception, with `revised_by` pointing at the chapter, following the 20 September rule that revelation is tracked as revision.

**Constraints a chapter would carry.** The tier register for romance holds: longing, devotion and choice, of the senses and never displayed. Galahad's marriage, which he left before the Threshold posting, is on his page and not to be undone. And because Krenyi lore holds that a Krenyi is honest and does not deceive, Elvira's side of any affection could not rest on her hiding what she is, once she knows it.

## Krenyi blood on human skin, and how Galahad learns what Elvira is

Dermot, verbatim, later the same day:

> Kryeni blood would burn or irritate human skin. That is how Gallahad discovers that Elvia is Kyreni.

**Spelling readings, recorded and not asked.** *Kryeni* and *Kyreni* are the **Krenyi** (the canonical glossary's spelling; he has written *Kyreni* before, 17 October). *Gallahad* is **Galahad Thorne**. *Elvia* is **Elvira** (confirmed 28 July 2026, `intake-2026-07-28.md`; he has written *Elvia* before).

**The reading.** Two things are directed, and they are of different kinds.

The first is a species fact, and it goes on the open page now. Krenyi blood that touches a human's skin burns or irritates it. This extends the hazard already on record in `src/lore/krenyi.md`, *What a Krenyi Body Does With Foreign Material*: Krenyi immunity destroys foreign genetic material, so a transfusion of Krenyi blood leaves a human seriously unwell for days; the same immunity now acts on contact, at the skin, in a smaller way. *Burn or irritate* is read modestly, as a contact reaction and not a wound: a sting, a reddening, a weal that fades over a day or two and leaves nothing, the kind of thing a nettle does. The reaction is to the blood alone; nothing a Krenyi does by touch affects anyone, or the record's mixed crews and shared tables could not stand. The tone guardrail governs the wording on the page and in any chapter: unsettling, never horror, and hinted rather than shown. Realized on `krenyi.md` in that section, in the Archive's voice, naming nobody. A species fact is something the record's own clinicians could hold at its present, so it belongs on the open page under the 20 September rule, and having it on record before the chapter is what will make Galahad's discovery a recognition rather than a reveal: the fact was always there to be read.

The second is authorial intent about the story, and it goes nowhere public. **Elvira is Krenyi**, and Galahad Thorne is to learn it in a chapter, by her blood on his skin. This settles, as intent, what the previous section left to the record: her kind. It does not settle it *in* the record. Elvira's page keeps `species: Person, kind not established` and its Open Thread, Galahad's page keeps its warning against the obvious inference, and S01E03C05's canon facts stand, until the chapter that shows the discovery lands and carries the revision: her page's `species:` line and Open Thread revised to cite the chapter, as they already cite S01E03C03 and S01E03C05 (character pages carry no `revealed_by` or `revised_by` field, `lib/content-schema.js`), and `revised_by` on `krenyi.md` only if its own text changes. Her page's own rule holds: her kind is resolved *only by story events*, and this is the event.

**What it does not change now.**
- No chapter is drafted from this direction. The discovery is a planned beat, filed on the worklists (`narrative-gaps-checklist.md`, *Character arcs with promised beats*; `open-questions.md`, the *Kept thin on purpose* line), to be written when its slot is chosen.
- It asserts no romance. The previous section stands as written: the reserved singular exception applies only if she is Krenyi, and the record has still not said that she is. What has changed is that the condition is now known, to the author, to be met, so the exception is live rather than hypothetical. Whether affection forms is still a separate question, and still a chapter's to show.
- It does not touch the interbreeding refusal, the Krenyi origin absence, or the rule that Krenyi do not form romantic attachments to humans.
- It does not say how a Krenyi child came to be warden-heir of Dún Rí, or what the household's lies *for family reasons* concerned. Both stay marked absences, and the chapter that shows the blood need not explain either.

**Tensions the chapter must carry.**
- **Krenyi volunteer the blood hazard early**, because withholding it would cost someone something. An Elvira who does not know her own kind cannot volunteer it, and her not having done so is not deception, so the honesty floor holds. The chapter has to be written so that she does not know, or has not understood what she knows: she grew up among humans who lied to her for family reasons, and nobody at Dún Rí told her this. Once she knows, the previous section's clause applies in full: she could not hide it afterwards, from Galahad or anyone, and would not try.
- **Galahad already holds the fact.** His page says he has worked with Krenyi before and learned by practice how to be present without the standard errors, and the Krenyi volunteer this early, so he will have been told. That is what makes the moment a recognition under the 20 September rule: a man who has carried an unchanged face for twenty years, a woman who does not experience deep fear, a stillness Syra has already read, and a sting on his hand he has felt before. The ending must change how the beginning reads, and this one does: the step at Dún Rí, *that should have been harder*, and every page of her file read again.
- **A Krenyi heals fast and whole**, so her blood would be brief: a graze, a thread cut too close, a hand on a stone. The chapter needs the ordinary occasion and no more, and the reaction kept to what the page says.
- **Syra's unfiled reading** stays unfiled. The discovery must come by the blood and Galahad's skin, not by Syra deciding to say what she read; her refusal to place anyone by a kind is hers and is not to be spent for the plot.
- **What Elvira does with it** is the harder half. She takes a correction as familiar from her own people; the previous chapter showed her take one and go on with nothing in her shoulders. A woman learning what she is from a mark on another person's hand, in a people whose deep fear is not available to her, is not a scene of shock, and the register for it is the one S01E03C05 set: taken apart in order, filed, and the one thing that is hers kept back.
- **The singular exception is now in play**, not asserted. The chapter that shows the blood is not the chapter that shows affection, and need not be; if both are ever one chapter, the romance constraints above bind it in full.
- **The word she did not like** was a folk word for the wrong light. The locals' *Enchantress* and the folk register around her have been human misreadings of a Krenyi all along, which is the mythification problem the Krenyi page describes in its most personal form; the chapter may let that land without saying it.
