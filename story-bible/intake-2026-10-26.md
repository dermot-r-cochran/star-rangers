**How it is read, flagged here so he can overrule it.**

- **The sentence explains the epistemology and invents no fact.** It says the record has no picture, or why one is hard to take (light it cannot see by, a scale no frame holds, a people that declined), in the terms the page already gives, and it implies no shape the page does not state. - **Nothing is applied by this change.** No page gains the sentence here. Candidates are put to him as they arise.

## Orbital Five-O and the series it echoes

Dermot, verbatim, while the open question on Five-O's second season was listed:

> lost interest in the Hawaii Five-O series about halfway through; either too violent or too formulaic

**The reading.** Orbital Five-O borrows its namesake's brand and nothing else, and the two reasons he gives for losing interest name two failures for the Five-O thread to refuse:
- **Too violent.** Already refused by the standing rules: the peaceable present, no guns-and-spaceships, and courage with patience and discretion rather than violence (CLAUDE.md). This adds that the namesake's violence is a known reason a reader leaves.
- **Too formulaic.** New as a stated failure for this thread. A case-of-the-week season, in which each chapter opens on a crime, runs the same investigation and closes on the same arrest, is the formula. It sits with the shadow-shaped rule and its corollary that nothing acquires a repeatable answer: a Five-O case may start where a procedural starts, with the local bureau, but it does not resolve the same way twice, and the season's shape is not a repeating loop.

**What he kept from it.** Dermot, verbatim, minutes later:

> What did appeal about Hawaii Five-O and most detective series is the sense of inevitable justice and the confrontation of evil rather than fear of evil

**The reading.** This is what Orbital Five-O takes from its namesake, and it sits with the standing rules rather than against them:
- **Inevitable justice.** The reader can trust that a wrong will be reached. In the peaceable present that is the record's own machinery: the bureau sends the case up, Five-O takes it, the Rangers are invited when it touches something larger, due process holds, and quiet prevention means few wrongs get far. The certainty is in the ending, not in a chase or an arrest scene.
- **Confrontation, not fear.** A wrong is faced openly, by someone with standing, at a table, with an instrument and a question; the register is courage with patience and discretion, never dread. It is the tone rule's positive half: a dark fact is hinted, and it is met, not cowered from.
- **Evil, read through the cast rules.** No person in the record is a true villain, so what is confronted in a person is the wrong done and the misguided purpose behind it, and the person keeps their standing. The Unfounded, the one true villain, are confronted in the same spirit, unafraid, but countered and starved, never addressed.
- **Where it meets the shadow-shaped rule.** A shadow stays unsolved; justice is not a solution to it. A Five-O case can end with the wrong reached and the larger shadow it touched left marked, which is the pairing the thread needs.

**What it does not do.** It decides none of the open Five-O questions (the handover beat, who on the Rangers' side is called in), and it changes no page. One word to watch, not changed here: `images.md`'s register table describes Orbital Five-O's pictures as *brighter, procedural, busier*. That is the visual register of a working office, not the story's shape, and it stands unless he says otherwise.
