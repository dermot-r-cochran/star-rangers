## What makes a flexure

Dermot, verbatim, after asking how the forty seconds at Threshold are possible: *"I cannot justify the existence of boundaries or what might cause them to form, unless they caused by folds?"* Three options were put to him (primordial, design, marked absent), with folding and a gravity-like pull between membranes already excluded by the record. His answer, verbatim:

> A, primordial (my pick): flexures are creases frozen in when the membranes formed

**The reading.** An extension of `src/lore/concordant-membranes.md`, which said what a flexure is and that nobody builds one, and was silent on why one exists. It now says, in *Where a flexure comes from*: a crease set when the membrane formed, as flaws are set into a crystal that cooled unevenly; nothing makes one since; a crease is part of the sheet, so it keeps its address. The canonical guide's *Flexure* entry carries the same line as *Origin*.

**How it is read, flagged here so he can overrule it.**
- **It extends and alters nothing.** *Nobody builds one, nobody collapses one, and nothing travels along one* stands, and is now explained rather than only asserted. The fold/flexure distinction is untouched: folding is transit inside a formed universe, so it cannot be a cause.
- **It says nothing about what the membranes formed from**, or when. The record has not established either, and this does not.
- **Design is not excluded as a reading.** The Cascade's design strata fix possibility space, so a devotional source may still read the creases as authored, in its own voice, under the three-way reading. The Archive's own position is the physical one.
- **What a crease does over time is left as the record has it.** The Sceir Light's register shows the shore clock's corrections growing across a register two centuries old, while the station's forty seconds hold; that is the expression at one place, not the crease moving, and this ruling asserts nothing about it.
