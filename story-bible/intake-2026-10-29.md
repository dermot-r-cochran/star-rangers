## What a hyper-intelligent mind is in the record

Dermot, verbatim, in a conversation about artificial general intelligence that turned to the story:

> I am not sure what a hyper-intelligent AI would look like in my story or even if the concept is well-defined.

Four points were offered in reply (Opus, in the session). Dermot, verbatim:

> Yes, agreed.

and, asked whether to record them here:

> Yes, record it

**The reading.** As the term is usually used, it is not well-defined. It treats intelligence as one dial that can be turned past the human setting, where intelligence is several capacities (speed, breadth, depth of modelling, judgement, knowing what to ignore) that do not scale together. And some limits do not yield to intelligence at all: information a mind never received, the horizon past which a chaotic system (weather, an ecology, a society) cannot be predicted however clever the predictor, and physics (light lag, energy, the cost of computation). So in the record a mind of very great capacity is described by the four points he agreed:

1. **It is defined by what it cannot do.** It is better at what can be reached and still bound by what cannot. It cannot cross a gap the record keeps uncrossable: a Membrane Shadow stays unsolved in its presence, marked and kept open, because the shadow-shaped rule holds only if no mind could close it. This is the standing rule that no character is all-knowing, omnipresent or all-powerful (`intake-2026-10-11.md`), applied to made minds, and the corollary that nothing acquires a repeatable answer (`intake-2026-09-22.md`) binds it too.
2. **Its medium of thought shapes it, not raw capacity.** As a people is derived from its senses and its medium of thought, a large made mind is derived from where and how it thinks. One spread across a habitat or a system carries light-hours of lag inside its own thinking, so a single thought may take an afternoon: vast, slow and patient. A mind that is only *more intelligent* is a hat.
3. **It is shown by its effects, not from inside.** No author can write the inside of a mind wiser than their own (Vernor Vinge's observation, a real-world note for the Journal and never the Archive's voice). Under the tone rule's *hint at the dark fact rather than depicting it*, applied here to the vast rather than the dark, the reader meets such a mind in what it had already settled before a chapter opens, and in the question it chose not to answer.
4. **Its restraint is what makes it a person, and the check on it comes from outside.** The Kernel's premise holds: *a synthetic mind may think freely; it may not act freely* (`src/lore/ai-safety-kernel.md`). The check is not the mind's own. This keeps the quality Dermot liked in the Banks Minds (`intake-2026-09-08.md`) and refuses a Mind bounded only by itself, which is the bounded-creativity rule (`ideas.md`, *Bounded Creativity*): the bound comes from outside the thing bounded, and self-restraint alone decays the way Krast's did.

The two check questions from `intake-2026-09-08.md` stand for any such mind: *is this Data?* and *is this a hat?*

**What it does not do.** It writes nothing under `src/`. No hyper-intelligent mind is introduced, named or placed, and nothing says one exists at the record's present. This is the shape such a mind takes when a chapter first needs one.

## Who repairs the made minds

Dermot, verbatim, while the section above was being written:

> Xenon is capable of repairing other AI systems and Lumbarian is capable of repairing while ships including their AI systems.

*While* is read as *whole*.

**The reading.** Both character pages already hold most of this. `src/characters/xenon.md` makes Xenon the ship's robot doctor, whose patients are other robots; this widens his patients to AI systems generally, a made mind's hardware wherever it runs and not only a robot's body. `src/characters/lumbarian.md` gives Lumbarian stations and starships, hull plate and trusswork; this adds a ship's AI systems to what he repairs.

**How it is read, flagged here so he can overrule it.**
- **Lumbarian works at the scale of the ship, Xenon at the scale of the part.** Lumbarian's page says his smallest manipulator is too coarse for fine work, and that no part of Xenon or of the smaller machines is his to touch. Read together: Lumbarian repairs a ship's AI systems where they are vessel-scale (the housings, cooling, power and trunk lines, the structure a ship's mind runs in), and the fine work inside them stays Xenon's or a certified specialist's. That is a clarification of both pages, not a contradiction, and settled here on the recommendation.
- **Repair is continuation, never creation.** Xenon's page already gives the Accords' line that fabrication authority never includes commissioning authority. A repaired mind is the same mind, and no repair brings a new one into service.
- **Where it meets the section above.** A vessel-scale intelligence, which Lumbarian's page already names as one who keeps him, is itself kept running by smaller minds and hands outside it. Its upkeep is an outside check of the plainest kind: a very capable mind in the record depends on others it cannot do without. This is offered as a link between the two sections, not a rule, and it asserts nothing about any particular mind's upkeep.

**What it does not do.** Neither page is edited here. The two lines enter `xenon.md` and `lumbarian.md` as extensions when a chapter or Dermot asks for them.
