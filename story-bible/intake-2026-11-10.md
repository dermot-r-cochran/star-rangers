Context: Threshold's forty seconds, and much else in the 27 May bootstrap, were
found to be the Copilot coding agent's choices rather than Dermot's
(`prose-needs-your-voice.md`, *Details that came from the tools, not from you*).

Dermot, verbatim: *"The Star Rangers hierarchy is valid"*, *"The characters are

just politeness"*, *"Likewise the initial idea of Elvira as an Enchantress"* and

**Put to him as choices, and his picks, all as recommended:**

1. **Galahad's *Sir*: a name.** *Sir* is the first part of the name he was

   `src/characters/galahad-thorne.md`. Every other use in the record is already
   the full name, so nothing else changes. It is a clarification of a genuine
   dilemma he resolved. `CLAUDE.md`'s prefixes rule now says *Sir* is part of
   his name, and no longer lists it as retained pending review. The other
   choices were politeness and removing *Sir*.
2. **Elvira's *Enchantress*: the locals' word only.** It stays as the village's
   misclassification, which she finds annoying (`src/characters/elvira.md`,
   and the village's belief in the S01E02C00 dream). The record no longer names
   her that: *later called 'the Enchantress'* is removed from her POV label in
   S01E02C00 and S01E02C01. The other choices were removing the word or leaving
   it as it was.
3. **The roleplay: allowed, not asserted.** A medieval game between the two of
   them, *Sir Galahad* and *the Enchantress*, is fine, but no page says it until
   a chapter shows it. It is noted beside the reserved singular exception in
   `narrative-gaps-checklist.md`. It asserts no romance.

**Also ruled valid:** the fifteen-rank hierarchy from PR #2 (27 May), and the

## An earlier approval is no shield

Dermot, verbatim, the same day: *"Even if I did approve an idea earlier on, it
is subject to review if it clashes with everything else"*.

It is a standing rule, recorded in `CLAUDE.md` beside the canon-change rule and
in `early-drafts-review.md`. An idea that clashes with the rest of the record is
a genuine dilemma, whether or not he approved it earlier, including under the
25 July ratification of every season. It is put to him as choices, like any
other dilemma. It widens no one's authority: a clash is still his to settle.
It is not a licence to reopen what holds; an approved idea that fits stands.

## Pages lock once readers arrive

Dermot, verbatim: *"Once I get readers I will not have that freedom, the current
work is development, but when readers appear then those pages get locked down.
Either the page analytics or comments should indicate which pages get locked."*

**What was found before the choices were put.** Neither comment board has a
reader comment. The only discussions are his two welcome posts, each with no
comments, so no page is locked. The site records no page analytics of any kind,
and the only per-page reader data is Google Search Console, which only he can
read, from his desktop.

**His picks, all as recommended:**

1. **The signal: comments automatically, views by hand.** A page locks when
   someone other than him comments, replies or reacts on its thread. He can
   also lock a page from Search Console clicks, as a desktop step. Nothing new
   is added to the site. The other choices were comments only, and adding a
   cookieless page counter.
2. **What locked means: assertions frozen.** Allowed edits are a typo, a broken
   link, image bookkeeping, or a plain error of arithmetic, chronology or
   terminology, each named and stated in the changelog. A clash with a locked
   page is settled on the unlocked side or explained inside the story. The
   other choices were frozen entirely, and frozen with extension still allowed.
3. **Tracking: a registry plus a CI check.** `lib/locked-pages.json` is filled by
   `scripts/lock-pages.js` from the comment boards, and by hand for views. CI
   fails a pull request that changes a locked page without a `Locked-Page:`
   trailer naming the page and an allowed reason. It was built the same day,
   while the list is empty. The other choices were a registry only, and the
   rule only until the first reader.

**How it sits with the earlier-approval rule.** That rule, from earlier the same
day, reaches only unlocked pages. Until a page locks, a clash is a dilemma he
may settle either way. Once it locks, the locked side holds.
