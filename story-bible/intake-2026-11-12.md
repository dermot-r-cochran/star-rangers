## Boundary effects near fold routes: folds are quiet

Dermot, verbatim: *"I have a dilemma about concordant boundaries effects near
fold gates."*

**The conflict, as put to him.** Three pages say the second decoherence
channel, the loud Etheric texture, comes from boundaries:
*A Generalisation of Quantum Mechanics* (*"loud near boundary zones and thin
places"*; the extension vanishes in *"boundary-remote space"*), *The Scope of
Physical Law* (fold geometry is a formcluster fact of the Common Manifold; the
extension's local values are Concordant-scoped, *"quiet here, loud near a
boundary"*) and the *Concordant* glossary entry (folds occur *"in their
thousands inside a single Concordant"* as routine infrastructure, while a
boundary discrepancy is rare). *Quantum Computing and the Cavern Constraint*
added fold routes to the list of loud places (*"Near boundary zones, overlap
windows, fold routes and thin places that texture is not faint"*) and sited
quantum computers *"far from any … fold corridor"*. Read literally, every fold
route and every staging site, Earth–Sun L5 included, would be a noise source.
It also spoke of a *"fold lock"*, which is the harmonic channel's word; a fold
has an aperture.

**Put to him as choices:**

- **A. Folds are quiet; only the boundary is loud** (recommended).
- B. An open aperture is briefly loud while it forms, holds and collapses.
- C. Fold routes are standing noise sources.

**His pick: A** (*"A: folds are quiet"*). The second channel comes from where
the Interval narrows (boundary zones, overlap windows, thin places) and from
nowhere else. A fold adds nothing to it, since fold geometry belongs to the
Common Manifold, not to the Etheric extension. A fold route is loud only when
it runs close to a boundary, as Eden's does. The direction the record already
agreed on is unchanged: boundaries harm folds (instability near active
boundary zones; Dock Seven's aperture widening along the flexure).

## Fluency: the Archive reads fluently; the prose follows the head it is in

Dermot, verbatim: *"Review lore and glossary pages for fluency when reading,
whereas narrative prose style may vary according to whose point of view is
being explained."*

**How it is read (Opus, settled as a recommendation, not a dilemma).** Lore
and glossary pages speak in one voice, the Archive's, and are held to reading
fluently straight through on a first reading. A chapter's prose is held to
the voice of whoever's POV block it is, which may be clipped, tangled, formal
or plain as that person is, and is never smoothed toward the Archive's
register. The Codex follows its author, by the standing *valid-for-its-author*
rule; the direction does not reach it, and a Codex entry is not put through a
fluency pass unless its author would have written plainly. He can overrule
that reading.

**What a fluency edit is.** A clarification under the canon-change rule: the
page says the same thing, more readably. One claim to a sentence where the
claim allows it; dash asides and semicolon chains broken into sentences; a
list carried inside one sentence set out as sentences. Every fact, link,
date, name and qualification stays; nothing is added, and nothing is dropped
to make a sentence shorter. Precision still outranks plainness (the
contemplative-register rule of 6 September): a defined technical term stays
rather than being swapped for a vaguer plain word. A locked page takes no
fluency edit, since its assertions are frozen and fluency is not among the
allowed reasons (`lib/locked-pages.json` was empty on the day).

**The worklist is derived, not kept.** `scripts/fluency-report.js` ranks every
lore and glossary page by crude strain signals (sentences over 45 words, mean
sentence length, parentheses, semicolons and em dashes per 1,000 words). A
high score is a prompt to read the page, never a verdict. The canonical
glossary and migration guide ranks first and is a reference table more than
running prose; it is left for its own pass.

**First pass, same day:** the eight pages that ranked highest after the guide,
other than the guide itself, read and rewritten: *Membrane Shadow*, *Cyborg*,
*Luminescent Ethereal Beings* and *Higher-Dimensional Folding* (glossary);
*Orbital Compute Complexes*, *Saltvik*, *A Generalisation of Quantum
Mechanics* and *The Knarr Line* (lore). All eight now rank in the bottom
third. The next on the list (as ranked after the pass): *The Orbital Habitats
Compact*, *Verdance*, *Quantum Space Harmonic Wave*, *The Scope of Physical
Law* (glossary), *Knarrheim*, *The Survey Corps*, *The Undersong Belt*.

**Who does the pass** (Dermot, later the same evening, verbatim: *"May
delegate to Fable as needed"*). A fluency pass is Opus's by default and may
be handed to Fable where the page's voice is the hard part, narrated history
above all. Opus reviews every Fable pass against the page as it stood: each
fact, link, date and qualification kept, nothing added. Batch the pages into
one Fable request, since its quota is the one that runs out.
