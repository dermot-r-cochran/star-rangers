## Boundary effects near fold routes: folds are quiet

Dermot, verbatim: *"I have a dilemma about concordant boundaries effects near
fold gates."*

**The conflict, as put to him.** Three pages say the second decoherence
channel, the loud Etheric texture, comes from boundaries:
*A Generalisation of Quantum Mechanics* (*"loud near boundary zones and thin
places"*; the extension vanishes in *"boundary-remote space"*), *The Scope of
Physical Law* (fold geometry is a formcluster fact of the Common Manifold; the
extension's local values are Concordant-scoped, *"quiet here, loud near a
boundary"*) and the *Concordant* glossary entry (folds occur *"in their
thousands inside a single Concordant"* as routine infrastructure, while a
boundary discrepancy is rare). *Quantum Computing and the Cavern Constraint*
added fold routes to the list of loud places (*"Near boundary zones, overlap
windows, fold routes and thin places that texture is not faint"*) and sited
quantum computers *"far from any … fold corridor"*. Read literally, every fold
route and every staging site, Earth–Sun L5 included, would be a noise source.
It also spoke of a *"fold lock"*, which is the harmonic channel's word; a fold
has an aperture.

**Put to him as choices:**

- **A. Folds are quiet; only the boundary is loud** (recommended).
- B. An open aperture is briefly loud while it forms, holds and collapses.
- C. Fold routes are standing noise sources.

**His pick: A** (*"A: folds are quiet"*). The second channel comes from where
the Interval narrows (boundary zones, overlap windows, thin places) and from
nowhere else. A fold adds nothing to it, since fold geometry belongs to the
Common Manifold, not to the Etheric extension. A fold route is loud only when
it runs close to a boundary, as Eden's does. The direction the record already
agreed on is unchanged: boundaries harm folds (instability near active
boundary zones; Dock Seven's aperture widening along the flexure).
