## The forty seconds are Threshold against Lúnasa

Dermot, verbatim, while reviewing the Season 1 banner: *"See the digital
atomic clocks in the Long White Trail for reference"*, then *"Why not a
discrepancy between Lunasa and Threshold Station rather than two clocks side
by side?"*

**Put to him as choices (Opus).** The first question was whether to keep the
record's two clocks side by side, or move the discrepancy to Lúnasa against
Threshold. The recommendation was to keep it, with three reasons:

- Lúnasa was the film's invention. His Journal note of 10 November had settled
  the film at *fictional codex at best*, with nothing entering canon from it.
- An orbit-against-ground gap invites the reading *time dilation*, which the
  record's timekeeping treats as routine bookkeeping.
- Two clocks in one room leave no such excuse.

**His pick: Lúnasa against Threshold.** Three shape questions followed, and he
took the recommendation on each:

1. **Shape: Lúnasa's time on the wall.** Dock Seven keeps its two
   chronometers. The left one, above the pressure seal, is still the station's
   own, and the watch log is still kept by it. The right one, above the inner
   hatch, is a repeater of the reference standard kept at Lúnasa. Most
   published scenes stand with small changes, and the banner still shows two
   clocks.
2. **Lúnasa is a Survey Corps station in Earth orbit.** It keeps the reference
   standard Threshold is checked against. It has none of the film's habitat
   ring, rail, *Brightwater* or Carrigbawn.
3. **History: the shore against the standard.** The lighthouse finding
   stands. The offset has always been between this shore and whatever kept
   the standard time of its day: the lighthouse clock against the old
   broadcast signals, Threshold today against Lúnasa. Lúnasa is only the
   present holder of the far end.

**Settled by Opus, as clarifications he may overrule.**

- **Not dilation.** The forty seconds are the residual left *after* the
  routine reduction. The correction between Earth orbit and the surface is
  microseconds a day, and the link's light-time is a fraction of a second.
  Both are applied before the comparison, and forty seconds is what
  survives. This is the closure residual already described in *Universal
  Cosmic Stardate*.
- **Not a master clock.** That page says the record keeps no master clock, so
  Lúnasa holds *a* reference standard, one member of the UCSD ensemble. The
  word *master* is not used.
- **Not solved.** What produces the forty seconds stays unknown. Only the
  comparison is now named.

**Standing of the film.** This narrows his 10 November Journal ruling for one
idea only, under the 6 November rule that an idea from one of his films may
enter canon at his word, per film and per idea. The station's name and its
role as the far end of the comparison enter. Nothing else from the film does.
The film stays an impression in the Journal, and the Lúnasa page credits it.

- An audit of the 66 pages that mention the chronometers, the forty seconds or
  Dock Seven, with each conflicting passage corrected as a clarification:

## A contradiction that makes no sense is fixed

Dermot, verbatim, the same evening, after asking how two clocks side by side
could diverge unless the boundary ran exactly between them: *"My rule about
not changing canon does not apply to a contradiction that makes no sense,
unless the page is already locked"*.

This is a fourth ground beside extension, clarification and genuine dilemma.
It applies where published canon contradicts itself, or contradicts physics
the record does not license, in a way no in-story reading can make sense of.
Such a passage is corrected and named as a correction, and he is told. A
locked page stays frozen, and the fix goes on the unlocked side. A
contradiction the story *can* make sense of is still answered in the story.
Recorded in `CLAUDE.md`, under the canon-change rule.

Opus also noted for him that the forty seconds are the cleanest of several
readings at Threshold, not the only one. The others are the narrowed
Interval itself, the 3:7 overlay of 2826 with the Causeway residue and the
grove's oldest tree, the Type II rhythm, and 2831. The Lúnasa and Threshold
pages will say both things: why two clocks side by side could not diverge,
and that the forty seconds are the clearest reading, not the only one.

## A correction note on a changed locked page

Dermot, verbatim: *"If I do modify a locked page it would need a correction
note attached to it"*.

**What it means (Opus, settled as a recommendation he may overrule).**
- He may change a locked page's assertions himself. No session does so
  without his instruction in that session.
- The commit trailer names the reason `correction`.
- The page gains a dated entry in a new `corrections:` front-matter list,
  each entry a `date` and a `note`. `base.njk` shows the list under the text
  and above the discussion, headed *Corrections*.
- An arithmetic, chronology or terminology fix to a locked page also changes
  what the page says, so it carries a note too. A typo, link or image fix
  does not.

**Enforced:**
- `lib/locked-pages.js` adds `correction` to the allowed reasons, names the
  reasons that need a note, and checks the field's shape. Both are pinned in
  `test/locked-pages.test.js`.
- `scripts/check-locked-pages.js` fails a change that needs a note and adds
  none.
- `validate-content.js` checks the field on every page.

No page is locked yet, so nothing carries a note today. The rendering was
checked on a test build and removed.

## The "View from" buttons: name only — RULED

**Dermot's line, verbatim:** *"POV headers too verbose?"*, on S01E00C00.

What was found: the labels follow the 7 October convention, and Elvira's bracket is the wording he chose that day. The length came from repetition. Each 50- to 64-character label showed twice within one screen, once as a button and once as the block header in capitals. Across the corpus, 298 labels averaged 40 characters, 84 ran over 45 and the longest ran to 87. A button's job is to pick whose view, and the name alone does that.

**Ruled, his *"Buttons: name only"*:** the buttons show the rank and name, the text before the label's trailing bracket (`lib/pov-button-name.js`, the `povButtonName` filter). The full label stays in front matter and still shows on the block header, the scene-POV pages and the button's tooltip. The 7 October convention and its *not to be re-proposed* line are otherwise untouched: no label was edited.

**Then the header, same day.** His screenshot of S01E00C00, and *"The POV header breaks awkwardly across multiple lines"*: Elvira's label ran to three lines of letter-spaced capitals, breaking inside *Dún Rí*. Now the header's heading is the name, and the bracket is a `pov-header__frame` line beneath it, in ordinary case and the same colour. The 7 October ruling declined muting the bracket for typography's sake. This is a different reason, a reader-facing one he raised himself, and the bracket's words, colour and order are unchanged.

**Then the kind, same day.** His question, verbatim: *"Do I need to include the kind of each person, or let the reader discover from the context?"* The reading put to him was to keep it. Under the 28 September usage rule a human is one case among the cases, so the bracket names every viewpoint's kind or none. Naming it only for non-humans would make human the baseline, and naming none would let a reader assume human until the prose said otherwise, the default *familiarity is not evidence* refuses. *(Cat)* is also a reading key for Aldera's blocks. Elvira's early signal was weighed and kept on 7 October. With the bracket now on its own smaller line, its cost on the page is low. **Ruled, his *"Keep the kind"*:** every POV label keeps its frame first in the bracket, as the 7 October convention has it. Not to be re-proposed without a new reader-facing reason.

His reason, verbatim, minutes later: *"My dilemma between precision and readability but I think is better to be impossible to misunderstand than merely possible to understand"*. It is the 6 September register rule for the Journal and `story-bible/` (*being impossible to misunderstand outranks plain language*), now applied by him to the reader's apparatus on chapter pages: where precision and readability pull apart on a label, precision wins, and layout carries the readability.

## The Fellowship holds no worship ceremonies — RULED

**Dermot's words, verbatim,** on S01E00C00: *"The Fellowship does not use incense or chanting or hold any formal worship ceremonies"*.

**What it corrected.** The chapter had Elvira brace for *"incense and chanting — the ordinary furniture of the Fellowship's public worship"*, and its description repeated the incense and chanting. *The Flight of Aldera* had her brace for the same. Sen's *Three Disciplines of the Record* gave the contemplative houses *"a liturgy"*. Each is changed: Elvira now braces for the abbey's quiet, which the chapter states as having no incense, no chanting and no ceremony; Sen's houses have *a rule*. `fellowship-of-light.md` gains one sentence stating the ruling. None of the pages is locked.

**What it does not reach.** The household sage's incense at Dún Rí in *The Flight of Aldera* is a private rite of the castle, not the Fellowship's. Cnoc na mBeach's night office and the Communion of the Called are not Fellowship chapters (each page says so), and the Tideward Sisterhood's offices are the Five Islands'. Ovruhn law *chanted* on Thavren is not worship.

**Then the quiet, same day.** His words, verbatim: *"Fellowship not to be confused with Quakers - gentle quiet with soft music and vocals rather than pure silence"*. So a Fellowship house is not silent: it keeps a gentle quiet with soft music and voices. The chapter's *"plain silence"* became *"the gentle quiet a Fellowship house keeps, soft music somewhere within and a voice or two low beneath it"*, *The Flight of Aldera*'s *"silence"* became *"gentle quiet"*, and the lore page says the houses are not silent. It also answers the open point on the Arilon page: the ecumenical hymns and the *Hymn of the Thin Places* stand as soft music and voices, and only *"liturgy"* went from *"the body of song and liturgy"*. The comparison stays here: under the 9 October rule the record never compares the Fellowship to a real tradition.
