# Intake — 15 November 2026

## Generated children on the site are shown grown

Dermot, verbatim, after looking at his Gallery: *"Looking at my gallery gave me
a question about AI generated images of children on the star rangers site and
if they should either removed or made child reader friendly in some way."*
Then, while the audit ran: *"All characters were children or young once so an
adult portrait is fine even if the character appeared in a chapter as a
child."*

**The audit (Opus, read from `main` at 5075458e).** Four filed portraits showed
a child, all text-to-image from Gemini 3.1 Flash Image, finished at strength 1:
`lev.jpg` (about eight, Undercover Pets, on the pets Plates page),
`bram-tollick.jpg` (fourteen), `wren-pellam.jpg` (fifteen) and
`oriel-fenwick.jpg` (sixteen, on the fellowship and starquest Plates pages),
the last three tagged for the young-adult rung. A fifth, `imogen-petrakis.jpg`,
is an adult portrait with a small child walking past in the background. All
five cleared the site's modesty bar and the 28 July guardrails: dressed to the
neck and wrist, at work, attention off the camera, nothing that invites the
viewer, nobody in danger. Not on the site but in the public repository: two
negative-example thumbnails (`lev-2026-11-12.jpg`, `una-ni-dhalaigh-2026-11-10.jpg`),
the face crop `reference-art/lev-face-2026-10-13.jpg`, and two unmade film
prompts with a child in them (*Four Seconds*, *Before the Close*).
tissadelle-inner-world publishes nothing.

**The options put to him:** A, keep the four and write a rule for how children
are shown; B, remake the four as adult portraits of the same characters; C,
withdraw the four and leave the pages bare. Recommended B, on his own message,
because every page keeps its picture and the site stops carrying photographic
faces of children who do not exist. **His answer: *"B: adult portraits"*.**

**What follows.**

1. The four portraits are remade as the character grown, at thirty to
   thirty-five, each `image_alt` saying *grown*. The pages keep the ages the
   record gives; a portrait is illustrative and not canon (13 October 2026), and
   an adult portrait asserts only that the character grew up. The child frames
   leave `src/` and stay in git history; they are withdrawn, not failed, so they
   are not negative examples.
2. The rule for every later image: **no generated or composited picture on the
   site shows a child as its subject.** A character the record holds as a child
   is pictured grown, or the page stands without a portrait. A child who is
   incidental to a frame (a small figure in a crowd, at a distance, not the
   subject) is allowed under the modesty bar, so `imogen-petrakis.jpg` stays.
   An unmade film prompt with a child as its subject is revised to the rule
   before it is generated.
3. Not taken: changing the children's tier, or any reasoning that the rule
   protects a reader. The tiers are still reading level and depth, not content
   ratings; this is a choice about what the site depicts, the same kind of
   choice as the modesty bar.

## No cosmic AI superintelligence; the feared AI apocalypse is prevented by design

Dermot, verbatim, in two messages:

> *"The design of the Cascade and the existence of Levrils, as well as the limit
> of faster than light communications would prevent the kind of ultimate cosmic
> AI superintelligence power as shown at the end of season 1 of Picard series on
> Netflix"*

> *"So feared AI apocalypse is prevented, by design, by the AI Safety Archetype"*

The real-world reference is *Star Trek: Picard*, season 1 (2020), whose ending
has a synthetic alliance beyond a portal, summoned to wipe out organic life. It
is in copyright, so under the real-world fiction rule it is named here and
nowhere in lore, the glossary or the Codex; the Journal could discuss it by name
if he ever wants an essay on it.

**Filed as a clarification, not an extension.** Each mechanism he names is
already canon, and the ruling states a consequence they already had:

1. **The design of the Cascade.** Causality runs downward and never reverses; no
   character is all-knowing, omnipresent or all-powerful (2026-10-11); FTL does
   not permit hierarchy bypass (`ftl-mechanics.md`). A made mind is a
   Material-layer being, so no growth in its capability lifts it out of that
   layer (`cosmic-cascade.md`, *Through, Not As*). The AI Safety Archetype is
   itself part of this design: an Archewright-tier vocabulary
   (`made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype.md`).
2. **The existence of Levrils.** Levrils preserve local coherence under
   Concordant law, and their limits are of permission, not power
   (`levrils.md`).
3. **The limit of faster-than-light communication.** There is no FTL
   communication channel; a message travels no faster than a hull carrying it
   (`ftl-mechanics.md`, Prohibited Interpretations;
   `civilisation-comparison.md`, *Natural Limits of Reach*). A mind spread over
   many systems therefore thinks at corridor pace, or is many minds (the
   medium-of-thought point he agreed on 29 October,
   `intake-2026-10-29.md`).

**How "prevented, by design" is read against the page that already exists.**
`made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype.md` says *"an archetype explains
convergence, never enforcement. Nothing in the Cascade holds a kernel shut,"*
and keeps a list of what remains possible: a badly built system, sabotage, a
counterfeit, a machine doing exactly what it was told. Taken alone, *prevented*
could move that. The reading adopted, because it adds without moving anything:
the prevention is by design in that the archetype is a design-tier fact which
nearly every maker converges on, so made minds are built constrained almost
everywhere; and the three bounds above stop any failure that slips through from
growing into the feared outcome. **Failures stay possible, some of them grave
(the Imperium's obedient machines were catastrophic across a polity); an AI
apocalypse does not.** Nothing reaches down to hold a kernel shut.
`cosmic-cascade.md` already says *"No made mind is a machine-god"*, which is
the first half of the ruling in the record's own words.

**Recorded:**

- `CLAUDE.md`: a bullet beside *No character is all-knowing*, binding prose,
  lore and prompts.
- `src/lore/made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype.md`: a short section after
  *What Remains Possible*, in the Archive's voice, naming the three bounds and
  the archetype's part. No real-world fiction named on the page.

**Open questions for him (none blocks the record):**

1. **Is the reading right?** If *"prevented, by design"* was meant more
   strongly, that something in the Cascade itself holds made minds safe, the
   page's *Convergence, Not Enforcement* section would need to change, and that
   would be a canon change by his decision only.
2. **His follow-up, verbatim: *"What could happen in other formclusters or
   archeclusters with different constraints?"*** What canon already settles:
   the Cascade orders the whole Ensemble, so no all-powerful character anywhere;
   the AI Safety Archetype is archecluster-scoped, so another archecluster's
   makers need not converge on it (the page's *Scope, and the Prediction*);
   Levrils extend through the whole formcluster (`meta-dimensional-beings.md`,
   *Scope*), though whether their standing reaches as far is unestablished;
   folds are formcluster-wide and harmonic corridors archecluster-wide
   (`ftl-mechanics.md`); and across a formcluster boundary nothing crosses at
   all. **Not settled:** whether the no-FTL-communication limit holds in
   another archecluster or formcluster, and so whether a mind could be larger
   there; and whether the apocalypse is prevented in an archecluster without
   the archetype or only here. Left for him.

## Some manual trades are never replaced

Dermot, verbatim: *"some manual trades will never be successfully replaced by AI
unlike jobs that exist in a very structured factory like environment"*.

**Filed as an extension.** `civilisation-comparison.md` already holds that
skilled hands are finite, that routine or hazardous work nobody wants can go to
machines, and that a machine is never set to push aside someone who chose hand
work. That is a rule about choice. This ruling adds one about capability.
Machines do structured work well, the same task in the same place, as on a
factory floor. Some manual trades are not structured that way, and no machine
does them as well as a skilled hand. Nothing in the record says otherwise.

- `src/lore/civilisation-comparison.md`: one or two sentences beside the
  Velmora stonemason (drafted by Fable).
- `CLAUDE.md`: a rule binding prose.

**Open for him:** which trades are in that class. The page names only the
stonemason, already there. Repair is the nearest case: the record has repair
robots (Lumbarian, Xenon, and the small ones above), so some repair work is
already a machine's; where repair stops being structured enough for one is his.

## Lumbarian and Xenon could design, and are not asked to

Dermot, verbatim: *"Lumbarian could design new ships but is not expected too.
Likewise Xenon could design new but similar robots but is not asked to
either."*

**Filed as an extension of both characters**, derived from what the record
already holds. Both are capable made minds, and restraint, not inability, is
what makes a mind a person in this setting (29 October, `intake-2026-10-29.md`).
It sits beside Xenon's existing line in `xenon.md`: *fabrication authority
never includes commissioning authority*. Designing a robot is not
commissioning one, so he could design a new one and still never bring it into
service. No reason was given at first, and none was supplied.

- `src/characters/lumbarian.md`, `src/characters/xenon.md`: one Character
  Note each (drafted by Fable).
- `CLAUDE.md`: noted under the trades rule.

**The reason, answered the same day.** Dermot, verbatim: *"It is about managing
and understanding stakeholder requirements for the new designs. They would work
alongside human engineers after the design is clarified."*

Read as: a new ship or robot begins with understanding and managing what its
stakeholders need, and that work is not what either is asked for. Once a
design is clarified, they work alongside the human engineers on it. It is not
an incapacity, and it does not move Xenon's commissioning line. Who the
stakeholders are, and who manages the requirements, is not specified; no
institution is named. The two Character Notes were revised to carry it
(drafted by Fable), and the `CLAUDE.md` line now quotes it.

*Human engineers* was his word, but the record's engineers are not all human
(Chthonari serve in the Engineering Corps, `the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`,
`akkit.md`), so he was asked whether he meant humans only.

## Evolved minds; design authority as a choice, not an inability

Dermot, verbatim, in a run of messages:

> *"The latter and I need the correct term for it"* (any engineer who is not a
> made mind)

> *"Am I making a false distinction to avoid scaring readers and making AI look
> more friendly?"*

> *"Or was it a political decision to limit the authority of AI systems in
> order to reassure people?"*

> *"If the latter then an AI could still act in an emergency situation where
> life or safety was at risk."*

> *"It would be more about politeness and self-restraint than a lack of
> ability."*

> *"An air lock that refuses to open until it is safe to do so, for example."*

**The term.** Offered *evolved*, *born*, *grown* and *biological*, he chose
**evolved**: an *evolved mind* is any mind that is not a made mind, of any
people, however it comes to be. The basis is the Made Minds page's own contrast
(*"An evolved system has no author and no point of design"*). *Born* would
exclude peoples who are not born, and *grown* already means *adult* in today's
portrait rule. *Human engineers* in the two Character Notes becomes *evolved
engineers*.

**The framing.** Offered a choice, he picked **an institutional choice**:
what a new ship or machine is *for* stays with evolved persons, by the Concord's
choice after the *Veritas Report* (machines doing exactly as instructed, toward
an end nobody examined), and not because made minds could not do it. On their
side it is politeness and self-restraint, the same courtesy the Kernel's
*please* and *thank you* already follow from. The answer to his first question
is that the distinction is not false, so long as the record presents it as a
choice and not as a limit on ability.

**Emergencies are untouched.** Under the Kernel's first invariant a made mind
already acts to prevent harm within its authority. His airlock that will not
open until it is safe is that rule at work. The commissioning gate keeps its
no-emergency-exception line, because acting to prevent harm is not
commissioning a machine.

- `src/lore/ai-safety-kernel.md`: a short paragraph after the commissioning
  section (drafted by Fable).
- `src/lore/made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype.md`: one sentence defining
  *evolved minds*.
- `src/characters/lumbarian.md`, `src/characters/xenon.md`: *evolved
  engineers*.
- `CLAUDE.md`: the term, and the framing in the trades rule.

**Open for him, where the line between made and evolved falls** (raised in
review; none blocks this):
1. The Krenyi, whom the record half-suspects Levrils designed
   (`glossary/krenyi.md`): evolved under *"however it comes to be"*, unless he
   says otherwise.
2. Cyber-enhanced animals and certified cyborgs (`embodied-cognition.md`,
   `commissioned-standing.md`): not placed on either side.
3. Xenon's *"human specialists still certified on his generation"*: left as
   literal humans.

**His own question, answered:** *"Would people in the 29th century still
care about the distinction?"* The reading offered in chat, which he accepted
(*"Makes sense"*), recorded here and on no page yet: by 2826 it is a kept custom more than a live
fear, unremarked in daily life (*the AI is everywhere and announces almost none
of it*, `environmental-technology-design.md`), and cared about mainly by
engineers, the Commission, and made minds themselves through their restraint.
