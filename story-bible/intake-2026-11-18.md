## The hand-kept ledger and the Sceir Light's timekeeper — RULED by Dermot the same day

**His words, verbatim.** *"In https://fianilchruinne.com/seasons/s01/e01/s01e01c03/ the need for a evolved natural organic timekeeper and paper ledger is resilience to boundary effects than influence all digital electronic devices to some extent"*. Asked how *influence* sits beside the published line that instruments near a boundary are accurate for their position, he chose **"Pulled to agree"**. Asked what *evolved natural organic timekeeper* means, he chose **"The keeper herself"**.

**The reading ruled.**
- **Influence is on what a device keeps, not on its accuracy.** A digital device that keeps time near a boundary is pulled, to some extent, toward agreement: it re-synchronises against its reference and corrects itself, so it keeps the corrected value and loses the difference. Every device still reads its own position correctly, so `src/lore/boundary-zones.md`, `src/glossary/instrument-drift.md` and `src/lore/lunasa.md` stay true as written. Nothing is corrupted, and no physics is added.
- **The timekeeper is the keeper.** An evolved person (Nuala Beirne, and the attendants before her) reads the light's clock, sets it and writes the time down by hand. The movement she reads is mechanical, as S01E01C03 already says, and has no electronics. That is the open question's point 1, reading (a) with its clarification (b) applied, and it settles point 2's movement as non-electronic.
- **What this is not.** It is not a protective measure: a hand ledger keeps the difference; it does nothing to the boundary, so nothing acquires a repeatable answer. It does not make paper as a material resist anything (Shepherd's log is leaves and a stylus and keeps the difference the same way). It does not relate the light's corrections to the station's offset, which stays not established (his pick of 10 November).

**Applied.** `src/glossary/instrument-drift.md` gains one paragraph, *Why the difference is kept by hand*, in the Archive's voice: a clarification under the canon rule, since S01E01C03 and S01E01C04 already show both records doing it. The page is not locked.

**Still open from the 16 November question, point 2.** How the mechanical movement shows its time to the second (a readout that is not electronic, a mechanical counter, or a form not yet named) is not settled, and binds only prompts and films until a chapter needs it. The room stills stay without a clock.
