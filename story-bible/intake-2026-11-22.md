## Edge reasoning as a general principle — a DIRECTION, readings to confirm

**His words, verbatim.** *"Edge AI as a general principle in my story, reducing reliance on data centers, LLMs, frontier models or advanced chips"*

**Reading.** A made mind does its thinking where it is used: in the ship, the habitat, the instrument, the robot, the body it supports. No mind depends on a distant central machine to think, and no general model is consulted by everyone. Applied as a direction over the whole record, not as a new technology: the record's own word for it is still to be chosen (the modern terms *edge AI*, *data centre*, *LLM* and *frontier model* stay out of the 2826 vocabulary, as the 29 August ruling kept *large language model* out).

**What the record already holds that points this way.** None of this is new; the direction gathers it.
- **No FTL communication** (`ftl-mechanics.md`). A ship beyond its home system cannot consult anything at home in time to matter, so it has to carry its own mind. Edge reasoning is forced by physics wherever a vessel travels.
- **A mind is shaped by its medium** (29 October, the hyper-intelligence points). A mind spread across a system thinks with light-hours of lag. The fast, attentive minds of the record are therefore local ones.
- **Every people has its own form of made mind, if any** (29 August). That leaves no room for one general model shared across peoples.
- **Lumbarian and Xenon** (29 October, 15 November). A ship's mind at the ship's scale and a repair mind at the part's scale: both are minds resident in what they serve.
- **The Twelve Sultans** (`codex/the-twelve-sultans-of-ai.md`). Imperium-era AI was controlled by twelve figures. A Concord that reasons at the edge reads as the settled answer to that concentration. *Reading only:* the codex page does not say so, and this would be an extension.
- **Small repair robots, nothing microscopic** (15 November), and **paper may survive boundary effects better than digital recording** (16 November, an idea parked for Fable). Both point to hardware that can be made, mended and trusted locally.

**"Advanced chips", read.** The record's machines are not primitive and the clean 29th-century look stands. The reading is that no mind depends on a narrow, distant fabrication chain: its hardware is the kind a settlement or a ship's own shop can make and repair. A possible in-world reason, *flagged, not canon*: the finest features are the first spoilt near a boundary zone, so robust hardware is preferred wherever reliability matters. This would extend the cavern constraint's decoherence argument from quantum to classical machines, and it is Fable's to test against `quantum-computing-and-the-cavern-constraint.md` before anything is written.

**Where it meets a published page.** `src/lore/orbital-compute-complexes.md` calls the orbital complexes *"the physical seat of the Concord's bulk computation"* and says they *"host resident intelligences"*. `what-the-record-refuses.md` keeps *the weightless cloud* as *"Kept, priced"*. Under the cosmology rule (improve, never move), the recommended repair is additive: keep the complexes, their radiators and their image, and add that they hold the shared archive, simulation, traffic and the narrow quantum queue, but not the minds of the record and not everyday reasoning, which happen at the edge. Their *resident intelligences* would be read as the complexes' own keepers, minds at the edge of that installation like any other. Nothing in `src/` is changed by this file.
