# Intake, 23 November 2026 — the core theme

## Truth and trust

Dermot, verbatim:

> Truth stays absolute, but trust is what the observer builds from partial
> evidence - and it's never finished. So truth and trust turn out to be
> fundamentally complex at least to the observer even if truth itself is
> absolute. That seems to be the core theme of my story.

He approved the reading below with *"Yes, draft it"* and then *"Yes, land
it"*. It is recorded as a clause in `CLAUDE.md`, at the head of the mystery
family, because every rule in that family turns out to be a case of it.

### The reading

- **Truth is one and does not move.** The complexity he names belongs to the
  observer, never to the truth. So no voice in the record settles a question
  by making truth relative, and the Codex reader test (canon no less settled
  after reading) is the theme applied to in-world sources.
- **Trust is the observer's work.** It is built from partial evidence, graded,
  revised when evidence arrives, and never complete. It runs between people as
  well as toward facts, which is where it costs someone something: a crew
  trusting a reading, a bureau trusting a Ranger, a Ranger trusting a source.
- **The Archive is an observer too.** This is the one reading added to his
  words, and the reason for it is a seeming clash: lore is the ground *a reader
  can trust as flatly true*, while trust is never finished. The two fit once
  the Archive is read as an observer whose settled pages are its best trust at
  the record's present. They are reliable at the grade they state and change
  only by revision, never by contradiction, which the revision rule of
  20 September already provides. Lore stays trustworthy, and the record never
  claims it is finished.
- **Shown, never stated.** No page names the theme or argues for it. A thesis
  sentence in narration would make it a moral rather than a story.

### Where it already stands in the record

The fit was found, not forced. Each of these was ruled earlier and for its own
reason:

- the middle rung and faith (`intake-2026-09-22.md`): partial evidence earns a
  belief, not certainty;
- Sen's four genera in `src/codex/standing-claims.md` (verified, consistent,

  no trust in a familiar reality;
- the Archive knows only what the record knows, and revelation is revision
  (`intake-2026-09-20.md`): trust never finished, at the level of the record.
