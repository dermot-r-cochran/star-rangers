# Intake, 24 November 2026 — working ranges for the story's units

## The question

Dermot, verbatim:

> Ideal number of scenes per chapter, chapters per episode, episodes per
> season and seasons per thread or arc?

## The answer he took

Opus answered with ranges rather than fixed numbers, because three standing
rules already bear on the question: an episode reads as a self-contained
short story and needs no fixed shape (5 November); no structure becomes a
repeatable answer the reader learns to expect (*Vary the way in*, 30
October); and the POV block is the only unit with set lengths (4 August).

- POV blocks per chapter: 2–4 (about 1,500–3,500 words at the general tier).
- Chapters per episode: 3–6.
- Episodes per season: 3–6.
- Seasons per thread: open, since a thread has no planned end.
- Seasons per arc: not a measure, since an arc is a character's trajectory.

Asked whether to record them, he answered *"Yes, as guidance"*.

## Where it is recorded

`story-bible-summary.md`, *Craft: working ranges for the units above the POV
block*, just before the POV block length targets. The section carries the
counts of 24 November (most episodes one or two chapters long) and the
rule that nothing published is rewritten to meet the ranges. `CLAUDE.md` is
not changed: the ranges are guidance under its 5 November rule, not a new
rule beside it.

## Extra chapters: the triage

Dermot's *"Draft extra chapters as needed"* asks which of the 90 short
episodes need a chapter to stand as a short story. The answer is one.

**How it was read.** On 24 November every episode of one or two chapters on
`main` (90 of 111) was read in full by five Opus readers, season by season,
against the 5 November rule and the endings rule. The brief was the ruling's
own: an episode that already stands is left alone, and a lack has to be named
(no turn, no recognition, an ending only a later episode resolves, an opening
a reader starting there cannot follow, a thread opened and dropped, or an
unseen scene between two chapters). Being short or quiet is not a lack. All
90 were read as standing. Because that brief leans toward *stands*, the six
closest calls (S00E01, S02E02, S05E01, S05E05, S06E06, S08E01) went to Fable
for a second read with a neutral brief and no verdict attached. Fable found
five standing and one not: **S08E01, *The Night Office***. Under *Who approves
whom* Fable settles what Opus escalates, so its reading stands, and is named
here so Dermot can overrule it.

**The lack in S08E01.** A stance and no turn: Fintan sets the smoker down in
the third paragraph and is the same man at the Amen; the half-tone is no
larger and no better lit at the end than at dusk, and nobody on the page pays
for the watch. S08E03 later says what the season cost, but the episode itself
does not.

**What was drafted.** One new last chapter, **S08E01C02, *The Morning
Watch*** (Fable, 24 November; Brother Fintan and a bee of the third hive).
The morning he marked the hive for comes up white. He goes down meaning to
open it, and the first frost of the year has reached the stand first; noon
makes four degrees, and the figure for opening a winter cluster is ten. The
watch he chose at dusk is now the only one the season allows, and he puts
the cost to himself on it in the commonplace book's two columns. The turn is
that the stillness he chose has become a constraint; the recognition is that
he had been expected at a post and mistook which one, and it re-reads c01's
closing line. It ends with him still holding the dusk was right, which
S08E03's own turn needs. Overlay only: no `canon_facts`.

**What it commits to (overlay).** The first frost of 2827 UCSD on the hill
fell the night after the half-tone (two below at first light, four at noon,
wind into the north); the keepers' figure for opening a winter cluster is
ten degrees; the commonplace book is ruled in two columns, confirmed and
everything else (S08E03 and S08E04 already use the columns); the autumn
entry's wording. Psalm 147:16–17 in the World English Bible. It leaves to
S08E03 the hive's diagnosis, the half-tone thinning by midwinter, his
noticing in the second month how readily he believed the standing, and the
cost "paid by something whether he looked or not"; one word was added on
review (*what the dusk had cost him*) to keep that last one S08E03's, and the dusk is *perhaps ten degrees*, on the opening figure rather than under it (the critic's arithmetic fix, landed by the counter-critic).

**The other 89 stand as published.** The list, one line each, is in the pull
request that records this section. The nearest calls, all judged standing on
the endings rule: S02E02 (the re-survey and the plans-office interview are not
shown, and the door is a held mystery the Codex keeps open, so a chapter
through it would breach it); S00E01 (the disappearance is learned only in
E02, but the episode's own question, *correct* against *confirmed*, is
settled inside it); S05E01 (told more than shown, a style matter, not a
missing chapter); S06E06 (the countersign refusal is set up for E07, but
reads inside E06 as a thing still needed). Nothing in this triage is put to
Dermot as a choice: no genuine dilemma arose.

## Background canon held in the story bible

Asked whether the story bible holds secret but canonical knowledge, the
answer named the held-back identifications (the Noögenic Protouniverse,
Tobble and Tissadelle's inner world, in `tissadelle-arc-s6-7.md`; the
Saint Aoife reading; the Krenyi origin; what the Aurelin Herdlands herd)
and noted that `story-bible/` is readable in the public repository though
no edition builds from it. **Ruled, verbatim:** *"The story bible secrets
are fine as background knowledge and may be reflected or shown subtly in
the story even if never mentioned directly, as per the "Show not Tell"
guideline for writers."* So held-back canon stays where it is, the
repository stays public, and a chapter may let such a fact shape what
happens, what a character notices or how a scene is lit without ever
stating it. Nothing is owed a reveal.

## No single present year; the story reads forwards

Raised while the lore line's "recent" value was being fixed to one year.
**Ruled, verbatim:** *"The story is mostly 29th Century but the 'present'
year would depend on the season or episode"*, *"A season could span
several years or decades and seasons can have overlapping timelines"*, and
*"However the story should read forwards in a linear way without too many
flashbacks or jumping back and forward in time."* So the present is the
season's or episode's own, not one year for the whole record (the 2826
"story's present" in CLAUDE.md stays the anchor for character status only),
and the lore line has no "recent" value (`story-bible/known-in-story.md`).
Seasons may overlap in time, but the reading order moves forwards: a
flashback is the exception, used sparingly, never the structure.

## Why cyborgs are restricted: safety, ownership, accountability

Dermot, the same evening, verbatim: *"The political ban or tight restrictions on cyborgs rather than on AI in general"*, *"It is rooted in safety concerns as well as blurred lines of ownership and accountability"* and *"The AI safety constraints would apply to the cybernetic part of a person and by extension to the whole person."* Landed as an extension of the entry's account of the tightening: safety (a made part fails inside the person and cannot be withdrawn without harm; the Kernel's constraints bind the part and, by extension, the whole person), ownership and accountability (a part is both the person's and somebody's built thing, made, certified and maintained by different parties), beside the welfare literature and the political reaction; and the restriction falls on the body, not on artificial intelligence in general.

Then, verbatim: *"Even AI glasses or other tools would be tightly bound by the AI Safety Kernel."* Read with the published Smart Pet entry (no human carries an AI subsystem on or in the body, the commissioning gate has never passed one): the binding is why none has been passed, so both stand. The entry says so and links that section.
