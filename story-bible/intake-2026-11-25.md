## Aoife, the servant girl, and a vision called Brigid — a direction

Verbatim:

> A servant girl Aoife sees a vision that she calls St. Brigid but later
> re-interpreted as perhaps being a Levril

Measured against the record (below), the direction was put back to him as a
choice between changing what the *Life* says and adding a telling beside it.
He chose, verbatim:

> A new telling

The context he gave for it the same day: a high-fantasy register inside the
Cascade, *"the subtle wisdom of healers and nonviolent wizards"*, a Levrilic
champion in a high fantasy context, *"the 13th century tales of St. Aoife"*.

### One line

A pilgrims' telling of Aoife, taken down at Cill Aoife about two generations
after her death, in which the road calls her a servant girl and calls what
she met Brigid; the keeper who enters it marks where it parts from her, and a
Union scholar of 2819 reads the change of name as what a Levril encounter
would leave behind.

### Measured: what the record holds

- **The naming is canon and it is Muirenn.** The *Life*
  (`src/codex/life-of-saint-aoife.md`, Brother Daire, 1216 AD): *"I knew it
  for Muirenn, for who else comes to a well on her own eve — and yet I confess
  that nothing about the shape resembled the good Sister as she is carved above
  our shrine."* The lore page, the character page and the reader-school
  *Legend* repeat both halves. Nothing in the record names Brigid at the well.
- **Dates that bind** (`src/characters/saint-aoife.md`): born c. 1186 AD; at
  the thorn well from her fourteenth year; the vision in the spring of her
  thirtieth year, c. 1216, on the eve of Muirenn's feast; no husband, no dowry
  accepted; three offers of a religious house refused; died winter 1241.
  *Shown, not healed*.
- **Her standing.** The *Life*: *daughter of no house the record troubles
  itself to name*. The river abbey's telling says bondage (*cumal*), as the
  abbey's word (27 September). Nothing says *servant*, and nothing forbids it.
- **Canon facts.** S06E01C03: the Union's scholars *read* the vision as an
  early, unrecognised Levril encounter. S07E01C01: what Aoife met, *and whether
  it held any Cascade standing at all, has never been settled*.
- **What a Levril looks like** (`src/lore/levrils.md`): *the perceiver supplies
  one*; an encounter *tells a careful reader considerably more about the
  witness than about the Levril*; reports cluster among the contemplative and
  devout as *a selection effect, not an endorsement*; *"Levrils choose to
  appear to the faithful"* is listed as false.
- **The house's book.** Two leaves of it are already in the Codex (*The
  Apparition at Cill Aoife*, which carries no year, and the abbey's claim to
  the other book), and *What Was Carried* is a later unnamed hand of the same
  house. That text records her asking *that nothing be said of her that she had
  not said herself*, her wish that *the well be kept for the sick*, her burial
  under the thorn, and its author's warning: *a later hand will want it to
  have run warm … and will be tempted to supply what the morning withheld. Do
  not.*
- **Brigid in the record.** Once: Drithane's star is the Bríd System, *named
  by its founders for Brigid of the hearth and the forge*
  (`src/lore/planets/drithane.md`).
- **The 3 September line**: *"Levrilic intervention" is Asteria's reading,
  never the record's*. The same shape binds here: the Levril reading is the
  Union scholars', never the Archive's.

1. ***A new telling* read as a Codex leaf, valid for its tellers.** The
   *Life*, the lore page and the character page keep Muirenn exactly as they
   stand. **For his confirmation.**
2. **The teller is the road.** Pilgrims who come to the well on the eve of
   her feast, one of them an unnamed *woman from beyond the river, who had it
   from her mother*; taken down by a later keeper of the house's book, also
   unnamed. No new named person and no new institution. **For his
   confirmation.**
3. **The date: about two generations after her death, near the turn of the
   fourteenth century.** The leaf carries no year, as the house's leaves do
   not. Its keeper says the last of the house who saw her living, *a child in
   the well-house in the fever year* (1241), was buried old the winter before.
   The Archive note says *about two generations … near the turn of the
   fourteenth century* and fixes it no closer. This is late for *"13th century
   tales"* by a decade or two at the outside. **For his confirmation.**
4. ***She calls St. Brigid* read as the road's naming, not put into Aoife's
   mouth.** The teller says *And Brigid came. Who else comes to a well?* The
   girl is quoted only on *shown, not healed* and on the carving. The reason:
   the *Life* has her own words, and a leaf of the same house would not record
   different ones without saying which was false. The drift from Muirenn to
   Brigid is then the tradition's, which is what the keeper's first mark says.
   **Confirmed 25 Nov 2026, his words: *"The road, as drafted"* (choice 1a).**
5. ***Servant girl* read as the road's word**, and as borrowed: the telling
   gives Aoife Brigid's own beginnings (the bondwoman's child at the churn).
   The keeper marks it and adds nothing to Daire. It is neither the *Life*'s
   word nor the abbey's *cumal*. **For his confirmation.**
6. **The wonder is the teller's, and each piece of it is one of the *Life*'s
   three details in Brigid's dress.** The air that folded as cloth folds
   becomes her cloak with no seam; *every distance at once* becomes one woman
   at the well, at Kildare and at every well in Ireland the same minute; the
   three days' warmth becomes a coal of the Kildare fire. Nothing is added to
   the vision that is not a costume for something already in it. The keeper's
   mark: *She said the air folded* as *cloth folds. She did not say there was
   cloth.* **For his confirmation.**
7. **The visitor gives her nothing.** The telling's one line of speech for
   Brigid is *You'll do. You were doing already.* That is the Champion rule
   (recognised, not granted) in a pilgrim's mouth, without the words
   *champion*, *chosen* or *Levril*, which stay the abbey's and the
   scholars'. The keeper marks that Aoife gave no words to what was said to
   her. **For his confirmation.**
8. **The healer's wisdom is plain.** *She had no herb the next woman didn't
   have*: she could sit by a person and want nothing from them. This is the
   *subtle wisdom of healers* read as the *Life*'s stillness, with no craft
   or power added. No wizard appears; the nonviolence is that nothing in the
   telling is opposed by force. **For his confirmation.**
9. ***Later re-interpreted as perhaps being a Levril* read as a second
   annotation by M. Ó Fiaich**, the Union Cultural Archive scholar whose 2819
   UCSD annotation already closes the *Life*. He says *may have been*. His
   argument is derived from `levrils.md`: if the perceiver supplies the form,
   then neither Muirenn nor Brigid was ever the datum, and a tradition that
   changes the name has changed only what the witnesses brought. He adds the
   caution that a costume that fits is evidence about the tailor. The Archive
   note then states the record's position unchanged. **For his
   confirmation.**
10. **Real tradition used, lightly.** Brigid's bondwoman mother, the churn and
    the butter, the cloak that covered the plain at Kildare, and the Kildare
    fire that leaves no ash are the old tradition of a real saint, told as a
    pilgrim would tell them. No modern author or work is cited. **For his
    confirmation.**
11. **Muirenn's feast is given no calendar date.** The teller says *the eve of
    the feast, at the start of spring*, which matches the *Life*'s *spring*
    and leaves the day unfixed. **For his confirmation.**

### What was drafted

`src/codex/brigid-at-the-thorn-well.md`, category Hagiography, author
*Traditional — the pilgrims' telling at the thorn well … taken down by a later
keeper of the house's book at Cill Aoife, unnamed*. Four parts: the keeper's
short preface; the telling in the teller's spoken voice (*The Servant Girl*,
*The Eve of the Feast*, *What She Would Say About It*); the keeper's marks
(*Where It Parts From Her*); the 2819 annotation and the Archive note.

Its ending test: the preface opens *She asked the house to say nothing of her
that she had not said, and the house has not. The road was never asked.* The
keeper's last mark finds that the road, unasked, kept the one sentence that
mattered word for word, did not understand it, said so, and called its own
gloss its own. The road turns out to have kept her rule in the only place she
ever insisted on it.

Also changed: one additive sentence on `src/lore/saint-aoife.md` after the
third-telling paragraph's last sentence, and one link in that page's See
Also. An extension; no existing sentence is altered. A title card,
`src/images/codex/brigid-at-the-thorn-well.jpg`, drawn by
`make-codex-cover.ps1` in the Houses of faith family (command in
`image-prompts.md`).

**What it commits the story to.** Nothing in canon: a Codex leaf is its
source's account, and it carries no `canon_facts`. Small in-universe facts it
asserts by existing: that the house's book at Cill Aoife holds a leaf of this
kind, carried in the Currach Fleet's transcription and shelved in the
Ceridwen Archive after *What Was Carried*; that pilgrims came to the well on
the eve of her feast in growing numbers by about 1300; that the last of the
house to have seen her was a woman who had been a child in the well-house in
1241; that the stone above the shrine still stood; and that M. Ó Fiaich
annotated this leaf as he did the *Life*. One sentence of his leans on lore
already published: that Aoife's feast, kept on the anniversary of the vision,
is still Muirenn's eve on every charter world.

### The four tests, and the never-without-him list

- **Derived:** from the *Life*'s three details, *What Was Carried*'s warning
  about the later hand, and `levrils.md` on who supplies the form.
- **Adds without moving:** a new leaf and one additive sentence.
- **Closes no shadow, gives no remedy:** what came to the well stays
  unplaced; no mechanism is new; the Archive note files no miracle.
- **Recognised as already true:** that a small local saint's eve would be
  taken over, on the road, by the great saint of wells is how such
  traditions go.
- **Never-without-him:** no second singular exception, no exception to the
  peaceable present, nothing ruled unestablished, no designed body, no tier
  gate, no shut mystery opened. The Dún Rí question is not touched: the leaf
  names Daire only as the scribe of the *Life*.

### Validity for its tellers

A pilgrim of about 1300 would know Brigid and not Muirenn, would tell it at
the water in a spoken voice, would not know what *shown, not healed* meant and
would repeat it anyway because it is the one thing everybody gives the same. A
keeper of that house, trained on Daire's habit and on the request in *What Was
Carried*, would take the telling down unmended and mark it, and would not read
it in the house. A Union cultural scholar would read the change of name
against the Levril page and would hedge. After reading, canon is no less
settled: the Archive note says in plain words what the *Life* holds and that
the second name settles nothing.

### Unsure of

- Whether the story of the cloak and the king at Kildare was told as early as

  attested before her century. If he wants it safe, the cloak sentence can
  lose the king and keep the cloak.
