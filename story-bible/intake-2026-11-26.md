## Saint Aoife encounters a Membrane Shadow — a direction

Verbatim:

> St. Aoife encounters a Membrane Shadow

The context is the one he gave on 25 November for the first tale (`intake-2026-11-25.md`): a
high-fantasy register inside the Cascade, *"the subtle wisdom of healers and nonviolent
wizards"*, a Levrilic champion in a high fantasy context, *"the 13th century tales of St.
Aoife"*. *Brigid at the Thorn Well* (#2137, merged on his *"Yes, merge as drafted"*) is the
model for how a telling sits beside the *Life* without moving it.

### One line

A leaf of the house's book at Cill Aoife, entered at Aoife's own asking about six years
after her vision: a dark figure stands at the far edge of the lower field on the evenings
of one harvest, the townland wants it sent away, she walks down to it, reports what fitted
and what did not, says it is not what came on Muirenn's eve, and does nothing to it. A Union
scholar reads her account beside the Survey Corps' Membrane Shadows; the Archive files the
reading as a reading.

### Measured: what the record holds

- **A Membrane Shadow** (`src/lore/membrane-shadows.md`, `src/glossary/membrane-shadow.md`):
  *a dense-edged silhouette, rim-lit where passing light bends around it*; *It casts no
  shadow of its own*; *It is silent*; no thermal or mass signature, and *Only a
  cross-membrane gravimeter shows anything at all*; *it cannot act on this side of the
  boundary, at all, under any circumstance*; *The gravitational gradient near a strong one
  is real, not perceptual*; it exists only where an Interval has narrowed to Type III.
- **The fit the page itself offers.** *A tradition that files fairies, tree-people and the
  dark thing at the field's edge in one category of* not-quite-there *had noticed a real
  kinship centuries before anyone measured an Interval. The vocabulary was pre-scientific.
  The taxonomy was correct.* The Union's own term is *Scáth Seicne*, built on the Irish
  *scáth*, a shade.
- **Two things the page keeps open, which the leaf must also keep.** *Benevolence is not a
  property of the mechanism*, and whether any Shadow is shaped on purpose to be seen:
  *both readings stay open*.
- **Not a being here.** A Shadow is not a Class III incursive and is outside the Cascade
  *by category*: *the Cascade governs beings, and a Membrane Shadow is not a being. It is a
  symptom of one, read through a gap.*
- **The Caomhari and Photographic Beings** (glossary): the Caomhari are met only at certain
  thin places on Tír na nÓg, *and none has been reported anywhere else*. A Photographic
  Being is the bright case, seen by day only, and rarer: *one documented image to some tens
  of Shadows*.
- **The vision of 1216** (*Life*): it *spoke without a voice I could place in my ears*, *named
  things in me*, was *at every distance at once*, and *the water in the well ran warm for
  three days after*.
- **The open file.** `src/codex/the-warm-edge-correlation.md`: *the correlation is real, the
  identification is not, and the file stays open*. `src/lore/rivers-old-lakes-young.md`:
  *Where a flexure runs close, boundary physics is a lawful answer too*, and the record
  names no source for the thorn well's warmth.
- **Aoife's dates and habit** (`src/characters/saint-aoife.md`): born c. 1186; at the well
  from her fourteenth year; the vision in the spring of 1216; died winter 1241. *She told
  her chronicler what didn't fit as readily as what did.*
- **What each leaf already places.** The *Life* is 1216. *The Apparition at Cill Aoife*
  carries no year, and the record does not place it before or after the vision. *What Was
  Carried* is 1241 and after, and says the well *did not run warm* that morning. The
  *Champion* gives c. 1200, 1216 and 1241. *Brigid at the Thorn Well* is about 1300. Nothing
  in the record is placed in the 1220s.
- **The house's book.** A keeper of it was in the room in 1241 and *wrote it down* (*What
  Was Carried*). *The Apparition at Cill Aoife* is a leaf of it entered from what Daire told
  the house.

### The reading, and the one rejected

**Drafted on: a separate, second encounter, some years after the vision. For his
confirmation.**

**Rejected: re-reading the vision of 1216 as a Membrane Shadow.** Reasons from the record:

- The vision spoke, stood at every distance at once and was followed by three days'
  warmth. A Shadow is silent, stands in one place, has no thermal signature and cannot act
  on this side. A re-reading would contradict the *Life* on each point.
- It would close the file the Warm-Edge Correlation and the hydrology page keep open, by
  handing the warm well a boundary to be warmed by.
- It would turn the Levril reading and the devotional reading, which the Union has
  declined to set against each other, into two readings beaten by a third.

So the leaf must not explain the warmth, the vision or the Warm-Edge Correlation, and must
not establish Cill Aoife as a measured boundary zone. In the draft Aoife makes the
separation herself (*They are two things. Do not let the one be said for the other.*), and
the Archive note repeats it in the record's voice.

### Readings, flagged

Each is the interpretation the draft proceeds on. **All are for his confirmation; none is
confirmed.**

1. ***Encounters* read as: she walks down to it, stops at six paces, and looks.** Nothing
   passes between them. She does not touch it, address it, bless it or oppose it. **For his
   confirmation.**
2. **The form is the dark silhouette.** It is the commonest form, it is the lore page's own
   *dark thing at the field's edge*, and it is as unlike the vision as a thing can be, which
   is what keeps the two apart for a reader. It also sets the hardest tone test, a dark
   figure with no horror in it, which her steadiness answers. **For his confirmation;
   alternatives under *Choices put*.**
3. **The teller is the keeper of the house's book, writing in her lifetime, at her asking.**
   The first tale was the road's, far from her and embroidered. This one is as near as the
   record allows without giving her a pen: the *Life* says she kept no writings, and
   everything of her passes through another hand. Her reason for asking is practical and
   hers: the women who mind the well after dark will see it, *and they should have
   something to go on other than the townland*. No new named person and no new institution.
   Daire is not in it. **For his confirmation.**
4. **The date: the seventh harvest since the water ran warm, so 1222 or 1223 AD (1422 or
   1423 UCSD)**, when she was thirty-six or thirty-seven. The leaf gives the harvest and
   not the year, as the house's leaves do. After 1216 because she can then set it against
   the vision herself, and because the *Life* was already closed. The Archive note gives
   both years, since a house may or may not count the harvest of the vision's own year.
   **For his confirmation.**
5. **What she reports is the lore page's list in a thirteenth-century mouth**: dark with
   *nothing in it*, a brightness along its edge, no shadow where the stooks throw long
   ones, no sound, the stubble unbent, no warmth and no cold, gone with the light *the way
   the shadow of a post goes*. One detail is derived from the page's *Hazards*: near it *the
   ground leaned*, to her feet and not her eye. She says she does not know what to do with
   that. **For his confirmation.**
6. ***It was at work.*** The figure goes down and comes up along the mearing *the way a
   woman goes along a row*. This is the page's *moving with what reads as intent because
   the source across the gap is moving with intent*, and the glossary's *a neighbour with a
   habit*. She will not give it hands, and does not see what it works at. **For his
   confirmation.**
7. **The healer's wisdom is knowing who has not come to be tended.** *It is not sick, and
   it has not asked.* The first tale's line was that she could sit by a person and want
   nothing off them; here she extends that to a thing she cannot name. No craft or power is
   added. **For his confirmation.**
8. **The nonviolence is that there is nothing to oppose, and she says so.** The townland
   offers the reaping hook, a bowl at the ditch and a sending-away. She declines each
   without a rule: *There is nothing in that field to cut but what you cut already*; *You
   cannot put out of the house what never came in.* This is no remedy and no leverage: the
   Archive note files her answers *as what she said, not as a method*. **For his
   confirmation.**
9. ***Benevolence is not a property of the mechanism*, in her words**: *A man standing on
   the far bank of a river is neither good nor bad to you for standing there. I was not
   afraid of it. That tells you about me. It tells you nothing about it.* And the open
   question: *I stood where it would have seen me, if it sees.* **For his confirmation.**
10. **Her pre-scientific word is the old women's, not hers.** One of them calls it a
    *scáth*, a shade, *of the same people as the ones in the hill and the ones that keep to
    certain trees*. Aoife *would not take their word off them* and adds nothing to it. The
    filing the lore page praises is the townland's; her own contribution is the
    observation. **For his confirmation.**
11. **The ending is her own thought, marked by her as not known**: that she may have stood
    as the dark thing at the edge of someone else's field. The mechanism patterns strain
    *according to where mass sits on either side*, so the thought is lawful; the Archive
    note says the record holds no account from any far side. **For his confirmation.**
12. **The scholar is M. Ó Fiaich again, in a new annotation dated 2826 UCSD.** His two
    earlier annotations are 2819. The timeline dates the grading of Intermembrane Bleed to
    about 2819 and the Gravimetry Desk's paper to 2825, so a cultural scholar citing the
    Survey Corps' Shadows page is safer after both. He says he passed over the leaf in
    2819. He gives three cautions that he says outweigh the fit, and says it was not the
    Good People. **For his confirmation.**
13. **The Archive note does not file the figure as a Shadow, and does not file the lower
    field as a boundary zone.** It says the record holds no measurement of a narrowed
    Interval at Cill Aoife in any century, and that a description cannot supply one. It
    separates the leaf from the vision, from the Warm-Edge Correlation and from *The
    Apparition at Cill Aoife*, and does not order the two leaves. **For his confirmation.**

### What was drafted

the end, also the one being looked at.

### The four tests, and the never-without-him list

- **Derived:** from the Shadows page's signature, its *Hazards*, its benevolent-case
  section and its open question; from her character page's habit of reporting what did not
  fit; and from *What Was Carried*'s *do not let the second be said for the first*.
- **Adds without moving:** a new leaf and one additive sentence.
- **Closes no shadow, gives no remedy:** the vision stays unplaced, the warm well stays
  unexplained, the lower field is not filed as a boundary zone, and nothing she does has
  any purchase on the figure.
- **Recognised as already true:** the lore page says the old traditions had the dark thing
  at the field's edge. This is one such account, from the one witness in the record who
  would not have improved it.
- **Never-without-him:** no second singular exception, no exception to the peaceable
  present, nothing ruled unestablished, no designed body (the figure is given no limbs, no
  face and no far-side description), no tier gate, no shut mystery opened. The Dún Rí
  question is not touched: Daire is named only in the Archive note, as the scribe of the
  *Life*.

### Validity for its teller

A keeper of that house in the 1220s would write what the house saw from the door, would
take down her account twice-told and unmended, would list the questions and answers
because *the answers were short, and I am sure of them*, and would count evenings because
she asked for a count. The keeper could not know what a Shadow is and does not guess. A
Union cultural scholar on Tír na nÓg, who lives beside the thin places, would see the fit
and hedge it. After reading, canon is no less settled: the Archive note says in plain words
that it files neither a Shadow nor a boundary zone, and that the leaf explains nothing of
the vision.

### Unsure of

- *Scáth* for a shade or phantom is old in Irish, and the glossary already builds *Scáth
  Seicne* on it; the leaf reaches the record through a nineteenth-century English
  transcription, which is why *mearing*, *stooks* and *the going of a person* are in it.
  The language-design skill was not run on the one Irish word.
- The feast of the Cross (14 September) and the feast of Michael (29 September) are real
  feasts used as a keeper would use them. The nine evenings fit between them if the field
  was cut by about the 18th.
- The annotation's year, 2826 UCSD, is chosen to fall after the Gravimetry Desk's paper of
  2825. If the Survey Corps' Shadows doctrine is meant to be later than that in-world, the
  year should move with it.
- `images.md` § 110 still counts ten Houses of faith cards; the Brigid card made eleven
  and this makes twelve. Left for a mechanical pass.
