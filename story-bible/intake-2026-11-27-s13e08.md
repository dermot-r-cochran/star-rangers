**The reading drafted.** One day, three places, and nobody in any of the three chapters holds more than one record. Only the reader holds all three.

- *As Long as a Name* (C01, Wren and Osbern Kell). Under the fig the chronicle is said by the court's reciters and every house's together. After the ninth door every mouth stops, Wren says her line alone and stops where a name goes, and the reciters come in together when a name's length has passed. She says the next line at the pace of a line and finishes after them. The chronicle gains the new Crown's name.
- *The Day's Line* (C02, Oriel and la Chapelle). The Abbey does not go. The day's line is entered after the evening office, and Oriel is given it to copy: a crowning, five islands, a tide, the walls kept, in black ink. It does not say who wrote it. She can still tell her copy from the page.
- *As Heard* (C03, de Courtney and Strozzi). The delegation was told the day and not asked. The saying carries over the roofs, with one break in it. La Chapelle files the write-up: what the instrument had, and a list of what the delegation has not been told. De Courtney does not send it back.

**How it answers *the placing*.** The write-up says that the placing *"could be read by being told, and in no other way known to this officer"*, and that the delegation has not been told. It reads nothing. De Courtney had thought of the write-up as the start of putting the gap right, and finds that it says where reading stops.

**The ending test.** Each strand's first chapter reads differently from here. E01C01 has Wren think that the court *"had never once seen anyone go"*; under the Grove she sees that every house's reciter was once sent to Krilzat, so the houses are where the seeing is kept. E02C01's *"The book does not get older"* is something Oriel was told; here she is handed a line made that evening. E02C01's *"I write down what I'm told. I was told five"* ends on a day when la Chapelle is told nothing and files that.

## Every assertion beyond Season 13

Each is marked against the four tests where it is more than ordinary.

1. *The Kingdom's monarch died at the end of the storm season of the survey's third year, old, in the court quarter.* **New, and the largest assertion here.** A crowning cannot be had without it. No cause is given beyond age, in the harbour's telling. It is a natural death and no exception to the peaceable present. It ends what the Kingdom's page calls *the current reign*. Choice 1.
2. *A crowning was held under the Elder Grove in the survey's fourth year, the fourth held under it.* The count follows from the page's three.
3. *The new monarch's name is Averil. The chronicle's line is "Averil, who stands under the Grove, of the Four Islands".* **New.** No sex, age or relation to the old monarch is stated. The count of four is the chronicle's own, from the page. Choice 2.
4. *Wren and the keeper call the monarch "the Crown" and "the old Crown".* A usage, drawn from the page's *the Crown* so that no pronoun is needed.
5. *At a crowning the chronicle is said by the court's reciters and each house's reciter together, in one voice; the houses stand by tier, the Knights at the rim, the four stewards behind them, the people on the walks.* An extension of *"recited and cross-checked at every coronation"* and of the page's precedence (*"sits nearer the throne than any elected official ever will"*).
6. *A line new since the last crowning is said under the Grove first, alone, by the house in whose time it was given.* **New institutional fact.** It follows from E01C02 (*"it was her house's turn to say it"*) and E07C01 (*"she would say"*). It is what makes the stop audible.
7. *Each house's reciter was once its youngest, was sent to Krilzat, and saw; by custom none says so.* Derived from E01C01's first `canon_facts` line and Bram's father's *"finding out was the point"*. It is Wren's recognition, and it refines her thought of the first night.
8. *Wren was called from Krilzat by a letter, with the ways for "rather more than half the names". Bram had told her of the six evenings without a lamp. He said "With the hole in."* Ordinary, and in keeping.
9. *The reciters came in together after the stop, nobody beating time. Wren finished the next line after them.* The first is new, and rests on Kell's *"A line of the Castle's part closes on its name and on what the named one does"*. The second is E07C02 carried under the tree.
10. *Kell's second crowning. He carried the crowning's line up the Abbey's steps ten days before and was answered. Another reciter carried the old monarch's close.* What the Abbey answered is not said, so no third disagreement on succession is asserted or denied.
11. *A gardener's boy says "It stands, then". An old reciter of a Baron's house says "Fourth door" in passing.* The boy is E05C01's. The old man is new and unnamed.
12. *Wren means to go back to Casimor, unsent.* New, small, and a lean forward. Choice 7.
13. *The Abbey kept its ordinary day, and the Abbess did not cross.* New. It is in keeping with her *"deliberately narrow portfolio"*.
14. *The Accounting's line for a day is entered after the evening office.* **New institutional fact.** It rests on E02C01, where the Abbess goes to the book after the office.
15. *The line for the day: "The crowning. On Vellamar, under the fig: Averil, crowned over the Five Islands. Tide to the third stone at the first office. The walls kept." The column beside it is empty.* New. The Accounting's count is five, from the page. It says *the fig* where the court says *the Grove*. It names no reciter and no line of the chronicle. Choice 5.
16. *Oriel is still a novice; this is the first line she has been given on the day it was made; her copy can still be told from the page.* Her page's *"Whether she will be let near the Accounting, and when"* stays unanswered. Choice 6.
17. *Oriel's father crossed for the crowning. The terraces brought home that Pellam's youngest "left the end off".* Ordinary.
18. *La Chapelle had written the note seven times and handed none in. A clerk gave the delegation the day a fortnight before and did not ask it to come. De Courtney ruled an ordinary day.* **New.** It keeps her page's *"has not been inside the Elder Grove"* true. Choice 4.
19. *The saying carried to the compound. Strozzi heard one break, something over twenty beats by his pulse, and the first line after it quicker than the rest.* New. The break is Wren's line and her stop together, since one voice does not carry; no character says so, though it is one of five things Strozzi can think of and does not prefer.
20. *The instrument returned once on the day, for 00:03:16; it was the two hundred and fourteenth return. Its log shows nil for six evenings together in mid-season.* The six evenings are E05C01's. The count is new and is given as a count.
21. *The note's text, with no band. De Courtney did not send it back. Her covering line.* New. The three readings and their band are unchanged.
22. *The Kingdom's tongue has one form of thanks for being told a thing and another for being given one.* New, small, and in de Courtney's block. Choice 10 lists it with the flagged lines.

**What no assertion says.** Which record is right. The name the Accounting holds, which is not printed here and which neither Wren nor the court has. Who stands in the room, or what is in it without a lamp. Who placed the instrument or who brings the lamp: the delegation is not told, and the reader of the coda alone is not told either. What the Abbey answered when the crowning's line went up the steps. Whether the delegation's absence was noticed. What the fifth name is. Anything of Dún Rí. When, or whether, Oriel is let near the book. No figure of light is on the page in any chapter; the only trace is the log's own line, in the delegation's flat format, as E02C02 and E04C01 already quote it.

## Choices for him

**RULED 28 November 2026**, his words: *"Yes, as recommended"*; all 12 settled on (a), as drafted.

1. **The old monarch's death, and how soon the crowning comes.**
   - (a) *The monarch dies of age at the end of the third year's storm season, and the crowning is in the fourth year.* **Recommended, and drafted.** Basis: it keeps every age on the page, keeps Oriel a novice and Kell alive without a new fact for either, and leaves la Chapelle's write-up still owed, as E06C01 left it. Wren's *"some year"* arriving at once is the kind of turn the season already makes.
   - (b) *A crowning some years on.* It would need an age for each of five people, an answer to whether Oriel has been let near the book, and a reason the write-up had waited.
   - (c) *Hold the episode until he says who reigns.* Nothing published names or describes the monarch.
   - Changing to (b) is the timestamps, two paragraphs of C01 and a pass for ages.
2. **The new monarch's name.**
   - (a) *Averil, with no sex, age or relation stated.* **Recommended, and drafted.** Basis: the Kingdom's page never gives the monarch a pronoun, and the name is in the Kingdom's register (Aldous, Mereth, Selwyn, Isolde) and is used nowhere else.
   - (b) *A name of his.* A find-and-replace in three chapters.
   - (c) *No name on the page.* It would cost the plan's own phrase, *the chronicle gains a name*, and the Accounting's line.
3. **Wren's line, with or without three more words.**
   - (a) *Without.* **Recommended, and drafted.** Basis: E07C01 has her refuse the name on a stated ground and E07C02 has her say *"I'm going to say it like that."* With the name she would have had to ask, which the season's finale settles.
   - (b) *With.* Not drafted; it would unmake E07.
4. **The delegation and the crowning.**
   - (a) *Told the day by a clerk, not asked, and did not ask.* **Recommended, and drafted.** Basis: it keeps *"has not been inside the Elder Grove"* and *"It will not ask for a second thing"*, and it gives the write-up its honest size. An invitation would be a reward, which the brief rules out.
   - (b) *Asked to stand on the walks.* A new act of the court toward the delegation.
   - (c) *Not told the day at all.* Possible; the clerk's visit is what lets de Courtney rule on something.
5. **The Accounting's line.**
   - (a) *As drafted: the crowning, the fig, the name, five, a tide, the walls.* **Recommended.** Basis: the hand's manner in E02C02 (*"in two or three words, the way the Accounting did everything"*), and the two counts set down on one day with nobody pressing either.
