# Rulings history

The full original wording of the standing rules in `CLAUDE.md` whose compact form there ends *(History: `story-bible/rulings-history.md`.)*. Moved here verbatim on 2026-10-20, when `CLAUDE.md` was slimmed below Claude Code's size limit at Dermot's direction (*"moving the long histories out and keeping every rule"*). `CLAUDE.md` holds the operative rule; this file holds its history, worked examples and full verbatim quote chains. Where the two seem to differ, the original wording here is the fuller statement of the same rule; neither adds to nor narrows the other. Paths and links are written relative to the repository root, as they were in `CLAUDE.md`. Like the rest of `story-bible/`, this file is never published.

One section per rule, in `CLAUDE.md` order; each heading is the rule's bold lead in `CLAUDE.md`. A later ruling that amends one of these rules is recorded in `CLAUDE.md` and, if it carries history, appended to its section here.

## Every chapter ends with "In the Archive"

- **Every chapter ends with "In the Archive"** (since 2026-10-02, Dermot's choice among three shapes after asking whether chapters need a commentary or summary beside the text): the lore, glossary, codex and journal pages the build includes whose source cites the chapter by URL or `/c/` alias, or names it in `revealed_by` / `revised_by`, derived every build by `lib/archive-backlinks.js` through the `archiveBacklinks` filter and never authored. It sits after the prose and the Canon Facts, because the general tier is *the same record with its reasons under it* and the reasons go under the text, not beside it; nothing is explained on the page. (Re-grounded 2026-10-05, below: *reasons* stay under the text; *vocabulary* may sit beside it, where the posture asks.) The chapter's own one-paragraph `description` renders on the episode index where a reader chooses a chapter (`.chapter-list__desc`) and not on the chapter page, which shows only its meta. The Journal remains the home for authored commentary.

## Every chapter may carry "From the Archive"

- **Every chapter may carry "From the Archive"** (since 2026-10-05, Dermot's *Recommendations approved* on his own direction that the Archive is *the in-story equivalent of The Book in the Hitchhikers Guide to the Galaxy tv series, in which the glossary accompanies the narrative* — `story-bible/intake-2026-10-05.md`, last section; the device, never the register): the glossary, lore and codex entries that accompany the chapter, each with its own one-line account in the Archive's voice — a glossary entry's `short`, which stands alone by the 2 October rule, a lore or codex page's `description`, the `plain:` line on a children's-tier build. An entry accompanies a chapter for two reasons only, both authored, because the prose and the Archive do not share vocabulary at the headword level (the measurement: 9 glossary titles named across 16 of 94 chapters, so a Book derived by headword would fire on fewer than one entry a chapter): the page's `revealed_by` names the chapter, or the chapter's own **`related:`** list names the page by exact title, resolved like every other `related:` list against the build's own collections (`lib/archive-companions.js`, the `fromTheArchive` filter, `test/archive-companions.test.js`), so a narrowed edition never shows an entry it excludes and `check-related-terms.js` catches a stale title. **Where it sits is the posture's**, which is the one structural rule a presentation mode carries beyond custom properties: beside the prose in a margin column in the `archive` and `contemplative` postures at the wide breakpoint, under it in `story` and `primer`, the DOM order the same either way. That re-grounds the 2 October placement rather than reversing it: a `short` says what a word means and nothing about what the chapter meant, so *nothing is explained on the page* holds, the reasons stay under, and only vocabulary sits beside. The Book's own device — an authored Archive interruption inside the chapter — was listed and not taken; the Archive speaks about the text, never in it. Authoring habit: a chapter's `related:` names the entries its prose meets; the first pass covered the seventeen chapters whose bodies name or link an entry, and the rest fill as chapters are read.

## Existing canon changes only by extension, clarification, or genuine dilemma

- **Existing canon changes only by extension, clarification, or genuine dilemma** (Dermot's direction, 2026-08-25). Published canon — lore and narrative alike — is not intentionally changed otherwise. An edit to a published canon page must be one of three things: an **extension** (adds without altering what stood), a **clarification** (says better what was already true), or the resolution of a **genuine dilemma** (canon commitments that cannot all stand). Anything else is new material, or it is nothing. This names a practice the corpus already ran on rather than a new regime — genuine errors in arithmetic, chronology or terminology were already fixes (a clarification in this sense), chartered works were already superseded-not-retconned, and every published chapter already binds. Worked example from the day of the ruling: an in-session direction that would have reopened `umbral-moon.md`'s "do not have a from" position was put back to Dermot and dropped in favour of the entry as published, because nothing forced the change. The operational habit that follows: before editing a published canon page, say which of the three the edit is; if it is none of them, it waits. **Addendum, same day: a genuine divergence may be realized differently in different formclusters.** Resolving a dilemma need not mean one horn wins everywhere — the cosmology supplies the escape valve, because two formclusters share no arena (`src/glossary/formcluster.md`): realizations seated in different clusters cannot meet to contradict each other, and the record's own reach ends at its cluster's edge, so the home record states its realization plainly while the other stands as structure rather than observation. Two bounds keep this honest. It is a device for **genuine dilemmas only**, never a general licence to fork canon per cluster — and it is in-world structure, a different axis entirely from the editions rule that canon is centralised and variants non-canonical: every domain still shows one record, and that record may itself say "here it is thus; a differently-seated cluster could realize it otherwise."

## Four canon statuses

- **Four canon statuses** (see `story-bible-summary.md`'s canon-status table). **Canon** — the published seasons, plus lore and the glossary: binding. **Valid-for-its-author** — the Codex: a named source's account, filed inside the record, not binding. **Overlay** — currently only `church-space`: a devotional commentary layer laid *across* the whole record rather than a document inside it, reading the same events through a declared lens, opted into per domain and never load-bearing for the main story. An overlay is neither canon nor codex; don't file overlay material as either, and don't let the main story come to depend on it. **Chartered** (settled 2026-07-26) — approved derivative work by another hand, above a fan work and below canon, governed by one rule: **canon binds it; it does not bind canon.** Consistent with canon as it stood at approval; superseded rather than retconned when later canon contradicts it; never load-bearing; carries no `canon_facts`. It is also the **ceiling**: any television, streaming or cinematic adaptation is chartered at most and never canon (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-12 — the screen never becomes the record, and the record never adjusts to the screen). What separates the four is *who wrote it and where they stand* — Codex is in-universe authorship, Overlay is a lens across the same events, Chartered is new events by another hand, out-of-universe and endorsed. **None exist yet**, and the repo will **register** chartered works rather than host them (a third party's work has a different rights holder — filing it under `src/` would put it inside a CC BY-NC-ND scope Dermot can't license). Related but independent: fan images and music may be linked from the site as **referenced, not endorsed** — a link is a pointer, not approval, and referencing something never makes it chartered.

## Codex viewpoints add flavour and in-character debate — never doubt about canon

- **Codex viewpoints add flavour and in-character debate — never doubt about canon** (Dermot's ruling, 2026-08-21). This bounds the rule below rather than contradicting it. An entry may be partial, self-serving, devotional or mistaken *in its author's voice*, because that is what gives the Codex its texture; what it may never do is leave the reader unsure what is actually true in the world. The test is the reader, not the author: after reading, is canon **less settled** than before? If yes, the entry has overstepped, however well-attributed it is. Two habits keep the line: state the record's own position plainly where one exists rather than staging an unresolved contest between equals, and let a tradition keep its language without conceding that the thing the language names is an open question. `src/codex/the-borrowed-silhouette.md` is the worked example — it says outright that the category it examines cannot be occupied, and still lets the devotional annotation have the last word, because the annotation disputes the filing rather than the finding.

## Real-world fiction may be cited in the Codex and the Journal only when it is in the public domain on both sides of the Atlantic, and only as fiction

- **Real-world fiction may be cited in the Codex and the Journal only when it is in the public domain on both sides of the Atlantic, and only as fiction** (Dermot's direction, 2026-10-17 — `story-bible/intake-2026-10-17.md`, last section, at his questions *"which real-world fiction before a certain date can be referenced as codex without confusion?"* and *"anything already fully out of copyright and already in the public domain for example?"*). **Both dates, and a work must pass both:** published in the United States 95 or more years ago (as of 2026, published in or before 1930), and its author dead more than 70 years (as of 2026, died in or before 1955), which is Ireland's and the EU's term; each 1 January moves both lines forward a year. Anonymous and ancient works pass. **The translation must pass too:** a public-domain work quoted in a translation still in copyright is not public domain, so use a translation that meets both dates, or one of Dermot's own. **Where:** a Codex entry, in its in-world author's voice, and the Journal; never lore, the glossary or Archive narration, which name nothing from our century under the rule above. **As fiction:** named by title and author as a story, quoted briefly, and never offered as foreknowledge, prophecy, evidence or history, which is the confusion the rule guards against; a work whose premise touches a canon mechanism (an old Martian invasion, a voyage to the Moon) is cited only as the old fiction it is. The other standing rules still bind the citation: no named political ideology, no comparison of the Fellowship to real traditions, and scripture by its own convention (the World English Bible, itself public domain). A fiction outside the dates may still be discussed in the Journal by name, never quoted at length, as the 2026-10-17 trap notes on Vulcans, Elves and the Haruchai are.

## The Codex is not canon; each entry is canon-*for-its-author*

- **The Codex is not canon; each entry is canon-*for-its-author*.** This is the rule that makes the lore/codex boundary below work, and it cuts both ways. A codex entry is never the story asserting a fact — it is a named in-universe source's account, and it may be wrong, partial, self-serving, devotional, or flatly contradicted by `src/lore/`, by a chapter, or by another codex entry. What it may *not* be is arbitrary: it has to be a document that source would actually have produced, valid by their own lights — their era, their access, their institution's house style, their blind spots, the things they have reason to omit. Test an entry by asking "could this person have known this, and would they have written it this way?", not "is this true?". **The narrative mode is the author's** (Dermot's ruling, 2026-09-12): first person, an apparently omniscient narrator, a ballad, a transcript, a school reader — any mode a document of that kind would be written in — since the `::::: scene` / `::: pov` structure and the house prose style govern chapters, not the Codex; the mode changes nothing about the entry's standing, and an omniscient Codex narrator is *apparently* so, a posture the reader weighs. Two consequences: `author` is a **required** field on every codex entry (`lib/content-schema.js` enforces it — an entry with nobody behind it has no point of view to be valid from and silently becomes Archive-voice fact); and codex entries carry **no `canon_facts`**, unlike chapters, because they establish nothing. Ratification of the seasons as canon (2026-07-25) does not extend here.

## Explain gaps, inconsistencies and complexity in-story wherever possible

- **Explain gaps, inconsistencies and complexity in-story wherever possible** (Dermot's direction, 2026-08-15). A narrative gap, an apparent contradiction between two entries, or a tangle of overlapping names and tiers is **part of the story and should be answered from inside it**, not quietly tidied away and not left as an authorial shrug. The setting already supplies the machinery, and the rule is mostly an instruction to reach for it rather than invent a new one: the **Archive is an in-universe narrator with limits**, so *"the Archive has not established this and has not been asked to"* is a real answer, and a truthful one, in a way *"this is underspecified"* never is. The existing devices, in rough order of reach — the record declining to fix a date or a cause; [What the Record Refuses](src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md) for whole classes of thing the setting will not carry; the **lore/codex boundary** above, which relocates a contested reading to a named source rather than picking a winner; the **three-way reading** for contested events (divine influence / purely physical account / dispute the transmission itself, the third being the one normally dropped); *valid-for-its-author* codex framing; and deliberate mysteries that stay shut until their own season. **Complexity gets the same treatment as contradiction.** Where a structure is genuinely tangled — many names for one tier, two bodies with overlapping mandates, an office answering on two axes — the tangle is usually evidence about the thing, and saying *why* it is tangled is better writing than smoothing it flat. Worked example: the Safety Corps' investigation authority looked like a flat contradiction of two charter lines and resolved on the word *cross-disciplinary*, which was more interesting than either line alone.

  **This does not override *"mark the absence; do not explain it"***, and the two are easy to mistake for opposites. Sen's [Marked Absences](src/codex/marked-absences.md) doctrine governs gaps **in the in-universe record**: where the Archive's knowledge stops, you mark it and invent no cause, because *"an explained gap invites the record to defend a guess forever."* This convention governs gaps **in the shape of the work** — two entries that appear to disagree, a name that has drifted, a structure a reader will find bewildering. The line between them is exact and worth holding: **explain the epistemology, never invent the fact.** Saying *why* the record is thin here — limited instruments, contested transmission, three traditions naming one thing differently, a question of the wrong shape — is explaining shape, and is welcome. Saying *what* the missing fact was is inventing, and Sen is right that it costs more than it pays. When in doubt, the absence stays marked.

  Two further limits. This licenses explaining a gap, never manufacturing one for effect. And it is **not** a licence to leave real errors standing: a genuine mistake in arithmetic, chronology or terminology gets fixed, not diegetically excused.

## Changelog entries are plain and complete, not compressed; the Journal carries the reasoning

- **Changelog entries are plain and complete, not compressed; the Journal carries the reasoning** (agreed 2026-08-16 as a terseness rule; amended 2026-08-31 — Dermot's ruling that the terseness half was overstated, after the clipped register it produced read oddly in practice). The two files answer different questions and the changelog had drifted into answering both. **CHANGELOG answers *what changed*** — complete, every change, for retrieval, and its value is being exhaustive. **The Journal answers *what did I learn*** — selective, essayistic, for reading, and its value is being chosen. So an entry states what changed and where in ordinary prose, and may carry a sentence of why when that helps the reader — what it does not do is grow into a 200-word essay with quotable lines, which makes the changelog neither complete nor readable and has a second cost: the file is past a thousand lines, and **a large append-at-top file is exactly what conflicts and gets clobbered** — the essayistic style is part of why `[1.17.0]` and `[1.18.0]` were destroyed. Since 2026-10-15 `npm test` measures it: `scripts/check-changelog-length.js` warns on any `[Unreleased]` entry past about 120 words, so a long entry is trimmed while it still may be. The same day the file, at 1.24 MB, had its released sections older than the newest five moved verbatim into `changelog/` (one file per ten minor versions); a released section may be moved there, never edited. If the reasoning is worth real prose it belongs in a journal entry; the worked example is 2026-08-16: the Kieme changelog entry and the *Kieme Means Gill* journal entry say much the same thing, and only one of them needed to.

## The Journal and story-bible/ are written at the contemplative reading level; being impossible to misunderstand outranks plain language

- **The Journal and `story-bible/` are written at the contemplative reading level; being impossible to misunderstand outranks plain language** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-06 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-06.md`, fifth section). This is a register, not a gate: journal entries stay on every edition that carries the section, and it is the *contemplative* tier's register — unhurried, exact, deep — that they are written in. What it means in practice: a technical term or a proper name is welcome when it is defined on first use, and is preferred to a vaguer plain word; a claim that is only loosely true is a misunderstanding waiting to happen, so a precision pass checks facts and attributions as well as wording; the 800–1,200-word band from `story-bible/edition-reader-profiles.md` applies, and an entry that outgrows it splits along its section breaks into cross-linked entries rather than compressing. The same standard governs `story-bible/` itself — intakes and notes are written to be exact and verifiable, not simple — which is the internal half of the same rule. There is no `plain:` register for either. The four entries of 2026-09-06 (*The Picture the Maths Was Invented to Replace* and its three successors) are the worked example: one 2,700-word entry became four, and the pass corrected three historical attributions the first draft had wrong. **Who the Journal is for** (Dermot, later the same day, same intake, sixth section): not the child reading the pets thread — the children's edition deliberately carries no Journal entry, since `isTopicPageIncluded` admits one only by matching tag and none carries the pets topic — but the parent or teacher reading that thread aloud, and beyond them *critics, reviewers, peer authors and students of the writing*. The register follows from those readers rather than from the tier, and it gives the Journal its publication test: would one of them learn something from the entry about how the work is made? The About page's disclosure of how the site is written is load-bearing for the same readers; don't weaken it.

## The children's glossary follows the links

- **The children's glossary follows the links** (Dermot, 2026-10-05, verbatim *Option 1, on CHILDREN_TIER*, choosing among three shapes put to him at his question *Should I make almost all glossary entries available to the Undercover Pets edition?* — `story-bible/intake-2026-10-05.md`). A glossary entry joins the children's tier when a page the tier carries links it — a chapter, a cast page, a timeline entry, a lore or codex page, or an edition's own copy in `lib/editions.js` — and it carries a `plain:` line. It joins by taking the tier's topic tag, `undercover-pets.com`, which `CHILDREN_TIER` names and every children's edition inherits, so the filter needed nothing new. Not almost all, for three reasons: the pets prose links one entry, so nothing on the site leads a child to the other eighty; the index shows the adult-register `short`, and the reader profile's named failure is a wall of text; and the tag means a page chose the tier. Five entries today — Smart Pet, Cyborg, Plural Minds, UCSD and Boundary Zone — where the pets edition had two. On a children's-tier build `src/glossary/index.md` shows each entry's `plain:` line in place of its `short`, under a plain intro. `scripts/check-children-glossary.js` (in `npm test`) is the gate, run once per edition at the tier with that edition's own filter: a page the edition carries may not link an entry it renders as a placeholder or one without a `plain:` line, an entry on the tier without one fails, and an entry nothing on the tier links warns. **One hop, not a closure:** a carried entry's own onward links to other entries are not enforced and stop at the tier's edge, where the child meets the plain-register placeholder — the gate's first run enforced them and found Boundary Zone alone reaching six cosmology entries and through them the whole web, which is the almost-all shape declined; an entry one onward link reaches may still join on it (UCSD did, from Cyborg), and the gate prints the edge count per edition rather than warning on each. The habit that follows: a pets or Told page that links a new glossary entry carries that entry's `plain:` line and tag in the same change, and the `plain:` is a clarification of the entry, never a second account of it.

## Images

- **Images:** live in `src/images/<characters|lore|codex|hero>/`, ~1600px long edge for lore/hero and ~1200px for portraits, JPG (quality ~85). `image_alt` must describe what the image *actually shows* — the July 2026 audit found many alt texts describing an image the file no longer contained. Codex entries use designed title cards, not photographs; regenerate those with `scripts/make-codex-cover.ps1`, landscape lore emblem cards with `scripts/make-emblem-card.ps1`, and PENDING placeholder cards with `scripts/make-placeholder-card.ps1` (see `story-bible/images.md`) rather than an image generator, which cannot spell. **The portfolio site's own-work-only rule does not apply here, and the exemption has a written shape** (Dermot's ruling, 2026-09-03, stated in full under *Conventions* in `story-bible/images.md`): a generator may make only what no camera can — a non-human body, a habitat, an artificial mind, an artefact, a portrait of someone who does not exist; anything a camera can photograph comes from Dermot's own frames or takes a card; and every image the pipeline touches is registered in `story-bible/image-prompts.md` and stays in this repository, never returning to the portfolio or a competition. **The same rule governs video** (Dermot, 11 October 2026): a generated clip may show only what no camera can film, its prompt is kept in `story-bible/video-prompts.md`, and the modesty bar and tone rule apply to every frame. A generated still or clip looks as if taken by a 29th-century camera or recording device held or mounted by someone in the record, with no overlays or date stamps, and a scene from before cameras existed is shot the same way, as a reconstruction made at the story's present (Dermot, 2026-10-11). **A generated photograph is finished toward his own on the way in** (Dermot, 2026-10-07: the generator *"looks like it was training on stock photos"*, an *"imperfect photograph taken in the future"* is welcome, strength 1 the default): `import-image.ps1` runs `scripts/photo-finish.py`, which lifts the black floor, eases saturation, softens a touch and adds grain only to the measured shortfall against `scripts/photo-profile.json`; cards, emblems and his own plates take `-Finish 0`. **Which pages carry an image** (same day): a character with a body gets a portrait and a mind does not, and a body the record says nothing of, for a character known only through what they wrote, said or left on an instrument, is not relevant, so that page is complete without one until a page states the body (Dermot, 2026-10-16, clarifying; `story-bible/images.md`, *When the body is not relevant*); a place, people, thing or sky gets an illustration, an institution an emblem card, an abstract concept, doctrine or law none, and a page without one is complete, not pending.

## Dates are absolute, never relative

- **Dates are absolute, never relative** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-06 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-06.md`, ninth section). The story's present is 2826 UCSD (`src/lore/year-zero.md`), and Season 1's prequels, the timeline and several in-universe documents once dated themselves against it as *Year -4*, *Year -11*, *Year 0*. Those labels are retired: a date is `<year> UCSD` (with a season and day where the source gives one — `2815 UCSD, Late Summer, Day 47`), and an event before the standard's adoption within the Concord's framework carries an AD or CE date, with the UCSD conversion beside it where the record gives one (`1216 AD (1416 UCSD)`, `2137 CE (2337 UCSD)`). The adoption is the Concord's founding under the Tycho Accords, 2543 UCSD (2343 CE), so anything earlier is written CE first (Dermot, 2026-10-13, accepting the recommendation; the year turns on the old New Year, not the solstice, so the offset is exactly 200 on every day; the Celtic Union's CE was reconciled to Sol's on contact). *The 29th century* names the present in UCSD (2801–2900 UCSD, the 27th century CE); where a sentence sets it beside our own time, label the other century CE or say *six centuries*, so the gap does not read as eight; UCSD is CE plus 200 (`src/glossary/ucsd.md`). Keep the voice's own era word — devotional sources say AD, the Archive says CE — and never leave a pre-adoption year bare. The `year-minus-` timeline filenames are permalinks and stay as they are. One in-universe exception is deliberately untouched pending his ruling: Threshold Station's own report numbering (*Report 7 (Year 3)*) in S01E01C02 and its two echoes, whose base the record has not established.

## Alien species may carry their own pronoun systems

- **Alien species may carry their own pronoun systems** (Dermot's direction, 2026-08-07). A species is not obliged to fit *he/she/they*: uniquely different personal pronouns are welcome, as a **per-species canon vocabulary decision** made once and applied consistently — declared in the species' lore entry, with the paradigm (subject/object/possessive forms) registered in the glossary like any coined term, never improvised per-scene or varied per-character-whim. Individual variation within a species remains possible where the species' own system provides for it, and that too is stated, not ad-libbed. Existing usage stands (Syra *she*, Qiren Tal *they*, Sethka Ru *he*): no retrofit is mandated, and giving an established species a new system later is a canon change needing a corpus sweep plus a migration-map row. Reader legibility governs the writing: introduce novel forms where context teaches them, and let the glossary carry the grammar. One craft option worth stating: the record's default is translation — alien grammar rendered into the reader's pronouns the way alien calendars are converted to UCSD — so a species whose *own* forms are kept untranslated in the record is making a statement by it, the same statement the Krenyi make by keeping their own calendar.

## Hard science fiction by default

- **Hard science fiction by default** (Dermot's direction, 2026-08-09). ## Each rung's register, named

  - **Each rung's register, named** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-22, verbatim: *"The child reader tier should be interesting and playful, the young adult tier should be more mysterious with a sense of exploration and adventure, the general tier should go deeper, and the contemplative tier is allowed to question everything"*). The bullet above said the ladder selects by register without ever saying what each register is; this supplies it, and it is an authoring instruction, not a new gate. **Children — interesting and playful:** short scenes and plain words, a story enjoyed rather than decoded, and delight is the measure. **Young adult — sensual and romantic as decency allows, with exploration and adventure:** the reader goes out and finds things. *Sensual* is meant in its root sense, of the senses: the felt texture of places and of attraction, attention and nearness, rendered and never displayed. *Romantic* means longing, devotion, courtship and choice. Nothing physical goes past what a parent reading aloud would pass, romance between young characters stays with characters of an age for it, and the modesty bar governs every image. **General — deeper, mysterious and strategic:** the same record with its reasons under it, the institutions, the cost and the long game, the Lore and Codex behind the chapter, and the mystery an invitation rather than a weight. **Contemplative — allowed to question everything:** nothing is off the table there, including the record's own premises and this file's own rules. **Re-grounded 2026-10-19** (`story-bible/intake-2026-10-19.md`, verbatim: *"The young adult reading tier should be as sensual and romantic as decency allows. The general adult reading tier should be mysterious and strategic. Courage and heroism, with patience and discretion rather than violence."*, readings *"A A A A"*): *mysterious* moved from the young adult rung to the general rung, the young adult rung gained the sensual and romantic register, and the general rung gained the strategic one. Both are added to what each rung already had. **At every rung, courage and heroism come with patience and discretion rather than violence.** That is a standing rule, the positive face of *no guns-and-spaceships* and *the case ends marked, not shot*: a hero here is the one who waits, withholds, chooses the harder restraint, and is still there when it matters. Read it as escalating permission rather than escalating seriousness — a playful page is not a shallow one, and a questioning page is not a grim one; the tone guardrail and the shadow-shaped rule hold at every rung. **The one line that keeps this from breaking the ladder's invariant: questioning is not contradicting.** The contemplative tier may ask anything and may leave the asking open; what it may never do is withdraw or deny a reading a lower rung was given, because the tiers nest and *nothing read on a lower rung is withdrawn or contradicted on a higher one* (`src/tiers/index.md`). A question asked at the top rung is an addition like any other. This is also why the epistemics can surface there explicitly — the middle rung of belief, the marked absences, the gradings — while the rungs below simply practise them.

## Mysteries are shadow-shaped, never crime-shaped

- **Mysteries are shadow-shaped, never crime-shaped** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-08 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-08.md`, sixteenth, eighteenth and nineteenth sections, verbatim: *"Several unsolved mysteries. Not murders, just shadows."*, *"Shadow option 3 is preferred to murder mysteries"*, *"ordinary minor crimes still occur but local detectives often find themselves touching on cosmological mysteries that require calling in the Star Rangers"*, and, for the register, *"the mystery and enigmatic quality of Sapphire & Steel without the darker elements of horror"*). A mystery in this record is a real thing seen through a gap that cannot be crossed — a Membrane Shadow, a marked absence, a reading nobody was there for — and it stays unsolved because it is unsolvable, marked and kept on Sen's doctrine rather than closed. No murder mysteries, and no detective plot as the engine of a season. Ordinary minor crime still happens and stays where canon keeps it (a habitat bureau's caseload of theft and contract disputes); a local detective is the entry point, and the case turns when it touches something cosmological and the Rangers have to be called in — by invitation, since Rangers hold no authority inside a self-governing habitat. The ladder has three rungs (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-08, twenty-fourth section: *"Five-O intermediate between the Star Rangers and the local detective bureau of each habitat"*): a habitat bureau sends a case that runs past its edge up to Orbital Five-O, and Five-O, on the Governor's authority, is the tier that recognises a case has stopped being a Compact matter and issues the invitation. The bureaus do not call the Rangers, and the Rangers do not come uncalled. The register is Sapphire & Steel's mystery and enigma — the confined ordinary setting, the small stable wrongness, the sparing operatives who are called in, the anomaly never explained — without its horror, which the tone rule below refuses, and without its devices, which the trope default refuses. The first season shaped this way is *Below the Roof*'s second (`story-bible/below-the-roof-second-season-treatment.md`).

## Endings are recognitions, not reveals

- **Endings are recognitions, not reveals** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-20 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-20.md`, verbatim: *"Most detective stories seem ordinary, almost mundane. I prefer something more mysterious, existential and enigmatic. I thought the Hydrogen Sonata, by Iain M. Banks, fell flat at the end as did the The Lady in Lake as the end of the Witcher series."*; the test below drafted at his word and approved the same night). The ending test for any arc, season or mystery in this record: **the ending must change how the beginning reads.** A reveal that leaves the reader where they were is the flat ending, however much it explains; a recognition that re-lights what came before is the enigmatic one, even when nothing is explained. The two failures named are the two to refuse: the answer that is smaller than the question and changes nothing once found (*The Hydrogen Sonata*), and the ambiguity reached by exhaustion, threads cut rather than tied or deliberately left, the register shifting as the author leaves the room (*The Lady of the Lake*). This is the other half of the shadow-shaped rule above: that rule says a mystery stays unsolved because it is unsolvable and is marked, not explained; this one says what the marking must do to the reader. An arc may end on an open question only if the question is now larger and better lit than when it was asked, and the cost of asking it has been paid on the page by someone in the record.

## Familiarity is not evidence: the reader may assume nothing about what is real

- **Familiarity is not evidence: the reader may assume nothing about what is real** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-22 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-22.md`, verbatim: *"So the mysterious enigmatic quality of Fian Ilchruinne is that the reader can never make assumptions about what is real and what reality consists of, even when the surroundings look familiar"*; name and shape settled by him the same day). The laws of this cosmos are stable, knowable and promised — `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md` is not softened by this rule and nothing in it is withdrawn. What is never promised is that a familiar *surrounding* is seated on a familiar *reality*. An ordinary corridor, an ordinary noon, an ordinary human word may each rest on something the reader has no instrument for, and the record will not signal which. This is the third member of the mystery family and sits beneath the other two: the shadow-shaped rule says a mystery is unsolvable and marked; the ending test says what the marking must do to the reader; this says the reader is standing on unassumable ground the whole time, not only at the gap. It follows that the record never uses familiarity to reassure, never lets a resemblance carry an inference the prose has not earned, and never resolves an enigma by returning the reader to solid ground — the ground was never the thing that was solid.

## The corollary: nothing acquires a repeatable answer

  - **The corollary: nothing acquires a repeatable answer** (his ruling the same day that this is a clause of the rule and not a fourth one). What kills an enigma is not explanation but **leverage that repeats** — the named failures are *The Witcher* and the fantasy RPG, where the monster becomes ordinary at the moment its answer becomes routine. A creature, boundary phenomenon or Unfounded manifestation may be described, measured, classified by frame and written up at length; it may never acquire a counter-move a competent officer can run twice and expect the same result. The test for any new monster, protocol or protective measure is exactly that question. The record is already built this way and the reasons are worth knowing, because they are what a new page must not break: **monsters are rare exceptions, valid under boundary conditions** (his words, same day), so the protection is ontological rather than editorial — a boundary condition cannot be scheduled, manufactured or farmed; `src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md` predicts nothing it was not built from and says so; the Unfounded are *countered not addressed, starved not destroyed*; and the instruments register without conferring leverage. `src/lore/meta-dimensional-beings.md` is the page to watch: it lists monster types with feeding behaviours, which is a bestiary's shape, and what keeps it from being one is that it says what a thing *does* and never what anyone does back. Detectable precursors are warning, not leverage. The entry it must never gain is a remedy.

## The reader-facing instrument: the silhouette that fires and is never confirmed

  - **The reader-facing instrument: the silhouette that fires and is never confirmed.** A people may almost read as a familiar mythic type and not be one — the technique Dermot brought back from *Pendragon: Rise of Merlin*, 2026-09-22, where the effect was carried by clothing and bearing and one near-dwarf people was of ordinary height. Two things make it work and neither is decoration. The carriers must be **choices** — dress, carriage, custom, register — and never physiology, because a pointed ear is a checkable claim and checking it collapses the effect whichever way it goes. And the one feature that would sort the impression is the one to withhold. It is stabler here than in the work it came from, which has magic in it and so can never resolve the reading: this record's refusals are already published, so the referent is known to be unavailable and the impression can only ever be the reader's own. Vehicle is the human isolates — the Kingdom of the Five Islands, the Celtic Union's worlds, Plainmark, the Generation Ark descendants — never the Veyr or Chthonari, who are too far from any myth for the effect to start. The record never confirms the impression and never denies it page by page; the standing refusal did that once, in its own voice. ## The science-fiction form of the same dilemma: aliens

  - **The science-fiction form of the same dilemma: aliens** (Dermot, 2026-09-22, verbatim: *"A similar dilemma for science fiction is aliens who become too familiar and too humanoid unless that is the point is the story, but aliens as space monsters is worse, in my opinion"*). Two failures, and he ranks them. **Too familiar, too humanoid** is the first: a people becomes a person in a costume, the reader's own frame goes unchallenged, and the encounter stops being one — with his own exception, *unless that is the point of the story*, which is exactly what the human isolates are for. The Kingdom of the Five Islands is human by descent and the point is that it is human; Strozzi's whole position is that this makes the work harder rather than easier, and the *tribute* nine months are the proof. **Aliens as space monsters is worse**, because the first failure flattens a people and the second deletes the category: a people becomes a hazard, which is the corollary's failure and a breach of the cast principles at once — antagonists misguided and never pure villains, war never assumed of a people, minds not ranked. The record is already built against both and the machinery is worth knowing: no other people in it is a metazoan; the Corps files peoples by *frame* and *lineage* with no claim of kinship, while monsters are classified somewhere else entirely as meta-dimensional, and keeping those two filings apart is what stops *alien* collapsing into *monster*; and the record says in its own voice that nothing prefers a humanoid (`src/lore/arilon.md` on Champion selection, and the Dryad which is not a humanoid being). So a new people is built from its world and its senses outward — the Chthonari feel through rock, the Veyr are lattice-framed — and *humanoid* appears only where it belongs, as an off-worlder's yardstick and never as the shape a people happens to have. **The usage rule that follows** (Dermot, 2026-09-28 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-28.md`, verbatim: *"humans are not privileged in the Star Rangers compared with other sapient beings, words like alien or xeno as in xenolinguists refer to comparative studies or studies of communication between beings with different physiology, no-one is called an 'alien' unless unknown to the Star Rangers"*): in the Corps' own voice a discipline's *xeno-* names a comparison across peoples or physiologies and never a class of being; *alien* is a word for a being unknown to the Star Rangers and for nothing else; and a human Ranger's own culture is one case among the cases, never the baseline the others are measured from. Prose and image prompts follow it. ## The middle rung is a belief, and faith sits on it

## The modesty bar: an image clears two bars, and the repository's is the lower one

- **The modesty bar: an image clears two bars, and the repository's is the lower one** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-18 — `story-bible/intake-2026-09-18.md`, the addendum's evaluation made a standing rule at his instruction, verbatim *Add the modesty bar to CLAUDE.md as a standing rule*). **The public-repo bar** is decency in the ordinary sense: no nudity, nothing a hosting service's terms refuse, nothing that would embarrass on a public commit; a file that clears it may exist in the repository. **The site's bar** is the 4 September principle made concrete for pictures — *nothing is unsafe or unsuitable for anyone of any age to view* — so an image on any page, hero, portrait, gallery or codex art, is one a parent reading the children's edition aloud would pass without noticing: dressed for the work or the place and never to be looked at, no swimwear or underwear register, no neckline the eye goes to first, and for a named character nothing that invites the viewer (the 28 July portrait guardrails in `story-bible/image-prompts.md` — attention off-frame, at work, *this is Tissadelle, not a type*). No stratum above the Celestials appears in a scene as itself, has a POV block, a line of dialogue or a character page. What such a stratum does reaches the page through one of five channels: structure (laws and invariants), design (possibility space and archetypes), an avatar, a Champion, or a prophet. Each channel is bounded, and so is everyone a reader can meet, from a Celestial down to a person. What traditions call a theophany is usually a Levril and more rarely a Celestial (Dermot, same day, verbatim *"Theophanies are usually either Levrils or more rarely Celestials."*): a bounded being from the immanent tiers, never a god, so the no-theophany line now stands above the Celestials, not above the Levrils. These lower strata may be considered messengers, or ordinary angels in the broader, more general sense (Dermot, same day). The word *angel* still stays in the Codex and the overlay under the canonical guide's register rule, and the archangels stay unmapped. A prophet is filed like a Champion: a material being speaking in their own voice. The record detects no message to a prophet and files no foretelling, because the future is open. It neither makes nor denies a claim of inspiration, which it files under the subtle influence, and it reads a tradition's foretold-and-fulfilled as that tradition's reading of the eternal strata (Dermot's *"Option A"*, same day). In-world statement: *Through, Not As* in `src/lore/cosmic-cascade.md`. The rule binds prose, lore and image prompts. A codex author may still describe someone as unlimited, in their own voice, and the record reads that as Partial Observation. All five readings were ruled the same day as recommended (*"Scope A, Prophet A"*, *"3A 4A 5A"*). One of them concerns the single Kieme-perspective narration the story bible allows: it stays, and it is impartial, not all-knowing, so it cannot tell which branch the present will settle on.

## Tone

- **Tone:** the setting may be unsettling but must not tip into horror — hint at the dark fact rather than depicting it. This applies to prose and to image prompts alike (a cyber-revenant portrait showing exposed throat machinery was rejected on exactly this line; the approved version sealed the collar and left one silver seam). **Quiet jokes are allowed; sarcasm, mockery and cynical horror are not** (Dermot's direction, 2026-10-18 — `story-bible/intake-2026-10-18.md`, verbatim: *"Quiet jokes are allowed. I would never accept the sarcasm and mockery of Red Dwarf or the cynical horror of Dr. Who."*). A quiet joke is one the record does not signal, made by structure or a character's dry precision, and it costs nobody their dignity: *Ask and It Will Be Reviewed* (prayer filed as a permissions office), the two true survey entries that disagree until the range column is read, Barsik's file that says *mascot* and does not go very far. Refused: a joke at a character's expense, a put-down as a running gag, a narrator who mocks the people in the record; and dread used to argue that hope was foolish. The test is whether the person the joke is about would recognise themselves in it without being diminished, and whether a dark passage leaves hope where it was. A Codex author or a character may be dry in their own voice; the record's own voice never takes the mocking register. Undercover Pets keeps its own *cute, cool and clever*. **Erotic writing has no place in the published work at any rung** (Dermot, 2026-10-19, choosing shape A after his note that it *"would distract from the overall quality of Fian Ilchruinne"*): intimacy is written in the house voice, of the senses and never displayed, because the record's strength is what it withholds. It is a craft rule and not a moral one (his clarification the same day). What it guards against is superficial or crude explicitness, and it passes no judgement on the genre.
