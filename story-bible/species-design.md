# Designing the species — what is already forced, and what is open

Started 13 August 2026, after the ruling that **the Krenyi are the only other
sapient humanoids** and that no archetype requires a mind to have a body plan at
all. That closed a question and opened five: the Veyr Basaltborn, Pelagene
Littoral, Serephine Dunekin, Verdani and Vessik all have characters on the
roster and **no described body anywhere in prose**, which is why three of their
portraits had quietly drifted humanoid.

This file is the working ground for fixing that. It is not a decision record
yet — the decisions are Dermot's.

**The premise above is sharpened, not overturned — 16 August 2026, #449.**
Dermot ruled the Pandoids of Fliade sapient and *"one of very few humanoid like
aliens"*, then that they derive from **a bear-like alien ancestor, not
terrestrial bears, despite the name**. The published refusal now turns on the
**human frame** rather than on posture: the Krenyi remain the only other people
built on it, and an upright body arrived at independently is not a second entry
on that list.

**The frame is the point — 30 September 2026.** Dermot's direction, verbatim:
*"Krenyi are the strangest non-human people because they look superficially
humanoid and could pass as almost human."* This is the 22 September exception
(*too humanoid unless that is the point of the story*) applied to the one
people on the human frame: the resemblance is the work, and every difference
lands as a correction to an assumption the frame invited. Realized on the lore
page as *The Nearest Frame Is the Strangest*. For the other species nothing
changes — they still owe the record a body that follows from a world, and the
Krenyi are not a licence for a second near-human people.

**So nothing here needs redoing.** The five species below still owe the record a
body that follows from a world, and *"could this be a human with features
applied?"* is still the test that fails a drifted portrait. What changed is that
the test is now stated in the terms it always meant — frame, not posture — which
if anything makes it easier to apply. The one new caution is naming: a familiar
animal in a species' name describes the observers who coined it, so it is never
a licence to reach for that animal's body when briefing an image.

## Scope: where a homeworld may be

Dermot, 13 August 2026: **anywhere in the known Concordant, or elsewhere in the
membrane where crossover is safe.**

So the map is not the constraint. A species need not come from anywhere near the
Solar System's neighbourhood, need not sit on an established fold route, and
need not be reachable conveniently — and where it is *not* conveniently
reachable, that is itself a fact worth using, because the Corps' relationship
with a people is shaped by how hard they are to get to.

## The good news: most of it is already written

Four of the five have their environment fixed in a line on the character page,
and an environment does most of the work of a body. Nothing below is invention —
it is what the existing text already commits to, gathered in one place.

### Veyr Basaltborn — Veyr-3

> Active basaltic volcanism, dense mineral aerosols, micro-shocks, and a
> gravitational field heavy enough that **human joints would degrade in a
> generation**.

The gravity is the loudest constraint in the whole set and it is unforgiving:
**a tall bipedal frame is not available.** High gravity favours low, broad,
short-limbed, heavily braced — weight close to the ground, load paths short,
probably more than four points of contact. Dense mineral aerosols argue for
sealed or filtering respiration and for eyes protected against abrasion rather
than glare. Micro-shocks argue for a body that tolerates constant vibration,
which may mean sensing *through* it rather than being troubled by it.

*Qiren Tal is an engineer doing pressure-safe hull diagnostics and thermal
systems work — a career that follows directly from being built for pressure,
heat and vibration. The species produced the specialism.*

### Pelagene Littoral — an ocean world

> Built for **buoyancy control and dissolved-oxygen variance**. On a station
> she breathes standard atmosphere with **assistive hydration cycles**, and
> performs best in humid, high-pressure conditions.

*Littoral* is doing real work in the name: this is the shore and shallows, not
the abyss — a zone of tides, turbidity, salinity change and daily exposure,
which is a far more variable place to evolve than deep water and argues for a
tolerant generalist rather than a specialist. Buoyancy control implies a
dedicated organ. Assistive hydration cycles imply an integument that loses water
to dry air — so a Pelagene on a station is not merely uncomfortable, she is
**slowly drying**, and every posting has a clock on it that nobody else's does.

*Mira "reads the water before the instruments do" — an environmental surveyor
whose whole professional value is a sensorium tuned to a medium.*

### Serephine Dunekin — Kharis Belt IV

> Thin atmosphere, high UV flux, and a thermal inversion between day and night
> that **would kill an unprotected person in under an hour on either side**.
> Light-scattering eye membranes, water-conservative metabolism, respiratory
> efficiency that makes dense-atmosphere work manageable **but never easy**.

Thin air is the constraint that most strongly rules out a compact body: gas
exchange at low partial pressure wants surface area, which is an argument for
something extended, folded, or many-surfaced rather than a torso with two lungs
in it. High UV argues for an opaque, reflective or actively shielding
integument. The lethal thermal swing argues for either heavy insulation or a
scheduled inactivity — and *scheduled* is interesting, because Sethka's own page
has him planning movement around exposure windows, which may be less a
professional habit than a biological one he has made professional.

**Note the direction of the respiratory clause.** Dense atmosphere is *harder*
for him, not easier. Station air is a burden he carries all day.

### Vessik and the Chthonari — the Undersong Belt

This one is not open. It is **already solved, and it is the model for the rest.**

> A vessik is a burrow commensal — small, low, **six-limbed**, and built on the
> same physical principle as the people whose tunnels it lives in. It has no
> ears… Thrum's alarm is a hard, arrhythmic **drumming of the forelimbs against
> deck plate**, and it carries through the structure for most of the length of
> the deck.

Three things worth taking from it. It is **six-limbed and low** — no apology, no
explanation, and no reader has ever needed one. Its sensory world is
**substrate-borne**, so it is deaf to airborne sound and blind to a human face,
which makes it the one resident of Deck 7 who cannot be charmed — the biology
generates the character. And crucially: *built on the same physical principle as
the people whose tunnels it lives in*, which means **the Chthonari are already
committed** to being low, six-limbed and substrate-hearing. That commitment was
made by describing an animal.

### Verdani — Verdance

> Distinct thallenwort strains, adapted over generations to the **light quality,
> humidity, and canopy density** of one of Verdance's four biomes… Verdani
> cannot interbreed with any other known species; the symbiosis itself cannot be
> transplanted into a non-Verdani host.

The word doing the work is **host**. A Verdani is a partner to a photosynthetic
symbiont, so light is the currency and surface area is the wealth — which argues
for a body that presents area to the sky rather than minimising it, and possibly
for a life stage that does not move much. Canopy density as an adaptive variable
implies vertical structure in the habitat and therefore in the people.

**The four biomes are a gift and a trap.** They give the species genuine internal
variety — the [cast principle](/story-bible) against monoculture, built into the
biology — but four regional variants is also exactly how a species becomes four
stereotypes. Use them for texture, not for temperament.

## The questions that actually decide a body

Rather than starting from a silhouette, these are the ones that produce one.
Answering three or four is enough to brief a portrait.

1. **What does the world do to a body?** Gravity, pressure, atmosphere,
   temperature range, light. This alone rules out most shapes.
2. **How does it sense?** The single highest-value question for character, as
   Thrum proves. A sensorium determines what a person can be wrong about.
3. **How many limbs, and what are they for?** Manipulation and locomotion need
   not be the same limbs, and in most of these worlds there is no reason they
   would be.
4. **Is it symmetrical, and about what?** Bilateral is a chordate accident, not
   a law. Radial, rotational and spiral bodies are all available.
5. **What is its life cycle?** The [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/)
   already have two adult forms and no hierarchy between them; a species with a
   sessile stage, a moult, or a colonial phase is more interesting than one with
   a childhood.
6. **What does it find hard about a Ranger station?** The most useful single
   fact for the fiction, because it is the one that costs a character something
   daily. Mira's hydration cycles and Sethka's respiratory burden are both this.
7. **What did the biology make them good at, and what job followed?** Every one
   of the five already has a specialism that fits their world.

## Is dexterity a requirement? (Dermot's question, 13 August)

*"Other primates have hands but are they less intelligent than dolphins or
elephants? Is dexterity an actual requirement for space faring sapient beings?"*

**Dexterity yes, hands no — and it is probably not the real bottleneck anyway.**

What is actually required is **manipulation finer than the manipulator**: the
ability to make a thing more precise than the body that made it, and to hold
something steady while acting on it with something else. Hands are one solution,
late and awkward. The steadiness half is the half that gets forgotten, and it is
what dolphins lack — not intelligence, not sociality, just any way to *hold*
anything. Elephants are the sharper case, because a trunk is a muscular
hydrostat with finer control at the tip than a human hand, and they have no
technology. So a fine manipulator is not sufficient either.

Alternatives that genuinely work: muscular hydrostats (trunks, tentacles — an
octopus arm has distributed control and eight of them is a better workshop than
two hands); mouthparts and palps; **many small limbs in concert**, which this
record has already committed to via the six-limbed Vessik and therefore the
Chthonari; building by growing or secreting rather than assembling; and
**cooperative manipulation**, where the unit of work is several bodies — termites
build climate-controlled towers and no individual has dexterity worth the name.

**The bottleneck worth using instead is high-energy materials processing** —
what gets a people from shaped stone to metallurgy to pressure vessels. It is
harder to route around than manipulation and, unlike manipulation, it is
*different on every one of these worlds*, so it generates history rather than
anatomy:

| Species | Bottleneck | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Veyr** | None — active basalt hands them heat and pressure at the surface | Metallurgy early and free; the *hard* problem is precision on a world that shakes, so their engineering is about margin and tolerance. **Worked up in full: `src/lore/veyr-basaltborn.md`.** |
| **Pelagene** | No open combustion underwater | A metallurgy problem, not a hands problem. Vents, electrochemistry, or a long detour through biology |
| **Serephine** | Thin atmosphere burns poorly | But high UV is free energy and the daily thermal swing is enormous — a gradient civilisation rather than a fire one |
| **Chthonari** | — | They **excavate**: the Undersong Belt's own page calls it *"excavation as composition"*. Building by removing material needs little dexterity and a great deal of judgement |
| **Verdani** | Symbiont-dependent, so energy budget is light-limited | Slow, and possibly a people for whom haste is genuinely expensive |

**And the setting already grants the deeper point.** The Krenyi build no agentic
machines — *"needing no AI and wanting none are, to them, one fact rather than
two."* A species can be fully capable and simply decline a technology. Fold
transit exists, and the Chthonari engineer who left Thrum on Eden was posted in,
so **a people can reach space by being reached**. Spacefaring does not require
having solved everything alone.

**Design instruction that falls out of this:** give each species a *different
bottleneck and a different way round it*. That produces five civilisations with
distinct histories, rather than five body plans with the same story attached.

## Traps specific to this setting

- **The Earth smuggled in by a single word.** Dermot, 13 August: *"it might not
  be water as we know it"*, and *"undersea does not have to mean H₂O."* The
  danger is not that a brief says water — it is that a brief says **aquatic**,
  or **desert**, or **jungle**, and imports an entire Earth with it: tides, a
  breathable atmosphere above the surface, sunlight to a useful depth, a
  shoreline, and a chemistry that supports the same reactions ours does. None of
  that follows from *a world covered in fluid*. Ask instead what the medium
  actually fixes — pressure, density, opacity, thermal behaviour, what will
  dissolve in it — and design from those. Nerath turned out to be **several
  fluids lying stably on one another**, which is why its people's specialism is
  *fluid-boundary anomalies*, a phrase that means almost nothing on a
  single-ocean world. Now a standing refusal on
  `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`.
- **The allegory.** A species designed as a metaphor for a human subgroup
  or country — the Star Trek pattern, rejected by Dermot outright
  (29 August 2026, verbatim in `intake-2026-08-29.md`; theme note in
  `ideas.md`). The test: if the page summarises as "they're basically X"
  for any human group, start over from the world. This file's method is
  the antidote — a people derived from gravity, cold, or dense air has no
  room to be a cipher, because the world got there first.
- **The planet of hats.** One environment, one temperament, one job, no internal
  disagreement. The cast principles forbid it for humans and the same reasoning
  applies here. The Krenyi page is the standard to match — a whole practice, a
  named cost, a failure mode they would name themselves.
- **The humanoid frame with features applied.** The Qiren Tal portrait failed on
  exactly this: insectoid head and carapace on a bipedal two-armed body. Adding
  features to a human silhouette is not designing a species.
- **Technology that announces itself.** These peoples get the same treatment as
  everyone else — no glowing rectangles, no holograms, matte objects, and
  settlements that look like whatever suits their world rather than like the
  future. A high-gravity volcanic civilisation may well look ancient.
- **Explaining the biology in dialogue.** The record's habit is to state a fact
  once, flatly, and let the reader do the work. Thrum's page never explains why
  a burrow animal drums; it says what the drumming means where it comes from and
  what it means on Deck 7, and stops.
- **Making difference into difficulty only.** Sethka finds station air hard;
  that is good. But a species should also be *better* at something than
  everyone around it, or the difference reads as a disability rather than a
  different world.
- **The human concept with alien furniture.** A people whose body was derived
  from their world and whose family, career, status, death and personhood
  are ours with the nouns changed. Dermot's direction, 14 September 2026:
  *alien cultures have very different concepts of relationships, life, death,
  family, career, role, status, legacy, individuality and personhood* — see
  `mind-design.md`'s eighth question, which asks each of the ten for every
  people and allows *absent* and *unaskable* as answers. The body note gets
  a people's senses right; if the mind note is skipped, the senses end up
  serving a human life.

## The uniform equivalent (Dermot's direction, 10 September 2026)

*"Aliens have uniform equivalents"* (`intake-2026-09-10.md`, fourth
section), on the day the Rangers' uniform was given its reason — the
hospital's: a mark a stranger in a working place reads to know who does
what, built for the work, announcing a function and not a claim on anyone.
That adds one question to the ones above that decide a body, and it is a
question about the *medium*, since the job is universal and the form is
each people's own. `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md` now says this
generally and asserts nothing per species; this is where the per-species
answer gets derived when a page needs it. Nothing below is canon.

- **Krenyi** — a clothed, humanoid, calendar-keeping people; cloth and a
  worn mark are available and would be the boring answer. Shapes: (a) worn
  marks of their own grammar, deliberately unlike the Rangers' geometry;
  (b) a mark carried in the calendar-keeping itself — what a Krenyi
  officer is, told by which count she keeps.
- **Pelagene Littoral** — an ocean people; cloth is a poor medium in water.
  Shapes: (a) colour or pattern on the body, controllable, as some
  cephalopods have; (b) a carried object that reads in the water — a
  sound-maker, a tool worn where it can be seen.
  **Taken for Mira of Brine's portrait prompt, 13 October 2026** (Fable,
  under *Who approves whom*; his to overrule; `images.md` § 12): she is a
  Ranger on duty (`src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md` names Pelagene
  surveyors like Mira among the modular shell's wearers), so the prompt
  gives the Rangers' shell as a Pelagene wears it, a few small fitted
  sections seated between the folds with nearly all of the integument left
  open, since the integument is the organ of perception and must stay wet
  (`src/lore/pelagene-littoral.md`); and one plain seated tab with no
  colour, her corps and rank being off the record, on the Veyr precedent
  below. That is the Rangers' uniform on a Pelagene, not the people's own
  equivalent, which stays open as (a) or (b).
- **Veyr Basaltborn** — shapes: (a) a mark worked into the body's own
  surface; (b) a worn plate, on the Rangers' own magnetic principle,
  which would explain why the Veyr took to the Rangers' tab standard
  easily.
  **Ruled 12 October 2026** (Dermot, choosing *"Marks seated on the body"*
  for Qiren Tal's portrait): a Veyr on duty wears no garment. The corps
  colour and the rank tab are seated directly on the lattice body, as
  Rook-7's marks are on its chassis (*bolted to a different surface*,
  `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`), which is shape (b) on the
  body itself. Where a Veyr's corps or rank is not on record, a prompt
  shows one plain seated tab with no colour. The Veyr's own mark, if
  they have one beside the Rangers', is still undescribed.
- **Serephine Dunekin** — a scout people; shapes: (a) posture or gait —
  a role told by how a body carries itself, which a desert people who
  read distance would find natural; (b) a worn mark.
- **Chthonari** — thought as the Undersong (`mind-design.md`); the obvious
  medium is sound. Shapes: (a) a role carried as a standing tone or a
  phrase in the Undersong, so that who does what is heard, not seen;
  (b) nothing — a people who cannot read one another needing no mark, and
  saying so.
- **Verdani** — the lore page notes strain-linked coloration; that is
  biology, not a mark, and must not be conscripted as one. Shapes: (a) a
  worn or grown token, distinct from strain colour; (b) an absence, with
  the reason stated.
- **The Told** — read the three Below the Roof chapters before claiming
  anything: a people whose names are tellings may carry role the same
  way, and a visual mark for a deep-side people is the first thing to
  doubt. Shapes: (a) a role is told, not shown; (b) nothing yet, absence
  marked.
- **Pandoids** — the Fliade entry and Teddy's page describe an observational
  register; nothing about marks. Absence marked.
  *Scale and frame, ruled 7 October 2026* (Dermot, verbatim: *"Pandoids are
  less humanoid than the Kyreni but more humanoid than almost any other
  people."*, and the same evening *"Pandoids look more like cute giant teddy
  bears than like sloths"* and *"A human in a cold suit would almost be the
  size of a Pandoid and almost blend in if the suit had black and white
  markings."*): upright on two legs with forelimbs of equal length, about
  the size of a suited human, a round head on a round body; the Krenyi are
  the only people on the human frame (`src/lore/krenyi.md`), and the
  Pandoids sit just below them on the humanoid scale, above every other
  people the record holds. The cold-suit reading is noted in
  `intake-2026-10-07.md` and not yet on a page.

Rule for using this list: pick a shape only in the page that needs it, in
that page's own voice, and log the pick in the canonical guide if it coins
a term. A people whose equivalent is *nothing* is a valid answer and an
interesting one, and the lore page's *"does not guess"* line is there to
keep it available.

## Marks of belonging (Dermot's direction, 11 October 2026)

Asked whether non-human peoples have an equivalent of a flag, he chose a principle and no designs yet. A people marks belonging in its own medium, as it marks a role (the uniform equivalent above): a phrase, a struck rhythm, a colour, a posture, a scent, whatever its senses carry best. Nothing is defined per people until a page needs it, and then it is derived here first, from the people's senses and world, never borrowed from a human flag. The Celtic Union's ceremonial flag (six stars for six arks, `celtic-union-of-planets.md`, same day) is the one human polity's flag on record.

## Where this goes

Each species that gets a body plan gets a lore page on the pattern of
[Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) — physical and social characteristics,
something the species is structurally unable to do, the thing outsiders reliably
get wrong — and its blocked portrait becomes briefable. Four of the five have no
page at all; the Verdani have one that never says what a Verdani looks like.

Currently blocked on prose, with placeholder cards standing: **Sethka Ru**
(Serephine), **Qiren Tal** (Veyr), **Isren Farrowkin** (Verdani).

## Prismeri — Prismere (derived 3 September 2026, Dermot's direction: *"derive the prismere body form based on their planetary ecology please"*)

Drafted into `src/lore/prismeri.md` as three sections (*A Body for Thin Ground
and Thick Air*, *What They Are Structurally Bad At*, *The Thing Outsiders Get
Wrong*) on the Krenyi pattern; draft-and-stop. The seven questions, answered
from `src/lore/planets/prismere.md` and what the Prismeri page already held:

1. **What the world does to a body.** Low gravity, dense particulate air, a
   biosphere that lights itself from below, a silent apex glider that hunts
   from above at dusk. So: flight is cheap and honest; a light, long, level-
   spined frame; wings as forelimbs, not as an addition to arms; an upward-
   looking threat sense; a curfew written into the body.
2. **How it senses.** Two forward eyes low on the head for a flier's depth of
   field; a crystalline facet band along the skull ridge that images nothing
   and reads change in the overhead light (the ray-shadow sense); in Full
   Wings, the canon tendrils from the same ridge, reading corridor geometry
   directly. The Lattice is a thing the world shows; the tendrils are the
   organ that reads it without the haze.
3. **Limbs and what they are for.** Four. Wing-forelimbs with three free
   clawed fingers at the leading edge (the hands); prehensile long-toed hind
   feet (the hold). Quadrupedal on the ground, knuckle-walking on folded
   wings, four-point launch — the umbral ray's own method, so the world
   already has it. Manipulation finer than the manipulator comes from the
   division: feet hold, fingers work, which needs the body to hang.
4. **Symmetry.** Bilateral; flight favours it and nothing here needs
   otherwise.
5. **Life cycle.** Already canon: two adult forms, an irreversible moult in a
   self-secreted mineral shell, not universal, no hierarchy. The derivation
   adds only that the forms are one body at two hardnesses and that the
   weight of Full Wings is what divides the work — forest flight versus haze
   flight — so neither would trade.
6. **What a Ranger station costs them.** Gravity: cannot fly, cannot hang,
   spends the posting on four knuckles with nothing overhead. Plus the
   spectrum and corridor-geometry accommodations already in canon. Plus the
   drone startle: a shape crossing overhead is the one thing the body cannot
   be talked out of noticing.
7. **What the biology made them good at, and the job that followed.** Sky-
   reading, hence the astrogation guilds (canon). And the materials
   bottleneck: no fire on a glass world, so light-concentration instead —
   the lens foliage taught them to fuse silica with focused sun; no
   metallurgy of their own; the Federation's alloy is Aspenar's, and
   Prismere's contribution is expertise "because expertise is what the world
   made cheap" (the planet page already says the contribution is expertise;
   this gives it a reason).

**Neck and tail, confirmed 12 October 2026** (Dermot, confirming two readings the Codex plates of the Prismeri had added, after every photographic attempt drew the Prismeri as dragons, and his test that same day, verbatim *"The Prismeri would no longer be confused with Levrilic dragons"*). The derivation above fixed the spine, the limbs and the head and was silent on both; it now says: **the neck is short, the head carried close against the shoulders, and there is no tail**, the body ending at the hind legs with the membrane running between wing and leg. Both follow from the frame already derived: a flier that rests by hanging and works from the hang gains nothing from a long neck or a tail, and both are the features that would sort a Prismeri outline into a Levril's dragon. Realized as a clarification in `src/lore/prismeri.md`'s *A Body for Thin Ground and Thick Air*.

**Traps avoided.** No human frame: the spine is level, the wings are the
forelimbs, rest is hanging, ground travel is quadrupedal. No insectoid head
on a biped (the Qiren Tal failure). No planet of hats: the two forms do
different work for a physical reason, and the outsiders-get-wrong section is
about posture, which is the Krenyi standard applied.

**Readings flagged in `intake-2026-09-03.md`, eleventh direction.**

### What the record already fixes (derived)

Gathered in one place, as the sections above do for the other peoples.
Nothing here is new.

- **Nine centres, one person.** A dominant central brain for judgement,
  language and planning; eight primary limbs, each carrying its own
  semi-autonomous ganglion with sensory processing, reflexes and a measurable
  preference and mood of its own; the central brain negotiates with the
  limb-ganglia and does not fully override them (`src/lore/cerebraun.md`,
  *Physical and Cognitive Characteristics*). A single Cerebraun is one person
  with nine processing centres, not nine persons (the same page: *a single
  Cerebraun can hold…*). Under `mind-design.md`'s standing constraint a
  people whose cognition is distributed is written in its own terms, never as
  a disorder or a hive, and the page already does this: its physicians treat
  a limb acting against the central brain's stated intention as ordinary.
- **Frame: Hydrostata.** Fluid pressure, *no rigid skeleton at all*, the row
  shared with the Pelagene Littoral (`src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`,
  the frames table). The frame says how a body is held up and nothing about
  kinship; sharing a row with the Pelagene makes the two taxonomic neighbours
  and no more.
- **The skin is a channel.** Chromatophore and texture-changing tissue
  capable of rapid, high-bandwidth display, used for identity signalling,
  emotional broadcast and a formal display-language reserved for precise
  technical and diplomatic work (`cerebraun.md`). The display resolves
  finely: Sohrel's dissent is *a slow color shift across four centimeters of
  skin* (S03E01C01), so a patch a few centimetres wide carries a statement.
- **Two channels at once.** Spoken or written language from the central
  brain; skin-display from the limb-ganglia beneath it (`cerebraun.md`,
  *Communication*; the `canon_facts` of S03E01C01; the Xenolinguistics
  Handbook's first failure mode). So a Cerebraun speaks aloud, reads and
  writes: the body has a voice and reads a page.
- **The limbs are told apart by position.** *The limb-ganglion governing
  Sohrel's third dorsal arm* (S03E01C01; *the third dorsal arm's ganglion*
  again in C03; C02 has *the limb-ganglion beneath the desk*). Dorsal arms
  exist and are counted, which fixes a dorsal side and a ventral one: the
  body has an up and a down, and the eight limbs are not an undifferentiated
  ring.
- **The limbs are not all alike in what they do.** The third dorsal arm
  argues, hums faintly beneath a desk, goes still as *the shape of a
  disagreement choosing not to spend itself*, and reads as recognition (C01,
  C02, C03). A limb can act, sound and signal on its own. Where in the limb
  the ganglion sits is **not** on record: the designed card for Sohrel draws
  a node at each tip, and that is the card's device, not canon.
- **Sohrel sits at a desk, reads, drafts and signs** (C01, C02, C03), on a
  relay station with humans aboard (C01: *unreadable to any human on the
  relay station*). So the body works at a surface, in a room humans also use,
  and is not confined to a tank or a medium of its own. Whether it breathes
  the same air is not on record.
- **A people who need instruments for a water people's channels.** Cerebraun
  administrators on Meridian *still rely on translation instrumentation* to
  follow the reef-cities' underwater claw-percussion and chemical signalling
  (`src/lore/planets/meridian.md`). A hint, not a finding: a people native to
  a chemical-signalling ocean would be expected to have had a nearer sense.
- **The likeness is the observer's.** *Cephalopod-like to a human eye*; no
  descent from Earth's octopus or squid; a description or picture that turns
  the likeness into the Earth animal *has turned the likeness into a claim the
  record does not make* (`cerebraun.md`; `cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`, *A
  Likeness Belongs to Whoever Sees It*). And the general rule under it: no
  other people in the record is a metazoan (same page, *It Goes Deeper Than
  Ancestry*).
- **What the record declines.** The homeworld is unnamed: the Hegemony is
  *the dominant power across its home cluster* and nothing more
  (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`). No pronoun system is declared; the three Sohrel
  chapters and the character page use the name throughout and no pronoun,
  which is a usage that stands (CLAUDE.md's pronoun rule: existing usage
  stands, and a new system is a per-species vocabulary decision). No scale,
  no colour at rest, no sense organs, no mode of locomotion, and no lifespan
  beyond Sohrel's three centuries in one post (`sohrel.md`).

### What a human sees (derived, restated for a prompt)

A soft body with no bones in it; eight limbs; a skin that changes colour and
texture while you watch. Those three facts are the whole of the likeness, and
they are why the generator reaches for an octopus every time: on Earth they
occur together in exactly one lineage. The job of the points below is to say
what is there that an octopus does not have, and what an octopus has that is
not there, so that the likeness fires at a glance and fails on inspection,
which is the record's own pattern for a people (the Krenyi, *The Nearest
Frame Is the Strangest*).

### What is actually there (proposed, each a choice)

Answered by the body note's seven questions, in their order, from the facts
above; nothing from the world, because the world is not on record.

1. **What the world did to the body: not on record, and this draft does not
   supply it.** Two shapes. **(a) Leave the homeworld unnamed** and derive
   only what the body already fixes, naming the world when a page needs it
   (Dermot's scope ruling of 13 August: anywhere in the known Concordant, or
   elsewhere in the membrane where crossover is safe). **(b) Name a world
   type now** (a dense-atmosphere world where a boneless body is partly borne
   by the air, or a shore world of shallow fluid) so the body can be derived
   forward as the others are. **Recommended: (a).** Nothing on any page needs
   the world, the Meridian hint argues against the obvious ocean, and a world
   named to explain a picture is the error the whole method exists to avoid.

2. **How it senses: no head, no pair of eyes; sight along the limbs.** Each
   limb-ganglion does its own sensory processing (derived), and a people who
   read a four-centimetre colour shift on their own and one another's limbs
   need colour vision close to the skin. So: **no head**, and **no two large
   forward eyes**; instead many small eyes along the limbs, several to a
   limb, each ganglion seeing its own neighbourhood of skin and the room
   beyond it, and the central brain seeing through the limbs. This is the
   single point that does most against the octopus attractor, whose
   silhouette is a head with two eyes, and it is the point that makes the
   people's own habit (nine centres, each with its own view) visible in a
   photograph. **Alternative:** a cluster of small eyes on the central body
   and none on the limbs. **Recommended: along the limbs.**

3. **Limbs: four dorsal, four ventral, and they do different work.** *Third
   dorsal arm* fixes a dorsal set of at least three (derived). Proposed: eight
   in two tiers of four, the **ventral four** short, broad and load-bearing,
   the body resting on them, and the **dorsal four** longer and freer, the
   working and signalling limbs; a Cerebraun at a desk rests on the lower
   tier with the upper tier on the surface or draped beneath it, as Sohrel's
   third dorsal arm is. No suckers and no hooks: a limb's inner surface is
   the same textured skin as the rest, and it holds by wrapping and by the
   texture-change tissue raising a grip (the tissue is derived; the grip is
   proposed). **Alternative:** eight alike around the body, *dorsal* meaning
   only the upper half in posture. **Recommended: two tiers**, because it
   gives the generator a body plan an octopus does not have and gives the
   prose something to do with *which limb*.

4. **Symmetry: dorsoventral, not radial.** Follows from 3. A top and a
   bottom, an upper tier and a lower, and **no front**: a Cerebraun addresses
   a room with whichever limbs are nearest, and *facing* someone is a courtesy
   of turning the signalling tier toward them, not a fact of anatomy. (The
   first half is derived from *dorsal*; the no-front is proposed.)

5. **Surface: dry, matte and finely textured at rest.** Nothing says the body
   is wet, and the Meridian hint says the people are not native to a chemical
   ocean. Proposed: an integument dry to the touch, matte, with a fine raised
   grain the texture tissue can smooth flat or lift into ridges; colour at
   rest **quiet and uniform**, one muted tone across the whole body, so that
   a display reads as a change from stillness (the formal display-language is
   *reserved*, which implies a resting state that says nothing). This refuses
   the generator's wet gloss, the octopus's second tell after the eyes. The
   resting tone is not chosen here; the two cards use violet and teal, and
   either would do. **Alternative:** a wet skin kept moist on a station, on
   the Pelagene pattern. **Recommended: dry.**

6. **Life cycle: not on record; nothing proposed.** Sohrel has held one post
   for about three centuries (`sohrel.md`), so a Cerebraun lives at least
   that long; whether the limbs and the central brain age differently,
   whether a limb is ever lost and regrown, and how a young Cerebraun comes
   by nine centres are open and better answered by a chapter than by a note.
   Marked absent.

7. **What a station costs them: the second channel, unread.** Derived from
   the page and the chapters: on a human station nobody reads the skin, so
   half of what a Cerebraun says lands nowhere, every day. The body note's
   sixth question was answered by the lore before the body was described,
   which is unusual and worth keeping: the daily cost is not physical. One
   physical cost is proposed and small: a boneless body at human-station
   gravity tires in the lower tier, and a Cerebraun working long at a surface
   rests the body on it, which a human reads as slumping and should not.

8. **What the biology made them good at, and the job that followed
   (derived).** Holding a correct position and an unresolved one at once
   without pretending the second is not there (`sohrel.md`), which is the
   Administrator-Voice's whole craft; and the Prime Ganglion is held by
   *whichever Cerebraun demonstrates the clearest capacity to hold the whole
   polity's competing internal signals in coherent balance*
   (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`). The polity's structure is the neurology at
   scale, by the species' own account. Nothing to add.

**Scale, for the frame only (proposed).** No page gives a size. For Sohrel's
portrait the body is drawn at about the bulk of a seated adult human, low and
broad on its ventral tier, the dorsal limbs reaching a desk built for it; the
desk is Sohrel's own on a Hegemony relay station, so its height is the
species' and not a human's. A scale fixed for a picture is not canon, and the
record need not state one until a chapter does.

### Marks and the uniform equivalent (open, his)

The Cerebraun are not Rangers, and the Hegemony is a polity with its own
military and its own offices (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`). The page already says
the skin carries *identity signaling*, so the natural reading of a Hegemony
mark of office is a display, not a worn thing; whether an Administrator-Voice
shows one, and what it looks like, is not on record and is not needed for
Sohrel's portrait, which draws no mark. Listed under *The uniform equivalent*
above by this pointer only; the pick is made in the page that needs it.

### The Earth animal, refused by name (proposed as a prompt rule)

Under the 12 October convention (`images.md`, *An Earth likeness is the
observer's*), a Cerebraun prompt says the body from this section and negates
the Earth animal by name. The features that would turn the likeness into the
animal, none of which the record gives the Cerebraun: a head; two large eyes;
a mantle with a cavity and a siphon; suckers; a beak; ink; fins; a wet gloss;
arms tapering to a point; a body that swims. The prompt negates *octopus,
squid, cuttlefish, cephalopod, tentacles with suckers, beak, siphon, fins*,
and the generator's stock phrases *sea creature, alien octopus, Cthulhu,
kraken*. A frame that draws any of them fails, however photographic it is.

## Living with other life — companions, working animals and commensals, people by people

Written 14 October 2026 at Dermot's direction, verbatim: *"Pets and companion
creatures for non-humanoid peoples"*. Every line is marked. **Derived** means
the record already says or plainly implies it, and the page is cited.
**Proposed** means a choice made here, and it is his to take or leave.
**Not established** means the record is silent, and this note leaves it so
rather than filling it. Nothing here is written to `src/`.

**What the question is asking.** The human word *pet* bundles five things:
an animal kept close, fed, named, owned, and kept for company rather than for
work. The record's own pets are human-side and are a legal category before
they are anything else. A [Smart Pet](/star-rangers/glossary/smart-pet/) is an
animal carrying a certified cognitive subsystem in a collar, under a welfare
regime written because the animal cannot consent. Its kin on Eden are cats and
rabbits carried by people. So the question per people is which of the five
has any footing, and what the relation is called when it is not *pet*.
[Cross-Biosphere Taxonomy](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/)
already settles the first word: *animal* travels across biospheres as a
declared translation, because what it predicts (a mobile, sensing, behaving
organism that is not a person) holds on every surveyed world. Whether *pet*
travels the same way is the first open choice below.

**The method for anything proposed.** It is the body note's method, turned on
the animals.

1. The creature comes from the people's own biosphere and is built from that
   world's medium and chemistry outward.
2. It is legible in the people's own dominant sense. A vibration people's
   animal is read through substrate, a heat people's through warmth.
3. It has a reason to be near the people before it has an affectionate one.
   The vessik is the model: a granary tenant first, a fracture alarm second,
   and fond feeling only on Deck 7, where it is *unmistakably not theirs*.
4. It is named by what it does, never after an Earth animal. If it is ever
   drawn, the prompt negates the nearest Earth animal by name, under the
   12 October convention (`images.md`).
5. No space-dog, no dragon kept as a pet, no cute monster. Monsters are filed
   elsewhere entirely, as meta-dimensional, and are rare and valid only under
   boundary conditions (CLAUDE.md). They are never kept, tamed or befriended
   on any page. [The Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/) wear *the
   shape of a companion animal* among other founded shapes. So a companion
   creature in prose must be plainly founded: ordinary biology from its own
   world, nothing uncanny about it, and no hint that it is more than it is.

### Chthonari — the Undersong Belt

- **Derived.** The vessik is a burrow commensal, *"small, low, six-limbed"*
  and built on the same substrate-sensing principle as the Chthonari. It is
  *tolerated in the walls* for a granary reason and for a structural one: *"a
  burrow whose vessik have gone still is a burrow with a stress fracture in
  it"*. Every Chthonari child learns to check this *before they learn to read*
  (`src/characters/thrum.md`). It is filed as an animal on the taxonomy page,
  in Cataphracta beside the people (`cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`).
- **Derived.** A vessik is *"not livestock, not a companion animal under any
  Compact schedule, and not a Smart Pet"*. Thrum reached Eden *"in a sealed
  transit burrow, in the luggage of"* a Chthonari engineer, and stayed
  (`thrum.md`). Why she carried one is **not established**.
- **Derived.** The vessik cannot be charmed, and `mind-design.md` (the
  Chthonari, first answer) reads that as the people's own virtue at animal
  scale.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. The relation is with the burrow, not with a person.
  The vessik lives in the structure the people listen to, and its stillness
  is part of what the structure says. *Commensal*, the record's own word, is
  exact.
- **Proposed.** The Chthonari count the vessik as part of a burrow's
  listening, in the same way they build a monitoring intelligence into a
  scaffold. To them *"a structure that can also listen is … a completion"*
  (`chthonari.md`, *Machines in the Work*). On that reading the engineer did
  not bring a pet to Eden. She brought her quarters' listening with her, and
  left it in a structure that had none. This stays here and goes into no
  chapter unless he takes it (open choice 2).
- **Welfare.** Thrum's own *Open Question* is the record's statement of the
  gap: no framework on Eden covers a vessik. Nothing is added here.
- **Chapters and Undercover Pets.** Already in. Thrum is the thread's one
  non-human people's animal, and its value is that it is the *one clean
  measurement* in the file.

### Veyr Basaltborn — Veyr-3

- **Not established.** No page names any non-sapient life on Veyr-3.
  `veyr-basaltborn.md` gives the world (heavy gravity, active basalt, mineral
  aerosol, constant micro-shock) and the people's senses: distributed
  compound eyes tuned to contrast and motion in haze, and limb-tips that read
  resonance. *"If a panel sounds wrong under strain, a Veyr trusts the
  resonance."*
- **Proposed: a living plumb, tended and not kept.** A sessile lattice-grower
  of the basalt crust. It grows slowly in fine mineral struts, as the Veyr's
  own frame does, and it re-sets its growth toward the true vertical after
  every shock, so its layers record the ground's shock history. Built from
  the world outward: heavy gravity favours a low, braced organism, and
  constant aerosol favours a filter-feeder on settling mineral dust. Matched
  to the senses: its alignment shows as a contrast line to compound eyes, and
  its struts ring true or false under a limb-tip. A people who *calibrate by
  material behaviour rather than by instrument* would keep colonies at a
  workshop as the reference that does not drift. Any fondness for it is a
  craftsman's fondness for a true tool.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. On the proposal, the relation is calibration. The
  people's temperament as the record gives it (*composure*, *tolerant of
  noise*) supplies no reason for keeping animals for company, and none is
  invented.
- **Chapters.** A Veyr Ranger's quarters could hold a colony without
  breaking anything, since it is sessile and asks nothing of a habitat's
  welfare regime. It is a detail for a Qiren Tal scene, not a thread.

### Serephine Dunekin — Kharis Belt IV

- **Not established.** No page names any other Kharis life.
  `serephine-dunekin.md` fixes the two lethal extremes each day, the
  closed form and the open form, the crepuscular rhythm, and *buried thermal
  mass used as a battery*.
- **Proposed: fellow waiters.** On a world where everything alive must avoid
  the same two lethal hours, the life that shares the Serephine's day shares
  their shelters. Buried thermal mass is warm through the cold and cool
  through the heat for whatever lives in it. The proposed relation is
  co-sheltering: organisms that close when the people close and open at the
  same margins, tolerated in the buffer walls and neither fed nor owned. The
  creature is matched to the light-scattering eye membranes. It is read by
  how it opens in the raked light of the margins, the one time a Serephine
  is also open.
- **Does "pet" fit?** It mislabels the relation. Company on Kharis happens in
  the windows and nowhere else, and *"the safe hours are for living"*
  (`serephine-dunekin.md`). Something that waited out the heat beside you
  shares your day. It is not kept.
- **Chapters.** None needed. If Sethka Ru's file ever wants a home detail,
  this is the one available.

### Pelagene Littoral — Nerath

- **Not established.** No page names any other Nerath life.
  `pelagene-littoral.md` fixes the stratified, non-water fluid, a body that
  senses pressure, flow and chemistry over its whole surface, radial
  symmetry about the vertical, and the rule that *"nothing on Nerath ever
  stays where it was put"*.
- **Derived, and it decides the question.** A kept animal needs a place to be
  kept in, and the Pelagene have no fixed places, no stable inventory and no
  landmarks. *Keeping*, the core of *pet*, is the fixed state the record
  says they are structurally poor at.
- **Proposed: the drift-fellow.** The relation that fits is accompaniment.
  Small organisms ride the same layer interface a Pelagene holds, trimming
  at the same depth. Each is legible by the wake and the chemical trace it
  leaves, which a whole-body chemical sense reads without looking. What a
  Pelagene would value is *who holds the band with you*, which is a rate and
  a direction. A possession is neither.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. Accompaniment is the word.
- **Welfare and Undercover Pets.** A Nerath organism could not cross to a
  station casually. It would need its own layered fluid carried with it, so
  no Thrum-like stowaway is plausible, and none should be written.

### Verdani — Verdance

- **Derived.** Thallenwort is an obligate photosynthetic symbiont in the
  kinbeds. It is passed by kinbed-to-kinbed contact in infancy, and that
  passing is *"the foundational kinship act, more binding in practice than …
  shared parentage"* (`verdani.md`). It is a partner and a kin-lineage, and
  the record files Verdani by it (`cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`).
- **Derived.** Verdani industry is cultivation: *"grown materials, bred
  organisms, processes measured in seasons"* (`verdani.md`). The steppe
  nation, the **Aurelin Herdlands**, is governed by **Migratory Councils**
  *"that follow the traditional herding routes"*, and the high terraces have
  *"alpine farming communities"* (`planets/verdance.md`). The Verdani are the
  record's one non-human people with herding on the page.
- **Not established.** What the Herdlands herd. Whether any Verdani keep an
  animal for company.
- **Does "pet" fit?** It is the wrong word in the most instructive way. The
  closest thing a Verdani has to a creature carried close all their life is
  thallenwort, and calling a kin-partner a pet inverts the relation. Herding
  is work and politics (the Councils against the Waystation Compact), and the
  record gives it no companion register.
- **Proposed, lightly.** If a chapter needs the herds, they are built from
  the Verdani energy budget. The herds move at the pace of the light and the
  water, and the herder's day is measured in light taken, as the people's
  own is. What the animal is stays open (open choice 3).
- **Trap.** A herding people is the easiest place on the record for the
  allegory trap, *basically a pastoral human culture*. The route against it
  is the one the Verdani page already uses: the light budget, not the
  landscape.

### Ilveth — Sardain

- **Derived.** Sardain's life crowds into *"geothermal provinces where the
  biosphere itself has always crowded"*. Heat is the only wealth, and
  anything warm is visible *"from horizon distance"* to the mirror-pits
  (`ilveth.md`). Warm-blooded visitors are *"lanterns that cannot be put
  out"*.
- **Proposed: watched, not kept.** A people who see every warm body to the
  horizon never needed to keep an animal close to know where it was. Their
  relation to warm life is watching it at a distance. A warm animal inside a
  building would make the building *shout*, which is the indiscretion
  heat-thrift exists to prevent. So Ilveth keep no warm animals indoors, on
  the record's own logic, and any commensal that does live in their walls is
  cold-running. It is invisible to the pits and known only to the small
  photic eyes.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. A kept warm animal would read to an Ilveth as a
  fire kept burning for company, which is an extravagance and not a comfort.
- **Chapters and Undercover Pets: the best opening in this survey.** An
  Ilveth Ranger aboard Eden sees every Smart Pet as a small warm lamp through
  any wall or vent. It is the one observer an undercover cat cannot hide
  from. By the Sardain courtesy of answering what is said rather than what
  a body shows, they are also likely to keep what they see to themselves. It needs no new creature and breaks nothing: it is `ilveth.md`
  applied to Deck 7.

### Ovruhn — Thavren

- **Not established.** No page names any other Thavren life. `ovruhn.md`
  fixes the far sense as sound, the under-song (honest, involuntary, carrying
  tens of kilometres), and that *"nothing alive is acoustically quiet"*.
- **Derived, at the edge.** Comfort among Ovruhn *"arrives before it is asked
  for, because the asking was audible an hour ago"*. Ovruhn steady a crowded
  shelter deck with a low under-song (`ovruhn.md`). The record says this of
  people and is silent on animals.
- **Proposed.** Of all the peoples here, *company* fits the Ovruhn best. An
  animal's distress is as audible to them as a person's, and their ethic is
  answering it. The difference from a human pet is privacy. No animal near
  an Ovruhn belongs to one listener: everyone in range hears its heart. A
  creature kept close would be a household of the whole band, not of one
  keeper. A Thavren creature is built from dense air and short sight: a body
  read by its sound, not its shape.
- **Does "pet" fit?** Partly. There is company and care. There is no private
  ownership, which the human word assumes.
- **Chapters and Undercover Pets.** An Ovruhn Ranger on Eden hears Barsik's
  heartbeat change before Barsik moves, and would offer comfort to a cat
  whose cover depends on looking unbothered. It needs no new creature, and
  it is in register for both threads.
- **Trap.** The first survey compared the Ovruhn to three Earth animals in
  one paragraph and the press made *the hybrid people* of it (`ovruhn.md`). A
  Thavren creature must not be built the same way.

### Prismeri — Prismere

- **Derived.** Prismere's fauna is on the page (`planets/prismere.md`).
  **Driftjellies** are harmless aerial filter-feeders. **Thornbacks** are
  crystal-spined ground grazers whose shells are *"shed naturally during
  growth molts and collected rather than harvested from a living animal"*,
  and are an export. The **umbral ray** is the silent apex glider behind the
  dusk curfew and the *"Lattice-reader's escort"*. First Wings *"tend
  lumenspire settlements"* (`prismeri.md`). The lumenspires are living
  mineral towers fed by a shared root-mat, and the people live in them.
- **Implied.** That the Prismeri are the ones collecting the shed shells:
  the export is Prismere's, and the page does not name the collector.
- **Derived from the body, and it rules out the obvious pet.** The facet band
  on the skull ridge exists to catch a shape crossing the light overhead, and
  a Prismeri *"who startles at a drone passing overhead is not nervous"*
  (`prismeri.md`). Anything airborne kept above a hanging Prismeri would
  trip that sense all day. A kept flier is the one thing this people could
  not live with.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. The relations are tending (the lumenspires, which
  are architecture as much as life) and gathering (the shed shells). Nothing
  is kept for company.
- **Trap.** The Prismeri have already been drawn as dragons more than once
  (`species-design.md` above). A Prismeri beside any winged creature in a
  picture invites the *dragon-pet* reading, and the 12 October negation
  applies to both bodies.

### Cerebraun — homeworld unnamed

- **Not established.** The homeworld is unnamed (ruled 13 October, option
  (a), above), so no Cerebraun biosphere exists to build a creature from, and
  none is proposed.
- **Derived.** Each limb-ganglion has *"a measurable degree of localized
  preference and mood"*. The central brain negotiates with the limbs and
  does not override them (`cerebraun.md`).
- **Proposed: attachment held by a limb.** If a Cerebraun forms an attachment
  to an animal, the record's neurology says where it would sit: possibly in
  one limb's ganglion rather than in the central voice. It would be a
  preference the voice tolerates and negotiates with, shown on the second
  channel, and never stated on the first. This needs no homeworld (open
  choice 4).
- **Does "pet" fit?** Undecided until a page needs it. The proposal above is
  the Cerebraun shape of the question.
- **Meridian.** The Cerebraun live beside Meridian's biosphere as
  administrators. There is no relation to its animals on record.

### Meridian sapients — Meridian

- **Derived.** The seas are dense with *"armored, segmented"* life, from
  *"filter-feeding reef-builders the size of a closed fist"* to apex hunters
  of several tonnes *"in coordinated hunting groups"*. The sapients' cities
  are built *"in secreted mineral composite"*, and their channels are
  claw-percussion and chemistry diffused into the water (`planets/meridian.md`).
  *Crustacean* is the record's likeness word, read as the taxonomy page reads
  every likeness.
- **Not established.** Whether the reef-builders have any part in the
  cities. The page leaves open whose secretion the composite is.
- **Proposed.** If a page needs it, the reef-cities are co-built: the
  sapients arrange, and fist-sized reef-builders lay composite where a
  chemical trace in the water tells them to. That makes the relation a
  partnership in building, legible on both of the people's channels. It is a
  choice, and it touches the near-quarrying incident's premise (the cities
  read as natural growth), which it would strengthen rather than break.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No.
- **Welfare and Undercover Pets.** None can apply. No human can breathe
  Meridian's chemistry, and no Meridian organism survives a human habitat.

### Ollune — Cirrane

- **Derived.** Ollune technology is *"entirely biochemical"*: *"their
  equivalent of an industry is a cultivated lineage of organisms"*. The
  shoals are living bladder architecture, *"tended, pruned, and extended
  across generations"* (`ollune.md`). Cirrane's large fauna are soft,
  buoyant, translucent filter-feeders (`planets/cirrane.md`).
- **Derived, and it decides the question.** *"Anything an Ollune carries must
  be lifted for as long as they own it."* A kept animal that cannot hold its
  own lift is a permanent metabolic charge, and the word for an owner of
  such things is *heavy*, *"and is not flattering"*. Companionship on
  Cirrane is the **hold**: people carrying people through the night.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. The one creature an Ollune could keep close is one
  that pays its own lift, and a creature that does that is free to drift
  off, which makes it a fellow drifter and not a possession. The nearest kept
  life is the shoal's cultivated lineages. Whatever affection there is
  attaches to the shoal.
- **Welfare and Undercover Pets.** None can apply: no shared facility exists
  that an Ollune and a human can occupy unsuited.

  names stand as two sides of one encounter, as *Pandoid* and *the Told* do.
- **Derived.** The survey's proposal to collar the Anstolik was refused at

- **Derived.** The record's one image of a Told holding something that sleeps
  is Carried-It-Sleeping holding the survey's relay through the squeeze

- **Does "pet" fit?** No, and the record already says why. The Told's

### What holds across the peoples

Four things hold across the record. First, no non-human people is shown
keeping an animal for company alone. Every derived relation has a working
reason or none: the vessik in the walls, the thornback shells, the Ollune
lineages, the Verdani cultivation and herds, the runners left to their
ground. Second, the relation is with a place more often than with a person:
the vessik with the burrow, the lumenspires with the settlement, the
lineages with the shoal, the runners with their own ground. Third, each
people reads other life in its own medium, and so would read a human's pet
in its own medium too. To a Chthonari a Smart Pet is too light to feel. To
an Ilveth it is a lamp, and to an Ovruhn a heartbeat. None of them sees a
face. Fourth, the welfare frameworks are human-side, written for collars and
subsystems, and a non-human people's animal on a human habitat falls through
all of them. Thrum's page already states that, and it holds for any second
case. *Pet* is the human case, one relation among the relations. Like the
human Ranger's culture, it is never the baseline the others are measured from
(CLAUDE.md, the 28 September usage rule).

### Open — his choices

1. **Does *pet* travel?** (A) The record keeps *pet* for human-side kept
   animals (the Smart Pet register) and names every other people's relation
   in its own terms: commensal, tended, herded, watched, accompanied,
   neighbour. (B) Allow *pet* as a declared translation, as *animal* is.
   **Recommended: A.** *Animal* travels because what it predicts survives
   the crossing. *Pet* predicts ownership and company kept for its own sake,
   and none of the relations derived here has both.
2. **Why the engineer carried Thrum.** (A) Leave it a marked absence: the
   gap in the paperwork is the point of the page. (B) Adopt the proposal that
   a Chthonari on a long posting carries a vessik as her quarters' listening.
   **Recommended: A** in canon, with B kept in this note as the reading a
   chapter may reach for.
3. **What the Aurelin Herdlands herd.** (A) Leave it unestablished until a
   chapter needs a herd. (B) Fix a steppe grazer now, from the light budget.
   **Recommended: A.** Nothing needs it yet, and a herd named early is where
   the pastoral allegory gets in.
4. **The Cerebraun attachment held by a limb.** (A) Record it as the
   standing reading for any page that needs a Cerebraun attachment to an
   animal or anything else. (B) Drop it. **Recommended: A.** It comes
   straight from the neurology on the page, needs no homeworld, and is the
   opposite of a human concept with alien furniture.
5. **A second non-human people's animal on Eden.** (A) Thrum stays the only
   one, and other peoples enter the Undercover Pets thread through their
   Rangers' senses (the Ilveth who sees every cat, the Ovruhn who hears
   one). (B) Bring a second creature aboard. **Recommended: A.** Thrum's
   value is that it is the one clean measurement, and most of the creatures
   above could not survive a human habitat anyway.
6. **Runners and Anstolik.** (A) Leave the identification to the reader, as
   the two names for the people are left. (B) State it on the Fliade page or
   in the glossary. **Recommended: A.** `fliade.md` keeps the survey's side
   on purpose, and the chapters keep the Told's.
