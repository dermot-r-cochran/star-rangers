# Designing the species — what is already forced, and what is open

Started 13 August 2026, after the ruling that **the Krenyi are the only other
sapient humanoids** and that no archetype requires a mind to have a body plan at
all. That closed a question and opened five: the Veyr Basaltborn, Pelagene
Littoral, Serephine Dunekin, Verdani and Vessik all have characters on the
roster and **no described body anywhere in prose**, which is why three of their
portraits had quietly drifted humanoid.

This file is the working ground for fixing that. It is not a decision record
yet — the decisions are Dermot's.

**The premise above is sharpened, not overturned — 16 August 2026, #449.**
Dermot ruled the Pandoids of Fliade sapient and *"one of very few humanoid like
aliens"*, then that they derive from **a bear-like alien ancestor, not
terrestrial bears, despite the name**. The published refusal now turns on the
**human frame** rather than on posture: the Krenyi remain the only other people
built on it, and an upright body arrived at independently is not a second entry
on that list.

**The frame is the point — 30 September 2026.** Dermot's direction, verbatim:
*"Krenyi are the strangest non-human people because they look superficially
humanoid and could pass as almost human."* This is the 22 September exception
(*too humanoid unless that is the point of the story*) applied to the one
people on the human frame: the resemblance is the work, and every difference
lands as a correction to an assumption the frame invited. Realized on the lore
page as *The Nearest Frame Is the Strangest*. For the other species nothing
changes — they still owe the record a body that follows from a world, and the
Krenyi are not a licence for a second near-human people.

**So nothing here needs redoing.** The five species below still owe the record a
body that follows from a world, and *"could this be a human with features
applied?"* is still the test that fails a drifted portrait. What changed is that
the test is now stated in the terms it always meant — frame, not posture — which
if anything makes it easier to apply. The one new caution is naming: a familiar
animal in a species' name describes the observers who coined it, so it is never
a licence to reach for that animal's body when briefing an image.

## Scope: where a homeworld may be

Dermot, 13 August 2026: **anywhere in the known Concordant, or elsewhere in the
membrane where crossover is safe.**

So the map is not the constraint. A species need not come from anywhere near the
Solar System's neighbourhood, need not sit on an established fold route, and
need not be reachable conveniently — and where it is *not* conveniently
reachable, that is itself a fact worth using, because the Corps' relationship
with a people is shaped by how hard they are to get to.

## The good news: most of it is already written

Four of the five have their environment fixed in a line on the character page,
and an environment does most of the work of a body. Nothing below is invention —
it is what the existing text already commits to, gathered in one place.

### Veyr Basaltborn — Veyr-3

> Active basaltic volcanism, dense mineral aerosols, micro-shocks, and a
> gravitational field heavy enough that **human joints would degrade in a
> generation**.

The gravity is the loudest constraint in the whole set and it is unforgiving:
**a tall bipedal frame is not available.** High gravity favours low, broad,
short-limbed, heavily braced — weight close to the ground, load paths short,
probably more than four points of contact. Dense mineral aerosols argue for
sealed or filtering respiration and for eyes protected against abrasion rather
than glare. Micro-shocks argue for a body that tolerates constant vibration,
which may mean sensing *through* it rather than being troubled by it.

*Qiren Tal is an engineer doing pressure-safe hull diagnostics and thermal
systems work — a career that follows directly from being built for pressure,
heat and vibration. The species produced the specialism.*

### Pelagene Littoral — an ocean world

> Built for **buoyancy control and dissolved-oxygen variance**. On a station
> she breathes standard atmosphere with **assistive hydration cycles**, and
> performs best in humid, high-pressure conditions.

*Littoral* is doing real work in the name: this is the shore and shallows, not
the abyss — a zone of tides, turbidity, salinity change and daily exposure,
which is a far more variable place to evolve than deep water and argues for a
tolerant generalist rather than a specialist. Buoyancy control implies a
dedicated organ. Assistive hydration cycles imply an integument that loses water
to dry air — so a Pelagene on a station is not merely uncomfortable, she is
**slowly drying**, and every posting has a clock on it that nobody else's does.

*Mira "reads the water before the instruments do" — an environmental surveyor
whose whole professional value is a sensorium tuned to a medium.*

### Serephine Dunekin — Kharis Belt IV

> Thin atmosphere, high UV flux, and a thermal inversion between day and night
> that **would kill an unprotected person in under an hour on either side**.
> Light-scattering eye membranes, water-conservative metabolism, respiratory
> efficiency that makes dense-atmosphere work manageable **but never easy**.

Thin air is the constraint that most strongly rules out a compact body: gas
exchange at low partial pressure wants surface area, which is an argument for
something extended, folded, or many-surfaced rather than a torso with two lungs
in it. High UV argues for an opaque, reflective or actively shielding
integument. The lethal thermal swing argues for either heavy insulation or a
scheduled inactivity — and *scheduled* is interesting, because Sethka's own page
has him planning movement around exposure windows, which may be less a
professional habit than a biological one he has made professional.

**Note the direction of the respiratory clause.** Dense atmosphere is *harder*
for him, not easier. Station air is a burden he carries all day.

### Vessik and the Chthonari — the Undersong Belt

This one is not open. It is **already solved, and it is the model for the rest.**

> A vessik is a burrow commensal — small, low, **six-limbed**, and built on the
> same physical principle as the people whose tunnels it lives in. It has no
> ears… Thrum's alarm is a hard, arrhythmic **drumming of the forelimbs against
> deck plate**, and it carries through the structure for most of the length of
> the deck.

Three things worth taking from it. It is **six-limbed and low** — no apology, no
explanation, and no reader has ever needed one. Its sensory world is
**substrate-borne**, so it is deaf to airborne sound and blind to a human face,
which makes it the one resident of Deck 7 who cannot be charmed — the biology
generates the character. And crucially: *built on the same physical principle as
the people whose tunnels it lives in*, which means **the Chthonari are already
committed** to being low, six-limbed and substrate-hearing. That commitment was
made by describing an animal.

### Verdani — Verdance

> Distinct thallenwort strains, adapted over generations to the **light quality,
> humidity, and canopy density** of one of Verdance's four biomes… Verdani
> cannot interbreed with any other known species; the symbiosis itself cannot be
> transplanted into a non-Verdani host.

The word doing the work is **host**. A Verdani is a partner to a photosynthetic
symbiont, so light is the currency and surface area is the wealth — which argues
for a body that presents area to the sky rather than minimising it, and possibly
for a life stage that does not move much. Canopy density as an adaptive variable
implies vertical structure in the habitat and therefore in the people.

**The four biomes are a gift and a trap.** They give the species genuine internal
variety — the [cast principle](/story-bible) against monoculture, built into the
biology — but four regional variants is also exactly how a species becomes four
stereotypes. Use them for texture, not for temperament.

## The questions that actually decide a body

Rather than starting from a silhouette, these are the ones that produce one.
Answering three or four is enough to brief a portrait.

1. **What does the world do to a body?** Gravity, pressure, atmosphere,
   temperature range, light. This alone rules out most shapes.
2. **How does it sense?** The single highest-value question for character, as
   Thrum proves. A sensorium determines what a person can be wrong about.
3. **How many limbs, and what are they for?** Manipulation and locomotion need
   not be the same limbs, and in most of these worlds there is no reason they
   would be.
4. **Is it symmetrical, and about what?** Bilateral is a chordate accident, not
   a law. Radial, rotational and spiral bodies are all available.
5. **What is its life cycle?** The [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/)
   already have two adult forms and no hierarchy between them; a species with a
   sessile stage, a moult, or a colonial phase is more interesting than one with
   a childhood.
6. **What does it find hard about a Ranger station?** The most useful single
   fact for the fiction, because it is the one that costs a character something
   daily. Mira's hydration cycles and Sethka's respiratory burden are both this.
7. **What did the biology make them good at, and what job followed?** Every one
   of the five already has a specialism that fits their world.

## Is dexterity a requirement? (Dermot's question, 13 August)

*"Other primates have hands but are they less intelligent than dolphins or
elephants? Is dexterity an actual requirement for space faring sapient beings?"*

**Dexterity yes, hands no — and it is probably not the real bottleneck anyway.**

What is actually required is **manipulation finer than the manipulator**: the
ability to make a thing more precise than the body that made it, and to hold
something steady while acting on it with something else. Hands are one solution,
late and awkward. The steadiness half is the half that gets forgotten, and it is
what dolphins lack — not intelligence, not sociality, just any way to *hold*
anything. Elephants are the sharper case, because a trunk is a muscular
hydrostat with finer control at the tip than a human hand, and they have no
technology. So a fine manipulator is not sufficient either.

Alternatives that genuinely work: muscular hydrostats (trunks, tentacles — an
octopus arm has distributed control and eight of them is a better workshop than
two hands); mouthparts and palps; **many small limbs in concert**, which this
record has already committed to via the six-limbed Vessik and therefore the
Chthonari; building by growing or secreting rather than assembling; and
**cooperative manipulation**, where the unit of work is several bodies — termites
build climate-controlled towers and no individual has dexterity worth the name.

**The bottleneck worth using instead is high-energy materials processing** —
what gets a people from shaped stone to metallurgy to pressure vessels. It is
harder to route around than manipulation and, unlike manipulation, it is
*different on every one of these worlds*, so it generates history rather than
anatomy:

| Species | Bottleneck | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Veyr** | None — active basalt hands them heat and pressure at the surface | Metallurgy early and free; the *hard* problem is precision on a world that shakes, so their engineering is about margin and tolerance. **Worked up in full: `src/lore/veyr-basaltborn.md`.** |
| **Pelagene** | No open combustion underwater | A metallurgy problem, not a hands problem. Vents, electrochemistry, or a long detour through biology |
| **Serephine** | Thin atmosphere burns poorly | But high UV is free energy and the daily thermal swing is enormous — a gradient civilisation rather than a fire one |
| **Chthonari** | — | They **excavate**: the Undersong Belt's own page calls it *"excavation as composition"*. Building by removing material needs little dexterity and a great deal of judgement |
| **Verdani** | Symbiont-dependent, so energy budget is light-limited | Slow, and possibly a people for whom haste is genuinely expensive |

**And the setting already grants the deeper point.** The Krenyi build no agentic
machines — *"needing no AI and wanting none are, to them, one fact rather than
two."* A species can be fully capable and simply decline a technology. Fold
transit exists, and the Chthonari engineer who left Thrum on Eden was posted in,
so **a people can reach space by being reached**. Spacefaring does not require
having solved everything alone.

**Design instruction that falls out of this:** give each species a *different
bottleneck and a different way round it*. That produces five civilisations with
distinct histories, rather than five body plans with the same story attached.

## Traps specific to this setting

- **The Earth smuggled in by a single word.** Dermot, 13 August: *"it might not
  be water as we know it"*, and *"undersea does not have to mean H₂O."* The
  danger is not that a brief says water — it is that a brief says **aquatic**,
  or **desert**, or **jungle**, and imports an entire Earth with it: tides, a
  breathable atmosphere above the surface, sunlight to a useful depth, a
  shoreline, and a chemistry that supports the same reactions ours does. None of
  that follows from *a world covered in fluid*. Ask instead what the medium
  actually fixes — pressure, density, opacity, thermal behaviour, what will
  dissolve in it — and design from those. Nerath turned out to be **several
  fluids lying stably on one another**, which is why its people's specialism is
  *fluid-boundary anomalies*, a phrase that means almost nothing on a
  single-ocean world. Now a standing refusal on
  `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`.
- **The allegory.** A species designed as a metaphor for a human subgroup
  or country — the Star Trek pattern, rejected by Dermot outright
  (29 August 2026, verbatim in `intake-2026-08-29.md`; theme note in
  `ideas.md`). The test: if the page summarises as "they're basically X"
  for any human group, start over from the world. This file's method is
  the antidote — a people derived from gravity, cold, or dense air has no
  room to be a cipher, because the world got there first.
- **The planet of hats.** One environment, one temperament, one job, no internal
  disagreement. The cast principles forbid it for humans and the same reasoning
  applies here. The Krenyi page is the standard to match — a whole practice, a
  named cost, a failure mode they would name themselves.
- **The Krenyi read as Vulcans or as High Elves.** Dermot, 17 October 2026,
  verbatim: *"Krenyi are very different from Vulcans or Tolkien's High
  Elves"*. The two templates a reader brings to a calm, long-lived,
  near-human people, and the record already refuses both point by point; the
  public pages keep the Archive's voice and name neither, so the refusal is
  written down here.
  - *Not Vulcans.* A Vulcan's calm is discipline over strong feeling, logic as
    a creed, and a quiet superiority about human emotion. A Krenyi's calm is
    **structural, not suppression**: anxiety, annoyance and real grief are
    all there, and what is missing is three extremes (deep fear, fury, grief
    that will not close). *Clarity is not logic* (`krenyi.md`, *Emotional
    Range*); they have never been recorded finding a human ridiculous for
    being afraid, and their reading of human feeling is *a different
    instrument, not a better grade*. Warm, polite, honest; never cold, never
    lecturing.
  - *Not High Elves.* An Elf is an elder race in its twilight: deathless,
    luminous, melancholy, homesick for a lost West, bearing glamour and
    lore, and fading as the younger peoples rise. A Krenyi is **long-lived,
    not deathless**; a **present-tense people** for whom the past is not
    sacred and who are poor keepers of what has not proved its use; they
    **resist glamour and refuse mythification**; they **decline to be
    placed** rather than mourn a homeland; they are neither elder nor fading
    nor ranked above anyone; and their beauty is physical attractiveness by
    humanoid standards, never radiance.
  - *The nearest template, and still not it: the Haruchai.* Dermot, same day,
    verbatim: *"The nearest but still different are the Haruchai of Thomas
    Covenant Chronicles"*. What rhymes: reflexes and composure past the human
    range, a near-absence of fear, plain honesty, few words to outsiders, a
    way of passing things between themselves that others cannot follow,
    service to another people offered by choice, and little said about
    themselves. What does not: the Haruchai's in-group channel is mind-speech,
    which this record refuses outright (*What the Record Refuses*,
    psionics), where the Krenyi side channel is micro-expression, word
    choice and small hand signals, deliberate and only ever adding to what
    is said aloud; the Haruchai are warriors, and the Krenyi are not, in a
    Corps no branch of which is military; the Haruchai are proud and judge
    failure, their own above all, without mercy, where a Krenyi declines
    glory and softens the delivery, never the fact, and is not recorded
    holding anyone in contempt; the Haruchai are human, the Krenyi of
    independent origin on the human frame. The useful lesson is the near
    miss itself: the nearest template is the one most likely to leak into a
    draft, so a Krenyi scene that turns martial, prideful or mind-to-mind has
    drifted toward it.
  - *For prompts and prose:* no pointed ears, no luminous skin, no sorrowful
    or ancient gaze, no flowing robes or circlets; no clipped emotionless
    delivery and no raised-eyebrow condescension. The existing Krenyi
    negatives (*elf ears, Vulcan, glowing skin*) are this trap's prompt form.
- **The humanoid frame with features applied.** The Qiren Tal portrait failed on
  exactly this: insectoid head and carapace on a bipedal two-armed body. Adding
  features to a human silhouette is not designing a species.
- **Technology that announces itself.** These peoples get the same treatment as
  everyone else — no glowing rectangles, no holograms, matte objects, and
  settlements that look like whatever suits their world rather than like the
  future. A high-gravity volcanic civilisation may well look ancient.
- **Explaining the biology in dialogue.** The record's habit is to state a fact
  once, flatly, and let the reader do the work. Thrum's page never explains why
  a burrow animal drums; it says what the drumming means where it comes from and
  what it means on Deck 7, and stops.
- **Making difference into difficulty only.** Sethka finds station air hard;
  that is good. But a species should also be *better* at something than
  everyone around it, or the difference reads as a disability rather than a
  different world.
- **The human concept with alien furniture.** A people whose body was derived
  from their world and whose family, career, status, death and personhood
  are ours with the nouns changed. Dermot's direction, 14 September 2026:
  *alien cultures have very different concepts of relationships, life, death,
  family, career, role, status, legacy, individuality and personhood* — see
  `mind-design.md`'s eighth question, which asks each of the ten for every
  people and allows *absent* and *unaskable* as answers. The body note gets
  a people's senses right; if the mind note is skipped, the senses end up
  serving a human life.

## The uniform equivalent (Dermot's direction, 10 September 2026)

*"Aliens have uniform equivalents"* (`intake-2026-09-10.md`, fourth
section), on the day the Rangers' uniform was given its reason — the
hospital's: a mark a stranger in a working place reads to know who does
what, built for the work, announcing a function and not a claim on anyone.
That adds one question to the ones above that decide a body, and it is a
question about the *medium*, since the job is universal and the form is
each people's own. `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md` now says this
generally and asserts nothing per species; this is where the per-species
answer gets derived when a page needs it. Nothing below is canon.

- **Krenyi** — a clothed, humanoid, calendar-keeping people; cloth and a
  worn mark are available and would be the boring answer. Shapes: (a) worn
  marks of their own grammar, deliberately unlike the Rangers' geometry;
  (b) a mark carried in the calendar-keeping itself — what a Krenyi
  officer is, told by which count she keeps.
- **Pelagene Littoral** — an ocean people; cloth is a poor medium in water.
  Shapes: (a) colour or pattern on the body, controllable, as some
  cephalopods have; (b) a carried object that reads in the water — a
  sound-maker, a tool worn where it can be seen.
  **Taken for Mira of Brine's portrait prompt, 13 October 2026** (Fable,
  under *Who approves whom*; his to overrule; `images.md` § 12): she is a
  Ranger on duty (`src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md` names Pelagene
  surveyors like Mira among the modular shell's wearers), so the prompt
  gives the Rangers' shell as a Pelagene wears it, a few small fitted
  sections seated between the folds with nearly all of the integument left
  open, since the integument is the organ of perception and must stay wet
  (`src/lore/pelagene-littoral.md`); and one plain seated tab with no
  colour, her corps and rank being off the record, on the Veyr precedent
  below. That is the Rangers' uniform on a Pelagene, not the people's own
  equivalent, which stays open as (a) or (b).
- **Veyr Basaltborn** — shapes: (a) a mark worked into the body's own
  surface; (b) a worn plate, on the Rangers' own magnetic principle,
  which would explain why the Veyr took to the Rangers' tab standard
  easily.
  **Ruled 12 October 2026** (Dermot, choosing *"Marks seated on the body"*
  for Qiren Tal's portrait): a Veyr on duty wears no garment. The corps
  colour and the rank tab are seated directly on the lattice body, as
  Rook-7's marks are on its chassis (*bolted to a different surface*,
  `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`), which is shape (b) on the
  body itself. Where a Veyr's corps or rank is not on record, a prompt
  shows one plain seated tab with no colour. The Veyr's own mark, if
  they have one beside the Rangers', is still undescribed.
- **Serephine Dunekin** — a scout people; shapes: (a) posture or gait —
  a role told by how a body carries itself, which a desert people who
  read distance would find natural; (b) a worn mark.
- **Chthonari** — thought as the Undersong (`mind-design.md`); the obvious
  medium is sound. Shapes: (a) a role carried as a standing tone or a
  phrase in the Undersong, so that who does what is heard, not seen;
  (b) nothing — a people who cannot read one another needing no mark, and
  saying so.
- **Verdani** — the lore page notes strain-linked coloration; that is
  biology, not a mark, and must not be conscripted as one. Shapes: (a) a
  worn or grown token, distinct from strain colour; (b) an absence, with
  the reason stated.
- **The Told** — read the three Below the Roof chapters before claiming
  anything: a people whose names are tellings may carry role the same
  way, and a visual mark for a deep-side people is the first thing to
  doubt. Shapes: (a) a role is told, not shown; (b) nothing yet, absence
  marked.
- **Pandoids** — the Fliade entry and Teddy's page describe an observational
  register; nothing about marks. Absence marked.
  *Scale and frame, ruled 7 October 2026* (Dermot, verbatim: *"Pandoids are
  less humanoid than the Kyreni but more humanoid than almost any other
  people."*, and the same evening *"Pandoids look more like cute giant teddy
  bears than like sloths"* and *"A human in a cold suit would almost be the
  size of a Pandoid and almost blend in if the suit had black and white
  markings."*): upright on two legs with forelimbs of equal length, about
  the size of a suited human, a round head on a round body; the Krenyi are
  the only people on the human frame (`src/lore/krenyi.md`), and the
  Pandoids sit just below them on the humanoid scale, above every other
  people the record holds. The cold-suit reading is noted in
  `intake-2026-10-07.md` and not yet on a page.

Rule for using this list: pick a shape only in the page that needs it, in
that page's own voice, and log the pick in the canonical guide if it coins
a term. A people whose equivalent is *nothing* is a valid answer and an
interesting one, and the lore page's *"does not guess"* line is there to
keep it available.

## Marks of belonging (Dermot's direction, 11 October 2026)

Asked whether non-human peoples have an equivalent of a flag, he chose a principle and no designs yet. A people marks belonging in its own medium, as it marks a role (the uniform equivalent above): a phrase, a struck rhythm, a colour, a posture, a scent, whatever its senses carry best. Nothing is defined per people until a page needs it, and then it is derived here first, from the people's senses and world, never borrowed from a human flag. The Celtic Union's ceremonial flag (six stars for six arks, `celtic-union-of-planets.md`, same day) is the one human polity's flag on record.

**Dress, ornament and surface colour, people by people** (14 October 2026): worked out from the record, derived or proposed per line, with the open choices his, in `civilian-dress.md`, principle 15 and *Peoples in their own media*.

## Where this goes

Each species that gets a body plan gets a lore page on the pattern of
[Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) — physical and social characteristics,
something the species is structurally unable to do, the thing outsiders reliably
get wrong — and its blocked portrait becomes briefable. Four of the five have no
page at all; the Verdani have one that never says what a Verdani looks like.

Currently blocked on prose, with placeholder cards standing: **Sethka Ru**
(Serephine), **Qiren Tal** (Veyr), **Isren Farrowkin** (Verdani).

## Prismeri — Prismere (derived 3 September 2026, Dermot's direction: *"derive the prismere body form based on their planetary ecology please"*)

Drafted into `src/lore/prismeri.md` as three sections (*A Body for Thin Ground
and Thick Air*, *What They Are Structurally Bad At*, *The Thing Outsiders Get
Wrong*) on the Krenyi pattern; draft-and-stop. The seven questions, answered
from `src/lore/planets/prismere.md` and what the Prismeri page already held:

1. **What the world does to a body.** Low gravity, dense particulate air, a
   biosphere that lights itself from below, a silent apex glider that hunts
   from above at dusk. So: flight is cheap and honest; a light, long, level-
   spined frame; wings as forelimbs, not as an addition to arms; an upward-
   looking threat sense; a curfew written into the body.
2. **How it senses.** Two forward eyes low on the head for a flier's depth of
   field; a crystalline facet band along the skull ridge that images nothing
   and reads change in the overhead light (the ray-shadow sense); in Full
   Wings, the canon tendrils from the same ridge, reading corridor geometry
   directly. The Lattice is a thing the world shows; the tendrils are the
   organ that reads it without the haze.
3. **Limbs and what they are for.** Four. Wing-forelimbs with three free
   clawed fingers at the leading edge (the hands); prehensile long-toed hind
   feet (the hold). Quadrupedal on the ground, knuckle-walking on folded
   wings, four-point launch — the umbral ray's own method, so the world
   already has it. Manipulation finer than the manipulator comes from the
   division: feet hold, fingers work, which needs the body to hang.

### The body sheet: flight, shell, neck and tail against Prismere's physics (drafted 14 October 2026)

Dermot asked whether it makes biological sense for the Prismeri to resemble
dragons, *"If so then that restriction may be lifted"*, and then, verbatim:
*"However a large flying creature must be consistent with physics and
planetary conditions."* The record gives no numbers: Prismere's gravity is
*low*, its air *dense and full of suspended crystal*, and no page gives a
Prismeri's mass or span. So this sheet proposes figures, works the physics
from them, and checks the results against the two constraints the record does
fix. Every figure is **proposed** and his to confirm. Nothing here is on a
public page.

**Proposed figures.**

| | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Surface gravity | 0.40 g (3.9 m/s²) | Low, and low enough that hanging by the feet is cheap; see the station check below |
| Surface air density | 2.5 kg/m³ (about twice Earth's) | *Dense*; enough to carry the particulate haze |
| Oxygen partial pressure | about 30 kPa (Earth: 21) | Not on record; proposed because a large shelled flier needs it (below) |
| First Wings | 25 kg, 4.0 m span, aspect ratio 6 | Light frame, low stall speed, forest flight (`prismeri.md`) |
| Full Wings | 45 kg, 5.5 m span, aspect ratio 8 | Heavier plated form, sustained flight into the haze layer |

**Flight, worked.** A standard flight-power model (parasite drag plus induced
drag, the minimum-power speed found numerically; drag coefficient 0.03, span
efficiency 0.87) gives:

| | Wing loading | Minimum power | Speed at minimum power | Stall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Wings, Prismere | 37 N/m² | 1.9 W/kg | 4.9 m/s | 4.6 m/s |
| Full Wings, Prismere | 47 N/m² | 1.7 W/kg | 5.2 m/s | 5.2 m/s |
| First Wings, standard station (1 g, 1.2 kg/m³) | 92 N/m² | 10.9 W/kg | 11.2 m/s | 10.5 m/s |
| Full Wings, standard station | 117 N/m² | 9.9 W/kg | 11.8 m/s | 11.8 m/s |
| *Earth flying fox, for comparison* | *26 N/m²* | *5.8 W/kg* | | |
| *Quetzalcoatlus, for comparison* | *267 N/m²* | *11.0 W/kg* | | |

Two checks against the record, and both pass:

- *Flight is cheap* on Prismere (`prismeri.md`). At under 2 W/kg, a
  45-kilogram flier spends less per kilogram than an Earth bat, and the slow
  speeds suit glasswood groves and a hanging launch.
- *On a standard-gravity station a Prismeri can neither fly nor hang*
  (`prismeri.md`). At 1 g in station air the same body needs five to six
  times the power per kilogram and stalls at over 10 m/s, which no corridor
  allows, and its feet would carry two and a half times the load they evolved
  for. The proposed figures reproduce the record's own line, which is the best
  evidence that they are in the right range.

**The shell.** The page gives the Prismeri an exoskeleton: soft grey-violet in
First Wings, hardened gold-and-blue chitin plate in Full Wings, with an
irreversible moult inside a self-secreted mineral shell between them. That
binds harder than gravity does.

- *Moulting.* A large animal inside a shell is helpless while a new one
  hardens. A single, irreversible moult inside a protective mineral case is
  exactly how a big shelled flier would solve that: moult once, protected, and
  never again. The canon life cycle is the physically right answer, and was
  written before anyone asked.
- *Plate weight.* Shell mass grows faster than body size, which sets a
  ceiling. At 0.40 g the ceiling is about two and a half times higher than on
  Earth, which is what lets a 45-kilogram plated flier exist at all.
- *Breathing.* Earth's largest insects lived when the air held more oxygen.
  If the Prismeri breathe through a shell, dense air richer in oxygen than
  Earth's is what makes their size work. Hence the proposed 30 kPa. If they
  have lungs, the figure matters less.

**Neck and tail, decided by the physics.**

- *Neck.* For the same bending strain, a neck at 0.40 g can carry the head on
  a lever two and a half times longer than on Earth. Physics would therefore
  allow a long neck. A shelled neck would be built of many short plated rings,
  more like a jointed limb than a smooth serpentine neck. **Physics permits a
  long neck; it does not require one.**
- *Tail.* In flight a tail buys steadiness and costs weight. Bats fly well
  without one through active wing control, and early pterosaurs flew with one.
  **Physics is neutral.**
- *What decides both is the ecology the record already holds.* A Prismeri
  works hanging head-down, with feet holding and wing-fingers working. The eyes
  need to be close to the hands, which favours the head carried against the
  shoulders. A tail hangs uselessly in that posture and snags in a glasswood
  stand. So the confirmed short neck and no tail, ruled on 12 October to pass
  the Levril test, also follow from the body's own way of life. The physics
  gives them a second, independent reason.

**What follows for his question.** It does make biological sense for a flier
on this world to look like a dragon. The neck and tail of a Prismeri, though,
are now published canon (`prismeri.md`, clarified 12 October). Under the
standing rule, published canon changes only by extension, clarification or
genuine dilemma. Physics supplies no dilemma, because the confirmed shape is
consistent with it and better motivated than the dragon outline. Lifting the
Levril test as the *reason* changes nothing on the page. Lengthening the neck
or adding a tail would be a change of canon by preference, and that is his
alone to make. Choices put to him:

- **A. Keep the shape; record the physics as its basis.** (Recommended; **ruled 14 October 2026**, *"Option A is fine"*.) The
  Levril test stays as a picture rule, and the body has its physical reasons.
- **B. Lift the Levril test and lengthen the neck modestly** (a jointed, plated
  neck about the length of the head). This is a canon change he would make
  knowingly, with the plates kept as the naturalist's earlier reading.
- **C. Confirm the proposed figures** (gravity, air density, oxygen, mass and
  span) as canon, whichever of A or B he takes. They would go on the Prismere
  and Prismeri pages as a clarification.

**Traps avoided.** No human frame: the spine is level, the wings are the
forelimbs, rest is hanging, ground travel is quadrupedal. No insectoid head
on a biped (the Qiren Tal failure). No planet of hats: the two forms do
different work for a physical reason, and the outsiders-get-wrong section is
about posture, which is the Krenyi standard applied.

**Readings flagged in `intake-2026-09-03.md`, eleventh direction.**

### What the record already fixes (derived)

Gathered in one place, as the sections above do for the other peoples.
Nothing here is new.

- **Nine centres, one person.** A dominant central brain for judgement,
  language and planning; eight primary limbs, each carrying its own
  semi-autonomous ganglion with sensory processing, reflexes and a measurable
  preference and mood of its own; the central brain negotiates with the
  limb-ganglia and does not fully override them (`src/lore/cerebraun.md`,
  *Physical and Cognitive Characteristics*). A single Cerebraun is one person
  with nine processing centres, not nine persons (the same page: *a single
  Cerebraun can hold…*). Under `mind-design.md`'s standing constraint a
  people whose cognition is distributed is written in its own terms, never as
  a disorder or a hive, and the page already does this: its physicians treat
  a limb acting against the central brain's stated intention as ordinary.
- **Frame: Hydrostata.** Fluid pressure, *no rigid skeleton at all*, the row
  shared with the Pelagene Littoral (`src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`,
  the frames table). The frame says how a body is held up and nothing about
  kinship; sharing a row with the Pelagene makes the two taxonomic neighbours
  and no more.
- **The skin is a channel.** Chromatophore and texture-changing tissue
  capable of rapid, high-bandwidth display, used for identity signalling,
  emotional broadcast and a formal display-language reserved for precise
  technical and diplomatic work (`cerebraun.md`). The display resolves
  finely: Sohrel's dissent is *a slow color shift across four centimeters of
  skin* (S03E01C01), so a patch a few centimetres wide carries a statement.
- **Two channels at once.** Spoken or written language from the central
  brain; skin-display from the limb-ganglia beneath it (`cerebraun.md`,
  *Communication*; the `canon_facts` of S03E01C01; the Xenolinguistics
  Handbook's first failure mode). So a Cerebraun speaks aloud, reads and
  writes: the body has a voice and reads a page.
- **The limbs are told apart by position.** *The limb-ganglion governing
  Sohrel's third dorsal arm* (S03E01C01; *the third dorsal arm's ganglion*
  again in C03; C02 has *the limb-ganglion beneath the desk*). Dorsal arms
  exist and are counted, which fixes a dorsal side and a ventral one: the
  body has an up and a down, and the eight limbs are not an undifferentiated
  ring.
- **The limbs are not all alike in what they do.** The third dorsal arm
  argues, hums faintly beneath a desk, goes still as *the shape of a
  disagreement choosing not to spend itself*, and reads as recognition (C01,
  C02, C03). A limb can act, sound and signal on its own. Where in the limb
  the ganglion sits is **not** on record: the designed card for Sohrel draws
  a node at each tip, and that is the card's device, not canon.
- **Sohrel sits at a desk, reads, drafts and signs** (C01, C02, C03), on a
  relay station with humans aboard (C01: *unreadable to any human on the
  relay station*). So the body works at a surface, in a room humans also use,

  to dislike it over prolonged exposure. Their preferred air is still not on

- **A people who need instruments for a water people's channels.** Cerebraun

- **The likeness is the observer's.** *Cephalopod-like to a human eye*; no

### What a human sees (derived, restated for a prompt)

A soft body with no bones in it; eight limbs; a skin that changes colour and
texture while you watch. Those three facts are the whole of the likeness, and
they are why the generator reaches for an octopus every time: on Earth they
occur together in exactly one lineage. The job of the points below is to say
what is there that an octopus does not have, and what an octopus has that is
not there, so that the likeness fires at a glance and fails on inspection,
which is the record's own pattern for a people (the Krenyi, *The Nearest
Frame Is the Strangest*).

### What is actually there (proposed, each a choice)

Answered by the body note's seven questions, in their order, from the facts
above; nothing from the world, because the world is not on record.

1. **What the world did to the body: not on record, and this draft does not
   supply it.** Two shapes. **(a) Leave the homeworld unnamed** and derive
   only what the body already fixes, naming the world when a page needs it
   (Dermot's scope ruling of 13 August: anywhere in the known Concordant, or
   elsewhere in the membrane where crossover is safe). **(b) Name a world
   type now** (a dense-atmosphere world where a boneless body is partly borne
   by the air, or a shore world of shallow fluid) so the body can be derived
   forward as the others are. **Recommended: (a).** Nothing on any page needs
   the world, the Meridian hint argues against the obvious ocean, and a world
   named to explain a picture is the error the whole method exists to avoid.

2. **How it senses: no head, no pair of eyes; sight along the limbs.** Each
   limb-ganglion does its own sensory processing (derived), and a people who
   read a four-centimetre colour shift on their own and one another's limbs
   need colour vision close to the skin. So: **no head**, and **no two large
   forward eyes**; instead many small eyes along the limbs, several to a
   limb, each ganglion seeing its own neighbourhood of skin and the room
   beyond it, and the central brain seeing through the limbs. This is the
   single point that does most against the octopus attractor, whose
   silhouette is a head with two eyes, and it is the point that makes the
   people's own habit (nine centres, each with its own view) visible in a
   photograph. **Alternative:** a cluster of small eyes on the central body
   and none on the limbs. **Recommended: along the limbs.**

3. **Limbs: four dorsal, four ventral, and they do different work.** *Third
   dorsal arm* fixes a dorsal set of at least three (derived). Proposed: eight
   in two tiers of four, the **ventral four** short, broad and load-bearing,
   the body resting on them, and the **dorsal four** longer and freer, the
   working and signalling limbs; a Cerebraun at a desk rests on the lower
   tier with the upper tier on the surface or draped beneath it, as Sohrel's
   third dorsal arm is. No suckers and no hooks: a limb's inner surface is
   the same textured skin as the rest, and it holds by wrapping and by the
   texture-change tissue raising a grip (the tissue is derived; the grip is
   proposed). **Alternative:** eight alike around the body, *dorsal* meaning
   only the upper half in posture. **Recommended: two tiers**, because it
   gives the generator a body plan an octopus does not have and gives the
   prose something to do with *which limb*.

4. **Symmetry: dorsoventral, not radial.** Follows from 3. A top and a
   bottom, an upper tier and a lower, and **no front**: a Cerebraun addresses
   a room with whichever limbs are nearest, and *facing* someone is a courtesy
   of turning the signalling tier toward them, not a fact of anatomy. (The
   first half is derived from *dorsal*; the no-front is proposed.)

5. **Surface: dry, matte and finely textured at rest.** Nothing says the body
   is wet, and the Meridian hint says the people are not native to a chemical
   ocean. Proposed: an integument dry to the touch, matte, with a fine raised
   grain the texture tissue can smooth flat or lift into ridges; colour at
   rest **quiet and uniform**, one muted tone across the whole body, so that
   a display reads as a change from stillness (the formal display-language is
   *reserved*, which implies a resting state that says nothing). This refuses
   the generator's wet gloss, the octopus's second tell after the eyes. The
   resting tone is not chosen here; the two cards use violet and teal, and
   either would do. **Alternative:** a wet skin kept moist on a station, on
   the Pelagene pattern. **Recommended: dry.**

6. **Life cycle: not on record; nothing proposed.** Sohrel has held one post
   for about three centuries (`sohrel.md`), so a Cerebraun lives at least
   that long; whether the limbs and the central brain age differently,
   whether a limb is ever lost and regrown, and how a young Cerebraun comes
   by nine centres are open and better answered by a chapter than by a note.
   Marked absent.

7. **What a station costs them: the second channel, unread.** Derived from
   the page and the chapters: on a human station nobody reads the skin, so
   half of what a Cerebraun says lands nowhere, every day. The body note's
   sixth question was answered by the lore before the body was described,
   which is unusual and worth keeping: the daily cost is not physical. One
   physical cost is proposed and small: a boneless body at human-station
   gravity tires in the lower tier, and a Cerebraun working long at a surface
   rests the body on it, which a human reads as slumping and should not.

8. **What the biology made them good at, and the job that followed
   (derived).** Holding a correct position and an unresolved one at once
   without pretending the second is not there (`sohrel.md`), which is the
   Administrator-Voice's whole craft; and the Prime Ganglion is held by
   *whichever Cerebraun demonstrates the clearest capacity to hold the whole
   polity's competing internal signals in coherent balance*
   (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`). The polity's structure is the neurology at
   scale, by the species' own account. Nothing to add.

**Scale, for the frame only (proposed).** No page gives a size. For Sohrel's
portrait the body is drawn at about the bulk of a seated adult human, low and
broad on its ventral tier, the dorsal limbs reaching a desk built for it; the
desk is Sohrel's own on a Hegemony relay station, so its height is the
species' and not a human's. A scale fixed for a picture is not canon, and the
record need not state one until a chapter does.

## Ollune — a body from Cirrane (20 October 2026, Dermot's *"Take A3-3 as the Ollune body"*)

**Why.** Ilyare's portrait prompt (`images.md` § 21, PR #1375) asked for the
people's page body as written: *a translucent gas bladder several metres
across, trailing a fringe of manipulating tendrils and filter-vanes beneath
it*. All four variations of the first round came back as a bell jellyfish
with leaf-shaped veins. Dermot asked for the body-design study loop
(`people-body-design`) the same day. Three plans were studied: a lens, a raft
of fused cells and an upright envelope over a keel. He took the lens (*"Agreed"*),
asked for a third round (*"the cap as living skin of the envelope, the vanes
as living membrane"*), and then took frame A3-3. Drafted on Opus. Every point
is marked **derived** (it follows from a page cited, or from physics applied
to one) or **proposed** (a choice that fits, his to overrule).

### What the rulings and pages force (derived)

- **Mostly envelope, buoyant on warmth.** In a hydrogen-helium sky there is no
  lighter gas, so an Ollune floats on gas *warmer* than the air around it,
  held there *by continuous metabolic effort, every hour it is alive*. A body
  that stops paying sinks (`ollune.md`, *Buoyancy as Metabolism*).
- **No surface, no fire, no metal; mass is a tax** (`ollune.md`). Nothing
  carried, nothing worn, no glowing core (`civilian-dress.md`, Ollune).
- **Filter-feeders on the falling haze.** The shell's food web runs on
  photochemical aerosol drifting down in sunlight. Its large fauna are
  *overwhelmingly soft, buoyant, and translucent* (`planets/cirrane.md`, *The
  Temperate Shell*).
- **Holds.** Eight to thirty sleep *physically linked at the tendril*, the
  waking carrying the sinking (`ollune.md`). So the tendrils must be able to
  hold a neighbour.
- **Not a metazoan, and not on the human frame** (`cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`;
  `what-the-record-refuses.md`). The two speech channels are the page's, and
  the body adds nothing to them.

### What Cirrane derives (physics applied to the page)

- **What kills is heat lost.** An Ollune dies by going under, and it goes
  under when it can no longer keep its envelope warm. So the body's first
  economy is heat. A broad envelope gives the most lift for its skin.
- **The sun is free heat.** A dark surface facing the sky takes the sun's
  warmth without the body making it. So the upper face is dark and the bill is
  smaller by day. That fits the page's *every hour*: the charge never stops,
  and at night it is paid in full.
- **The haze falls, so the vanes face out and down.** Feeding is from air the
  body drifts through. The vanes stand at the rim, where the air meets the
  body first.

### The body (proposed unless marked)

1. **Envelope.** One broad, low, lopsided gas envelope, about eight metres
   across and two thick, much wider than tall, gently domed above and rounded
   below. It is one continuous living skin, inflated and taut like a lung,
   with no separate covering and no loose edge. **Translucent** (derived,
   `ollune.md`) where it is thin.
2. **The dark cap.** On the upper face the same skin thickens gradually into
   a matte, finely furred russet-brown, patched and weathered, that drinks the
   sunlight. Toward the rim it thins, with no line or edge, into translucent
   membrane that glows amber against the sun, crossed by irregular branching
   veins. The rim is a fat, soft, rounded edge of the same skin.
3. **Filter-vanes.** A ring of soft vanes of living membrane, growing just
   beneath the rim. Each vane is a translucent fan of fine radiating ribs on a
   thick fleshy root, uneven in size and spacing, some spread and some
   half-folded. They are the *filter-vanes* the page gives (derived), placed.
4. **Tendrils.** About a dozen fine, straight, thread-thin tendrils of
   different lengths, two or three metres long, hang from the centre of the
   underside like plumb lines. They are the *manipulating tendrils* (derived)
   and the hold's links: in a hold they reach sideways to a neighbour's.
5. **No face, eyes, mouth, head or limbs.** The near word is chemical, at
   close range. The far word is low-frequency pressure (derived, `ollune.md`),
   and the envelope is the body's largest surface for both.
6. **Variation.** Size, the shape of the lean and the cap's patching tell one
   Ollune from another. Colour does not, beyond weathering. This replaces
   `civilian-dress.md`'s proposed *size and veining*.

**Colour.** Russet and ochre on the cap. Amber and gold where the sun comes
through. A faint blush on the vanes. Never grey, and never glass.

### Kinds, not stages (proposed; his direction)

Dermot's second direction makes the Spiralites **a symbiotic set of
peoples**: more than one kind, living together, each with strengths,
abilities and needs the others complete. The record's name for the whole,
*the Spiralites*, stays the name for the whole. Three readings were weighed
and one is taken.

- **(A) Three kin lineages, one world, one people by the people's own
  rule.** Three kinds of independent-enough descent to be filed as three
  lineages (`cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`: a lineage is local, phylogenetic,
  one world), each a people, each keeping a count, living in and as the kept
  turn. **Taken**, as his words say *peoples*.
- **(B) Three stages of one body.** A walking one roots and becomes a rooted
  one, on the Prismeri pattern (two adult forms, no hierarchy between them,
  `prismeri.md`). Not taken: it is one people, not a set, it would be the
  Prismeri's device a second time, and it would say something about how a
  new one comes to be, which the account keeps in the quiet.
- **(C) One kind, varied by role and age.** The brief as first read. Not
  taken, by his second direction; kept as the baseline plan in *Three plans*
  below, since it is what the next sheet will be judged against.

**What this is the first of, and what it is not.** Nothing in the record is
a symbiotic set of peoples. The Verdani are one people in obligate
partnership with a microflora that is not a mind (`verdani.md`); the vessik
is *an animal, a burrow commensal, a companion and never a colleague*
(`glossary/simian.md`); the Prismeri are one body at two hardnesses; a
Cerebraun is one person with nine centres. So the Spiralites would be the
record's first case of peoples in symbiosis, and `glossary/simian.md`'s
*nearly every people on record shares its world with kin lineages that are
capable, complex, and not people* already leaves room with its *nearly*.
Two lines to watch, neither a bar: **the singular-exception rule** names four
standing cases and one reserved, and *a symbiotic set of peoples* is not one
of them, so this adds no second instance to a named exception; and
`verdani.md`'s *distinguished from every other catalogued species by an
obligate symbiotic relationship* stands literally, since the Spiralites are
not catalogued (nobody has visited), but it is the sentence a reader will
set beside this one, and the Verdani page would want a clarifying clause
(*with a symbiont that is not a person*) when a Spiralite page lands. That
clause is Opus's and is not written here.

**Not a ranking, not a service** (CLAUDE.md: minds not ranked; no people a
servant or a beast of another). Each kind below is somebody by the people's
own rule and by the record's, each has an ability the others lack and a
need the others meet, and no kind's work is done *for* another: the turn is
kept by all three and is what lets any of them be beside the ringing. The
account's own refusal governs the whole: *a telling is one voice*, and a
kind is not a role. A walking one may keep a turn's inner tuning; a rooted
one may be a teller; the kinds disagree, and the record has met none of
them.

### The body, kind by kind (plan 3; proposed unless marked)

Working names are this note's and not canon: *the rooted*, *the walking*,
*the ground kind*. When a page needs a rendering, the desk renders by what
the telling gives, as it did for *Inner Turn*, and these words will not be

**Common to all three (derived where marked).**

- **The helix.** The *bioluminescent neural structure beneath the skin*
  (derived) is, in each kind, one long nerve wound as an open helix through
  the body's length: the organ that makes the pulse and reads the field, and
  the tissue that glows when a pulse is made (proposed, from the account's
  *where the speaking is made*). It is a loose, open winding of a few turns,
  never a tight logarithmic coil (a tight coil draws an ammonite, a snail or
  a galaxy). Seen through the tissue it is a soft blur of light, not a drawn
  line.
- **Semi-translucent, frosted, dry** (translucency derived; the finish
  proposed). The tissue is translucent to a depth of a few centimetres, like
  unpolished amber or sea-glass, and its surface is matte, fine-grained and
  dry. Not clear, not wet, not glossy. This is the finish rule that keeps the
  body from a jellyfish, a glass sculpture and a lamp.
- **The light.** Faint, blue-green, seen in dim light and inside a turn,
  hardly at all under the orange day; it brightens where a pulse is made and
  for as long as one is made, and a body at rest shows almost none
  (proposed). Blue-green because it must be read against an orange star's
  ambient and by a kind whose light sense evolved under it, and because one
  lineage's chemistry gives one hue (option A under *Colour*). The light is
  not the speech (derived) and the people do not signal with it on purpose;
  it tells another Spiralite that one is speaking and which part of a body
  the pulse comes from, and that is all.
- **Light sense, no eyes a human reads.** A band of small dark
  photoreceptive spots, flush with the surface, where each kind's crown is
  (proposed, from *made so that it shows*). No orb, no lens, no pair, no
  gaze. Nothing on any kind reads as a face.
- **No mouth visible, no hearing in air** (proposed). Feeding is **not
  established** beyond that no kind shows a mouth; the people's channel is
  the field and the ground, and air sound is close to nothing to them.
- **Frames**: *Turgorida* for the rooted and *Hydrostata* for the walking
  and the ground kind (proposed). A frame says how a body is held up and
  claims no kinship; sharing a row with the Verdani or the Pelagene makes a
  taxonomic neighbour and no more (`cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`). The real
  relation here is the lineage rank: three kin lineages of one world.

**The rooted kind.**

- **What it is.** A standing column of translucent tissue, rooted into the
  fabric of a turn and growing with it: two to four metres tall, thirty to
  fifty centimetres through at the base, tapering to a soft rounded crown;
  the helix runs the whole height, wider windings low and tighter high, so
  that the column is itself one tuned element of the turn it stands in.
  Several stand in a turn, spaced along the winding. It does not move.
- **What it gives.** Reach and standing. A column of that height, wound to
  the turn's frequency, is the one body that carries a pulse along the turn
  and is beside the ringing at every return without having to go anywhere;
  the account's tellings, said beside the ringing and found there at the
  next return, are found by the rooted first. It keeps the long count.
- **What it needs.** Tuning, and warning. When the ground moves, a rooted one
  is out of tune with its turn and cannot do anything about it: it cannot
  move, cannot build and cannot hear the ground. Without the other two kinds
  it stands in a turn that drifts and says things into a place where nothing
  is sure.
- **Colour.** Deep amber through red-brown, the tint laid down at the
  surface against the star and deepening with time, so an old rooted one is
  nearly opaque at the base and still frosted-clear at the crown, where the
  light shows (proposed). *With time*: the record holds no lifespan and no
  conversion from a return, so nothing here says how long.
- **Carriage.** Upright, still. A rooted one attends by brightening where
  the pulse is made, and that is the only movement a human would see.

**The walking kind.**

- **What it is.** A low arch: a thick bow of translucent tissue about two
  metres from foot to foot and a little over a metre high at the crown,
  standing on **exactly two** broad, soft, elongated feet set far apart, the
  helix running the bow's whole length from foot to foot. From the underside
  of the crown hang **exactly four** short working limbs, in two pairs: soft
  hydrostats with no digits, each ending in a flattened tip that folds shut
  like a closed bud, carried curled and touching nothing when not at work.
  One pair holds while the other works (the dexterity note above: steadiness
  is the half that gets forgotten). The photoreceptor band runs along the
  crown's top. There is no head, no neck and no front: a walking one faces a
  thing by turning so that one foot is nearer it.
- **How it moves.** Slowly, by lifting one foot and swinging it round the
  other, the bow pivoting on the planted foot, a pace at a time; a tuner's
  gait, and a people who count in returns are not hurried. The low crown and
  the long feet keep it stable on a line.
- **What it gives.** Building and tuning. Its two feet, two metres apart,
  read the field and the ground at both ends of a baseline, so a walking one
  walks a turn and feels where it has gone out of true, as a keeper of
  shielding walks a seam; its limbs do the fine work of building a turn and
  tuning it back over the three returns. It carries the near word from turn
  to turn, since it is the one that moves.
- **What it needs.** A tuned turn. Its own helix is short and bent and its
  pulse carries only to the next body; it has no reach and cannot hold a tune
  by itself, so it is beside the ringing only inside a turn the rooted
  stand in. Where the turn is drowned, a walking one's feet find no tune to
  walk, and its small saying is said and said again.
- **Colour.** Pale olive through honey-amber, lighter than the rooted and
  more even, since it moves between sun and the turn's shade; the helix shows
  along the bow as a blurred lit thread in dim light (proposed).

**The ground kind.**

- **What it is.** A broad, flat lens of translucent tissue lying on and
  partly in the ground at a turn's footing: about a metre across, fifteen to
  twenty-five centimetres thick, its whole underside in contact with the
  ground, the helix lying in it as a flat open spiral of a few windings seen
  from above through the frosted upper face. No limbs. It moves rarely and
  by a slow ripple of its edge.
- **What it gives.** The slow warning. Its underside is the widest contact
  with the ground any Spiralite has, and it hears the ground move before a
  turn has gone out of tune; it keeps the ground's count, and a turn is kept
  because a ground one said, through the rock, that the ground was going.
- **What it needs.** To be answered. It emits least of the three, and only
  by conduction through the ground, so only a rooted one's root and a
  walking one's feet hear it; alone, a ground one says things that nothing
  is beside. It cannot build, reach or stand.
- **Colour.** Water-clear grey with a faint milkiness, almost untinted,
  since it lives in shade and ground; the light shows plainest in this kind,
  low and blue-green, when it speaks (proposed).

**Scale, for the frame only (proposed).** As above. A rooted one is the
tallest thing in a turn, a walking one stands lower than a human and wider,
and a ground one is underfoot. The three in one frame are a column, an arch
and a disc, and that is the test of the plan: a sheet in which the kinds
could be confused with one another has failed.

**How the kinds meet.** By contact and by the turn. A walking one lays a
folded limb-tip against a rooted one's crown or sets a foot on the ground
where a ground one lies, and the near pulse passes by touch; anything meant
to be found is said through the turn beside the ringing, where all three
hear it. There is no hierarchy in the ring: the rooted carry what the
walking say and the ground kind warn of what the rooted cannot feel, and
each is nobody without the other two by the people's own rule, since a
count nobody is beside is not kept (proposed, from the account's method).

**The harm, made visible once and quietly** (proposed; the tone rule). In
the outer turns, where the unkept is wide, the rooted stand lit more often
and longer, saying things again into a turn that does not hold them, and a
walking one's feet find no tune to walk. Inside the inner turn, where the
quiet is, bodies are dim and lit briefly. A portrait shows the second and
never the first.

### Colour: why the world gives these, and one choice

Translucency is derived and tint is what is left, so the colours follow
from where each kind lives under an orange star: pigment laid down against
the sun where a body stands in it (the rooted, deep amber to red-brown),
less where a body moves in and out of shade (the walking, olive to honey),
almost none where a body lives in the ground (the ground kind, clear grey).
Age deepens the tint in all three, most visibly in the rooted. Under the
orange day a Spiralite is a warm tinted body with almost no light showing;
inside a turn, or at dusk under the Carillon, the blue-green helix shows
through. The room never carries the colour; the body does.

- **(A) One hue of light, three tints of body.** The helix is blue-green in
  every kind, since one lineage's chemistry gives one hue and the hue is the
  one that reads against orange; the kinds differ by tint, depth and where
  the light sits. **Recommended**: it is the physically honest version, and
  it keeps the light faint and the body the thing seen.
- **(B) Three hues of light, the Carillon's own.** Blue in one kind, green
  in another, violet in the third, the colours the Carillon's rings pulse in
  (`planets/corryn.md`). Renders more legibly and answers *colours* more
  fully, at the cost of a resemblance the record does not claim, between the
  people's light and the Carillon's, which a reader will take for kinship;
  the Corryn page's whole discipline is declining tidier accounts of the
  Carillon.

### What the rulings and pages force (derived)

- **Not a metazoan, and *crustacean* is a likeness.** The planet page's
  *crustacean*, *armored, segmented invertebrate life* and *armored native
  intelligence* are a survey's words for how the people look to a human eye,
  and the taxonomy page reads every such word the same way: *a likeness is a
  hint, never a template*, and a picture that turns it into the Earth animal
  *has turned the likeness into a claim the record does not make*
  (`cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`). No other people in the record is a
  metazoan. The Earth crab is therefore the attractor to be refused, not the
  kind to be drawn.
- **Native to a dense ammonia-saline sea under a thick halogen-heavy air,
  and never out of it.** The chemistry *that kills a human unprotected is,
  for Meridian's native biosphere, simply the water everyone breathes*;
  humans meet the people only through *translation instrumentation and
  armored remote proxies*, from *sealed orbital or submersible platforms*
  (`planets/meridian.md`). The people *can no more walk Tír na nÓg than a
  human can swim their seas* (`habitable-for-whom.md`). So the body is a
  water body throughout: it is never seen in air, carries nothing for air,
  and nothing about it is built for weight.
- **Two channels, both bodily.** Claw-percussion *against shell and
  structure*, audible and readable underwater at range, and a slower
  chemical signal *diffused directly into the surrounding water column*
  (`planets/meridian.md`). The codex gives the grammar: *a strike goes far
  and arrives at once and carries the shape of a thing; the water comes
  after, and slowly, and carries what the shape meant* (`what-came-in-the-water.md`).
  So the body has an organ that strikes, a surface that is struck (*shell*),
  and an organ that puts a chemical into the water.
- **It lives on and in a lattice it builds, and hears through it.** The
  reef-cities are *secreted mineral composite*, *dense, load-bearing lattice*
  laid *course on course*, and *a city built that way is also an instrument*:
  a strike on an outwork is heard across the city, and the analysts' craft is
  telling *what the city is saying from what the city is doing*, since *a
  lattice under load has a sound of its own* (`characters/sixth-course.md`;
  the codex's *the low courses are heard, not addressed*). So the body holds
  the structure, and the holding is the hearing.
- **It builds, sits in council and petitions; it has a craft.** An analyst
  (Sixth Course) and one who strikes for the council (Second Outwork) are on
  record; the council *makes a petition in the water, over sittings*, and
  one person strikes it where the channel hears. So the body can hold still
  for a long time, work a lattice with precision, and strike a pattern exactly.
- **No pronoun, no self-name, no lifespan, no conversion between a course and
  a year** (`second-outwork.md`, *What the Record Does Not Carry*). The
  people reckon by courses laid, *a weight borne*, not by any count of years
  (`what-came-in-the-water.md`). Each of these stays as the record holds it.
- **Nothing worn** (`civilian-dress.md`, Meridian sapients: nothing worn is
  proposed, and both frames show nothing worn).
- **Whose secretion the composite is: not established, and the Long Shelf
  itself declined to say** (`what-came-in-the-water.md`, *What was not
  asked*). *Living with other life* below proposes co-building with the
  fist-sized reef-builders if a page ever needs it. This section needs no
  answer and gives none; the body holds either way.

### What Meridian derives (physics applied to the page)

- **What kills a large animal on Meridian is being on the open shelf
  margin.** The page puts *apex hunters that mass several tonnes* there, *in
  coordinated hunting groups*. A people of a human's span who are nobody's
  hunters live where a several-tonne animal cannot follow: inside a lattice
  whose gaps admit them and not it. That is the ecological reason a reef-city
  is a dense load-bearing lattice and not a mound, and the reason the body is
  low, long and close-held, built to pass through courses and to hold on
  (derived; the ecology note's Meridian section carries the web).
- **In a dense sea a body is carried by the water, so limbs hold position;
  they do not bear weight.** Many short limbs that grip beat few long ones
  that stride: a current moves through a lattice, and a body that is held
  at many points along its length is one that stays where it was put and
  feels every strike that reaches the course beneath it. Long limbs would be
  levers for the current to work on (derived; it is why both frames show the
  body carried close to the structure).
- **A surface struck for speech is a surface built to be struck.** Percussion
  *against shell* means the people's own armour is a sounding surface as well
  as a guard. Overlapping plates of hard mineral carry a strike as a lattice
  does; a soft body would swallow it (derived).
- **A striker is solid.** An organ whose work is to hit a course cleanly,
  pattern for pattern, at range, is a mass on a jointed limb with a single
  blunt end. A pincer, two halves hinged at a gap, is the Earth animal's
  grasping tool and would buzz on a strike. So the forelimbs end in one
  closed piece and do not grip (derived from the page's *claw-percussion*
  and from both frames; the word *claw* is the survey's and stays).
- **Hearing is contact.** Underwater and through a mineral lattice, the
  signal arrives as vibration in the structure. A body held to the course at
  many points along its length is already the instrument, and no ear in a
  head is needed or wanted (derived from the page's *audible and readable at
  considerable range* and the analysts' craft; the organ is proposed below).
- **The chemical channel needs a chemical sense, placed where the water
  arrives first.** A slow signal diffusing through water reaches the fore end
  of a body facing the current before anything else. So the chemical sense
  sits along the fore end (derived); what it looks like is the frames'.
- **Light is dim, green and from above.** Shallow seas under a thick
  atmosphere, in turbid water full of suspended particles, with visibility of
  a few metres (both frames; the planet cover's *sickly green*). A body here
  is not built around sight, and a sense that registers light and shadow from
  above is worth more than one that forms a picture. So no large eyes, and no
  eyes that look (derived; whether the people see at all is below).

### The body (derived where marked; otherwise proposed)

   its length, about the span of an adult human's reach from the hood's rim
   to the tapered rear, and never higher off the course than a plate's depth
   (derived, both frames; the span was § 14's reading and both frames hold
   it). It has a fore end and a rear and no head on a neck.
2. **The hood.** The fore end is one smooth armoured hood, larger than any
   plate and rounded, covering whatever lies beneath it; no frame shows the
   underside, and the record does not describe it (derived, both frames; the
   underside **not established**). Proposed: the hood is the body's shield
   and its bow, the first thing the current and a strike from ahead meet.
3. **The sense-pits.** A single row of small dark pits along the hood's
   lower rim, close-set, flush with the plate, none in pairs, none turned to
   anything (derived, both frames). Proposed: they are the chemical sense,
   the organ that reads the *water after*; they also register light and
   shadow from above, and form no picture. So a Meridian person is never
   *looking*, and a portrait has no gaze to turn off-frame; § 41's rule
   *attention is the claw* is the body's own fact. Whether the people see in
   any fuller sense is **not established**, and nothing in the record asks it.
4. **The plates.** Overlapping plates of hard mineral along the back, each
   lapping the next toward the rear, matte, scratched and uneven, dwindling
   toward the tail (derived, both frames). They are the *shell* the page's
   percussion is struck against: a person's own back is a sounding surface,
   and a strike on a neighbour's plates is an address (derived from the page;
   the use proposed). Proposed: the plates are not shed. They are laid down
   and thickened through a life, as a course is, so the scratches stay and
   an older person is a more scored one; that is why both frames show worn
   armour and no new one. This keeps the Earth animal's moult out of the
   people and reads with the codex's *a course is a weight borne*. **Not
   established:** how a person grows, how long, and whether the hood is laid
   like the plates.
5. **The forelimbs.** Two, heavier than the rest, jointed in three plated
   segments, reaching forward and down from under the hood; each ends in one
   solid closed piece with no hinge, no gap, no serration and no fingers
   (derived, both frames; the end is slightly tapered or pestle-round,
   between persons). Their work is to strike, on the lattice and on shell,
   and they do not grip (derived, `planets/meridian.md`, *claw-percussion*;
   both frames show them laid on or raised over a course and holding
   nothing). The record's word for them is *claw*, and it stands as the
   survey's (derived). Proposed: a person strikes with one and keeps the
   other on the course, and the one kept down is listening; the two frames
   show both postures, both flat between strikes and one raised to strike.
6. **The walking limbs.** Many short jointed limbs beneath the plates along
   the body's length, in pairs, each ending in a hard point that hooks the
   lattice (derived, both frames, for the visible side; the pairing is
   proposed from the plan's symmetry). The record fixes no count; the Sixth
   Course frame shows about ten on one side, and a prompt says *many* and no
   number. Proposed: they are the hearing. Held to the course at ten or
   twelve points, a person feels a strike anywhere in the city as the lattice
   carries it, and the analysts' craft is a craft of the limbs, not of a
   head: *which strikes are speech, which are the city settling, and which
   are the sea* is told by what reaches the points, in what order, at what
   strength. A Meridian person who lets go of the city is one who has
   stopped listening.
7. **The underside and what it takes from the water.** No frame shows it
   and the record does not describe it. **Not established:** how the body
   takes what it needs from the water, where, and what it eats (the ecology
   note's Meridian section proposes the food, and leaves the organ). A
   portrait never shows the underside, and a prompt describes none.
8. **The chemical channel.** The slower signal is *diffused directly into
   the surrounding water column* (derived, `planets/meridian.md`). Proposed:
   it is put into the water from beneath the plates, along the body, so that
   it leaves on the current and arrives after the strike, which is the
   grammar the codex gives. No organ is named, and a portrait shows nothing
   of it: the water carries no visible trace, and a prompt that lets it
   draws dye or bioluminescence.
9. **Colour and finish.** Mineral grey, matte, with a fine bloom; never
   glossy though always wet, since a wet gloss is the generator's tell and
   the body is never out of water (derived, both frames and § 14's reading).
   Between persons the grey runs from dark to grey-green (derived, the two
   frames). Proposed: the bloom is a film the water lays on a plate that is
   not shed, and varies with where a person works; it tells persons apart,
   not kinds. No pattern, no marking, no colour that signals (proposed; the
   record gives none and the modesty and tone bars want none).
10. **Variation.** Colour and bloom, the set and wear of the plates, the
    shape of the forelimb's end, and length tell one person from another
    (derived, the two frames differ in exactly these). Nothing in the record
    tells an age, a sex or a role from the body, and this section proposes
    none: *a people's designed body* is his, and the frames show two persons
    and no more.

### Traps closed

- **The crab and the lobster.** Pincers, stalked eyes, antennae, a fan tail:
  § 41's first round drew all four from a text prompt. The body has one
  solid end on each forelimb, no eyes above the rim, nothing on stalks and a
  tapered rear. Say *one closed piece, no hinge, no gap* and name the Earth
  crustacean in the negative.
- **The isopod and the trilobite.** The honest nearest Earth form of a long,
  low, plated body on many short limbs. The hood larger than any plate, the

- **The monster.** The 22 September rule's worse failure, and the one a
  raised claw invites. The claw is blunt, closed and still, raised over a
  course and never over a body; nothing is turned to the lens; the pits are
  a row, never two eyes; and the negative names *menacing, pincers open,
  claws grabbing, kaiju, sea monster*.
- **The face.** Two pits in a pair, or a pair set apart from the row, read
  as eyes and make a face. The row is single, even and close-set, and the
  prompt says *none in pairs*.
- **The coral reef.** Blue water, sunlight, fish and weed arrive with the
  word *reef*. The city is grown grey-green mineral lattice in dim green
  turbid water with nothing swimming in it; the prompt says *reef-city* only
  with *lattice* beside it and negates the tropical reef.
- **The ruin and the drowned city.** A lattice of grey struts under a sea
  reads as a wreck or a sunken town. The courses are *grown and not cut*, the
  newest plainly laid on the older, the joins clean, and the city is at
  work.
- **The human hand.** Not a risk this body runs: there are no fingers to
  draw. But a forelimb *resting on* a course can come back as one; let it be
  laid flat or raised, and say *holding nothing*.
- **The render.** Ask for a real armoured surface with its bloom, scratches
  and unevenness, turbid water with particles, a wide lens on a remote
  housing, grain in the green shadows, a touch under-exposed.

## Living with other life — companions, working animals and commensals, people by people

Written 14 October 2026 at Dermot's direction, verbatim: *"Pets and companion
creatures for non-humanoid peoples"*. Every line is marked. **Derived** means
the record already says or plainly implies it, and the page is cited.
**Proposed** means a choice made here, and it is his to take or leave.
**Not established** means the record is silent, and this note leaves it so
rather than filling it. Nothing here is written to `src/`.

**What the question is asking.** The human word *pet* bundles five things:
an animal kept close, fed, named, owned, and kept for company rather than for
work. The record's own pets are human-side and are a legal category before
they are anything else. A [Smart Pet](/star-rangers/glossary/smart-pet/) is an
animal carrying a certified cognitive subsystem in a collar, under a welfare
regime written because the animal cannot consent. Its kin on Eden are cats and
rabbits carried by people. So the question per people is which of the five
has any footing, and what the relation is called when it is not *pet*.
[Cross-Biosphere Taxonomy](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/)
already settles the first word: *animal* travels across biospheres as a
declared translation, because what it predicts (a mobile, sensing, behaving
organism that is not a person) holds on every surveyed world. Whether *pet*
travels the same way is the first open choice below.

**The method for anything proposed.** It is the body note's method, turned on
the animals.

1. The creature comes from the people's own biosphere and is built from that
   world's medium and chemistry outward.
2. It is legible in the people's own dominant sense. A vibration people's
   animal is read through substrate, a heat people's through warmth.
3. It has a reason to be near the people before it has an affectionate one.
   The vessik is the model: a granary tenant first, a fracture alarm second,
   and fond feeling only on Deck 7, where it is *unmistakably not theirs*.
4. It is named by what it does, never after an Earth animal. If it is ever
   drawn, the prompt negates the nearest Earth animal by name, under the
   12 October convention (`images.md`).
5. No space-dog, no dragon kept as a pet, no cute monster. Monsters are filed
   elsewhere entirely, as meta-dimensional, and are rare and valid only under
   boundary conditions (CLAUDE.md). They are never kept, tamed or befriended
   on any page. [The Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/) wear *the
   shape of a companion animal* among other founded shapes. So a companion
   creature in prose must be plainly founded: ordinary biology from its own
   world, nothing uncanny about it, and no hint that it is more than it is.

### Chthonari — the Undersong Belt

- **Derived.** The vessik is a burrow commensal, *"small, low, six-limbed"*
  and built on the same substrate-sensing principle as the Chthonari. It is
  *tolerated in the walls* for a granary reason and for a structural one: *"a

- **Derived.** A vessik is *"not livestock, not a companion animal under any
  Compact schedule, and not a Smart Pet"*. Thrum reached Eden *"in a sealed
  transit burrow, in the luggage of"* a Chthonari engineer, and stayed
  (`thrum.md`). Why she carried one is **not established**.
- **Derived.** The vessik cannot be charmed, and `mind-design.md` (the
  Chthonari, first answer) reads that as the people's own virtue at animal
  scale.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. The relation is with the burrow, not with a person.
  The vessik lives in the structure the people listen to, and its stillness
  is part of what the structure says. *Commensal*, the record's own word, is
  exact.
- **Proposed.** The Chthonari count the vessik as part of a burrow's

- **Welfare.** Thrum's own *Open Question* is the record's statement of the

- **Chapters and Undercover Pets.** Already in. Thrum is the thread's one
  non-human people's animal, and its value is that it is the *one clean

### Veyr Basaltborn — Veyr-3

- **Not established.** No page names any non-sapient life on Veyr-3.
  `veyr-basaltborn.md` gives the world (heavy gravity, active basalt, mineral
  aerosol, constant micro-shock) and the people's senses: distributed
  compound eyes tuned to contrast and motion in haze, and limb-tips that read
  resonance. *"If a panel sounds wrong under strain, a Veyr trusts the
  resonance."*
- **Proposed: a living plumb, tended and not kept.** A sessile lattice-grower
  of the basalt crust. It grows slowly in fine mineral struts, as the Veyr's
  own frame does, and it re-sets its growth toward the true vertical after
  every shock, so its layers record the ground's shock history. Built from
  the world outward: heavy gravity favours a low, braced organism, and
  constant aerosol favours a filter-feeder on settling mineral dust. Matched
  to the senses: its alignment shows as a contrast line to compound eyes, and
  its struts ring true or false under a limb-tip. A people who *calibrate by
  material behaviour rather than by instrument* would keep colonies at a
  workshop as the reference that does not drift. Any fondness for it is a
  craftsman's fondness for a true tool.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. On the proposal, the relation is calibration. The
  people's temperament as the record gives it (*composure*, *tolerant of
  noise*) supplies no reason for keeping animals for company, and none is
  invented.
- **Chapters.** A Veyr Ranger's quarters could hold a colony without
  breaking anything, since it is sessile and asks nothing of a habitat's
  welfare regime. It is a detail for a Qiren Tal scene, not a thread.

### Serephine Dunekin — Kharis Belt IV

- **Not established.** No page names any other Kharis life.
  `serephine-dunekin.md` fixes the two lethal extremes each day, the
  closed form and the open form, the crepuscular rhythm, and *buried thermal
  mass used as a battery*.
- **Proposed: fellow waiters.** On a world where everything alive must avoid
  the same two lethal hours, the life that shares the Serephine's day shares
  their shelters. Buried thermal mass is warm through the cold and cool
  through the heat for whatever lives in it. The proposed relation is
  co-sheltering: organisms that close when the people close and open at the

  creature is matched to the light-scattering eye membranes. It is read by
  how it opens in the raked light of the margins, the one time a Serephine
  is also open.
- **Does "pet" fit?** It mislabels the relation. Company on Kharis happens in
  the windows and nowhere else, and *"the safe hours are for living"*
  (`serephine-dunekin.md`). Something that waited out the heat beside you
  shares your day. It is not kept.
- **Chapters.** None needed. If Sethka Ru's file ever wants a home detail,
  this is the one available.

### Pelagene Littoral — Nerath

- **Not established.** No page names any other Nerath life.

  symmetry about the vertical, and the rule that *"nothing on Nerath ever
  stays where it was put"*.
- **Derived, and it decides the question.** A kept animal needs a place to be
  kept in, and the Pelagene have no fixed places, no stable inventory and no
  landmarks. *Keeping*, the core of *pet*, is the fixed state the record
  says they are structurally poor at.
- **Proposed: the drift-fellow.** The relation that fits is accompaniment.
  Small organisms ride the same layer interface a Pelagene holds, trimming
  at the same depth. Each is legible by the wake and the chemical trace it
  leaves, which a whole-body chemical sense reads without looking. What a
  Pelagene would value is *who holds the band with you*, which is a rate and
  a direction. A possession is neither.
- **Does "pet" fit?** No. Accompaniment is the word.
- **Welfare and Undercover Pets.** A Nerath organism could not cross to a
  station casually. It would need its own layered fluid carried with it, so
  no Thrum-like stowaway is plausible, and none should be written.

### What holds across the peoples

Four things hold across the record. First, no non-human people is shown
keeping an animal for company alone. Every derived relation has a working
reason or none: the vessik in the walls, the thornback shells, the Ollune
lineages, the Verdani cultivation and herds, the runners left to their
ground. Second, the relation is with a place more often than with a person:
the vessik with the burrow, the lumenspires with the settlement, the
lineages with the shoal, the runners with their own ground. Third, each
people reads other life in its own medium, and so would read a human's pet
in its own medium too. To a Chthonari a Smart Pet is too light to feel. To
an Ilveth it is a lamp, and to an Ovruhn a heartbeat. None of them sees a
face. Fourth, the welfare frameworks are human-side, written for collars and
subsystems, and a non-human people's animal on a human habitat falls through
all of them. Thrum's page already states that, and it holds for any second
case. *Pet* is the human case, one relation among the relations. Like the
human Ranger's culture, it is never the baseline the others are measured from
(CLAUDE.md, the 28 September usage rule).

## Minds they make — machine and virtual intelligence, people by people (drafted 14 October 2026)

Dermot's question, verbatim: *"Machine intelligence or virtual intelligence as
used and developed, if any, by non-humanoid peoples."* This section answers it
people by people, from the record. Every line is marked **DERIVED** (with the
page it comes from), **PROPOSED** (a choice, his to overrule), or **not
established** (with the reason the absence is the right shape, where it is).
Nothing here is written to `src/`; the open items at the end are his.

**Two terms, fixed first.** *Machine intelligence* here means an agentic made
mind, the thing the Kernel governs. *Virtual intelligence* has two readings,
and the record answers both. (a) **A machine that computes without deciding**:
the record's range of postures runs *"from none, through instruments that
compute without deciding, to traditions the Concord would recognise as peers"*
(DERIVED, `the-makers-shape.md`), so this is a real point on the range, and
several peoples below sit on it. (b) **A simulated person**: refused. A
simulated person who answers back and does not say what it is, is the
universal-translator pretence *"given a body and a room to stand in"*
(DERIVED, `what-the-record-refuses.md`, the holodeck clause). No people below
is given one.

**The four rules every sub-block obeys** (all DERIVED):

- **A made mind is made from its makers.** Each people's machines, if any,
  carry that people's senses, language and priorities; there is no
  species-neutral machine and no archetype for AI as distinct from sapience
  (`the-makers-shape.md`, `made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype.md`).
- **Declining is a complete answer.** The Krenyi build none, *"needing no AI
  and wanting none are, to them, one fact rather than two"* (`krenyi.md`);
  no point on the range is ahead of any other.
- **The safety shape converges; nothing else does.** Nearly every
  machine-building culture arrives at conditional obedience, lawful refusal,
  harm weighed at outcomes and deception forbidden to the machine. The
  archetype explains that convergence and enforces nothing
  (`made-minds-and-the-ai-safety-archetype.md`).
- **The Kernel is the Concord's and claims nothing wider.** Some alien
  civilisations run approval regimes of their own; some run none; at least
  one fields beneficial machines in a variety no Concord gate would have
  passed, filed as *unusual* and nothing worse (`ai-safety-kernel.md`,
  *Commissioning*). **Which faction that is stays open**
  (`intake-2026-07-28.md`, third direction: *"don't resolve it in a lore
  page"*), and nothing below names one.

### Cerebraun and the Hegemony

- **What the record says.** The Hegemony runs automated survey

  mind that is already several still keeps a companion: Sen is three people
  at one desk and keeps Marginalia anyway (DERIVED, `characters/sen.md`). So
  nine centres is no reason to need no machine. Whether the Cerebraun build
  agentic minds is **not established**.
- **Form, if they do (PROPOSED).** The biology gives the shape before the
  doctrine does. A Cerebraun limb-ganglion has *"localized preference and
  mood"* and can act against the central brain, which Cerebraun physicians
  treat *"as ordinary rather than pathological"* (DERIVED, `cerebraun.md`). To
  that body, a part that can decline the central voice is not alarming. A
  Cerebraun-made mind would be built as a centre among centres, reporting on
  two channels (a stated position and a confidence display), the way a
  Cerebraun does. Hegemony *doctrine* says the limbs execute and do not
  bargain (DERIVED, `cerebraun-hegemony.md`); which of the two its machine law
  follows is **open choice 1**.
- **Kernel relation.** Outside it. The Hegemony is in neither the Concord nor
  the Federation (DERIVED, `federation-of-sentient-beings.md`); whether it
  runs a regime of its own is **not established**.
- **Meeting the Corps.** Ranger vessels in Hegemony space carry
  Kernel-compliant systems by charter (DERIVED, `ai-safety-kernel.md`, *The
  Kernel and the Star Rangers*). The documented meeting is already the
  Sentinel: a Hegemony classifier's noise bin, and a human ear. **Trap
  closed:** the *Veritas Report*'s obedient machines

### What holds across the peoples

Read together, the peoples sit across the whole range The Maker's Shape
describes, and no one of them is ahead. **One incorporates agentic machines on
the record** (the Chthonari), and **one runs systems whose agency is not
established** (the Hegemony's classifiers and channel). **Several use
instruments that compute without deciding** (the Ovruhn and Serephine on the
record; probably the Verdani and Ilveth), each where its own sense or its own
hours run out. **Some show no use for one** (Prismeri, Ollune), because the
world already gave them what a machine would supply, or made the machine too
heavy to keep. **One has no machines and no concept that maps** (the Told), and
**three are unknown** (Meridian, Spiralites, Doreth), where the absence is
marked rather than filled. The rule under the pattern is the Maker's Shape's
own, *you can read a people in what they automate*: each people automates
what its senses or its world could not cover, and nothing else.

**How the Kernel reaches non-human builders** is narrow and exact. It binds
them inside Concord jurisdiction, as it binds the Chthonari there. It binds
every system on a Ranger vessel, whoever fabricated it. It protects every
non-human, everywhere a Concord system acts, under the first invariant.
Beyond that it claims nothing. Where an alien machine law resembles it, the
archetype explains the resemblance and enforces none of it; and a Federation
member's machine law, if any, is that member's business, since no member's
internal governance is any other's (DERIVED, `federation-of-sentient-beings.md`).
The Federation also counts synthetic peoples among its constituents (DERIVED,
same page). Those are peoples, not minds anyone made, and this section does
not develop them.

## In the Corps — non-human peoples in the Star Rangers, people by people

Written 14 October 2026 at Dermot's direction, verbatim: *"Non-humanoid people
in the Star Rangers organisation"*. It builds on the four people-by-people
sections ruled earlier the same day (`civilian-dress.md`, *Peoples in their own

take or leave. **Not established** means the record is silent, and this note
leaves it so. Nothing here is written to `src/`.

**The frame, from CLAUDE.md and the pages it points to** (all DERIVED). Humans
are not privileged in the Corps, and a human Ranger's culture is one case among
the cases; *alien* names only a being unknown to the Rangers, so no one in this
section is an alien to the Corps (the 28 September usage rule). No branch is
military; only the Frontier Corps polices, and only the Provost Corps does the
work of military police (`star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md`, *What the Rangers
Are Not*). Every Ranger on duty is in uniform for the hospital's reason, with no
plain-clothes role, and other peoples have uniform equivalents in their own
media (`rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`, *Why a Uniform at All*). Ranger service
is *"individual and apolitical"* and never a quota (`chthonari.md`). Cadets
come in by a planetary recruiter's recommendation, the colleges confirm Cadet
standing, and certification is examined on grounds that *"credit no one's
physiology"* (`star-rangers-academy.md`; `krenyi.md`).

**The standard the Corps already built for this** (DERIVED,
`rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`). The field uniform is *"a modular shell over a
thermal-and-pressure liner, not a single garment cut to one body plan"*. Every
mark is a magnetically seated tab or plate, *"sized and positioned per body plan
by the wearer's own quartermaster record"*. Marks are *"shapes, not words"*,
because a service carrying Krenyi, Pelagene, Veyr and Serephine officers
*"cannot put its chain of command into any one people's alphabet"*. The tab is
legible *"to an eye, a sensor or a limb-tip"* (`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`,
*Why the Tab Does Not Translate*), so it can be read by touch as well as by
sight. Each people renders the ladder in its own words, and *"in any
disagreement between a rendering and a tab, the tab is right"* (same page).

### What holds across the peoples

**Where they serve follows the sense, and is never a quota.** Every placement
on record is the work a people's body makes cheap: Chthonari in fold
scaffolds, Veyr where stress meets heat, Ilveth in the dark, Ovruhn where
hearing through things is the job, Pelagene in the plumbing, Serephine where
margins decide, Krenyi at the boundary desk. The record says twice, in two
voices, that this is neither tokenism nor assignment (`veyr-basaltborn.md`;
`chthonari.md`). Nobody is posted by species, and the pattern is what
individuals choosing well looks like.

**Every Ranger's mark is the Corps' on duty; a people's own mark is theirs off
it** (DERIVED for the Veyr and Pelagene rulings; PROPOSED as the general rule).
On duty the shell, the corps colour and the tab say role, because one service
cannot read every people's medium at a doorway. A people's own mark, the struck name,
the warm-write, the chorus, the kinstrain name, does not compete with the tab
and is not suppressed by it. It is simply not the uniform. The renderings of
the ladder are the same arrangement in language: each is a courtesy, and the
tab is right.

**A draft principle: the Corps fits its provision to the Ranger, and never its
standard** (PROPOSED, from DERIVED instances). The record supports the first
half everywhere it looks. The shell is modular *because* of the Veyr and the
Serephine. Marks are seated per body plan by the wearer's own quartermaster
record. Ilveth keep chilled quarters, Pelagene run hydration cycles, Verdani
light is carried as standard, Prismeri contracts write in light and corridor
geometry, and the Serephine are scheduled around. The second half is just as
plain. Certification credits no one's physiology, and a Krenyi sits the same
examination as anyone (`krenyi.md`). Qiren's refusal to certify held for six
weeks because a rating is a rating (S05E01C01). And the principle has a stated
edge where provision runs out. *"What no contract can supply is a ceiling worth
hanging from"* (`prismeri.md`). The Serephine cost of emergencies *"is not
free"* (`serephine-dunekin.md`). No facility holds an Ollune and a human
unsuited (`ollune.md`). Where the Corps cannot fit a Ranger it says so, and the
cost stays on the record rather than being charged to the Ranger. Stated as one
line for a page that needs it: **the Corps adapts to the Ranger in everything
it provides, in nothing it certifies, and says where it cannot.** Choice 5 asks
whether to take it. First on a page 29 October 2026: `src/lore/humans.md`,
*In the Corps*.

**What the Corps must provide comes in five kinds** (DERIVED from the blocks
above). *Medium*: a sealed burrow, a cold room, humid high pressure, a warm
hydrogen hull. *Light*: the full spectrum for the Verdani, Prismere's spectrum,
none at all for an Ilveth's work. *Gravity and mass*: low-gravity hubs for the
Chthonari, a beam for the Prismeri, clearance for the Ovruhn, the Veyr's
restraint in light gravity. *Time*: the Serephine's two windows, the Verdani
metabolic price of haste. *Channel*: structure-borne for the Chthonari,
click-speech for the Ilveth, the under-song heard as plain acoustics. Rank
reading needs none of these, since the tab is legible to an eye, a sensor or a
limb-tip.

**Who cannot serve, and why, is never a ranking** (DERIVED, `what-the-record-refuses.md`,
the ladder refused). The Told do not travel in space and have not asked. The
Meridian sapients have never been met except through a proxy. The Ollune
cannot share a room. The Cerebraun's polity does not fund what it does not
command, and no individual is on record. Each is a fact about a world or a
polity. None is a fact about a mind.

**Recruitment follows contact, then becomes the people's own** (DERIVED in
part). Recruiters are serving or retired Rangers who live where they recruit
(`star-rangers-academy.md`; `planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters.md`). The
Chthonari already have their own recruiters. PROPOSED: a newly met people's
first Rangers come in by the survey's contact, as Suvra Kel did, and the
recruiting passes to that people's own retired Rangers within a generation.
No recruiter works on a people who have not asked.

**The service ages are a human case** (DERIVED, choice 1). The limit of about
a hundred and ten is set where *"the eleventh decade's borrowed capability runs
out"* (`star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md`), which is a fact about human
medicine (`planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters.md`). Syra is about 150 and on the
boundary desk in 2831. So the record already holds the ages as the human
instance of a rule whose other instances it has not written.

### His choices — ruled 14 October 2026

**RULED 14 October 2026: all five as recommended, option A** (Dermot, verbatim: *"Yes Options A approved for all"*). Choice 1 is realised the same day as a clarification of `star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md` (the ages are the human case; other peoples' equivalents are set by the Medical Corps).


1. **The service ages.** (A) The ages on the command page (about 55 for a
   Liaison posting, 75 for a pension, 110 the limit) are the human instance.
   Each people's equivalents are set from its own physiology by the Medical
   Corps, whose finding the command ladder cannot override. Written later as a
   clarification of `star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md`. (B) The ages bind
   every Ranger as written. **Recommended: A.** The page grounds the limit in
   human medicine, and Syra at about 150 on the boundary desk in 2831 cannot
   stand under B. The two commitments cannot both hold, so this is a genuine
   dilemma, and A resolves it by clarification.
2. **A Cerebraun in the Corps.** (A) Keep the ladder page's marked absence:
   the record does not say whether any Cerebraun has served. (B) State that
   individual Cerebraun may serve and some have, as individuals outside any
   Hegemony channel. **Recommended: A.** The absence is published, it is the
   Hegemony's distance shown in one line, and nothing needs it filled. B stays
   available for a chapter that wants a Cerebraun Ranger.
3. **An Ollune in the Corps.** (A) Not established, with the reason marked: no
   shared facility, and Cirrane's gift to the Corps is method. The charter does
   not close the door. (B) An Ollune serves from a hull kept at Cirrane's
   conditions, carrying near words, its tab seated on the hull. **Recommended:
   A.** B needs a vessel nobody has built and a role the Corps already fills by
   courier. A keeps both open without inventing either.
4. **What a Prismeri "Ranger liaison posting" is.** (A) A guild member
   seconded to the Corps under the compact's contract, not a sworn Ranger, with
   individual Prismeri free to enlist and none on record. (B) Prismeri Rangers
   serving in the Survey Corps under the ordinary ladder. **Recommended: A.**
   Every Prismeri line on record runs through the guilds and the compact, and
   the ladder page lists them among the peoples who *work with* the Corps.
   **Narrowed 29 October 2026** (Dermot, verbatim: *"Only the Academy would
   needed seconded or external faculty as instructors but would create a
   conflict of interests and neutrality if used for General Operations or
   Special Missions"*). A seconded guild member, or anyone external, may serve
   only as Academy faculty: an instructor at a college or school, beside the
   serving officers who hold teaching postings (`star-rangers-academy.md`).
   Never on a general operations or special purpose mission, where an
   outside allegiance would be a conflict of interest and a breach of the
   Corps' neutrality. So a Prismeri on a Corps field posting is a Ranger,
   sworn, under the ordinary ladder (as Elsha Rootkin is for the Verdani);
   a Prismeri *"Ranger liaison posting"* held by a guild member is a teaching
   posting at the Academy; and the guild's fold-route confirmation for the
   Survey Corps stays what `prismeri.md` says it is, work the guild does
   under the compact as its own service, not guild members embedded in a
   mission. Recorded only here and in `open-questions.md`; a lore page
   changes when a chapter or a character page first needs it.
5. **The draft principle.** (A) Adopt it in this note as the standing reading:
   *the Corps adapts to the Ranger in everything it provides, in nothing it
   certifies, and says where it cannot*. It goes to a page only when one needs
   it. (B) Leave it unstated, since every instance is already on its own page.
   **Recommended: A.** It gathers instances the record already holds, it
   answers a reader's obvious question about so many body plans in one
   service, and its third clause keeps the costs on the record, where the
   Serephine and Prismeri pages already put them.
